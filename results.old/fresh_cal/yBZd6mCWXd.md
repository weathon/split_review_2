## Summary

This paper introduces Weakly Incremental 3D object Detection (WI3D), a new task that extends a 3D detector to novel classes using only 2D visual prompts — eliminating the need for 3D annotations on novel classes during incremental learning. The proposed framework combines a class-agnostic Pseudo-label Refinement (PRF) module that denoises coarse 2D-to-3D projected boxes, Cross-modal Knowledge Transfer (CKT) via bipartite matching to align 2D and 3D region features, and Reweighting Knowledge Distillation (RKD) for base-class preservation. Experiments on SUN RGB-D and ScanNet under multiple incremental settings show the method outperforms baselines including an adapted SDCoT.

## Strengths

- **First formalization of the WI3D task.** The paper clearly distinguishes WI3D from prior class-incremental 3D detection (which requires continual 3D annotations) and from open-vocabulary 3D detection (which does not address incremental forgetting). This task formulation is a concrete contribution that opens a practical direction for label-efficient 3D incremental learning.

- **PRF module demonstrably improves pseudo-label quality (Table 3).** The refinement module (without BCH) raises novel-class mAP by +3.25% over coarse labels; adding BCH yields another +0.96%. The ablation on PRF inputs (Table 4) cleanly shows that both box coordinates and point-cloud context are necessary — neither alone suffices.

- **RKD preserves base-class knowledge better than alternatives (Table 7).** RKD achieves 51.52 mAP_base vs. 45.66 for KL-divergence (Hinton et al.) and 49.27 for L2 logit distillation (Zhao & Lee). This supports the claim that not all features should be distilled equally, and that objectness-based reweighting helps.

- **CKT with bipartite matching improves novel-class detection (Table 6).** CKT adds +1.43% mAP_novel, while a naive one-to-many assignment hurts (−0.35%), validating the design choice of bipartite matching for cross-modal feature alignment.

- **Consistent gains across settings, datasets, and 2D teachers (Tables 1, 2, 5).** Results are reported for 5 and 10 novel classes on SUN RGB-D and 3 and 4 on ScanNet. Robustness to different 2D teachers (Faster R-CNN, Ground Dino, human annotations) is demonstrated, with substantial gains in all cases (e.g., +3.74 base mAP, +9.49 novel mAP for Ground Dino).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **PRF training data generation is unspecified.** The paper states (Section 3.5) "we train PRF on $C_{base}$ in this stage, where $\mathcal{L}_{PRF} = \mathcal{L}_{box}$," but never explains how the *coarse input boxes* fed to PRF are obtained during base-class training. The PRF takes a coarse 3D box and point cloud as input and predicts an offset. Without knowing whether coarse boxes are generated by perturbing ground-truth boxes, using detector proposals, or some other mechanism, the training pipeline cannot be reproduced. This is the most critical gap: the refinement module is central to the method, and its training signal is described, but the source of its training inputs is not.

- **SDCoT adaptation is not described, weakening the key comparison.** The paper states it "modif[ies] the training of SDCoT to fit our WI3D setting" (Section 4.2) but provides no details on what modifications were made. SDCoT natively requires 3D annotations for novel classes; in WI3D no such annotations exist. Without knowing whether the adaptation is reasonable or cripples SDCoT (e.g., by giving it unfiltered noisy labels without any refinement), the claimed margin of +7.57 mAP_novel over SDCoT cannot be properly evaluated. Adding 2–3 sentences describing the adaptation would resolve this.

### Minor

- **The RKD weighting function is incomplete due to a parser artifact.** Equation (5) defines $\alpha_i$ as a "reweighting modulation factor obtained based on the proposal objectness $o_i$," but the specific functional form (e.g., $\alpha_i = o_i$, $\alpha_i = \sigma(o_i)$, etc.) is garbled in the text ("denoted Keoi eoi"). The concept is clear — weigh feature/logit distillation by objectness — but the exact formula is missing.

- **The 2D region encoder $\mathcal{E}^{2D}$ used in CKT is not specified.** The paper says "a pretrained image region encoder" (Section 3.4) but never states which model (CLIP, DINO, a detector backbone, etc.). The choice directly affects the quality of transferred features and reproducibility.

- **No variance statistics reported.** All results are single-run. Given the method's dependence on noisy pseudo labels, reporting means and standard deviations over multiple runs would strengthen confidence in the results.

- **Inconsistency between text and equation in CKT loss description.** The paper says CKT "assigns different weights to different instance samples" (Section 3.4), but Equation (4) is a plain sum over matched pairs with uniform weight. The text and equation do not align.

### Trivial

- The caption for Table 3 contains a typo: "pesudo" should be "pseudo."
- The paper uses heuristically set loss weights ($\beta_1=1, \beta_2=10, \beta_3=5$) without sensitivity analysis; while not required, a brief justification would be helpful.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting mAP@0.5 for at least the main SUN RGB-D setting would strengthen the evaluation, since mAP@0.25 is a lenient threshold. This is a suggestion, not a flaw — mAP@0.25 is consistent with prior incremental 3D detection work.

- The "one-to-many" baseline in Table 6 is mentioned as "built within VoteNet" but could be explained briefly (is it original VoteNet's assignment or a variant?) for clarity.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify PRF training explicitly.** State how coarse boxes are generated for PRF training on base classes (e.g., by perturbing ground-truth boxes with simulated noise, or using detector proposals). If using perturbation, describe the noise distribution and how it approximates the distribution seen from the 2D teacher.
2. **Add 2–3 sentences describing the SDCoT adaptation.** What specific changes were made (e.g., replacing 3D ground-truth with coarse/refined pseudo labels? disabling certain components?) so readers can assess the fairness of comparison.
3. **Specify the $\mathcal{E}^{2D}$ encoder model** (architecture, pre-training dataset, whether frozen or fine-tuned).
4. **Fix the RKD $\alpha_i$ formula** — provide the exact mapping from objectness $o_i$ to weight $\alpha_i$.
5. **Align the CKT loss text and equation** — either remove the claim about different weights or adjust the equation to reflect them.

## Score and Decision

The paper introduces a well-motivated new task and provides a technically sound framework with strong empirical evidence across multiple settings, datasets, and 2D teacher backbones. The weaknesses are real but surmountable — they concern missing specifics needed for reproduction rather than flaws in the core methodology or claims. The SDCoT adaptation gap and the unspecified PRF training data are the most significant issues, but both are addressable with additional description (not additional experiments).
