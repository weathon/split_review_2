## Summary

This paper tackles fine-grained Composed Video Retrieval (CVR). It contributes (1) FineCVR-1M, a new dataset of ~1M video-text triplets where modification texts are decomposed into retained, injected, and excluded components across attribute, object, scene, and action categories; and (2) FDCA, a framework that disentangles textual features at both the sentence level (via reference-guided cross-attention) and the token level (via DPC-KNN clustering into three component types), then aligns/fuses them with video features. Experiments on FineCVR-1M show that FDCA outperforms existing CVR and CIR methods.

## Strengths

- **First CVR dataset with explicit three-component modification texts.** FineCVR-1M structures modification text into retained, injected, and excluded components across four concept categories (attribute, object, scene, action). Table 1 documents this coverage; prior datasets (CoVR, EgoCRV) describe only changed elements. This is a concrete advance for fine-grained retrieval.

- **Two-level textual disentanglement (sentence + token) is novel and ablation-verified.** FDCA separates text at both sentence level (SFD, via reference-guided cross-attention, Eq. 1) and token level (ATD, via DPC-KNN clustering into component types, Eqs. 2–5). Table 3 confirms that removing either SFD or ATD degrades performance across R1/R5/R10/R50, and the full model achieves the best balance. The result that FDCA-CLIP outperforms the CoVR method fine-tuned with BLIP (Section 5.2) supports the claim that the disentanglement architecture itself, not just a stronger backbone, drives the gain.

- **Automated dataset pipeline scales to 1M+ triplets.** The construction process (Section 3) uses key-static-concept scoring (from annotations and frame detection) plus fine-tuned LLaMA2 for action differences, producing 1,010,071 triplets. This scale and the explicit three-component annotation go substantially beyond prior CVR datasets.

- **Ablation studies are well-structured.** Tables 3 and 4 systematically isolate the contribution of each module (SFD, ATD, individual loss terms) and each design choice (separate vs. unified Transformers for alignment and fusion).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Single-dataset evaluation limits generalization claims.** The paper states "All the experiments are conducted on FineCVR-1M" (Section 5.1) and claims FDCA "outperforms existing methods by a clear margin." Because FDCA and the comparison baselines are all (re-)trained and evaluated only on the authors' own dataset, it is impossible to distinguish genuine architectural superiority from dataset-specific overfitting — especially given that the three-component text structure is designed to match FDCA's architecture. Cross-dataset evaluation on existing CVR benchmarks (CoVR, EgoCRV) is necessary to demonstrate that the disentanglement approach generalizes beyond the specific construction of FineCVR-1M. This is the most significant weakness in the paper.

### Minor
- **Key hyperparameters missing from main text.** The values of β (softmax scaling in Eq. 2), margin *m* (triplet loss, Eq. 7), and λ (triplet loss weight, Eq. 8) are not reported. The triplet loss distance metric *d*(·) is specified only as "the distance metric" without identifying whether it is cosine, L2, or another choice (Eq. 7, line 161). Learning rate and batch size are also absent. These may reside in the (stripped) appendix but the main paper should at least reference the appendix for each.

- **Token-level clustering pipeline is under-specified for reproducibility.** The paper does not clarify whether the DPC-KNN clustering (Section 4.2) is performed offline (pre-computed) or integrated into the training loop; if the latter, how gradients are handled for a non-differentiable algorithm is not addressed. The positive text is generated by "removing negation words like 'instead of'" (line 110), but no procedure is given for modification texts that contain no negation words — a common case. These details are critical for reproducing the method.

- **Dataset training set lacks human quality validation.** The test set (10k triplets) is manually selected for accuracy (line 76), but the 1M training triplets — generated via a multi-stage automated pipeline (LLM-based action text generation, concept detection from noisy video frames, rule-based prompting) — receive no human evaluation for text accuracy, component coverage, or noise rate. This makes it difficult to assess how much noise the training set contains and whether FDCA's gains partly reflect learning dataset artifacts rather than robust retrieval.

- **Several design choices lack ablation or justification.**
  - Key static concepts are selected as the top 5 per type (Section 3.2.1) and DPC-KNN uses 2 clusters (Section 4.2); neither choice is ablated or motivated.
  - The size and curation of the fine-tuning set for the LLaMA2 action-difference generator are not described (Section 3.2.2).
  - The paper acknowledges bad cases in Figure 5 but does not analyze them for common failure modes (e.g., ambiguous negation, multiple objects), limiting diagnostic value.

## Suggestions

1. **Evaluate on existing CVR benchmarks.** Report results on CoVR and EgoCRV, even if those datasets do not have the three-component structure — this would demonstrate that FDCA generalizes and is not merely overfitted to FineCVR-1M's construction biases. This is the single highest-leverage improvement.
2. **Specify all key hyperparameters** in the main paper or clearly cross-reference the appendix sections that contain them (β, m, λ, the triplet loss distance metric, learning rate, batch size).
3. **Clarify the DPC-KNN training integration** — state whether clustering is performed offline or online, and if online, how gradient flow is handled for the non-differentiable clustering step.
4. **Validate a random sample of training triplets** (e.g., 500) with human raters for text accuracy and component coverage, reporting agreement statistics.
5. **Add ablation experiments** for the number of DPC-KNN clusters (2 vs. 3 vs. more) and the top-k concept selection threshold.
