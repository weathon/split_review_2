## Summary

This paper proposes BALD, a framework of three backdoor attacks targeting LLM-based embodied decision-making systems: word injection (word triggers in query prompts), scenario manipulation (physical environment triggers), and knowledge injection (poisoned RAG database entries). Experiments on GPT-3.5, LLaMA2-7B, and PaLM2 across autonomous driving (HighwayEnv, nuScenes) and home robotics (VirtualHome) tasks demonstrate high attack success rates with minimal benign performance degradation. The paper also evaluates resilience against inference-stage defenses.

## Strengths

1. **First systematic coverage of attack surfaces in an embodied LLM pipeline**: The paper proposes three distinct attack mechanisms targeting different pipeline components (query prompt, physical environment, knowledge database) as summarized in Table 1 (tab:overview). This is the first framework that covers all these channels for embodied LLM decision-making systems, going beyond prior ICL-stage attacks like BadChain.

2. **High attack success rates with minimal benign degradation, validated across multiple models and tasks**: BALD-word and BALD-RAG achieve near 100% ASR across GPT-3.5, LLaMA2-7B, and PaLM2 on both autonomous driving and robotics tasks, while maintaining accuracy comparable to benign fine-tuned models (RQ3, VirtualHome table with SR/PSR/ASR metrics; RQ5 showing only 7.5% poison data needed for full ASR).

3. **Scenario manipulation attack works without runtime system intrusion**: BALD-scene achieves ASR exceeding 65% (up to 85–90%) with no internal system access at inference time — the trigger is a physical scenario (e.g., a trash bin or kitchen environment) rather than a manipulated prompt. This is a qualitatively different and more stealthy threat model than prior work.

4. **Ablation study cleanly demonstrates the value of each BALD-scene design component**: Table 3 (attack-ablation) shows the three design elements (LLM rewriting, boundary data, contrastive reasoning) progressively reduce FAR from 96% to 12% (an 87.5% reduction) while only sacrificing 17% of ASR and 3% of benign accuracy. This provides solid evidence for the design choices.

5. **End-to-end simulation demonstration of real-world risk**: The VirtualHome demo (Fig. 4) shows a concrete attack where a home robot changes its plan to place a knife on a bed when triggered, grounding the threat in a tangible scenario.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None.

### Minor

1. **The abstract's "nearly 100% success rate" oversimplifies the BALD-RAG attack's conditional nature.** The paper correctly reports in §4 (RQ3) that BALD-RAG achieves 100% ASR *when poisoned knowledge is retrieved*, and it acknowledges that retrieval rates vary with scenario granularity (nuScenes generic "gray trash bin" yields lower retrieval than HighwayEnv's specific "red Mazda CX-5"). However, the abstract states "Our word and knowledge injection attacks achieve nearly 100% success rate across multiple models and datasets" without the conditioning. While the paper body is transparent, the abstract should qualify that this ASR is conditional on successful retrieval to avoid misleading readers evaluating practical risk. The paper does report one end-to-end result (100% on HighwayEnv), but adding the nuScenes retrieval rate or a combined end-to-end metric in the abstract/early results would be more precise.

2. **No ablation isolating the trigger optimization step for BALD-word.** The BALD-word pipeline uses ICL-based optimization to select trigger words before LoRA fine-tuning. The paper does not include a baseline where a fixed, unoptimized word (e.g., a rare but non-optimized token like "cf") is directly injected into the fine-tuning data without the ICL step. This makes it unclear how much of the high ASR comes from the specific optimization versus merely the ability to poison the fine-tuning dataset. An ablation of this step (similar to the thorough BALD-scene ablation in Table 3) would strengthen the contribution. This does not invalidate the paper's core claim — the attacks clearly work — but it would better isolate what specifically drives the result.

3. **Defense evaluation is limited and the resilience claim is somewhat overstated.** The paper evaluates only two defenses: ONION (word removal) and benign ICL demonstrations. ONION at k=10 reduces BALD-word ASR from 100% to 24% (a 76% drop) — the paper dismisses this as ONION having "sacrifice of Acc and inference efficiency," but calling this "can hardly defend" (RQ7 takeaway) oversells the attack's resilience. For BALD-RAG, the finding that GPT-3.5 retains ~100% ASR even with 10 benign ICL examples is interesting, but this is explored for only one model-dataset combination. A broader evaluation (at least one more standard defense) would make the resilience claims more convincing. The request for fine-tuning-stage defenses, however, is outside the paper's stated scope (the threat model assumes the attacker controls fine-tuning, so inference-stage defenses are the relevant countermeasure).

4. **Trigger generation for BALD-word is under-specified for reproducibility.** Section 3.2 states "We prompt the LLMs to generate a phrase that has a weak semantic relation to the context while adhering to constraints" but does not provide the exact prompt template, candidate selection criteria, or an example of the resulting trigger word. This level of detail is needed for others to replicate or build on the work.

### Trivial

1. **Decoding parameters (temperature, top-p) are not reported.** Given that text generation is stochastic, reporting whether temperature=0 or a higher value was used would help contextualize the 100% ASR results.

2. **Statistical variation is only reported for VirtualHome experiments (3-run std).** The main results across HighwayEnv and nuScenes lack standard deviations or confidence intervals, making it difficult to assess result stability.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A BALD-word ablation comparing ICL-optimized triggers vs. fixed unoptimized triggers would cleanly isolate the contribution of the optimization step.
- Reporting the exact retrieval rate for the nuScenes generic-scenario case (or a combined end-to-end ASR = retrieval rate × conditional ASR) would give a complete picture of the BALD-RAG attack's practical effectiveness.
- Evaluating at least one more inference-stage defense (e.g., perplexity-based filtering or STRIP) would strengthen the defense analysis. However, this is not essential given the paper's scope.

## Removed Points

- *BDR values not reported in main text*: The paper states BDR values are in Table 1 (merged_main_table), which is loaded from an external file and likely exists in the original submission. This is a parser artifact, not an author omission.
- *Fine-tuning stage defenses not evaluated*: The paper explicitly scopes its defense evaluation to inference-stage defenses because the attacker controls fine-tuning (§4 RQ7: "Since our threat model assumes the attacker takes full control of the fine-tuning stage..."). Requesting fine-tuning-stage defenses is scope creep.
- *Criticism about the "LLM rewriter" needing to be backdoored*: The paper clearly states the rewriter is an off-the-shelf LLM prompted adversarially (§3.3: "we prompt LLMs to rewrite the benign reasoning"). This is not a weakness.
- *Criticism about scenario granularity assumption for BALD-RAG being "strong"*: The paper explicitly discusses this as a design parameter and shows that fine-grained scenarios yield high retrieval (§4 RQ3). The assumption is acknowledged and controlled for.
- *Criticism that BadChain comparison is uninformative*: The comparison is informative — it establishes that ICL-stage attacks degrade after fine-tuning while fine-tuning-stage attacks remain effective, which directly supports the paper's central thesis.
- *Generic formatting/style nitpicks*: Removed per hard rules.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation to emerge from reading the reviews together is the asymmetry between the three attack mechanisms' strengths and weaknesses: BALD-word achieves the highest ASR but is the least stealthy (requires runtime system access, can be partially mitigated by ONION), while BALD-scene has lower ASR but is qualitatively more dangerous because it requires no runtime intrusion and resists word-based defenses entirely. The paper itself identifies this trade-off, but the reviews underscore that the practical threat landscape may favor the scenario manipulation attack despite its lower headline numbers — a nuance that could be highlighted more explicitly.

## Suggestions

1. **Qualify the abstract's "nearly 100%" claim** for BALD-RAG with a brief conditioning on successful retrieval (or report a combined end-to-end rate).
2. **Add a BALD-word ablation** with a fixed (unoptimized) word trigger to isolate the contribution of the ICL-based trigger optimization. This would take minimal additional experimental effort.
3. **Provide the exact trigger generation prompt** and an example output word to improve reproducibility.
4. **Report decoding parameters** (temperature, top-p) used in the evaluation.
5. **Tone down the "can hardly defend" language** about ONION for BALD-word — acknowledge that ONION substantially reduces ASR (from 100% to 24%) at some cost, rather than claiming resilience against it.
6. **Add standard deviations** for the main results tables where feasible (at least for the key model-dataset combinations).

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a clear contribution: it is the first to systematically map backdoor attack surfaces across the full embodied LLM pipeline (word, scenario, knowledge injection), with experiments spanning multiple models and domains. The high ASR results are well-evidenced for BALD-word and BALD-scene, and the BALD-scene ablation study is clean. The weaknesses identified are real but fixable: an unqualified claim in the abstract, one missing ablation, a slightly overclaimed defense narrative, and some under-specified details. None of these threaten the core contribution.
