## Summary

This paper presents the first systematic benchmark evaluating 25 defense methods (adversarial purification, adversarial training, and certified robustness) against 14 adversarial attacks across 7 no-reference IQA metrics, under both non-adaptive and adaptive settings. It releases a dataset of ~215,000 adversarial images and an online leaderboard for community extension. The experimental effort is large (~5000 GPU hours), the methodology is well-structured, and the paper is clearly written.

## Strengths

- **First systematic benchmark for IQA adversarial defenses with comprehensive coverage.** No prior work evaluates defenses across this many methods (25), attacks (14), and IQA metrics (7) under both adaptive and non-adaptive settings. This fills a clear gap in the literature. (Abstract; Sections 3.2–3.3; Tables 1–3)

- **Public release of an adversarial image dataset and an online leaderboard.** The benchmark shares over 215,000 adversarial images and a public leaderboard (URL provided in abstract), enabling reproducibility and allowing the community to evaluate new defenses under the same conditions. (Abstract; Sections 3.4, 5)

- **Methodology for adapting regression IQA metrics to classification-based certified defenses.** The paper divides the continuous score range into 12 quantized classes, enabling certified smoothing methods (originally designed for classification) to apply to IQA, and defines a "certified relative delta" for regression-based certified defenses. (Section 3.3, "Certified defenses"; Table 3)

- **Meaningful adaptive-versus-non-adaptive comparison.** The evaluation reveals that simple defenses (e.g., Flip) perform well when the attacker is unaware but degrade catastrophically under adaptive attacks (D_score jumps from 9.78% to 88.30%), while randomized defenses (e.g., DiffPure, Rotate) are comparatively robust — a practically relevant finding. (Table 1, columns "Non-adaptive case" vs. "Adaptive case"; Results Section)

- **Hyperparameter-controlled attack and defense strengths for fairer comparison.** Each attack is run at three strength levels and each parameterized defense with five parameter sets, allowing isolation of attack-intensity effects. (Section 3.2, Figure 2; Section 3.3)

- **Confidence intervals reported honestly.** The paper transparently notes that wide confidence intervals arise from high variability across attacks and metrics, and it does not overclaim statistical significance. (Table 1; Results Section, paragraph 1)

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Adversarial training evaluation is too limited to support the unified benchmark framing.** Adversarial training is tested on only 2 of 7 IQA metrics (LINEARITY and KONCEPT) and against a single attack type (APGD), while the other 23 defense methods are evaluated on all 7 metrics. The paper acknowledges this limitation (Section 3.4: "Due to the computational complexity of the adversarial training, we selected only two CNN-based NR IQA metrics"), and results are relegated to the supplementary material. However, this creates an asymmetry: the 25-method count in the abstract conflates methods with very different evaluation scopes. Readers cannot compare adversarial training against purification defenses on the same metrics in the main results. The benchmark would be stronger if adversarial training were either (a) evaluated on more metrics with a lighter attack, or (b) clearly positioned as a separate, preliminary investigation rather than part of the unified comparison.

- **Test sets for black-box attacks (n=50) and certified defenses (n=10) are too small for reliable conclusions.** The paper acknowledges computational constraints (Section 3.4), but the certified defense results in Table 2 (n=10) are particularly concerning. With only 10 images, reported standard deviations likely underestimate true variability, and comparisons across methods may be noise-dominated. The black-box attack set of 50 images is somewhat better but still modest given the large variance observed in the main results. This limits the reliability of the rankings drawn from these subsets.

### Minor

- **The Q_score metric (SSIM + PSNR/40) is ad hoc and unjustified.** The division of PSNR by 40 is arbitrary — no rationale is provided for this scaling, and different choices would change quality-based rankings. The paper does partially acknowledge the metric's limitations ("this method does not properly reflect visual differences between images in case of spatial transformations," Section 3.4), but the scaling choice itself is not defended. Since Q_score is included in the main comparison table (Table 1) alongside D_score and SROCC, the arbitrariness weakens conclusions about which defenses best preserve image quality.

- **Attack parameter alignment across families is not validated.** The paper states "We adjust varied parameters to align attacks' strengths" (Table 1 caption) and shows a schematic procedure (Figure 2), but it never explains how "strength" is measured or whether the weak/medium/strong levels are actually comparable across different attack families (e.g., I-FGSM vs. One Pixel vs. cAdv). Without calibration, some attacks may be unfairly stronger or weaker due to parameter choice, inflating variance and making cross-attack comparisons noisy.

- **Missing entries in the adaptive case (Table 1: JPEG, Color Quantization) are not explicitly explained.** The paper mentions integrating "differentiable defenses" into the attacked metric (Section 3.2), which implies JPEG and Color Quantization are omitted because they are non-differentiable. This should be stated explicitly in the table caption or the results section to avoid reader confusion.

- **"No defenses are effective across all scenarios" is a modest finding.** Given the high variability (large confidence intervals, different metrics, different attacks), this conclusion is near-trivially true. A more informative finding would identify defense characteristics (e.g., randomness, denoising strength) that correlate with robustness across scenarios, which the paper touches on only qualitatively.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Per-metric results (beyond averaging).** Averaging across all 7 IQA metrics may hide important interaction effects (e.g., which defenses work best for which metric types). Reporting per-metric rankings or heatmaps would increase the benchmark's actionable value.
- **Statistical significance testing.** Given the wide confidence intervals, pairwise significance tests (or a note that most differences are not significant) would prevent readers from over-interpreting small gaps in the rankings.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting cross-pattern from the reviews and the paper is the asymmetry between "simple but brittle" defenses (Flip, basic spatial transforms) that dominate non-adaptive settings but fail catastrophically under adaptive attacks, versus computationally expensive randomized defenses (DiffPure, Rotate, DiffJPEG) that hold up better when the attacker has full knowledge. This mirrors findings in the broader adversarial robustness literature, but the paper's explicit head-to-head comparison across 25 methods and 14 attacks in the IQA domain gives this insight empirical grounding that was previously missing. The finding that certified defenses based on diffusion denoising (DDRS, DensePure) achieve the lowest D_scores while also providing formal guarantees is another non-obvious result worth highlighting.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify the benchmark's scope regarding adversarial training.** Either expand the evaluation to more metrics (even with a lighter attack) or explicitly separate adversarial training into its own analysis section rather than presenting it as part of a unified 25-method comparison.
2. **Increase the sample size for certified defenses** (even to 50 images) or explicitly flag those results as preliminary in the main text, not just in the experimental setup.
3. **Replace or better justify Q_score.** Use a perceptually validated metric like LPIPS or DISTS, or provide a rationale for the PSNR/40 scaling (e.g., show it approximately equalizes the dynamic ranges of SSIM and PSNR on a validation set).
4. **Validate and describe the attack strength calibration** — explain how weak/medium/strong levels were set to be comparable across attack families, or report the actual perturbation magnitudes achieved.
5. **Add explicit notation** in Table 1 explaining why JPEG and Color Quantization lack adaptive results (non-differentiable).
6. **Include per-metric breakdowns** (even in supplementary) so readers can identify which defenses work best for each IQA metric.

## Score and Decision

Based on the paper's novelty (first systematic IQA defense benchmark), the scale of the experimental effort (25 methods × 14 attacks × 7 metrics × multiple parameters), the public release of data and leaderboard, and the transparent handling of limitations, this is a strong contribution. The weaknesses are real but mostly acknowledged, and none are fatal. The small test-set sizes and incomplete adversarial training evaluation temper the "comprehensive" claim but do not invalidate the core contribution. With the suggested revisions, this would be a high-impact resource for the IQA community.
