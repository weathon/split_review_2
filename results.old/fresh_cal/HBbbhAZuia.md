## Summary

DockedAC is a large-scale dataset (82,836 activity data points across 52 protein targets) that provides 3D protein-ligand complex structures for activity cliff (AC) analysis. The authors match ChEMBL bioactivity data to PDB structures, use molecular docking to generate complex structures, annotate ACs using a consensus of three similarity measures, and benchmark 11 models (4 ML + 7 DL) to characterize the dataset. The central finding is that 3D GNNs (IGN, SS-GNN) outperform 2D GNNs on AC prediction, demonstrating that the structural information in the dataset has value, even though traditional ML with ECFP fingerprints still achieves the lowest overall error.

---

## Strengths

1. **Scale and scope of the dataset.** DockedAC contains 82,836 activity data points across 52 targets with 3D complex structures — orders of magnitude larger than the prior 3DAC database (219 pairs, Section 2). This fills a genuine gap: previous AC research has been ligand-centric, lacking structural data at a scale suitable for modern deep learning.

2. **Empirical evidence that 3D structure improves AC prediction in GNNs.** Figure 4 and Section 5.1 show that 3D GNN models (IGN, SS-GNN) achieve lower RMSE and RMSE_cliff than 2D GNN models across 52 targets. IGN clusters in the lower-left corner of the RMSE vs. RMSE_cliff scatter plots. SS-GNN has the smallest gap between overall and AC-specific error (only two targets deviate by more than 0.2 log units). The 3D GNNs rank first and third in Pearson correlation between RMSE and RMSE_cliff. This directly supports the claim that 3D structural information benefits AC modeling.

3. **Rigorous dataset construction.** The pipeline includes careful steps: (a) consensus-based AC identification using three similarity measures (substructure, scaffold, SMILES); (b) stringent target-structure mapping requiring Tanimoto similarity > 0.99 between PDB and ChEMBL ligands; (c) local docking within a uniquely identified binding site; (d) removal of ligands with positive docking scores; and (e) double-stratified train/test splitting by cluster and AC label (Section 3.5).

4. **Comprehensive benchmarking.** The framework evaluates 11 models across four feature types (ECFP, MACCS, PhysChem, WHIM) on 52 targets, providing a useful baseline for future work. The honest reporting that ECFP-based ML outperforms all DL methods, including 3D GNNs, is transparent and consistent with prior work (Jiang et al., 2021b; Janela & Bajorath, 2022).

5. **Novel insight about AC ratio.** Section 5.3 (Figure 5) shows that the percentage of AC ligands in the training set significantly correlates with the gap between overall and AC-specific prediction error (p = 1.0e-4), while total training set size does not. This is a practically relevant finding for dataset design.

6. **Target-family analysis.** Table 2 reveals consistent performance rankings across target families (protease worst, kinase best) across all methods, motivating target-specific modeling approaches.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Docking pose quality is not systematically validated.** The entire value proposition of DockedAC rests on providing meaningful 3D structures, yet the only quality filter applied is removing ligands with docking score > 0 (Section 3.4). This is a weak sanity check that does not assess whether the predicted binding poses are physically plausible. The paper reports no validation against known experimental structures (e.g., redocking ligands with known crystal structures in PDBbind to report RMSD recovery rates), no cross-docking consistency checks, and no manual inspection of a representative sample. The paper acknowledges this in limitations ("complex structures generated by molecular docking may be inaccurate"), but for a dataset whose raison d'être is the 3D structure, this validation is essential, not peripheral. Without it, readers cannot assess what fraction of the 80,000+ complexes are reliable, and the benchmark results (which show 3D GNNs underperform ECFP-based ML) could simply reflect noise from poor-quality poses.

2. **Hybrid ECFP+IGN experiment mentioned but results not reported.** In Section 5.2, the paper states "we implement a hybrid approach combining the ECFP features with the features extracted from the last layer of the 3D IGN model" to validate the complementarity of 3D information, but no quantitative results are given. This experiment would be the most direct evidence that 3D structural information adds value beyond ligand-centric fingerprints. Its absence leaves the claim that 3D models "can be important complements" (Section 5.5) without concrete support.

### Minor

1. **No variance or confidence intervals in benchmark results.** Table 2 reports average RMSE_cliff without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or any measure of uncertainty. Given that performance varies substantially across targets (RMSE_cliff from 0.52 to 1.59 log units, Section 5.4), the reader cannot assess whether reported differences between methods are stable or reflect random split variation. This limits the interpretability of method rankings.

2. **Evaluation protocol ambiguity.** Section 3.5 describes an 80/20 stratified train/test split per target, while Section 4.3 states results come from "five-fold cross-validation." The relationship between these is unclear (e.g., is 5-fold CV performed within the 80% training portion? Are metrics averaged across folds?). The paper references appendices for details, but the main text should be self-contained on this key point.

3. **Cross-target experiments reported incompletely.** Section 5.5 mentions cross-target experiments with in-domain and out-of-distribution settings but the results are reported as a truncated range ("9 to 1.4") without any proper quantification. This is too vague to be informative.

4. **Missed opportunity for deeper analysis of target-family effects.** Section 5.2 observes that protease targets consistently yield the worst RMSE_cliff across all methods, but offers no investigation into why (e.g., binding site flexibility, ligand diversity, data distribution differences). The dataset is well-suited for such analysis, and exploring it would strengthen the paper.

None.

## Novel Insights

The cross-reviews surface a tension that the paper does not fully resolve: the dataset delivers 3D structural information at unprecedented scale for AC research, and the benchmark demonstrates that this information helps GNNs (3D > 2D), yet the best-performing method remains ECFP-based random forest, which completely ignores 3D structure. The paper interprets this honestly as motivation for better 3D-aware algorithms, but neither review fully grapples with what this means for the dataset's immediate utility. A genuinely novel insight that emerges is that the AC ratio in the training set (Section 5.3) matters more than overall data volume — this is a non-obvious finding with practical implications for how AC datasets should be curated and sampled, and it is a contribution somewhat independent of the 3D structure aspect.
