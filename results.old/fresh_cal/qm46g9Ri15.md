## Summary

The paper identifies two misalignment problems in CLIP fine-tuning (attention misalignment and predictive category misalignment) and proposes AlignCLIP, consisting of two components: (1) an Attention Alignment Loss (AAL) that uses a Siamese structure with a D-V attention branch to generate a guidance distribution for aligning attention, and (2) a Semantic Label Smoothing (SLS) technique that replaces the uniform smoothing distribution with class similarities derived from text embeddings. The method is evaluated across three open-world scenarios (domain shift, unseen classes, and combined) on multiple benchmarks, consistently outperforming baselines including CLIPood.

## Strengths

1. **Semantic Label Smoothing (SLS) is a clean, well-specified regularization that leverages pre-trained text embeddings to encode class relationships.** Unlike uniform label smoothing, SLS preserves prediction hierarchy (e.g., misclassifying a cat as a dog rather than as a computer). The idea is clearly described (Section 3.3), and Figure 3d shows it outperforms standard label smoothing by 0.89%–3.21% on three datasets. This is a distinct class-level regularization from instance-level approaches like TeS, as the paper explicitly notes.

2. **Comprehensive evaluation spanning three distinct open-world scenarios with consistent improvements.** Tables 1–4 cover domain shift (6 datasets), unseen classes (11 datasets), and the combined scenario (Office-Home + DomainNet). AlignCLIP outperforms strong baselines (zero-shot CLIP, CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIPood) in every setting, with gains that are modest but consistent across domains and datasets (e.g., harmonic mean on 11 unseen-class datasets: 76.82 vs. 75.64 for CLIPood, Table 3a).

3. **The problem diagnosis (attention misalignment + predictive category misalignment) is clearly illustrated with visual evidence in Figure 1.** This diagnostic framing goes beyond treating generalization degradation as a black-box issue and provides concrete motivation for the two-pronged approach.

4. **The ablation study (Figure 3a–c) provides useful mechanistic insight**, e.g., showing that aligning only the first four attention layers suffices for best results (Figure 3c) and that aligning both layer-attention and similarity-distributions (AL+AS) works best (Figure 3b). These go beyond simple "with/without" ablation.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **The D-V attention structure — the core mechanism of AAL — is not adequately specified, preventing reproducibility.** The paper states (line 87) that "D-V structure refers to an innovative attention structure by replacing the softmax-normalized product of Q and K^T with a diagonal matrix," but **never defines what the diagonal entries are**. Are they all 1 (identity)? Derived from V? Learned? The paper also says the D-V branch "shares the parameters with the original blocks" (line 89), but if the attention is replaced by a fixed diagonal matrix, what shared parameters remain? Without this detail, the AAL loss (Eq. 6) and the guidance distribution G_{D-V} cannot be reproduced. The reference to CLIP Surgery (Li et al., 2023) does not resolve this because that paper uses a V-V-V structure, not a diagonal matrix. This is the lynchpin of the attention alignment strategy and must be fully specified.

2. **The empirical gains over the strongest baseline (CLIPood) are modest, yet the paper uses overstated language.** Across all settings: +0.7 pp on DomainBed (Table 1), +2.0 pp on ImageNet variants (Table 2), +1.2 pp harmonic mean on unseen classes (Table 3), and per-domain gains of roughly 0–3 pp in the combined setting (Table 4). These are consistent but not transformative. Phrases like "markedly supersedes" (abstract) and "excelling in generalization methodologies" overstate the practical difference. The margins are small enough that it is reasonable to ask whether simpler regularization (e.g., stronger data augmentation, standard label smoothing with fine-tuned ε) would produce comparable improvements.

### Minor

3. **The ablation study does not isolate the contribution of Beta Moving Average (BMA) from Shu et al. (2023).** The "w/o AAL + w/o SLS" variant in Figure 3a presumably still includes BMA. While the comparison to CLIPood (which also uses BMA) partially controls for this, a pure baseline without BMA, AAL, or SLS (i.e., straightforward cross-entropy fine-tuning with no bells) would cleanly separate the effect of BMA from the proposed losses. This is a standard missing control.

4. **The SLS comparison in Figure 3d is conducted on three datasets (LabelMe, SUN, VOC2007) that do not appear in the main benchmark evaluations.** While this demonstrates SLS vs. uniform label smoothing, the comparison would be more informative if performed on the same datasets used for the main results (e.g., Office-Home or VLCS), enabling direct connection to the core claims.

5. **The SLS loss equation (Eq. 8–9) has an indexing ambiguity.** Equation (9) writes \( \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S}} = \sum_i \mathbf{S}_{ij} \log(p(y_i|\mathbf{x})) \) without specifying which class the similarity row refers to. Standard practice would sum over classes \(c\) with the row for the true class \(y\): \(\sum_c \mathbf{S}_{y,c} \log(p(c|\mathbf{x}))\). This is fixable but currently unclear.

6. **The first-four-layers claim (Figure 3c) is an empirical observation on two datasets (Office-Home, VLCS) offered without discussion of when it might not generalize.** The paper asserts that deeper layers are "very sparser" but provides no quantitative evidence (e.g., attention entropy measurements). This finding may be dataset-specific.

### Trivial

7. The paper writes "SSL loss" in Algorithm 1 when the expansion is "SLS" — minor inconsistency.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A "BMA-only" baseline (without AAL or SLS) in the ablation would cleanly separate the contributions.
- Reporting per-dataset results for the 11 unseen-class datasets (Table 3) in the main paper (or a scatter plot) would help readers assess variance across fine-grained vs. coarse datasets.
- A quantitative measure of attention misalignment reduction (e.g., average attention weight on foreground vs. background, or KL divergence to the guidance distribution) would strengthen the claim that AAL actually fixes the identified problem, beyond the qualitative attention maps in Figure 4.
- An explicit statement that inference is identical to standard CLIP (no D-V branch, no extra cost) should appear earlier in the paper.

## Suggestions

1. **Define the diagonal entries of the D-V structure explicitly.** Provide the exact equation, e.g., \(\text{Attention}_{D-V}(V) = D V\) where \(D = \text{diag}(1,1,\dots,1)\) (identity) or \(D_{ii} = f(V_i)\) for some function \(f\). Explain what parameters (if any) are shared with the original branch.
2. **Add a "no BMA, no AAL, no SLS" baseline** to the ablation (Figure 3a) to fully disentangle the contributions.
3. **Tone down the language** in the abstract and conclusion — "markedly supersedes" is disproportionate for a +0.7 pp average gain on DomainBed.
4. **Fix the indexing in Eq. (9)** to clarify that \(\mathcal{L}_S\) uses the true class row \(\mathbf{S}_{y}\) of the similarity matrix.
5. **Report the SLS comparison (Figure 3d) on at least one in-benchmark dataset** (e.g., Office-Home) to make the connection to the core claims more direct.

## Score and Decision

The paper tackles a well-motivated problem with a conceptually reasonable two-part solution, and the empirical evaluation is broad and internally consistent. The Semantic Label Smoothing contribution is clearly specified and independently useful. However, the core technical mechanism of the Attention Alignment Loss (the D-V structure) is critically underspecified — the diagonal matrix is never defined — which prevents reproducibility of the main claimed contribution. Additionally, the empirical margins, while consistent, are modest and the language overstates them. The paper is not ready for publication in its current form because of the specification gap, but the issues are fixable.
