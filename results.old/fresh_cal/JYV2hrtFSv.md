## Summary

This paper introduces Video-STaR, a self-training framework that converts any labeled video dataset (e.g., action recognition, action quality assessment) into video instruction-tuning data. The method cycles between: (1) prompting an LVLM to generate answers with rationales, (2) filtering via a Parser-Verifier that checks whether generated answers contain the ground-truth labels, (3) using "Label Rationalization" (providing the label and asking the model to rationalize it backward) for videos the model cannot answer correctly, and (4) finetuning the original pre-trained model on the surviving data. The paper evaluates on Video-LLaVA, showing a +10% gain on TempCompass (general VQA) and substantial gains on adapted downstream tasks (+20% on Kinetics700-QA, +15% on FineDiving).

## Strengths

- **First method to enable any labeled video dataset for instruction tuning via self-training**: The paper explicitly claims this (Sec. 1, contribution 1) and demonstrates it by converting three diverse source datasets (action recognition with Kinetics700, action quality assessment with FineDiving, spatial-temporal reasoning with STAR) with different label types (classes, scores, bounding boxes) into instruction-tuning data. This goes beyond prior caption-prompting approaches that dominate the field.

- **Substantial and convincing gains on adapted downstream tasks**: On Kinetics700-QA, accuracy improves from 50.0 to 59.9 (+20%); on FineDiving, from 17.6 to 20.2 (+15%) (abstract, Tab. 5). These are large absolute improvements on held-out test sets. Notably, the \base baseline (simple template-based conversion) does not improve and sometimes hurts, showing that the improvement is not merely from scaling data but from the self-training mechanism.

- **TempCompass improvement demonstrates genuine temporal understanding gains**: Accuracy rises from 45.7 to 50.3 (+10%) on a benchmark specifically designed to test fine-grained temporal attributes and resist hallucination (Sec. 4.2, Tab. 3). The \base baseline yields only +3%, isolating the benefit of the self-training approach.

- **Clean ablation isolates the critical role of Label Rationalization**: On FineDiving, removing Label Rationalization crashes accuracy from 20.2 to 12.8 (Tab. 6). This quantifies that the rationalization component is not optional—it is essential for datasets where the model initially cannot generate correct answers at all (zero direct-generation yield for FineDiving in Cycle 0, Fig. 4).

- **Large-scale generated dataset**: The method produces a 1M-instruction dataset (Video-STaR-1M) from diverse source datasets, demonstrating practical scalability (Contribution 4, Fig. 5).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Zero-shot VQA improvements are marginal on most benchmarks; only TempCompass provides strong evidence**: On MSVD-QA (+2.3%), MSRVTT-QA, TGIF-QA, and ActivityNet-QA, improvements are 0–2.3% (Tab. 3, Sec. 4.2). The paper attributes this to benchmark saturation, noting that Gemini 1.5 Pro performs comparably to open-source models on 1000-video subsets and that GPT-3.5 evaluation can vary by ±10 accuracy. While this explanation is reasonable, it means that for four out of five standard VQA benchmarks, the method does not demonstrate clear improvement. The paper's contribution (2) claims "improved zero-shot video question answering performance on various benchmark datasets" — technically true but the effect sizes are small enough to fall within evaluation noise. The core evidence for general VQA improvement rests almost entirely on TempCompass.

- **The "cycling" mechanism is partially decoupled from the model's progressive improvement**: The paper resets to the original pre-trained Video-LLaVA weights at the start of each training cycle (Sec. 4.1: "each cycle was initialized with the pre-trained Video-LLaVA weights"). This means the final model $\hat{M}^{3\star}$ has only been trained on data from cycle 3 (plus base instruction data), never seeing data from cycles 1 or 2. Improvement across cycles comes solely from higher-quality data generated by a better generator model ($\hat{M}^{1\star}$ generates data for cycle 2, $\hat{M}^{2\star}$ for cycle 3), not from accumulating training data or model weights across cycles. This is a coherent design consistent with STaR, but the framing (e.g., "improvement plateaus as the model fails to solve new problems," line 120, and the cycling narrative in Fig. 1/Fig. 5) could be read as implying iterative model refinement. The paper would benefit from explicitly stating that the value of multiple cycles is improved data quality rather than iterative model improvement, and from reporting whether a single-cycle run (training on the union of all cycles' data from scratch) achieves similar results.

### Minor

- **Parser-Verifier operational details could strengthen reproducibility**: The main text describes the verifier's components (NER + Regex for parsing, IoU for bounding boxes, BERT embedding similarity for sentence ordering, 5% margin of error for scores) at a reasonable level for a conference paper (Sec. 3.3). However, for each of the three label types used (700-class action labels, continuous integer scores 1-100, sentence ordering), the paper does not give concrete examples of how the parser extracts labels from free-form text variations. For instance, how does the regex distinguish "the dive deserves a 75" from "score: 75"? Given that the verifier is the central quality filter, a supplementary table with matching criteria per label type would substantially aid reproducibility. (Note: the appendix likely contains these details; they were stripped by the parser.)

- **No statistical significance or variance reporting**: All results in Tables 2–4 are reported as point estimates without confidence intervals or multiple-run variance. The paper itself acknowledges GPT-3.5-turbo's evaluation variability can cause ±10 accuracy swings (Sec. 4.2). While single-run evaluation is common practice in this field, the known variability of the evaluation LLM makes variance reporting particularly important here.

- **The "1M video instruction tuning dataset" claim is not directly verified with a final count in the main text**: Contribution 4 and Sec. 3.2 mention the dataset but do not report the final dataset size explicitly in a table—only yield percentages are shown in Fig. 5.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Ablation on number of cycles**: The paper uses three cycles but does not compare results after 1, 2, and 3 cycles in a table. Fig. 5 suggests most data conversion happens in cycle 1 for Kinetics700 and STAR, with FineDiving needing cycle 2. A table showing adapted task performance after each cycle would directly demonstrate the value of additional cycles.

- **Analysis of TempCompass subcategories**: TempCompass tests temporal attributes like speed, direction, and order. Breaking down the +10% gain by subcategory would directly test whether Video-STaR specifically improves temporal reasoning (which the source datasets also require) versus general QA.

- **Human evaluation of generated data quality**: The paper assumes that answers passing the label verifier are high quality, but an answer could contain the correct label while still being hallucinated in other respects. A small human evaluation of 100–200 generated QA pairs per dataset would calibrate trust in the self-generated data.

- **Comparison to a single-cycle baseline using all data**: Since the model is trained from scratch each cycle, a natural baseline is to take the union of all data generated across cycles, train once, and compare to the three-cycle model. This would isolate whether the cycling mechanism itself (improving data quality) adds value over simply generating more data.

## Removed Points

- **Criticism about parser-verifier being a "methodological gap" / underspecified for reproducibility**: The paper provides NER + Regex, IoU for bounding boxes, BERT similarity for ordering, and 5% margin (Sec. 3.3). This is reasonable detail for a main paper; the appendix (stripped) likely contains full specifications. Demoted from the critic's "Critical Issue" to Minor.

- **Criticism that the cycling narrative is "partially misleading"**: The paper accurately describes the mechanism (lines 86–87, 210). The critic's framing that improvements "come entirely from training on self-generated data once" is an oversimplification—data quality demonstrably improves across cycles (Fig. 5-6 show this), which constitutes progressive bootstrapping of the data. Retained as Major with nuanced framing.

- **Criticism about missing confidence intervals / GPT variability**: Retained as Minor (fair point) but removed the characterization that this "substantially weakens" the evidence.

- **Strength Finder's generic strengths**: Removed generic statements like "the motivation is well-articulated" and "Figure 1 is clear" as they lack specific content.

- **"Comparison to other self-training methods"** (from critic's "Places to Improve"): The paper adequately compares to template-based and distillation baselines. Comparing to other self-training methods would be nice but is not a missing requirement.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation from the reviews is the tension between the paper's two claimed benefits: the zero-shot VQA results are concentrated on TempCompass (a temporal-reasoning benchmark), while the adapted task results are strongest on Kinetics700 (action recognition) and FineDiving (action quality assessment, inherently temporal). This pattern suggests that Video-STaR's primary benefit may be in improving temporal and action-specific understanding—i.e., the method works best when the supervision in the source data aligns with the capability being tested. This is a more focused finding than the paper's broad framing of "improving general video understanding," and future work could productively investigate whether Video-STaR's gains are concentrated in temporal/action domains.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the zero-shot VQA claims**: Acknowledge directly that the strongest evidence is on TempCompass and adapted tasks, and that gains on MSVD-QA/TGIF-QA/ActivityNet-QA are within evaluation noise. This would make the paper's narrative tighter and more honest.

2. **Add variance reporting**: Even 3 runs with mean ± std on the key numbers (TempCompass, Kinetics700-QA, FineDiving) would substantially strengthen the empirical evidence.

3. **Explicitly state the role of cycling**: Clarify in the method section that resetting to pre-trained weights each cycle means the value of multiple cycles is in generating higher-quality data (through an improved generator), not in accumulating model knowledge. Add an ablation comparing single-cycle (union of all data) vs. multi-cycle to validate this.

4. **Provide a concrete Parser-Verifier specification table**: A small table showing the rule/pattern used for each label type (Kinetics class, FineDiving score, STAR temporal/bbox) would address reproducibility concerns directly.

## Score and Decision

Based on my assessment: This is a solid paper with a clear, well-motivated method and strong results on the tasks it is designed for. The two major weaknesses (marginal zero-shot gains on most benchmarks, and the decoupled cycling mechanism) are significant but not fatal—the core contribution (using any labeled video dataset for instruction tuning via self-training) stands, is convincingly demonstrated on adapted tasks and TempCompass, and is practically valuable. The paper is slightly overclaimed on general VQA improvement but transparent about the limitations. I rate this as a strong paper that should be accepted with revisions addressing the overclaiming and reproducibility details.
