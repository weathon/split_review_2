## Summary

This paper identifies temporal redundancy across time steps in SNNs — dubbed the "Feature Overlapping Phenomenon" — and proposes Temporal Differential Decoupling (TDD) to separate static (reusable) and dynamic features, combined with a Gradient Sensitivity Criterion (GSC) to prune unimportant dynamic features in the differential domain. The reported results show up to 80.9% reduction in spikes per timestep and 57.8% total spike reduction on CIFAR-10/100.

## Strengths

- **Addresses an underexplored angle in SNN efficiency.** Rather than reducing the *number* of time steps (which is the dominant paradigm), the paper targets *redundant computation within* time steps by exploiting temporal feature similarity. This is a worthwhile shift in focus (Section 2.3, lines 52–53).

- **Formal decomposition into static and dynamic features (TDD).** The paper introduces a clean mathematical decomposition in Section 4.1 (Eq. 8: O_l^T = O_l^{T-1} + Δ_l^T) that separates temporally redundant static features from informative dynamic features, providing a principled basis for selective computation.

- **Significant reported spike reduction numbers.** The empirical results (Table 2, Figures 4–5) show large reductions in spike counts — up to 80.9% per timestep and 57.8% total — on standard SNN benchmarks with both convolutional (ResNet-19) and transformer-based architectures.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **No accuracy numbers reported.** The paper's central claim is that DDLA reduces computation *while maintaining accuracy comparable to baseline models* (abstract line 4, contributions line 27, discussion line 262). However, the experiments section (Section 7) reports only spike counts and spike reduction percentages. Neither baseline accuracy nor DDLA accuracy is provided for any model/dataset/time-step configuration. Without accuracy data, the core value proposition is unsubstantiated — a method that reduces spikes by 80% is not useful if accuracy collapses. This omission is fatal to the paper in its current form.

### Major

- **Method description is too vague to reproduce.** The paper describes TDD conceptually (Section 4.1) but never specifies how "reusing static features" works operationally in the forward pass. Key implementation questions go unanswered: How are the differential parts computed without first computing the full feature map? Does the network skip computation for static neurons? The DDLA algorithm description (Section 5.2) is high-level prose with no pseudo-code or concrete architectural modifications. The reliance on serial computation (Section 7, line 240) is mentioned but its implications for wall-clock time and energy are not discussed. A reader cannot reproduce the method from this description.

- **Overclaimed novelty of "Feature Overlapping Phenomenon."** The paper claims to be "the first to discover" this phenomenon (contributions, line 24; abstract). However, temporal redundancy in SNN features across time steps is a well-recognized property (the paper itself states that "due to weight sharing and identical image encoding, the firing patterns of neurons are often very similar," line 14). The paper also inconsistently uses "feature shadowing" in the introduction (line 18) and "feature overlapping" everywhere else, suggesting imprecision. The formalization in Definition 1 is new, but the empirical observation is not.

- **GSC is a standard sensitivity measure presented as novel.** Definition 2 gives ζ(O_{l,i}^t) = |δℒ/δO_{l,i}^t · O_{l,i}^t|, which is the first-order Taylor approximation of the loss change when setting a feature to zero — a well-known saliency metric in the pruning and feature-importance literature (e.g., Optimal Brain Damage, gradient×input). The paper does not cite relevant prior work on pruning or sensitivity analysis and does not explain why this specific criterion is particularly suited to the differential domain.

- **Theory (Section 6) is disconnected from experiments.** Theorem 2 derives an error bound parameterized by the filtering ratio k, but no experiment varies k or reports accuracy-vs-sparsity trade-offs. The error bound is not empirically validated. The assumptions (independence of feature components, Assumption 1) are strong and unverified. The theory provides no testable predictions that the experiments leverage.

- **No comparison to existing methods.** The paper compares only to its own baseline (a standard TET-trained model) and does not compare against any existing method for reducing SNN computational cost, such as dynamic time-step methods (SEENN, DyRes), temporal pruning, or other spike-reduction techniques. It is unclear whether the reported 57.8% total spike reduction is competitive with prior approaches.

### Minor

- **Quantitative analysis of feature similarity is missing.** The claim of feature overlapping (Section 4) is supported only by a single visual example (Figure 1). No quantitative measure is provided — e.g., average cosine similarity, fraction of features that change between timesteps, or sensitivity to the threshold ε in Definition 1.

- **No runtime or energy measurements.** The paper uses spike count as a proxy for computational cost but does not report actual latency, energy consumption, or FLOPs. The serialization requirement (line 240) may introduce overhead that offsets the spike reduction benefit.

- **No statistical significance or variance reported.** Results are presented as single numbers without error bars or run-to-run variance.

- **Terminology inconsistency.** The introduction introduces "feature shadowing" (line 18), but the rest of the paper uses "feature overlapping." This suggests hasty writing.

### Trivial

- The paper states it tests on "both static and event-based datasets" (line 27) but only CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (static) are mentioned in the experiments.
