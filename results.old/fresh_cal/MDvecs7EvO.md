## Summary

This paper introduces SAE Match, a data-free method for aligning Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) features across layers of a neural network. The core idea is to solve a linear assignment problem on SAE weights, with a novel "parameter folding" technique that incorporates JumpReLU thresholds into the weights to account for cross-layer differences in hidden-state norms. Experiments on the Gemma 2 2B model evaluate matching quality via MSE, an LLM-based semantic judge, and a layer-pruning task.

## Strengths

- **Parameter folding is a novel and well-motivated technique.** The folding operation (Equation 4) integrates the JumpReLU threshold \(\theta\) into encoder/decoder weights, making the matching scale-aware without requiring data. This is a genuine contribution over prior weight-matching approaches (e.g., Ainsworth et al. 2022) and is supported by the observation in Figure 1 that \(\theta\) tracks hidden-state norm growth. Figure 3 shows that folded matching achieves lower MSE in the folded weight space than unfolded matching.

- **Composition of permutations is validated and practical.** The paper proposes (Hypothesis 3) that composing adjacent permutation matrices can approximate matching over non-adjacent layers. Figure 8 (feature persistence) tests this: for nearby layers, the composed and exact matches agree well, supporting the hypothesis. This provides a practical efficiency improvement for analyzing feature chains across many layers.

- **Empirical investigation of feature persistence.** Section 5.4 (feature persistence) provides a concrete analysis of how long features retain semantic similarity across layers (roughly five layers), using both exact matching and composition. This goes beyond a pure method paper and contributes an interesting qualitative finding about feature dynamics in Gemma 2.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Semantic evaluation relies on an unvalidated closed loop.** The paper's main validation of matching quality uses an LLM to judge whether Neuronpedia descriptions of matched features are "SAME," "MAYBE," or "DIFFERENT." But the Neuronpedia descriptions themselves are generated by GPT-4o from activation patterns. This is an LLM evaluating descriptions produced by a similar LLM, without any human calibration or independent behavioral proxy. The paper acknowledges potential errors in Neuronpedia descriptions (Limitations) but does not quantify their impact. A human evaluation on even a small subset, or a behavioral test (e.g., activation intervention), would substantially strengthen the central claim that the method "effectively captures feature evolution."

- **The layer-pruning experiment lacks critical baselines to isolate the effect of matching.** Section 5.5 prunes a single layer by encoding features at layer *t*, permuting via the learned mapping, and decoding to approximate layer *t*+1's hidden state. The paper reports low ΔL and high explained variance after layer 10. However, no baselines are provided — random permutation, identity permutation, or simply using the target layer's SAE on the source hidden state. Later layers in LLMs are known to be more robust to perturbations, so the small loss change could reflect model robustness rather than accurate feature alignment. Without a comparison that isolates the contribution of the learned permutation, the experiment conflates matching accuracy with confounding factors.

- **No comparison to any alternative feature alignment approach.** The paper compares "folded matching" to "unfolded matching" and "vanilla" (no matching). These ablations do not establish usefulness relative to other plausible approaches. A simple data-based baseline — matching features by activation correlation or top-k overlap on a small dataset — would directly test whether the data-free property is advantageous or merely convenient. Without such positioning, the significance of the claimed contribution remains asserted rather than demonstrated.

### Minor

- **Statistical reliability is not reported.** Experiments use 100 examples per dataset, truncated to 1024 tokens, and results are presented as point estimates without error bars, confidence intervals, or standard deviations (Figures 6, 7, 9, 10, 11). While the matching method itself is deterministic, the downstream evaluation metrics (ΔL, explained variance, LLM-based ratings) depend on the data sample and may exhibit variance. Reporting this variance is standard practice for credibility.

- **The Matching Score metric is underspecified.** Section 4 defines it as "the probability of paired feature activation between two matched layers." It is not clear what "paired feature activation" means, nor over what distribution the probability is computed. This metric is used in a key result (Figure 7), so the definition needs to be explicit.

- **MSE comparison between folded and unfolded matching is asymmetric.** Figure 3 caption states that MSE is evaluated with folded parameters in all cases — unfolded matching optimizes for unfolded MSE but is evaluated on folded MSE. This means folded matching is evaluated on the metric it was designed for, while unfolded matching is not. A fairer comparison would use an independent evaluation metric (e.g., LLM similarity or activation correlation on held-out data). The LLM-based evaluation partially addresses this, but the MSE comparison alone is uninformative.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Validate with behavioral or intervention experiments.** Beyond the LLM-based semantic judge, the claims would be strengthened by testing whether activating a matched feature in layer *t* has a similar causal effect on model outputs as activating the corresponding feature in layer *t*+1.
- **Analyze matching failures** (cases where MSE is low but LLM says "DIFFERENT," or vice versa) to understand the method's limitations.
- **Discuss computational cost** of solving linear assignment problems for large feature spaces across many layer pairs.
- **Compare to a simple data-based baseline** (e.g., activation correlation matching on a small sample) to demonstrate the value of the data-free property.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting observation to emerge from these reviews — one not foregrounded in the paper itself — is that the paper's claimed advantages (data-free matching) and its evaluation methods (LLM-based semantic judge) are in tension. The data-free property is motivated as a practical convenience, but the evaluation depends on LLM-generated descriptions that themselves encode data-driven knowledge. A cleaner validation strategy would match the data-free method against a simple data-based baseline on a shared evaluation benchmark, clarifying the actual trade-off. This tension between claimed and demonstrated contribution is a pattern common to papers that propose a method but evaluate it only on its own terms.

## Suggestions

1. Add a human evaluation on a subset (~100 pairs) of matched features to validate the LLM-based semantic judge. Report agreement rates.
2. Add three baselines to the layer-pruning experiment: (a) random permutation, (b) identity permutation, (c) encoding with layer *t*'s SAE and decoding with layer *t*+1's SAE without any permutation (to measure reconstruction error alone).
3. Compare folded matching to a simple data-based baseline: match features by the correlation of their activations on a small held-out dataset (e.g., 100 examples), and report MSE and LLM similarity for both methods.
4. Provide confidence intervals or standard deviations for all data-dependent metrics (ΔL, explained variance, LLM evaluation) across multiple random subsets.
5. Explicitly define the "Matching Score" metric with a clear mathematical formulation.
