## Summary

The paper presents OpenWaves, a large-scale dataset of over 16 million frequency-domain wave simulations based on the Helmholtz equation, designed for benchmarking neural wave equation solvers in ultrasound computed tomography (USCT). The dataset uses anatomically realistic breast phantoms (from the FDA VICTRE tool) across four tissue-density categories, with simulation parameters drawn from a real annular USCT system (256 transducers, 8 frequencies from 300–650 kHz). The paper benchmarks five neural operator architectures for forward wave simulation (UNet, FNO, BFNO, AFNO, MgNO) and three for inverse imaging (DeepONet, InversionNet, NIO), plus a gradient-based optimization approach using learned forward surrogates, with analysis of scaling behavior, frequency complexity, and out-of-distribution generalization. The core contribution is a more realistic, application-driven alternative to existing PDE datasets (OpenFWI, WaveBench).

## Strengths

- **Dataset scale and realism grounded in medical imaging:** OpenWaves provides 16M+ simulations across 8,000 anatomically realistic breast phantoms generated by the FDA VICTRE tool, with transducer geometry and frequencies matching a real annular USCT system (Section 3.2.1, Table 1). This directly addresses the gap between simplified PDE benchmarks and practical biomedical imaging requirements.

- **Comprehensive multi-task benchmark:** The paper evaluates five forward solvers and three inverse methods plus gradient-based optimization, with quantitative results in RRMSE, SSIM, and PSNR (Tables 3, 4; Figures 2, 3). This provides a structured baseline comparison for the community.

- **Analysis beyond single-metric comparisons:** The paper systematically studies performance across breast types, frequencies, dataset size scaling (4,000–8,000 phantoms), source location generalization, and OOD generalization (Figures 4, 5; Table 5). These analyses offer actionable insights for model selection and data design, strengthening the paper's value as a benchmark resource.

- **Use of real clinical system parameters:** Simulation settings (220 mm ring, 256 transducers, 300–650 kHz frequencies, water surrounding medium) are taken from a real USCT system (Section 3.2.1–3.2.2), making the benchmark directly relevant to practical imaging, unlike prior datasets using small ROIs or random media (Section 2.2).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No variance or statistical significance reporting in quantitative results:** Tables 3, 4, and 5 report all metrics as single numbers with no confidence intervals, standard deviations, or indication of multiple runs. For a benchmarking paper whose purpose is to characterize baseline capabilities, the reader cannot assess whether reported differences (e.g., MgNO vs FNO at certain frequencies) are reliable or due to initialization/data split variance. This is the most impactful weakness because it directly limits the dataset's utility as a benchmark. The authors should run at least 3 seeds and report mean±std, or at minimum state that a fixed seed was used and characterize single-run stability.

### Minor

- **2D restriction not flagged in the abstract or introduction:** The Limitations section (line 247) honestly acknowledges that simulations are restricted to 2D. However, the abstract and introduction describe the dataset as featuring "anatomically realistic human breast phantoms" without qualifying that the wave simulations are 2D. Since real USCT is inherently 3D (out-of-plane scattering, volumetric propagation), this omission risks misleading readers about the dataset's realism. The term should be qualified early — e.g., "anatomically realistic 2D breast cross-sections."

- **Missing quantitative CBS-based FWI baseline for inverse task:** Figure 3 shows a qualitative "reference" CBS-based FWI result, but Table 4 contains no corresponding quantitative row (PSNR/SSIM). The paper's claim that the neural-operator-based optimization approach "revealed significantly higher resolution than all direct inversion methods" is supported against other neural methods, but a quantitative comparison against traditional numerical FWI would ground the relative advantage (or even a note explaining why it was infeasible to run CBS on the full test set).

- **Per-category phantom distribution not reported:** The paper states 8,000 phantoms across four breast density categories (HET, FIB, FAT, EXD) but does not disclose how many phantoms fall in each category (line 113). If the distribution is imbalanced, performance differences could be confounded by class size. The train/validation/test split is also not explicitly stated.

- **Scaling factor range not specified:** The phantom generation step scales 3D VICTRE models "by a random factor to simulate breasts of varying sizes" (line 122) without giving the range of this factor. This affects reproducibility for anyone wanting to regenerate or augment the dataset.

- **Subsampling choices not justified:** The benchmarks use only 3 of 8 frequencies (300, 400, 500 kHz) and 64 of 256 source locations (line 184). While computational constraints are a reasonable motivation, the paper does not explain why these specific frequencies/sources were selected or whether conclusions are expected to hold for higher frequencies (650 kHz) or more sources.

- **Missing data format and access details:** A dataset paper should specify storage format (e.g., complex float32 vs real+imaginary, HDF5 vs NPZ), total disk size, and explicit download location. These details are standard expectations currently absent from the main text.

- **Computational cost of data generation not reported:** 16M simulations is a massive undertaking. A brief note on wall time, CPU/GPU resources, and solver iterations per simulation would help readers appreciate the effort and plan follow-up work.
