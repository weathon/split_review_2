## Summary

This paper addresses partial-set Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (SFUDA), where the source label space subsumes the target label space. It proposes a Machine Unlearning Framework that generates noise samples for source-only classes and uses them in a forgetting stage (alongside target pseudo-labels) to reduce negative transfer from absent classes, followed by an adaptation stage for target-domain finetuning. The framework is designed as a plug-in module that can be added to existing SFUDA methods.

## Strengths

- **Novel application of machine unlearning to partial-set SFUDA**: The paper is the first to explicitly introduce machine unlearning into the partial-set SFUDA setting to address the class-mismatch negative transfer problem (Section 1, Section 3.4-3.5). This is a genuinely new direction for an important and understudied problem.

- **Consistent empirical improvements across multiple baselines**: The method improves accuracy and reduces negative transfer counts across five different baseline methods (ResNet-50, TPDS, Sticker, CAiDA, SHOT) in both single-source and multi-source settings (Tables 1, 2, 3). For example, CAiDA accuracy on C_t25 (Office-31 MSDA) rises from 85.71% to 88.88%. This demonstrates the pluggable nature of the approach.

- **Large reductions in negative transfer samples**: The quantitative evidence directly supports the paper's central claim. On Office-31 MSDA, negative transfer samples for C_t6 drop from 312 to 23 for ResNet-50 (Table 1). On Office-Home SSDA, they drop from 504 to 327 for C_t15 (Table 3). These are substantial and meaningful improvements.

- **Ablation validates component necessity**: Table 4 shows that using only the forgetting stage (48.05%) or only the adaptation stage (53.62%) yields lower accuracy than the full framework (59.60%), and iterative training further reduces negative transfer samples. This confirms that both stages contribute positively.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Major

- **The forgetting mechanism's rationale is insufficiently explained and the connection to claimed forgetting is not well established.** The noise samples are generated by minimizing negative cross-entropy (i.e., maximizing cross-entropy) for a source-only class y (Eq. 9), producing inputs the model is least confident about for that class. Then in the forgetting stage (Eq. 10), the model is trained to minimize cross-entropy on those same noise samples with the same source-only label y_N. The paper states this "make[s] the model to forget about C_f" (Section 3.4) without explaining why fitting the model to predict class y on adversarially confusing inputs achieves forgetting rather than reinforcing the class association with a corrupted input pattern. While noise-based unlearning has precedents in the literature (Tarun et al. 2023, cited by the paper), the mechanism by which this particular formulation (gradient descent with the source-only label) achieves forgetting is not articulated. The empirical results suggest the approach does work to some degree (the "only forget" ablation improves accuracy from 28.51% to 48.05% in Table 4), but the paper provides no analysis of what the model actually learns from the noise samples — e.g., whether the model's predictions on source-only classes genuinely drop, or whether it simply overfits to noise. **Why it matters**: The core technical contribution is the unlearning mechanism, yet the paper does not convincingly explain why it should produce the claimed forgetting effect. This is a methodological gap that weakens the foundation of the contribution.
  
  *Verification from the paper*: Eq. 9 shows `argmin_N E_(θ)[-L(h_s, y) + λ||W_noise||]` (maximizing CE). Eq. 10 shows `L_f = L_ce(y_t, h_s(x_t)) + α L_ce(y_N, h_s(x_N))` (minimizing CE on noise with source-only label). Section 3.4 claims this "make[s] the model to forget about C_f" but provides no rationale for why this optimization direction causes forgetting.

- **The method assumes oracle knowledge of the target class set C_t, which is unrealistic in SFUDA and limits applicability.** The entire pipeline depends on knowing which classes exist in the target domain. Pseudo-label filtering uses `h_s(x_t) ∈ C_t` (Eq. 8), and noise generation requires `C_f = C_s - C_t` (Section 3.4: "We can easily obtain the category set C_f that only exists in the source domain through C_f = C_s − C_t"). In the standard partial-set SFUDA setting, the practitioner does not know which source classes are absent from the target — this is precisely the unknown that must be estimated. The paper evaluates by construction (simulating C_t from ground-truth labels) without discussing how C_t would be obtained in a realistic deployment (e.g., via a preliminary class-presence estimation step). **Why it matters**: The favorable results may reflect the use of privileged information rather than the efficacy of the unlearning mechanism. The method's applicability is severely limited in the setting it claims to solve.

  *Verification from the paper*: Eq. 8 filters samples by `h_s(x_t) ∈ C_t`. Section 3.4 computes `C_f = C_s − C_t`. No estimation or discussion of how C_t is known.

- **Comparisons with baselines do not control for the C_t information advantage, making it impossible to attribute gains to the unlearning mechanism.** The underlying baseline methods (ResNet-50, TPDS, Sticker, CAiDA, SHOT) do not incorporate knowledge of C_t, whereas the proposed framework explicitly filters predictions based on C_t and targets forgetting only for C_f. The observed improvements could plausibly come from knowing which classes to ignore rather than from the forgetting stage itself. The paper does not include a control experiment where baselines are given the same C_t information (e.g., by allowing them to discard predictions outside C_t after inference) or an ablation that quantifies the gain from C_t knowledge alone versus the full forgetting mechanism. **Why it matters**: Without this control, the paper overclaims the contribution of the unlearning component.

  *Verification from the paper*: Tables 1-3 compare baselines (no C_t knowledge) vs. the proposed method (uses C_t knowledge). No baseline variant with C_t information is provided.
