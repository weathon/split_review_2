## Summary

This paper proposes Partitioned Learned Count-Min Sketch (PL-CMS), which extends the single-threshold Learned Count-Min Sketch (LCMS) by partitioning the learned model's output space into multiple regions separated by several thresholds, each region handled by its own CMS data structure. The authors derive an FPR upper bound, formulate a convex program to optimize per-region space allocations, and empirically demonstrate FPR reductions of 10–22% over CMS and LCMS on Zipfian-like datasets. The paper also shows that the necessary dataset statistics can be estimated from training data, making the approach practical.

## Strengths

- **Empirical demonstration of lower FPR**: On three datasets (Macbeth, Bible, PM2.5), PL-CMS variants consistently achieve lower false positive rates than CMS and LCMS — e.g., ~10% FPR reduction on Macbeth (n/k=16), 10–20% on Bible (n/k=879), and 22% on PM2.5 (n/k=284) at certain space budgets (Section 4.5, Figures 3–4). These results directly support the core claim that multi-threshold partitioning improves heavy-hitter identification.

- **Convex optimization for space allocation**: Section 3.2 formulates the space allocation problem across multiple CMS structures as a convex program (Equation 7) solvable via conic exponential solvers (e.g., Mosek), given fixed thresholds. This is a clean technical contribution that enables principled parameter selection.

- **Parameter estimation from training data**: Section 4.4 describes methods to estimate dataset statistics (Eᵢ, Fᵢ, m) from training data — e.g., using Heap's law for Zipfian datasets. The estimated variants (2-CMS-E, 3-CMS-E) perform similarly to the idealized variants, demonstrating practical applicability.

- **Theoretical FPR upper bound**: Section 2.2 derives a closed-form upper bound on expected FPR (Equations 3–6) starting from per-item probability using 2-universal hash functions and Markov's inequality, providing a principled foundation for optimization.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Threshold selection procedure is ambiguous and raises potential data-snooping concerns**: Section 4.1 states: "For each variant, we report the false positive rates ... returned by threshold settings which return the lowest average FPR across different space settings." The candidate thresholds are generated by scaling frequencies from training data, but the paper does not clarify whether the *selection* among candidates (i.e., choosing which threshold values to report) was performed on held-out validation data or on the test set. If the latter, this would constitute data snooping and inflate reported gains. The paper must clarify this, or (preferably) re-run evaluation with thresholds fixed on training/validation data only.

- **LCMS baseline's internal CMS parameterization is not specified**: The paper treats LCMS as "equivalent to PL-CMS with a single Count-Min Sketch data structure" (Section 4) but does not state whether LCMS's CMS parameters (number of tables t, buckets m) were set using the same optimization formulas (Equation 5). If LCMS used a default or suboptimal CMS configuration, the reported gains may partially reflect better tuning rather than the partitioning itself. The authors should explicitly confirm that the same CMS optimization was applied to the LCMS baseline, or ideally treat LCMS as a 1-region special case of PL-CMS.

### Minor

- **The adjustment factor p=3 is empirically calibrated without theoretical justification**: Section 3.2 introduces p=3 to correct the looseness of the theoretical FPR bound. This constant is calibrated on the Macbeth dataset (Figure 2) and claimed to "work well across different datasets." While this is pragmatically reasonable and the paper acknowledges the need for future theoretical work, it makes the optimization procedure partially heuristic rather than fully principled. A sensitivity analysis or validation-based selection of p would strengthen the paper.

- **No absolute FPR values are reported**: The results (Section 4.5, Figures 3–4) report relative FPR reductions (e.g., "10% lower," "22% lower"). Without absolute FPR values (e.g., FPR=0.05 vs. 0.045), the practical significance of the improvements is difficult to assess. The y-axis labels on the figures likely contain this information, but the text should cite concrete absolute values to ground the claims.

- **No ablation on number of partitions**: Only 2-CMS and 3-CMS variants are shown. A sweep over 1–4 partitions on one dataset would demonstrate diminishing returns and help justify the choice. The paper acknowledges this limitation in the conclusion.

- **No analysis of estimation error propagation**: Section 4.4 describes how Eᵢ, Fᵢ, and m are estimated from training data, but the paper does not analyze how errors in these estimates affect the resulting FPR. The statement "in case these procedures underestimate the true count m ... we store the remaining elements in CMS₁" is vague about failure modes.

### Trivial

- Time overhead of multiple CMS structures (multiple hash computations per insertion) is not discussed. Latency can matter in streaming settings, though the paper focuses on space efficiency.

## Novel Insights

The key insight emerging from this review is that while the paper's primary contribution (multi-threshold partitioning + convex optimization for CMS space allocation) is well-motivated and technically sound, the empirical case is stronger for the *engineering* of the approach (calibrated bound, estimation procedures) than for the pure partitioning idea. The fact that 2-CMS and 3-CMS often perform similarly on larger datasets (Bible) suggests that the benefit of partitioning saturates quickly — the first additional partition does most of the work. This saturation pattern is worth explicit study: the marginal value of each additional partition depends on the skew of the frequency distribution, and the paper's results suggest a simple heuristic (2 partitions may suffice for many practical Zipfian settings). The convex optimization formulation is the cleanest part of the paper and could be applied independently of the partitioning idea to any multi-CMS arrangement.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify threshold selection**: Explicitly state whether thresholds were selected using training/validation data or test data. If the latter, re-run evaluation with a proper train-validation-test split.
2. **Specify LCMS CMS parameters**: State explicitly that LCMS's CMS was parameterized using the same optimization (Equation 5), or treat LCMS as a 1-region PL-CMS and re-run.
3. **Add absolute FPR values in the text** alongside the reported relative reductions.
4. **Add a simple ablation**: Show FPR vs. number of partitions (1–4) for one dataset to illustrate diminishing returns.
5. **Report confidence intervals** over multiple hash-function draws, or at minimum note that single-run evaluation is standard but variance is uncharacterized.
6. **Add a sensitivity analysis for p** (e.g., p ∈ {2,3,4}) on one held-out dataset to demonstrate robustness.
