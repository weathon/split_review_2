## Summary

This paper proposes MF-GFN, extending GFlowNets to multi-fidelity active learning where multiple oracles with different fidelities and costs are available. The core technical contribution is augmenting the GFlowNet state space to jointly sample both a candidate object *x* and the fidelity *m* at which to query it, trained on a multi-fidelity acquisition function (MF-MES) as reward. The method is evaluated on synthetic functions (Branin, Hartmann) and realistic molecular design tasks (DNA aptamers, antimicrobial peptides, small molecule redox properties).

## Strengths

- **First integration of GFlowNets with multi-fidelity active learning (Section 3.3, Figure 1).** The core novelty—having the GFlowNet jointly sample both the candidate and the fidelity—is a clear extension beyond prior single-fidelity GFlowNet active learning (Jain et al., 2022). The Random-fidelity GFlowNet baseline isolates and confirms the benefit of learning fidelity decisions jointly.

- **Consistent cost-efficiency gains across diverse, high-dimensional tasks.** In the DNA aptamer task, MF-GFN achieves the same top-100 mean energy as the single-fidelity baseline with ~20% of the budget (Figure 3a). In the AMP task, MF-GFN reaches the same top-50 score with ~100× less budget than single-fidelity active learning (Figure 3b). These are specific, quantitative claims supported by the plots.

- **Systematic ablation of oracle costs (Section 4.4, Figure 5).** The paper varies the cost ratio from 100:1 down to 2:1 on the DNA task, demonstrating that MF-GFN remains effective even when the low-fidelity oracle is only marginally cheaper. This goes beyond what is typically shown in multi-fidelity BO papers.

- **Evaluation on high-dimensional, practically relevant domains.** Beyond synthetic functions, the paper tests on DNA sequences (4^30 space), antimicrobial peptides (variable length up to 50), and small molecules (SELFIES up to 64 tokens). This breadth strengthens the practical relevance of the claims.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **No error bars or statistical significance reported for any result.** All main results (Figures 3, 4, 5) show single trajectories without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or any indication of variance across trials. The caption states "the curves indicate the mean score" but does not specify over how many independent runs (if any) this mean is computed. A grep for "seed," "trial," "replicate," "std," or "standard deviation" returns no matches in the entire paper. In active learning loops with stochastic policies (GFlowNet training, surrogate fitting, sampling), variance across runs is typically substantial. Without error bars, the reader cannot distinguish genuine advantage from noise in training dynamics or random seeds. This is the most significant evidential gap in the paper.

- **Diversity — a core claimed advantage — is not reported quantitatively.** Diversity is communicated only via marker colour in the plots (Figure 3, 4 captions: "darker colour...indicating more diversity") and brief qualitative statements ("MF-GFN captures as many modes as random generation, far exceeding that of MF-PPO"). No numerical diversity values (e.g., average pairwise sequence identity or Tanimoto distance) are reported in any table, figure annotation, or in-text number. The reader cannot assess *how much* more diverse MF-GFN is, whether the gap is practically significant, or how diversity evolves over budget. Since MF-PPO sometimes achieves higher score (Hartmann, molecules EA) and diversity is the stated compensating advantage, this omission leaves a core claim unsubstantiated.

### Minor

- **Diversity metric is not formally defined in the main text.** Section 4.1 lists "Mean pairwise similarity within the top-K samples" but gives no formula, no mention of which similarity measure is used. The reader must refer to the appendix (referenced as \cref{sup:metrics}) to learn that sequence identity and Tanimoto distance are used. Naming the measure and at least a sketch of the formula would improve self-containedness.

- **Section 3.3 description of the augmented state space is terse.** The text says "the set of allowed transitions is augmented such that a fidelity m > 0 must be selected once, and only once, from any intermediate state" without explaining how fidelity is concretely integrated as an action in the GFlowNet's DAG. The implications for trajectory balance (which sums over trajectories ending at (x,m)) are not discussed. This is understandable given page limits, but a slightly expanded description would help readers.

- **Cost analysis covers only three ratios (100:1, 20:1, 2:1) on one task.** The paper's motivation emphasizes scenarios where cost ratios are orders of magnitude, but the ablation tests only one such scenario (100:1). The (1,20) ratio tests the 20:1 regime and (10,20) tests 2:1. While the paper acknowledges this limitation in Section 5, including a wider range (e.g., 1000:1 or 10:1) would strengthen the cost analysis.

## Removed Points

1. **Criticism about missing standard multi-fidelity BO baseline on benchmarks:** The critic suggested "use a simple generative model or random generation of candidates, compute MF-MES, select top-B" — but this is exactly what the "Random" baseline already implements (lines 259, 277: "Quasi-random approach where the candidates and fidelities are picked randomly and the top (x,m) pairs scored by the acquisition function are queried"). The paper also notes that "quasi-random policies are known to be competitive strategies in Bayesian optimisation" (line 277). The criticism overlooks this existing baseline. The exhaustive MF-BO baseline is intractable for the large benchmark spaces, which is a legitimate reason for its absence.

2. **Complaint about hyperparameters being relegated to the appendix:** The rule removes nitpicks about reproducibility regarding undisclosed hyperparameters or details impractical for the main text. The paper does reference the appendix for hyperparameters, which is standard practice.

3. **Criticism that only one realistic cost scenario (100:1) is tested:** The paper actually tests three ratios (100:1, 20:1, 2:1), covering a meaningful range. This point was weakened to a Minor weakness above rather than removed entirely.

## Novel Insights

The harsh reviewer's framing of the error-bar and diversity-reporting gaps as jointly undermining the core claims is the most penetrating observation — these two weaknesses (no statistical evidence, no quantitative diversity evidence) are coupled: the paper argues that MF-GFN is better because it finds diverse high-scoring candidates, but the evidence for both "high-scoring" (no error bars) and "diverse" (no numbers) is presented in a way that cannot be independently verified or compared across methods. This observation goes beyond the individual weaknesses to illuminate a structural gap in the paper's evidential strategy: the two key advantages are both communicated through visual proxies (line position, marker color) that resist quantitative comparison.

## Suggestions

1. **Add error bars (mean ± std over at least 5 seeds) to every result plot.** This is the single highest-priority improvement. The caption already says "the curves indicate the mean score" — it is unclear whether this refers to a mean over multiple seeds or just the mean over the top-K batch. Clarify and show variance.
2. **Report diversity numerically.** For each task at a fixed budget point, report both mean top-K score and the diversity metric (average pairwise similarity) for each method in a table or scatter plot. This would make the diversity claim verifiable and allow readers to see the score-diversity trade-off directly.
3. **State the number of independent trials explicitly in Section 4.**
4. **Provide the diversity formula at least in a brief form in the main text** (e.g., "average pairwise sequence identity for DNA/AMP, average pairwise Tanimoto distance for molecules").

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a relevant problem with a clean extension of GFlowNets. The method is well-motivated and the evaluation spans genuinely challenging real-world domains. However, the two major evidential gaps — no statistical replication evidence and no quantitative diversity reporting — prevent the paper's central claims from being properly assessed. Both are fixable, but the current empirical presentation is not strong enough to support acceptance.
