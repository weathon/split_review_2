## Summary

The paper introduces DeepSPF, a rotation-equivariant encoder for 3D point clouds that combines patch-wise Spherical Gaussian (SG) representations with a learnable convolutional layer (PG-Layer) that adaptively adjusts patch sizes. The encoder is evaluated on three Scan-to-CAD tasks—registration, retrieval, and completion—with empirical improvements over several baselines. The core technical ideas (patch-wise SG representation, learnable patch radius, PG-Layer preserving SG form) are novel and backed by consistent quantitative gains.

## Strengths

- **Learnable adaptive patch radius (V(r) in Eq. 7):** The paper introduces a differentiable spherical volume term that controls each patch's spatial extent. The ablation (Table 1, condition V) shows this component alone reduces RRMSE from 0.751 to 0.095 on zero-intersection registration, directly supporting the claim that adaptive resizable patches are critical for rotation-error reduction.

- **PG-Layer preserves the SG form (Eqs. 13–14):** The convolution formulation is designed so that its output matches the original SPF structure, in contrast to prior work (Salihu & Steinbach, 2023) where convolution deforms the SG. This mathematical property enables stacking multiple layers, which is a genuine architectural advance.

- **Consistent quantitative gains across three S2C tasks:** Using the same backbone, DeepSPF improves registration (Table 1: RRMSE 0.095 vs. 10.985 for SGPCR), retrieval (Table 4: Top-1 d_C 0.016 vs. 0.019), and completion (Table 5: F-score 0.576 vs. 0.329 for PCN). These figures directly support the claimed empirical improvements.

- **Drop-in integration with existing pipelines:** DeepSPF replaces the encoder in DeepGMR (registration) and PCN (completion) without modifying decoders or loss functions (stated in Section 4.2). This demonstrates versatility as a backbone, not just a task-specific module.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Rotation-equivariance claim is not properly substantiated.** The proof in Section 3.2 relies on the assumption \(R_\nu \approx R_p\) — that the spherical directions \(\nu\) rotate approximately the same way as the point cloud. However, the spherical directions \(\nu\) are fixed during preprocessing (generated by Vogel sampling, Section 3.4), and no mechanism is described for rotating them in response to an input rotation. The derivations in Eqs. (10)–(11) show that *if* \(\nu\) were also rotated, the representation would be approximately unchanged, but this does not establish that SPF is SO(3)-equivariant under the actual fixed-\(\nu\) implementation. The title and abstract claim "SO(3)-equivariant" without qualification, but the paper's own analysis only supports approximate behavior under an uninstantiated condition. This creates a significant gap between the central theoretical claim and what is actually demonstrated.

- **Insufficient ablation to isolate individual component contributions.** The ablation (Table 1) tests only the cumulative addition of three components (E, U, V) on a single task (zero-intersection registration) and a single dataset (ModelNet40). Several critical design choices are not ablated:
  - Number of patches (FPS samples \(f_{no}\)) vs. fixed patch count
  - Graph neighborhood size \(K\)
  - Learnable patch radius (\(V(r)\)) vs. fixed radius baseline
  - Legendre polynomial term (\(U(P)\)) vs. a non-Legendre baseline (e.g., an MLP on raw coordinates)
  - Number of PG-Layers per SA layer (\(m\) vs. \(m=1\)) — important for the claimed depth-enabling property
  Without these controls, the reported improvements cannot be confidently attributed to specific claimed advantages (low-frequency analysis, adaptive radii, deeper architectures).

### Minor

- **Mathematical presentation is ambiguous in key places.** The edge function in Eq. (3) and the overall SPF construction in Eq. (2) do not specify the output dimensions of intermediate quantities (e.g., \(\phi\), \(\theta\), how the max over \(K\) reduces to a single value per \((\nu,p)\) pair). The graph construction between points and spheres is described conceptually but not precisely defined. This impedes reproducibility and makes it hard to verify the mathematical claims without reverse-engineering from the implementation.

- **Point cloud completion evaluation compares against limited baselines.** The paper only compares PCN and VN-PCN (Table 5, Section 4.6). The paper explicitly scopes this to "fair comparison between encoder structures," which is reasonable, but the headline claim of "up to 30% reduction in Chamfer Distance" lacks context relative to stronger contemporary completion methods (e.g., SeedFormer, SnowflakeNet). The conclusion should more clearly separate the encoder-comparison contribution from absolute SOTA claims.

- **Implementation details are incomplete for full reproducibility.** Specific values for \(m\) (PG-Layers per SA layer), \(f_{no}\) (number of FPS samples), \(C\) (sphere samples per patch), \(A_n\) (latent dimensions per layer), \(K\) (nearest neighbors in the edge function), and the initialization radii per SA layer are not reported. While the general architecture is described, these missing values hinder independent reimplementation.

- **No experimental validation of the depth-enabling property of PG-Layer.** The paper claims that PG-Layer "retains the original SGs form, allowing for deeper networks" (Section 1, Section 3.3), but no experiment compares networks with different numbers of PG-Layers to demonstrate that deeper stacks actually improve performance or that prior methods plateau without this property.

**Value to the research community:** The SPF representation and PG-Layer are potentially useful building blocks for equivariant 3D learning, but the theoretical gaps need resolution before the community can build on the approach confidently.

**Overall:** The paper has genuine technical contributions (patch-wise SG with adaptive radius, PG-Layer preserving SG form) and shows consistent empirical gains.
