## Summary

This paper introduces CIFAR-10-Warehouse (CIFAR-10-W), a dataset of 180 out-of-distribution domains sharing the same 10 classes as CIFAR-10. 143 domains are real-world images collected from seven search engines with varying colors and styles, and 37 are generated by Stable Diffusion with diverse prompts. The paper benchmarks 8 accuracy prediction (AccP) methods and 11 domain generalization (DG) methods across these domains, demonstrating that CIFAR-10-W poses a substantially harder challenge than existing synthetic-corruption testbeds (e.g., ~6–7% MAE for AccP vs. ~3–4% on CIFAR-10-C) and revealing that DG improvements are concentrated on near-OOD domains.

## Strengths

- **Largest multi-domain CIFAR-10 testbed by a wide margin**: CIFAR-10-W provides 180 domains (Table 1), far exceeding PACS (4), Office-Home (4), DomainNet (6), and CIFAR-10-C (19 corruptions). This enables evaluation of generalization across a genuinely broad set of distribution shifts rather than a handful of stylized ones.

- **Demonstrates that real-world distribution shifts are harder than synthetic corruptions**: Table 2 shows all eight AccP methods yield higher MAE on CIFAR-10-W (e.g., 6.65% average for ResNet44) than on CIFAR-10-Cs (3.62%), confirming that synthetic corruptions understate the difficulty of real-world OOD generalization — a finding that justifies the dataset's existence.

- **Reveals nuanced DG effectiveness that smaller benchmarks cannot capture**: Figure 3A shows that with 4 source domains, SD improves accuracy on most test domains, but with only 1 source, improvement is concentrated on near-OOD (KW) and does not extend to far-OOD (cartoon/generated). This granularity is only possible with a large, diverse testbed.

- **Responsible dataset curation**: Section 2 documents manual blurring of faces and license plates, clarifies license distribution (CC BY-NC 4.0), and describes the diverse collection methodology (7 search engines with documented color/style options), setting a good standard for dataset release.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Domain diversity is described qualitatively but not quantified.** The paper defines 180 domains by combinatorial factors (search engine × color × cartoon/natural flag) and claims broad coverage, but provides no quantitative analysis of pairwise domain similarity or divergence (e.g., FID between domain centroids in feature space, JS divergence of label distributions). Without this, readers cannot assess whether the 180 subsets are genuinely diverse or whether many are near-redundant (e.g., Google-red vs. Bing-red may differ more in search ranking than in visual content). This is a material gap for a dataset paper whose central contribution is domain breadth. The paper would be strengthened by computing and reporting domain similarity measures and showing the effective number of independent domains.

### Minor

- **Data cleaning process is under-documented.** The paper states that images were "manually remov[ed] noisy data from all sets" and that "incorrectly labeled images [were] recorded during the cleaning and annotation process" (Section 2, Section 5), but provides no detail on: the criteria used for identifying noisy images (e.g., mislabeled, duplicate, low quality), the number of images removed per domain, whether multiple annotators were involved (and if so, inter-annotator agreement), or what the "annotation process" consisted of. For a benchmark dataset, this level of detail helps users assess residual noise rates. The paper reports that these noisy-label records exist but does not release or describe them.

- **AccP-to-DG coherence could be clarified.** The AccP experiments (Section 3) use classifiers trained on CIFAR-10 (32×32), while the DG experiments (Section 4) train on four Yandex/diffusion source domains at 224×224 resolution. The paper does not discuss this asymmetry or justify why CIFAR-10 is not used as a source in the DG setting — a single-source DG experiment with CIFAR-10 as the training set would directly connect the two halves. (The paper is not wrong to use separate setups, but the omission of any bridging discussion weakens the narrative.)

- **Image resizing not discussed.** CIFAR-10-W is listed as 224×224 (Table 1), while classifiers trained on CIFAR-10 are 32×32. The AccP experiments resize all test images, but the paper does not discuss potential information loss or artifacts from upscaling 32×32 training data to evaluate on 224×224 test domains.

## Suggestions

1. **Quantify domain diversity** — compute pairwise FID (or a similar feature-space distance) between all 180 domain centroids and report the distribution of these distances. Show a heatmap or dendrogram to help readers assess effective domain diversity rather than raw count.

2. **Document the cleaning protocol** — provide the criteria for noisy-image removal, per-domain removal counts, annotation procedure, and (if applicable) inter-annotator agreement. Even a brief protocol in the appendix would substantially strengthen reproducibility.

3. **Add a single-source DG experiment with CIFAR-10 as the training set** (or at minimum discuss why this is not done) to bridge the AccP and DG sections, making the paper's two empirical halves tell a more coherent story.

4. **Acknowledge the resizing issue** briefly — discuss that CIFAR-10-W is at 224×224 while CIFAR-10-trained classifiers see 32×32, and note whether any artifacts are expected.
