## Summary

This paper draws a novel connection between adversarial training (AT) and catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL). The authors empirically demonstrate that AT models forget knowledge acquired in early training stages (Table 1), then propose Adaptive Multi-teacher Self-distillation (AMS) — a plug-and-play regularizer combining multi-teacher distillation with confidence-based sample reweighting (RLC) — to mitigate this forgetting. Experiments across CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, and TinyImageNet show that AMS yields consistent improvements in robust accuracy (roughly +2–4% under AutoAttack) when integrated with various AT methods, and substantially reduces robust overfitting.

## Strengths

- **Novel perspective connecting AT to catastrophic forgetting.** The paper provides a well-motivated argument (Section 3) that the shifting distribution of adversarial examples during AT creates a CL-like scenario. Table 1 systematically quantifies forgetting on four datasets under both ℓ∞ and ℓ₂ threat models using standard CL metrics (FAA, FF), establishing the phenomenon as general rather than dataset-specific. For instance, on CIFAR-100 under ℓ∞, FAA drops from 100% to 86.51%.

- **Consistent robust accuracy improvements across multiple AT methods.** Table 2 (with baselines quoted from prior work) and Table 3 (within-experiment, same protocol) show that AMS improves robust accuracy when combined with PGD-10, TRADES, Sehwag et al., and Wang et al. on multiple datasets and architectures. Gains on AutoAttack reach +3.5% on CIFAR-10 and +2.2% on CIFAR-100. The improvements are consistent rather than cherry-picked.

- **Substantial reduction in robust overfitting.** Table 3 shows that AMS shrinks the gap between best and final checkpoint robust accuracy from 7.23% (PGD-10) to 0.47% (PGD-10+AMS) on CIFAR-10, and from 3.28% to 0.39% for TRADES. This is a clean, practically significant effect that the paper documents clearly.

- **Ablation confirms RLC's contribution.** Section 5.6 shows that removing RLC reduces robust accuracy (e.g., on CIFAR-100: TRADES+AMS drops from 28.05% to 27.62%; PGD-10+AMS drops from 28.31% to 27.58%), confirming the reweighting component serves a real (though modest) role.

### Minor

- **Table 2 mixes different checkpoint selection protocols.** The paper reports "best checkpoint" accuracy for the AMS-augmented methods (line 181), while the baseline numbers are quoted from prior papers whose reporting protocol (best vs. final checkpoint) is not disclosed. This introduces a systematic ambiguity about the magnitude of the claimed gains. The within-experiment comparisons in Table 3 (same protocol for both baseline and AMS) credibly show improvements of ~2–3.5%, so this does not invalidate the core claims, but the Table 2 framing should be clarified.

- **Missing error bars / multiple seeds.** Robust accuracy in AT can vary across runs (hyperparameter sensitivity, random initialization). The improvements reported (e.g., +2–4% under AutoAttack) would be more convincing with standard deviations or multiple-seed averages. This is standard practice in recent AT papers and would strengthen confidence in the results.

- **No ablation of single-teacher vs. multi-teacher.** The paper motivates multi-teacher distillation by arguing that different stages are sensitive to different perturbation types (line 34), but provides no experiment to support this claim. An ablation comparing AMS against a variant using only the most recent teacher (or a single aggregated teacher) would directly test whether the multi-teacher design is necessary, or whether a simpler single-teacher distillation suffices.

- **Computational overhead not quantified.** The paper acknowledges that saving one model per stage and computing KL divergences against all previous teachers grows with training length (line 142), but reports no measurements of training time, GPU memory, or FLOPs overhead. For practitioners considering adoption, knowing the practical cost would be necessary.

- **Forgetting measurement could be complemented by a control.** The empirical study in Section 3 measures forgetting by testing the final model on adversarial examples collected from each stage. Because these examples were generated by earlier (weaker) models, the observed drop in accuracy partly reflects that the final model's decision boundary has shifted — which is the definition of forgetting, but it conflates "forgetting useful knowledge" with "the earlier adversarial examples being lower-quality or no longer on the decision boundary." A complementary experiment using a fixed set of natural images (or adversarial examples from a single independent model) would strengthen the case that genuinely useful discriminative knowledge is being forgotten.

- **Rhetorical overstatement in places.** The abstract claims AMS can "significantly enhance robust accuracy" — the actual gains of ~2–4% on AutoAttack are meaningful and consistent, but the phrase sets an expectation of larger magnitude. Similarly, "substantial improvements" in the conclusion is somewhat stronger than the numbers warrant. The evidence itself is solid; the framing should match it.

### Trivial
- The FAA metric is defined such that initial accuracy on each collected dataset is 100% by construction (only correctly classified adversarial examples are kept). The paper notes this (Table 1 caption) but does not discuss how this choice bounds the metric. A brief note on the limitation would be helpful.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface one genuinely useful observation beyond the paper's own claims: the forgetting measurement (Table 1) could be productively paired with a control experiment on natural images from earlier stages to verify that the observed accuracy drop reflects forgetting of useful decision-relevant knowledge rather than simply the evolving nature of adversarial examples. The reviewer's suggestion that adding error bars would substantially strengthen the headline results is also well-taken — the improvements are credible but their statistical significance is unverifiable from single runs. No other genuinely novel insight emerges beyond the paper's own contributions.
