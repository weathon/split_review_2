## Summary

This paper studies attention entropy collapse (attention distributions becoming one-hot) in Transformers. It empirically demonstrates that in a simplified Transformer (only self-attention layers, trained on in-context linear regression), softmax-based attention suffers from entropy collapse accompanied by gradient explosion, while kernelized attention using Lipschitz functions (ReLU, ELU+1) maintains stable entropy and bounded gradients. The paper also shows that QK-LayerNorm, a standard mitigation, does not prevent collapse. A controlled experiment (Section 3.3) cleanly isolates how softmax's sensitivity to logit variance drives entropy collapse, while Lipschitz kernels are robust. The paper attempts to mechanistically link entropy collapse to gradient explosion via the Frobenius norm of the attention matrix \( \|P\|_F \).

## Strengths

- **Direct empirical demonstration of the collapse difference (Figure 1).** Softmax and QK-LayerNorm both show entropy dropping to zero, gradient norms exploding, and loss diverging; ReLU and ELU+1 maintain high entropy, bounded gradients, and converging loss. This single figure supports the paper's core empirical claim clearly.

- **Clean controlled experiment isolating the effect of logit variance (Section 3.3, Figure 3).** By fixing the query norm and sampling keys with increasing variance \( \sigma^2 \), the paper shows that softmax produces sharply peaked distributions with near-zero entropy at high variance, while ReLU and ELU+1 maintain near-uniform entropy. This cleanly separates the sensitivity difference from confounds of training dynamics.

- **Negative result on a standard remedy (QK-LayerNorm).** Figure 1 shows that QK-LayerNorm, a commonly used method to control logit magnitude, still suffers entropy collapse and gradient explosion. This strengthens the claim that the softmax function itself — not merely logit scale — is central to the instability.

- **Training-dynamics analysis connecting variance and entropy (Section 3.4, Figure 4).** The paper shows that during training, softmax's attention logit variance grows abruptly while entropy drops (inverse relationship), whereas Lipschitz kernels maintain stable variance and entropy. This bridges the static controlled experiment to actual training behavior.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The mechanistic claim linking entropy collapse to gradient explosion is correlational, not causal, and the provided bound does not fully justify it.** Section 3.5 uses an upper bound from Dasoulas et al. (2021) on the derivative of the attention *output with respect to the input* (\( \|\mathbf{D}A_X\| \)), not the gradient of the *loss with respect to parameters*, and the bound contains a second term \( \sqrt{2}\|X\|_{(2,\infty)}\|\mathbf{D}Z_X\| \) that the paper simply ignores without arguing that it is negligible. Figure 5 shows that \( \|P\|_F \) and the gradient norm rise together, but this is purely correlational: no intervention (e.g., clamping \( \|P\|_F \), manipulating temperature, or regularizing it) demonstrates that increasing \( \|P\|_F \) *causes* gradient explosion, nor do the experiments rule out alternative explanations (e.g., the unbounded derivative of softmax itself). The paper's language ("leading to gradient exploding," "Therefore, the more extreme... the larger the gradient becomes") overstates what the evidence can support. The central empirical observations (softmax collapses, Lipschitz kernels don't) remain valid, but this core mechanistic claim is not convincingly supported.

- **The experiments are limited to a single toy task (in-context linear regression) with a highly simplified architecture (only self-attention layers, no MLPs, no residual connections, no layer norm except as a baseline).** The title "Transformer Training Instability" implies general claims, yet the evidence is drawn entirely from this narrow setup. The paper acknowledges this design choice but does not discuss it as a limitation or provide any evidence that the patterns transfer to more realistic settings (language modeling, image classification, or a full Transformer with MLPs and residual connections). This substantially limits the scope of what the paper can claim.

- **Insufficient comparison to existing mitigation methods.** The related work section mentions NormSoftmax, σReparam, and ReLUFormer — all methods designed to address the same issue — yet only QK-LayerNorm is tested as a baseline. Since the paper's thesis involves the *Lipschitzness* property, comparing to NormSoftmax (which controls variance but is not Lipschitz) would be especially informative for distinguishing whether Lipschitzness or simple variance control is the key. Without these comparisons, the paper cannot support its strongest claim about Lipschitzness being the decisive property.

### Minor

- **Variance comparability across attention mechanisms (Figure 4, Left).** For softmax, "attention logit variance" refers to the variance of \( Q_i K_j^\top \). For Lipschitz kernel methods, the effective "logit" is \( \phi(Q_i)\phi(K_j)^\top \). It is unclear whether these quantities are directly comparable or at the same scale. The paper should clarify how variance is computed for kernel methods and whether the scales are equivalent.

- **The bound on \( \|\mathbf{D}A_X\| \) involves the derivative with respect to the input, not with respect to parameters.** The paper treats this as directly explaining loss-gradient explosion without clarifying the relationship between the two. This gap weakens the claimed mechanistic link (related to the Major point above).

- **Single-seed runs for training curves (Figures 1, 4, 5).** Given known stochasticity in Transformer training, the paper would benefit from showing multiple seeds with mean/variance bands to ensure the reported patterns are not artifacts of a single run.

### Trivial

- The paper should explicitly define "gradient norm" (e.g., \( \|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}\|_2 \) over all parameters) rather than leaving the reader to infer what is plotted.

- Line 14: "we emphasize bridge that" appears to be a grammatical error ("bridge the gap that" presumably).

## Suggestions
