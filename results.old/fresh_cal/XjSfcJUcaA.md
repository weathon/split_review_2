## Summary

This paper proposes a method for generating unrestricted adversarial examples using latent diffusion models (LDMs). The key idea is to inject adversarial perturbations into null-text embeddings during the denoising process, thereby subtly modifying the cross-attention mechanism rather than directly perturbing latent features. The paper further introduces perceptual loss (from a pre-trained VGG16) into the adversarial optimization objective to mitigate the blurriness caused by L2 reconstruction loss. Experiments on 1,000 ImageNet images show strong transferability across 11 CNN and ViT classifiers, competitive image quality metrics, and reasonable robustness against defenses.

## Strengths

- **Large and consistent improvements in black-box attack success rate.** Table 1 shows that with RN50 as surrogate, the method outperforms baselines by 12.59–50.31% in average ASR across non-surrogate models; similarly strong ranges hold for MN-v2 (9.56–48.48%), ViT-B (7.58–52.77%), and Swin-B (12.84–57.54%). These numbers are directly reported in Section 4.2 and provide precise, model-averaged comparisons.

- **State-of-the-art or competitive image quality across multiple metrics.** Table 2 shows the proposed method achieves the best or second-best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and NIQE scores on all four surrogate models (PSNR > 23 dB, LPIPS < 0.2 in most cases). This supports the claim that incorporating perceptual loss (Section 3.4) significantly improves visual fidelity.

- **Strong white-box attack success rates (>90%).** Section 4.2 reports stable white-box ASR above 90% across RN50, MNV2, ViT-B, Swin-B, and Inc-v3, substantially exceeding the ~70% typical of prior unrestricted methods. This demonstrates that the adversarial null-text embedding optimization (Section 3.3) reliably produces strongly adversarial examples.

- **Superior robustness against adversarial defenses.** Table 3 reports 52.40% ASR on the strongest defense (Ensemble-IncRes-V2), surpassing NCF by +24.5%, DiffAttack by +19.3%, and ACA by +3.3%. This validates the claim that unrestricted examples generated by this method bypass existing defenses effectively.

- **Novel and well-motivated use of perceptual loss.** The paper identifies L2 latent loss as a source of blurriness (Section 3.4) and introduces feature-reconstruction perceptual loss into adversarial optimization for the first time. Figure 2 provides visual evidence (e.g., text on cardboard boxes preserved vs. blurred by DiffAttack).

- **Principled design of adversarial injection timing.** Section 3.3 and Figure 1 justify starting adversarial gradient injection only at the last 10 of 50 DDIM steps by showing that classifier accuracy on early-step outputs is near-chance (too much noise for useful gradients). This is a clean, empirically grounded design choice.

### Minor

- **The abstract's improvement claim omits the known exception.** The abstract states "improves the adversarial transferability by an average of 12.59–50.3%" without qualification. This range corresponds to the RN50 surrogate case. The paper body (Section 4.2) honestly acknowledges that with Inc-v3 as surrogate, the method *underperforms* ACA (43.75% vs. 44.73% Ave. ASR). The abstract should qualify the claim (e.g., "for most surrogate models") or report the range across all surrogate models including the Inc-v3 case. This is a presentation issue, not a methodological flaw — the paper is transparent in the body.

- **The value of the null-text optimization iterations (N) is imprecisely specified.** Algorithm 1 takes N as input, and Section 3.3 says "null-text optimization process usually takes 500 steps." However, the Implementation Details section (Section 4.1) does not explicitly state the value of N used in the main experiments, nor whether this varies across surrogate models or timesteps. This is a minor reproducibility gap.

- **No ablation of the adversarial start timestep `t_s^adv`.** The paper sets `t_s^adv = 10` and justifies it with Figure 1, but does not vary this parameter (e.g., 5, 10, 15, 20) to demonstrate robustness or identify the optimal trade-off between attack success and image quality. Given that `t_s^adv` directly controls the perturbation budget, an ablation would strengthen the analysis.
