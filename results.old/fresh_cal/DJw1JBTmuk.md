## Summary

This paper proposes the Robo-Centric World Model (RCWM), a model-based RL architecture that decouples robot dynamics from environment dynamics using separate branches, a cross-attention interaction model, and a mask-guided decoder. A small number of prior robot segmentation masks (generated by SAM2) are used to warm up the model, after which RCWM can self-segment during pre-training. Experiments on Meta-world show that pre-trained RCWM improves sample efficiency and robustness to visual disturbances compared to a vanilla DreamerV3 world model.

## Strengths

1. **Demonstrated sample efficiency gain on 8 Meta-world tasks**: Figure 6 shows RCWM with pre-training achieves higher success rates faster than vanilla DreamerV3 across multiple challenging manipulation tasks. The aggregate plot over 40 runs (Figure 6b) provides clear quantitative evidence for the paper's central claim.

2. **Quantified robustness to environmental visual disturbances**: Figure 8 reports that policies trained with RCWM maintain near-original success rates under texture changes, while vanilla WM policies suffer severe performance drops (evaluated over 100 episodes per task). This directly supports the claim that the decoupled architecture yields more robust policies.

3. **Precise robot dynamics prediction on unseen tasks**: Figure 4 shows that pre-trained RCWM, without fine-tuning, yields lower robot dynamics prediction error than vanilla WM on the unseen coffee-pull task. The error curves over 100 imaginary trajectories quantify this advantage — a direct measure of the benefits of explicit robot-dynamics decoupling.

4. **Novel decoupled architecture with cross-attention interaction**: The two-branch design (robot dynamics model that is action-conditioned, environment dynamics model conditioned on interaction features) with a cross-attention interaction mechanism (Section 4.2, Equations 3–6) is a well-motivated architectural contribution that differs meaningfully from prior single-dynamics models.

5. **Minimal prior-mask requirement**: The method uses only 16 trajectories (4000 steps) with SAM2-generated masks for warmup (Section 4.3), after which the world model predicts its own masks. This is an efficient way to introduce structured robot knowledge without large annotation cost.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The core claims (sample efficiency gains, robustness improvements) are supported by the experimental results. The issues below are evidential gaps and presentation overclaims, not fundamental invalidation of the method.

### Major

- **Claimed mask-free prediction capability is not quantitatively evaluated.** The paper states (line 134) "RCWM is still able to accurately predict robot masks without using prior masks," yet the robot dynamics prediction error (Section 5.2, line 163) is computed using *ground-truth* masks — it multiplies the imagined reconstruction with the *ground-truth* mask, not with RCWM's own predicted mask. No quantitative metric (e.g., mask IoU on unseen tasks after warmup) is reported. Since the pipeline's practicality hinges on RCWM reliably self-segmenting after a short warmup, this is a significant evidential gap. The downstream sample efficiency results implicitly suggest the mask prediction functions well enough, but a direct evaluation is needed to substantiate the claim.

### Minor

- **The "independent inference" language overstates the actual decoupling.** The abstract claims "independent inference of robot dynamics from the environment" (line 4). However, the robot branch's representation encoder still receives the full observation \(o_t\) as input (line 84: \(z_t^R \sim q_\varphi(z_t^R \mid h_t^R, o_t)\)). The paper itself acknowledges in Section 4.1 (line 73) that robot dynamics must account for "potential effects of the environment on the robot," and in Section 4.2 (line 115) that the robot branch does "not entirely disregard[] environmental information." The *dynamics prediction* is indeed independent (the robot dynamics model at line 90 takes no environment-branch inputs), but the representation is computed from the full image. The claims should be softened to reflect "separate modeling of robot and environment transitions" rather than full independence.

- **Robustness mechanism is asserted but not directly validated.** The paper attributes robustness (Figure 8) to the robot branch providing "robust robot representation," but does not directly test whether the robot latent \(s_t^R\) is less affected by disturbances than the environment latent \(s_t^E\). The robustness result itself is clear, but the proposed mechanism (that the policy relies on a stable robot representation) is not distinguished from alternative explanations (e.g., the policy simply learns more conservative behavior). Latent-level analysis (e.g., measuring change in prediction error on the robot region under disturbance) would strengthen the attribution.

- **Ambiguity about the 20-successful-trajectory pre-fill.** The paper states (line 159) that "we randomly select 20 successful trajectories to pre-fill the replay buffer at the beginning of training" to stabilize training on Meta-world. It is not explicitly stated whether this is applied to *both* RCWM and vanilla WM. The context (Section 5.1, "Implementation details") and the citation to Wu et al. 2024 suggest it is a standard practice applied to both, but a clear statement is needed to rule out unfair advantage.

- **Pre-training tasks not named in the main text.** The paper states (line 153) "We first select 4 simple tasks for pre-training data collection" but does not name them (references to appendix, which is stripped). Without knowing which tasks were used for pre-training and how they relate to the 8 downstream tasks, it is difficult for readers to assess the breadth of generalization. This information should be in the main text.
