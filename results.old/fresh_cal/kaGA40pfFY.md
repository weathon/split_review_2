## Summary

This paper proposes "Rationality of Thought" (RoT), a prompting method that injects structured rational thinking steps (inspired by Kahneman's System 1/System 2 framework) into LLMs before they answer questions. The authors construct a cognitive bias dataset with 29 bias types and 464 questions. They claim RoT improves GPT-4's accuracy on this bias dataset by 18.7%, and that in a zero-shot setting, RoT outperforms few-shot CoT on GSM8K (+0.4), AQUA-RAT (+4.8), and ARC-c (+0.7).

## Strengths

- **New cognitive bias dataset fills a gap**: The paper curates 29 cognitive bias types from psychology literature, filters them through five explicit criteria, and produces 464 questions with standard answers. This is a reusable resource for evaluating rational thinking in LLMs, an underexplored area. (Section 3.1)

- **Ablation study identifies the most critical step**: Stepwise removal shows that removing "Execution/data analysis" (step 3) causes a 9% drop on GPT-4, giving concrete insight into why RoT works rather than treating it as a black box. (Section 3.4, Table 2)

- **Consistent improvements on reasoning tasks**: RoT shows modest but consistent improvements over both Direct and CoT (zero-shot) across GSM8K, SVAMP, AQUA-RAT, and ARC-c. For GPT-4, the average improvement across these tasks is ~3%, and the paper includes a fair zero-shot vs. zero-shot comparison in addition to the few-shot comparison highlighted in the abstract. (Section 4.3, Table 3)

- **Theoretical grounding**: The method is explicitly motivated by dual-process theory (System 1 / System 2), providing a principled rationale for the six-step structured reasoning process rather than ad-hoc engineering.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Headline cognitive bias result (18.7%) is unverifiable from the paper**: The abstract and introduction claim that RoT improves GPT-4's accuracy on the cognitive bias task by 18.7%. The paper reports GPT-4_RoT's accuracy as 97.4 (Section 3.3, line 99), but **does not report the Direct (baseline) accuracy for GPT-4**. Without this number, the reader cannot determine whether the improvement is 18.7 percentage points (Direct ≈ 78.7 → 97.4) or relative (Direct ≈ 82.1 → 97.4). Figure 2 likely contains per-category Direct accuracies but is rendered as an unreadable image. This is a basic reporting gap for the paper's strongest quantitative claim.

- **Abstract frames comparisons selectively**: The abstract claims RoT (zero-shot) "outperforms inference enhancement techniques such as CoT using few-shot." While the paper's main body includes the fairer zero-shot-vs-zero-shot comparison (Section 4.3, line 157 notes improvements of +6% over CoT-zero-shot on AQUA-RAT and +4.1% on ARC-c), the abstract leads with the asymmetric zero-shot-vs-few-shot framing. The gains over few-shot CoT are marginal on GSM8K (+0.4) and ARC-c (+0.7), within noise range for a single-run temperature-0 evaluation, yet they are presented as headline numbers without caveats about lack of variance estimates.

### Minor

- **Single-run evaluation with temperature=0**: All experiments use temperature=0, producing deterministic outputs. While this is common practice, it means no variance or confidence intervals can be reported. Differences of <1% on GSM8K (+0.4) and ARC-c (+0.7) cannot be assessed for reliability. The paper does not acknowledge this limitation.

- **Contamination risk for the cognitive bias dataset**: 348 of 464 questions (75%) were generated by GPT-4 from 116 seed questions (Section 3.1). The same model (GPT-4) is then evaluated on these questions. While the manual screening step provides some quality control, the paper does not discuss whether performance differs on human-sourced vs. GPT-4-generated questions. A breakdown would strengthen the result.

- **Ablation limited to one model and one task**: The ablation study (Section 3.4) is conducted only on GPT-4 and only on the cognitive bias dataset. Whether the same step importance generalizes to the reasoning tasks (GSM8K, AQUA-RAT, ARC-c) is unknown.

- **RoT prompt not shown**: The six steps (Identification, Decomposition, Execution, Reflection, Computation, Assessment) are described at a conceptual level, but the exact prompt template is not provided in the main text. For a prompting paper, this limits the reader's ability to understand the precise formulation or to replicate it without reverse-engineering.

### Trivial

- Equation (2) defines ρ_rot = argmax_ρ over the instruction, but the paper does not actually perform any search or optimization over instructions — ρ_rot is a single hand-designed prompt. This formalism is decorative and potentially misleading.

- "Execution" and "Computation" are listed as separate steps but their functional distinction (both involve analyzing data/calculations) is unclear from the prose descriptions.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting the GPT-4 Direct baseline accuracy on the cognitive bias dataset (even as a simple number in the text) would resolve the largest ambiguity.
- Breaking down the cognitive bias results by question source (human-sourced vs. GPT-4-generated) would address contamination concerns.
- Extending the ablation to at least one reasoning task (e.g., AQUA-RAT) would increase confidence that the step importance generalizes.
