## Summary

This paper proposes SiDyP, a two-stage framework for learning from LLM-generated noisy labels in text classification. The method first retrieves true label candidates via KNN on clean/noisy split data, then dynamically distills these candidates using feedback from a simplex diffusion model that serves as a posterior estimator. Experiments across four NLP tasks, five LLM families, and three noise types show consistent improvements over strong baselines, with 2–5% gains over DyGen (the strongest prior method) and 6–8% over fine-tuning on noisy labels directly. The work is the first to specifically target denoising for LLM-generated label noise in text.

## Strengths

- **Consistent improvements across a broad experimental scope.** SiDyP outperforms all baselines across 4 tasks, 5 LLMs (Llama-3-70B, Llama-3.1-70B/405B, GPT-4o, Mixtral-8×22B), and 3 noise types (LLM-generated, synthetic, real-world) (Tables 2–4). Over the strongest prior method DyGen, the gains average 2–5% with consistent direction across all settings. This breadth makes the empirical evidence compelling.

- **First systematic evaluation of denoising methods for LLM-generated label noise.** While prior work studied synthetic and real-world label noise, this paper demonstrates that LLM-generated noise (which exhibits different structure than synthetic noise) poses distinct challenges and that prior methods (especially transition-matrix approaches) struggle more with it (Table 4, comparing LLM vs. synthetic noise gaps). This fills a clear gap given the growing use of LLMs for data annotation.

- **Ablation cleanly validates both proposed components.** Table 5 shows that: (1) replacing the dynamic prior with a fixed prior hurts by 1.53%; (2) substituting the simplex diffusion with Gaussian diffusion or Dirichlet VAE drops performance by 8.58% and 2.17% respectively. This confirms that both the dynamic distillation mechanism and the simplex diffusion choice are responsible for the gains, not just overall framework complexity.

- **Novel combination of dynamic candidate distillation with simplex diffusion.** The feedback loop where the diffusion model's own predictions update candidate label weights (Algorithm 2) is a concrete mechanism to progressively refine uncertain samples, going beyond static filtering approaches.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing implementation details that hinder reproducibility.** Several key components are underspecified: (a) the conditioning mechanism for the diffusion model is described only as conditioning on \(s^{\tilde{y}}\) and \(x\) (Equation 4), but how these are incorporated into the neural network (concatenation, cross-attention, feature summation, etc.) is never stated; (b) critical hyperparameters are not reported — the simplex scaling factor \(k\) (line 68), the filtering thresholds \(\lambda\) and \(\gamma\) (line 45), the warm-up epochs \(\alpha\) (line 51), the noise ratio \(\sigma\) used for clean/noisy splitting (line 45), the KNN neighbor count \(K\), the diffusion timesteps \(T\), and the evaluation interval \(\beta\) are all absent from the paper. These directly affect performance and a reader cannot reproduce the method from the text alone.

- **Unfair comparison with Co-Teaching and JoCoR baselines.** The paper acknowledges (Section 5.2, line 140) that these multi-model methods train "from scratch" while SiDyP uses a pre-trained BERT backbone. Since these baselines were not adapted to use pre-trained models (unlike NPC, DyGen, and the proposed method), their poor performance (Table 2) is at least partly an artifact of this asymmetry. The main comparison against DyGen is fair and sufficient to support the paper's claims, but including the Co-Teaching/JoCoR results without leveling the pre-training advantage inflates the apparent margin. The authors should either adapt these baselines to use pre-trained BERT or remove them.

- **The argmax projection during inference (Equation 7) introduces a hard decision that is not discussed.** The inference procedure projects the model's logit output to \(\{\pm k\}\) via argmax, then substitutes this hard prediction back into the reverse diffusion step. This is a non-standard operation in diffusion models. The paper does not analyze whether this breaks gradient information during training, introduces discretization error that compounds over denoising steps, or how it compares to soft alternatives.

### Minor

- **The clean/noisy splitting criterion is ambiguously described.** The paper states "noisy samples tend to exhibit larger mean and standard deviation of Euclidean distances towards their assigned labels" and splits by "cutting off the top \(\sigma\) percent of training trajectories" (line 45). It is unclear how "distance to assigned label" is computed when the label is discrete (e.g., class 3) — is this distance to a class centroid in embedding space? The term "training trajectories" is also undefined: does this refer to per-epoch distances, final-epoch distances, or some aggregation? While the approach follows DyGen (Zhuang et al., 2023), a self-contained description is needed for reproducibility.

- **The candidate weight update rule in the dynamic distillation (Section 3.2) is underspecified.** The paper says "if model's predicted label lies in the candidate lists, the matched label candidate will increase accordingly" but does not specify the increment amount, schedule, or whether there is a learning rate. The subsequent normalization step is mentioned, but the update rule itself is not given.

- **No sensitivity analysis for key thresholds.** The method involves several critical parameters — the estimated noise rate \(\sigma\), the candidate confidence threshold \(\lambda\), the dominant-candidate threshold \(\gamma\), and the simplex scaling factor \(k\). The paper reports no analysis of how performance varies with these choices, leaving open questions about brittleness. Since \(\sigma\) must be estimated from the noisy data and could be inaccurate, this is a meaningful gap.

- **No discussion of limitations or failure cases.** The method's reliance on KNN label candidate retrieval assumes well-clustered embeddings. Cases where this fails (e.g., fine-grained classification with high semantic overlap, few-shot settings with very small clean sets) are not discussed.

### Trivial
- The paper uses the term "corrupted labels" and "noisy labels" slightly interchangeably without an explicit definitional bridge.
- The "2.05&" on line 140 appears to have a formatting artifact.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic correctly identifies that the Co-Teaching/JoCoR baselines are at a disadvantage because they do not use pre-trained BERT, but this observation is partially mitigated by the paper's own admission. A more interesting insight emerges from Table 4: the gap between SiDyP and DyGen is larger on LLM noise (5.21%) than on synthetic noise (3.26%), suggesting that the dynamic candidate distillation is particularly valuable for the structured, context-dependent noise produced by LLMs (as opposed to the random or class-conditional flipping of synthetic noise). This points to a broader principle: denoising methods for LLM-generated labels may benefit more from methods that leverage semantic structure (embedding neighborhoods, dynamic candidate refinement) than from methods that estimate noise transition matrices.

## Suggestions

1. **Report all omitted hyperparameters** (k, λ, γ, α, σ, K, T, β) and describe the diffusion model's conditioning architecture. Even a one-sentence description (e.g., "noisy label simplex and BERT embedding are concatenated and projected via a linear layer") would suffice.
2. **Either adapt Co-Teaching and JoCoR to use pre-trained BERT or move them to an appendix** with a clear caveat. The main results comparison against DyGen is sufficient and fair.
3. **Add a brief sensitivity analysis** (even in an appendix) for the two most critical thresholds: the noise rate estimate σ and the candidate confidence threshold λ.
4. **Clarify the clean/noisy splitting procedure** by defining "training trajectories" (e.g., per-sample loss values or embedding distances aggregated across training epochs) and specifying the distance metric for discrete labels.
5. **Add a paragraph on limitations** in a discussion section.
