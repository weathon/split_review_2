## Summary

CEDNet replaces the conventional design of a heavy classification backbone + lightweight fusion module (e.g., FPN) with a cascade of encoder-decoder stages that perform multi-scale feature fusion from the earliest layers. By distributing computation evenly across stages and fusing features within each stage's decoder, CEDNet achieves consistent gains (1–3% box AP, 1–2% mask AP, 0.8–2.2% mIoU) over ConvNeXt baselines across multiple detectors, segmentation frameworks, and token mixer types, with comparable parameters and FLOPs.

## Strengths

1. **Direct ablation validates the core hypothesis.** Table "Effectiveness of early feature fusion" (lines 271–283) varies the proportion of parameters allocated before the first fusion stage and shows monotonic AP improvement from 40.6 (latest fusion) to 43.3 (earliest fusion). This directly supports the paper's central claim that early multi-scale fusion improves dense prediction.

2. **Consistent gains over strong, fairly-controlled baselines.** CEDNet-NeXt-T achieves 48.3 AP vs. ConvNeXt-T+FPN's 45.4 AP with nearly identical parameters (39M vs. 39M) on RetinaNet (Table 2b), and also outperforms BiFPN (46.7) and NAS-FPN (46.6). These baselines are all pre-trained on ImageNet-1K, making the comparison controlled and meaningful.

3. **Generalization across three encoder-decoder structures.** Hourglass-style (47.4 AP), UNet-style (47.9 AP), and FPN-style (48.3 AP) all surpass ConvNeXt-T+FPN (45.4 AP) and other early-fusion methods (HRNet 45.7, SpineNet 47.1, GiraffeDet 46.6), showing the cascade design is effective regardless of the specific encoder-decoder choice.

4. **Generality across diverse token mixers and tasks.** CEDNet improves over baselines using vanilla 3×3 conv (+3.6 AP), local window attention (+2.5 AP), depth-wise 7×7 conv (+2.9 AP), and cross-window attention (+1.5 AP) (Table "Different token mixers"). Gains also transfer to instance segmentation and semantic segmentation, confirming the method is not task-specific.

5. **Efficient parameter usage.** CEDNet-NeXt-T uses 39M parameters (same as ConvNeXt-T) and 255G FLOPs (vs. 243G). The LR CED block adds <1% parameters for a 0.4 AP gain, showing the improvement does not come from disproportionate compute scaling.

### Minor

1. **The "guidance" claim is asserted but not directly evidenced.** The paper repeatedly states that "high-level features from early stages guide low-level feature learning in subsequent stages" (abstract, Section 1, Section 3.1, Section 3.2). This is framed as a "hallmark" of CEDNet. However, the only evidence provided is correlational: the ablation on fusion time shows that earlier fusion improves accuracy. The paper does not include any analysis — feature visualizations, centered kernel alignment, probing experiments, or even a simple ablation that removes the high-resolution output from an early stage — that demonstrates the claimed guidance mechanism directly. The empirical gains stand on their own, but the narrative promise exceeds the evidence. The paper would be stronger if it either provided causal evidence or simply described the architecture without the "guidance" framing.

2. **Comparison with SpineNet and GiraffeDet is not controlled for pre-training.** These methods are trained from scratch on COCO, while CEDNet is pre-trained on ImageNet-1K. The table caption's note about this is a LaTeX comment (%) on line 109, meaning it does **not** appear in the rendered paper. The main text states "the CEDNet models surpassed other early feature fusion methods: HRNet, SpineNet, and GiraffeDet" (lines 134–135) without caveat. Since the pre-training advantage can account for a non-trivial portion of the gap, this comparison should be explicitly qualified in the visible text. (The core claim against ConvNeXt+FPN/BiFPN, which are also pre-trained, remains unaffected.)

This is not "negligible" — it represents a non-trivial fraction of total parameters. While the ratio would be small, claiming exactly 0% is an overstatement. This is a minor presentational issue that does not affect the empirical results.

## Suggestions
