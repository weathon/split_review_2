## Summary

This paper proposes P3DTrack, a 2D MOT method that learns 3D object representations from pseudo-3D labels generated via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) on the input video itself—without LiDAR or pre-trained depth models. The framework jointly learns 3D representation and association via a GNN, then uses a 3D Kalman Filter for online tracking. Experiments on KITTI and Waymo Open Dataset show improvements over baselines. The core idea—lifting 2D association to 3D space using only video-derived pseudo-labels—is novel and well-motivated.

## Strengths

- **Novel approach with clear motivation**: The paper identifies a real gap (2D association is hard, 3D association is easy) and proposes learning 3D representation from video alone, without LiDAR or pre-trained depth models. This is a genuine departure from prior depth-based MOT methods that rely on off-the-shelf depth estimators. (Section 1, Fig. 1)

- **Strong KITTI results**: On the KITTI test set (Table 2), P3DTrack achieves the best AssA (association accuracy) and the lowest number of ID switches among methods without additional training data or pre-trained models on autonomous driving datasets. The 3.0 AssA improvement over the next best comparable method is substantial. (Section 4.3, Table 2)

- **Ablations support the core claim**: Table 3 shows that adding the GNN association module + 3D representation learning improves MOTA by 4.2 and IDF1 by 3.8 over the baseline. Table 4 further isolates the contribution of the 3D representation: appearance + 3D representation outperforms appearance-only by 1.1 MOTA and 1.6 IDF1. (Section 4.4, Tables 3, 4)

- **Low computational overhead**: Inference latency increases by only 12 ms (~5.5%) over the baseline, with modest additional parameters and memory, showing practical viability. (Table 8)

- **Systematic hyperparameter analysis**: Tables 5–7 provide informative comparisons across different 3D representations (SfM-only, pre-trained depth, learned), detection/appearance thresholds, and the 3D-vs-appearance similarity weight, giving evidence that the chosen settings are reasonable. (Section 4.4, Tables 5–7)

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Pseudo-label generation pipeline lacks quantitative characterization** (lines 67, 71, 73). The entire method hinges on the quality of SfM-derived labels, yet the paper provides no statistics on: (1) what fraction of training sequences are discarded due to low ego-motion, (2) what fraction of tracklets receive pseudo 3D labels vs. are left unlabeled, or (3) any measure of label quality (e.g., residual error vs. ground-truth 3D positions on a held-out subset). Without this, the reader cannot assess how representative the training signal is or whether the method is secretly benefiting from a highly selective labeling process. The paper states "only label static objects" (line 73), making this characterization even more critical.

- **Unsupported claim about generalization to moving objects** (lines 105–106). The paper asserts: "the network can easily make the 3D position prediction of the moving object, because the network does not distinguish the object movement in one frame." This is a non-sequitur—a single-frame network still needs to predict 3D position from 2D cues, and it is not obvious that training only on static objects' 3D positions (generated by SfM, which explicitly filters moving objects) transfers to moving objects. No experiment (e.g., per-tracklet analysis by motion status) supports this claim. Table 5 shows the learned representation outperforms SfM-only positions, but this does not disentangle whether the improvement comes from generalizing to moving objects or from learning better static-object representations.

- **WOD comparison does not isolate the method's contribution** (Section 4.3, lines 192–193). P3DTrack is compared with QDTrack using a different detector (CenterNet vs. Faster-RCNN), different post-processing, and different matching strategies. The paper acknowledges that its method has lower IDF1 due to lower detection recall, yet still frames the result as "state-of-the-art" on WOD. A 0.3 MOTA gain could easily arise from detection threshold tuning or NMS settings rather than the claimed 3D representation. The KITTI results are cleaner, but the WOD SOTA claim in the abstract and contributions needs a properly controlled comparison (same detector, ablating only the association method).

### Minor

- **Missing ablation isolating GNN without 3D representation** (Table 3). The ablation goes: baseline → +low-quality detections → +GNN+3D. The 4.2 MOTA gain conflates the GNN architecture and the 3D representation. Table 4 partially addresses this by comparing appearance-only vs. 3D KF motion models within the same GNN framework, and the 1.1 MOTA gain from learned 3D representation is reasonable evidence. However, a cleaner ablation would add a "GNN + appearance only (no 3D representation)" row to Table 3.

- **No failure case or limitation analysis**. The paper correctly scopes itself to ego-centric videos (Section 4.1), but does not discuss what happens when SfM degrades (low texture, tunnels, traffic jams, purely translational motion). Since the pseudo-label pipeline is the method's foundation, some qualitative failure examples would substantially strengthen the paper.

## Suggestions

1. **Characterize the pseudo-label pipeline quantitatively**: Report the percentage of training sequences that yield usable labels after ego-motion filtering, and the percentage of tracklets (by object class) that receive pseudo 3D labels. If possible, compare predicted 3D positions against ground-truth LiDAR cuboid centers on a small held-out subset.
2. **Provide evidence for moving-object generalization**: Partition the test set by object motion status (static vs. moving, using ground-truth velocity) and report tracking metrics separately for each subset. This directly validates or refutes the claim in Section 3.2.
3. **Add a pure GNN-without-3D ablation** to Table 3 (or a note explaining why this is infeasible) to cleanly separate the effects of the GNN architecture and the 3D representation.
4. **On WOD, either provide a controlled comparison** (same detector, different association) or soften the SOTA claim to reflect the uncontrolled nature of the comparison. The KITTI results already carry the paper's main message.
