## Summary

This paper investigates mechanistic similarity between Transformer (Pythia-160M) and Mamba (130M) language models using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to extract interpretable features from the residual stream. The authors introduce a metric (Max Pairwise Pearson Correlation, MPPC) to quantify feature similarity across architectures, use skylines (seed-variant models, SAE variants) to isolate the effect of architectural differences, and find that most features have similar activation patterns across architectures (average MPPC 0.74 vs. 0.76 for same-architecture seed variants). The paper further analyzes induction circuits in Mamba via path patching, discovering a structurally analogous mechanism to Transformer induction heads with a nuanced "Off-by-One" motif where Mamba's local convolution merges token information into the SSM state one position later than a Transformer attention head would.

## Strengths

- **First SAE-based cross-architecture comparison.** The paper demonstrates that SAEs provide a viable, architecture-agnostic decomposition for comparing representational structure across Transformer and Mamba, whereas neuron-level comparison yields near-zero similarity (Section 4.4, Figure 2a). This opens a previously unavailable methodological path for studying architectural universality.

- **Skylines as a clever control framework.** The paper introduces a hierarchy of invariances (SAE seed variant → model seed variant → cross-architecture) and uses two skylines to bound the marginal effect of architectural change. The resulting MPPC difference distribution shows that ~60% of features have near-zero difference between cross-architecture and same-architecture similarity (Section 4.3, Figure 2b), providing quantitative evidence that most features are robust to architectural change.

- **Discovery of the Off-by-One motif in Mamba's induction circuit.** Through path patching, the paper identifies that Mamba's local convolution layer writes information from token B into the SSM state at position B+1 — a concrete architectural difference from the Transformer induction mechanism (Section 5.3, Figure 6). This is a novel finding that contributes to the understanding of how SSM-based models implement in-context learning.

- **Depth specialization extends to cross-architecture features.** The paper shows that matched features across architectures align at proportional depths (layer 2l in Mamba for layer l in Pythia, Section 4.5, Figure 2c), extending prior neuron-based depth specialization findings to cross-architecture SAE features and supporting the hypothesis of a universal depth-wise motif.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The MPPC metric lacks systematic semantic validation.** The paper's core claim — that "most features are similar" across architectures — rests on MPPC, a purely statistical correlation of activation patterns. While the paper acknowledges this limitation qualitatively and provides four case studies (Figure 3), it does not systematically validate whether high-MPPC matched pairs actually correspond to semantically related features. Given the massive search space (~300k Pythia features × ~590k Mamba features), the expected maximum correlation under a null model is not reported. Without a random baseline or a human-/automated-judgment study on a representative sample of matched pairs, it is unclear how much of the observed MPPC reflects genuine semantic alignment versus spurious correlation driven by sparsity or data distribution artifacts.

- **The GPT-4o complexity scoring is used as a core interpretive tool without validation.** The complexity-based interpretation of MPPC (Section 5) — specifically the claim that subtracting seed-variant MPPC from cross-architecture MPPC "cancels out the effect of feature complexity" — relies on GPT-4o-assigned complexity scores. No inter-rater reliability, human validation, or prompt details are provided. The assumption that the complexity effect is additive and identical across cross-architecture and seed-variant settings is unexamined. This makes the paper's central interpretive claim (that the remaining MPPC difference isolates architectural variation) fragile.

### Minor

- **No null baseline for the matching procedure.** With ~590k candidate features in Mamba per Pythia feature, the expected maximum Pearson correlation under random activations should be estimated (e.g., by shuffling feature labels or using a randomized control). Without this, it is difficult to assess what MPPC values indicate meaningful similarity versus chance.

- **Framing of "same induction algorithm" overstates the evidence.** Section 6.2 is titled "Mamba and Transformer Implement The Same Induction Algorithm," yet the paper's own analysis identifies a substantive architectural difference (the Off-by-One motif) and admits "we are still unclear why such mechanism forms." At the level of functional description ("mix information from the previous token and retrieve it later"), many sequence models would satisfy this definition. The framing should be softened to reflect the functional analogy and acknowledged differences more accurately.

- **Only induction circuits are analyzed.** The circuit-level universality claim is based on a single circuit type (induction). It is unclear whether the observed structural analogy generalizes to other circuits (e.g., IOI, indirect object identification, or factual recall). This limits the scope of the circuit-level universality conclusions.

- **No Mamba seed-variant skyline.** The model seed-variant skyline uses a different Pythia seed, but no symmetric Mamba seed-variant control is provided. While the main analysis focuses on Pythia→Mamba matching, a symmetric baseline would strengthen the interpretation of feature replicability in Mamba.

## Novel Insights

The single most novel insight emerging from these reviews — beyond the paper's own contributions — is that the **Off-by-One motif may be the more interesting finding than the claimed universality**. The harsh critic correctly notes that the paper oscillates between emphasizing similarity (to support universality) and identifying a concrete architectural divergence. If the authors reframed the paper around this tension — i.e., SAEs reveal largely shared feature-level representations, but circuit-level implementations diverge in specific, interpretable ways — the contribution would be more honest and potentially more impactful. The Off-by-One motif is the kind of granular architectural insight that mechanistic interpretability should produce, and it does not need to be subordinated to a universalist narrative to be valuable.

## Suggestions

1. Add a null baseline experiment: shuffle feature activation vectors or compute MPPC between Pythia features and randomly initialized (untrained) Mamba SAEs. Report the expected MPPC under the null to calibrate the reported values.

2. Conduct a small human-annotation study (or use a stronger automated protocol) on a stratified sample of ~100 matched feature pairs across the MPPC range to validate whether high-MPPC pairs correspond to semantically related concepts.

3. Validate or replace the GPT-4o complexity scoring: either report inter-rater agreement with human annotators on a subset, or use a more principled proxy for complexity (e.g., feature frequency, entropy of activation distribution, or number of unique token contexts that trigger the feature).

4. Soften the "same algorithm" claim in Section 6.2 to "functionally analogous mechanism" or "structurally analogous circuit" to better match the evidence, and reframe the Off-by-One motif as a nontrivial architectural divergence rather than a curiosity.

5. Add a symmetric Mamba seed-variant skyline to complement the Pythia seed-variant control, which would strengthen the interpretation of feature replicability on the Mamba side.
