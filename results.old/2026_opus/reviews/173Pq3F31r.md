## Summary
The paper proposes a unified transformer Seq2Seq framework that jointly models expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score *content* and a global performance *style* representation, trained on both paired (ASAP) and unpaired score/performance data via masked reconstruction. It further introduces a DDPM-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module that generates style embeddings from score content alone, enabling alignment-free EPR with automatic, score-conditioned styling.

## Strengths
- **Alignment-free Seq2Seq EPR achieves competitive accuracy against alignment-dependent baselines.** Table 3 shows Ours (PSR) reaches the highest alignment rate (92.27%) and lowest insertion rate (3.77%) versus DExter and VirtuosoNet, validating the central architectural claim that note-level alignment is unnecessary for competitive EPR.
- **Quantitative gap between style and content embeddings on identification tasks.** Table 4 reports Style→Composer 77.46% vs. Cont→Composer 29.99% and Style→Performer 42.07% vs. Cont→Performer 9.94%, providing concrete (though interpretively delicate — see Major #2) evidence that the two latent spaces capture different aspects.
- **Score-conditioned style generation without composer/performer labels.** Figure 4 shows PSR-generated style embeddings replicate the era-based clustering (Baroque/Classical/Romantic/Modern) of styles extracted from real performances, suggesting the diffusion prior captures meaningful score→style structure without explicit labels.
- **APT improvements on most MUSTER/ScoreSimilarity sub-metrics.** Table 1 shows Ours wins on $E_{\text{extra}}$, $E_{\text{onset}}$, $E_{\text{offset}}$, $E_{\text{avg}}$, $E_{\text{dur}}$, $E_{\text{pitch}}$ with statistical significance against the end-to-end baseline.
- **Joint EPR/APT framing is conceptually clean** (§3.2, Eq. 1–4) and unifies a previously fragmented literature, with paired and unpaired settings cleanly defined.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Joint-modeling claim is confounded with unpaired data.** §5.1's central conclusion — "joint modeling, together with training on additional unpaired data, leads to better EPR performance" — bundles two factors. The EPR-Only baseline in Table 2 differs from Ours (Target) in both (a) absence of APT joint training and (b) absence of masked-reconstruction unpaired supervision. The paper's own sentence concedes this. A symmetric APT-only ablation for Table 1 is also absent, so the "mutual supervision" narrative is supported in only one direction and confounded even there. A 2×2 ablation ({EPR-only, joint} × {paired only, paired + unpaired}) is needed to isolate the headline claim.
- **The disentanglement diagnostic points in the wrong direction.** §5.2 reads Style→Composer 77.46% as evidence of *disentanglement*, but a style vector that predicts a content/structural attribute (composer) better than the content vector itself (77.46% vs. 29.99%) is, prima facie, evidence of style/content *entanglement*. The paper offers a plausible post-hoc explanation ("pianists align style with composer conventions"), but the explanation is not tested. A more direct probe — hold content fixed, swap style across performer pairs, and measure (a) content preservation (note F1 against the score) and (b) style transfer success — would speak to disentanglement in a way Table 4 does not. The style-transfer test in Figure 5 with only 3 samples is too thin to fill this role.
- **PSR is evaluated against a metric mismatch.** Table 2's MAE and KL for Ours (PSR) are computed against a *specific* human reference, but by construction PSR generates one of many plausible styles for a given score, not the ground-truth style. The Ours (Target) row, in contrast, is handed the human's style at inference and is therefore expected to score well. The two rows are not comparable in their current form. PSR's evaluation should be distributional (e.g., distance between PSR-generated and human style distributions conditional on score) or perceptual (forced-choice listening test) rather than point-MAE against a specific reference. The current Figure 4 t-SNE-overlap argument is suggestive but qualitative.

### Minor
- **σ² interpretation is internally tense.** Table 2 scores variance by closeness to the human reference, but §5.1 then dismisses DExter's high variance via listening tests as "unstable dynamics rather than meaningful expressiveness." This is reasonable in isolation but uses listening tests to override the metric the same table treats as evidence. A brief discussion of what σ² alone can and cannot say would tighten the analysis.
- **ATEPP split protocol unspecified.** §5.2 states an 8:1:1 split for ATEPP but does not say whether the split is by piece, by performer, or both. If performer signatures appear in both train and test, performer identification becomes partly memorization. This should be specified, as the validity of Table 4 hinges on it.
- **EPR alignment metrics are underspecified.** "Alignment rate / insertion rate / missing rate" (§4.3) are named but the underlying alignment tool, tolerance, and matching procedure are not pinned down precisely; these names have varied across the EPR literature.
- **Pitch edit distance regression on APT deserves more than a one-line attribution.** Table 1 shows $E_p$ rises from 2.02 (Neural) to 3.08 (Ours), ~50% relative, while the text attributes this in one sentence to ornaments/trills. For a transcription system that is a non-trivial concession; some quantitative analysis of where these errors arise would strengthen the discussion.
- **Subjective study scale is small for the breadth of claims.** N=11 raters and 5 pieces (one per composer) for the main EPR listening test, and 3 samples for style transfer, are tight for the per-attribute, per-composer narrative in §5.1 and the "well-structured style space" claim in §5.2. Reporting confidence intervals on the current numbers and/or expanding N would help.

### Trivial
None worth listing beyond the items above.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Add a quantitative PSR-vs-human distributional comparison (e.g., a FAD-style distance on style vectors, or classifier accuracy on era labels for PSR-generated vs. real styles) to complement Figure 4.
- Brief discussion of why an architectural prior (sequence vs. single vector) plus KL on $z_s$ is expected to suffice without an explicit content-leakage objective (mutual-information minimization, adversarial removal of content from $z_s$, etc.).
- Frame the alignment-free contribution more precisely relative to Beyer & Dai (2024) (cited and adopted on the APT side) — the genuinely new part is moving EPR onto the same paradigm.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The most genuinely interesting framing is the PSR module — recommending a style from the score alone, mimicking how a pianist chooses an interpretive stance — and the unification of EPR/APT under a shared content space. Both are the paper's own contributions rather than insights surfaced through review.

## Suggestions
- Run a 2×2 ablation isolating joint training from unpaired data, and include an APT-only counterpart on Table 1, to support the bidirectional mutual-supervision claim that the paper currently frames as central.
- Replace the Style→Composer identification reading with a direct disentanglement probe: style-swap experiments measuring content preservation (note F1 against the source score) and style transfer success at scale (more than 3 samples).
- Re-cast Table 2's PSR evaluation in distributional terms — e.g., per-piece distance between PSR-generated and empirical style distributions, or a forced-choice PSR-vs-human listening test — rather than MAE against a single human reference that PSR was not trained to hit.
- Clarify the ATEPP split protocol (by-piece vs. by-performer) and precisely specify the alignment tool/tolerance used to compute the EPR alignment/insertion/missing rates.
- Add confidence intervals to the existing subjective ratings; expand N if feasible.

## Axis Evaluation
- **Originality:** Moderate. Joint EPR/APT and PSR are fresh framings for this community; the constituent ingredients (Seq2Seq with masked recon, content/style split with KL, DDPM on a latent) are standard.
- **Importance of research question:** Solid. EPR and APT are central MIR tasks and uniting them is valuable; making EPR accessible without manual style control or composer labels is practically useful.
- **Claims well supported:** Partially. APT numbers, alignment-free EPR accuracy, and the qualitative PSR clustering are well supported. The joint-modeling and disentanglement claims as written are not fully isolated from confounds.
- **Soundness of experiments:** Mixed. Datasets and protocol are reasonable; the EPR-only ablation, identification metrics, and listening-test scale leave gaps.
- **Clarity of writing:** Good. Methodology, losses, and architecture are clearly laid out.
- **Value to community:** Real. Even with the evidential gaps, an alignment-free unified EPR/APT system with a score-conditioned style prior is a useful artifact and a reasonable direction for follow-up work.
