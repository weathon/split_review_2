## Summary
The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework built entirely on SAM. It has two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that iteratively samples points from a similarity-derived distribution and updates probabilities based on verified positives/negatives, and (2) output-token-based verification that compares SAM's decoder output tokens between exemplars and candidate prompts using the TS-SS hybrid geometric metric. On CARPK (MAE 4.68) it outperforms all supervised baselines, and on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25) it leads SAM-only training-free methods.

## Strengths
- **Strong CARPK result with a SAM-only pipeline (Table 1):** MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13 surpasses every supervised baseline listed (SAFECOUNT 5.33, BMNet+ 5.76, LOCA 9.97, FamNet 18.19) while requiring no training and no encoder beyond SAM. This is the paper's most defensible empirical contribution and genuinely supports the "output token verification is enough" thesis.
- **Best among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147 (Table 1):** MAE 16.25 vs. TFOC 19.95, C-Anything 27.97, and naive SAM 42.48 — a clear improvement within the "no auxiliary encoder" regime the paper explicitly carves out.
- **Output token verification is empirically more discriminative than image-embedding verification (Table 5 + Figure 5):** With everything else held fixed, output tokens give MAE 16.25 vs. 16.94 for image embeddings, and the PCA plots in Figure 5 show separable positive/negative clusters in token space that overlap in embedding space. This grounds the central methodological claim with concrete (if modest) evidence.
- **The verification framework is genuinely architecture-clean:** Eq. 1 and §3.2 show counting reduced to similarity over SAM's existing decoder outputs, with no bolted-on encoder — a meaningful framing contribution given the trend toward stacking auxiliary foundation models.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Abstract directly contradicts Table 1.** The abstract states "Experimental results show our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." Table 1 shows LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62), and the training-free TFCAC (12.26) all beat the proposed method on FSC-147. The Introduction is more careful ("outperforms mostly existing training-free methods"), but the abstract — the most-read text — is false as written. This needs to be rewritten to scope the claim to "SAM-only" or to lead with CARPK, where the claim is supported.

- **TS-SS ablation gap is too small to support the geometric narrative.** §3.2 and §4.4 build an extended case that output tokens jointly encode "semantic(angular) and positional(magnitudinous)" information and that TS-SS is therefore required, with §4.4 saying non-hybrid metrics "have reached a performance plateau" that TS-SS "breaks through." But Table 3 shows: dot product 19.58, cosine 16.48, L2 16.53, TS-SS 16.25 — a 0.23 MAE gap from cosine to TS-SS, with no variance/std reported. Given multinomial sampling and an RMSE of 133, this gap is well within the range where it could be a hyperparameter-equivalent tweak. The conclusion may be right, but the evidence as presented does not establish a geometric insight; the language overstates what the table shows.

- **Large unexplained RMSE/MAE ratio on FSC-147.** The method posts MAE 16.25 alongside RMSE 133.11 (ratio ≈ 8.2), while methods with comparable or worse MAE post much lower RMSE (TFCAC 12.26/56.33 ≈ 4.6; LOCA 10.79/56.97 ≈ 5.3; BMNet+ 14.62/91.83 ≈ 6.3). A ratio above 8 implies a tail of images where the count is off by hundreds. §5 gestures at "dense distributions of very small instances" but offers no per-image or per-category breakdown. Whether the iterative sampler over-counts on flat post-zeroing distributions or terminates prematurely on dense scenes is left unexplained, and this hides where the FSC-147 method is failing.

### Minor
- **Efficiency story is in tension with Table 4.** The motivation against prior methods is that their cost "scales linearly with the number of prompts," yet the best configuration uses an average of 1,438 prompts per image (16 iterations × 128 prompts). Each prompt requires a SAM mask-decoder forward pass to obtain its output token. The paper reports 1.69 s/image and claims "more than twice as fast" than TFCAC on CARPK, but provides no head-to-head wall-clock or decoder-forward count vs. grid or SLIC prompting at matched accuracy. The probabilistic sampler may well be more efficient at matched MAE; the table just does not show that curve.

- **One-shot framing on FSC-147 is generous (Table 2).** MAE 18.57 vs. LOCA 12.53 and TFCAC 15.15 is a substantial gap — calling it "comparable" / "competitive" without acknowledging the magnitude is loose, though the gap to CFOCNet (28.60) and FamNet (26.76) is real.

- **Probability update rule in §3.1/Figure 3 is described in prose only.** Positives "set to 0," negatives "decreased" — no formula for the decrement, no statement of whether the distribution is re-normalized before the next multinomial sample. This is the central mechanism of the prompt generator and warrants a precise equation.

- **Hyperparameters reported as "based on validation experiments."** t-scale 0.066, verification threshold 300, NMS IoU, early-stop IoU 0.95 with patience 5 — the paper does not state whether these were tuned on a held-out split of FSC-147 or selected on the test set. Given that all affect accuracy, an explicit statement on the validation protocol would resolve the ambiguity.

- **Geometric assertion about magnitude = positional, angle = semantic is stated, not demonstrated.** §3.2 frames this as the motivating property of SAM's output tokens but offers no measurement supporting the decomposition. Figure 5's PCA supports "tokens separate better than embeddings" but does not specifically support the angular-vs-magnitudinous split that motivates TS-SS over cosine.

### Trivial
- **§5 discussion sentence breaks coherence:** "Our method, composed solely of SAM without additional models, demonstrates superior computational efficiency compared to state of the art. This limitation affects scalability for real-time applications and large datasets." The antecedent of "this limitation" is lost — likely a writing slip.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A controlled experiment that perturbs token magnitudes vs. directions separately, to test the "angular = semantic, magnitudinous = positional" claim directly rather than via downstream MAE.
- Per-category and per-density-bin error breakdown on FSC-147 to explain where the RMSE tail comes from.
- A wall-clock / decoder-forward comparison against grid and SLIC prompting at matched MAE, on the same hardware, to substantiate the efficiency framing.
- Variance / std reporting across exemplar choices, especially given that CARPK uses 12 random exemplars and FSC-147 sampling is stochastic — this would put the 0.23 (TS-SS vs cosine) and 0.69 (token vs embedding) gaps on a firmer footing.
