## Summary

The paper investigates localized machine unlearning through the lens of memorization literature. The authors systematically study two memorization hypotheses (deepest-layers vs. scattered critical parameters) and find that data-agnostic strategies cannot simultaneously achieve good unlearning quality and preserve utility. Building on insights from Maini et al. (2023)'s CritMem, they propose a new localization strategy using channel-level, weighted-gradient criticality scores computed non-iteratively (more efficient than CritMem), paired with a simple Reset+Finetune algorithm to form **DEL**. On CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and SVHN/ViT, DEL achieves near-oracle unlearning metrics while modifying only ~30% of parameters, outperforming both full-parameter and prior localized methods. Controlled ablations (random masking, granularity/criteria ablation, forget-set-specific analysis) convincingly isolate the source of improvement.

## Strengths

1. **Systematic derivation from memorization hypotheses, validated by controlled ablations.** The paper does not just propose a method; it traces two specific memorization hypotheses to concrete localization strategies (Deepest, Shallowest, CritMem, SalLoc), investigates their failure modes, and uses those insights to design a better strategy. The channel-level vs. parameter-level granularity ablation (Table 1) and the weighted-gradient vs. plain-gradient comparison cleanly isolate which design choices matter.

2. **Convincing evidence that parameter selection drives success, not just reduced parameter count.** The random-masking control (Table 3) shows that standard masks from any localization strategy strictly outperform random masks with the same layer-wise structure on all three metrics (Δ_forget, Δ_MIA, Δ_test). This is the right control experiment and it cleanly supports the core claim.

3. **State-of-the-art empirical results with robustness to budget.** DEL achieves the smallest absolute deviations from the oracle across both datasets, both forget set distributions (IID and non-IID), and both unlearning and utility metrics (Table 2, Figure 4). Critically, DEL maintains near-oracle performance across a wide range of parameter budgets (~12% to 100%), while other strategies degrade sharply as budget shrinks (Figure 2, Figure 3). This is a concrete practical advantage.

4. **Tailoring to the forget set is shown to matter, with honest characterization of when it does.** Table 4 demonstrates that forget-set-specific weighted gradients are especially beneficial for non-IID forget sets, while for IID forget sets most criteria perform similarly. The paper does not overclaim — it honestly delineates where its contribution matters most.

### Minor

1. **Budget selection for the main comparison table (Table 2) is not fully specified.** The paper reports that each localized method uses "its best identified parameter budget" and that hyperparameters are "tuned separately for each budget and localization strategy," but does not explicitly state whether the budget was selected using a held-out validation set or the test set. That said, this concern is substantially mitigated because Figures 2 and 3 independently show DEL's advantage across multiple budgets (not just the one selected for Table 2), so the headline finding is not an artifact of budget cherry-picking. Still, the paper should clarify the selection procedure and ideally confirm that a validation split was used.

2. **No wall-clock efficiency measurements.** The paper motivates localized unlearning partly on efficiency grounds ("modifying fewer parameters" → "more efficient") but reports only parameter budgets — a proxy, not actual runtime. Since DEL involves a non-iterative criticality computation (gradients + weighted averaging), it would be straightforward to report wall-clock time for the localization step and the unlearning step across methods. This would substantiate the efficiency claim directly.

3. **The hyperparameter `h` (number of top scores averaged for channel criticality) is not analyzed.** The channel criticality score averages the top `h` parameter-level weighted-gradient magnitudes within each channel (line 154). The paper does not discuss how `h` was chosen or whether results are sensitive to it. This is a free parameter that should either be justified (e.g., set to 1 as a principled default) or ablated.

4. **Only two datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN) and two architectures (ResNet-18, ViT).** While acceptable for a conference paper, adding one larger-scale experiment (e.g., CIFAR-100 or Tiny ImageNet) would strengthen the external validity of the claims. This is a scope limitation, not a flaw in the existing experiments.

5. **CritMem's budget cap is noted but the obvious control (override its termination criterion) is not run.** The paper attributes CritMem's poor performance to its small budget cap (line 130) but does not consider running CritMem with a larger budget by overriding its termination criterion. This would be a useful control to strengthen the comparison.
