## Summary

This paper introduces REPOGRAPH, a plug-in module that constructs a line-level code graph from repository source files using AST parsing (tree-sitter) and integrates retrieved ego-graphs into both procedural and agent-based AI software engineering frameworks. Evaluated on SWE-bench-Lite across four baselines (RAG, Agentless, AutoCodeRover, SWE-agent), REPOGRAPH achieves consistent +2.00–2.66pp resolve-rate improvements and reaches 29.67% when plugged into Agentless. Transfer experiments on CrossCodeEval show larger gains (EM from 10.5→28.7 with GPT-4o). The paper also includes variant analysis and error categorization.

## Strengths

- **Consistent improvement across four diverse baselines.** Table 2 shows that REPOGRAPH boosts the resolve rate for every tested method (RAG +2.66, Agentless +2.34, AutoCodeRover +2.33, SWE-agent +2.00), supporting the claim of general-purpose plug-in utility. The consistency across both procedural and agent frameworks is the paper's strongest empirical finding.

- **Strong transfer results on CrossCodeEval.** Table 5 demonstrates large gains on a distinct repository-level code completion benchmark (e.g., code EM from 10.5→28.7 with GPT-4o and 10.2→19.7 with Deepseek-Coder), providing complementary evidence of the graph's value beyond the SWE-bench setting.

- **Thorough variant ablation.** Table 4 systematically compares 1-hop/2-hop ego-graph retrieval and flatten/summarize integration strategies with node/edge/token statistics, clearly showing that 1-hop+flatten works best while 2-hop+flatten degrades below baseline — a non-trivial finding.

- **Cost efficiency demonstrated.** Table 2 reports average cost and token counts, showing the gains are not simply from increased compute (e.g., RAG +$0.04 for +2.66pp, Agentless +$0.05 for +2.34pp).

- **Fine-grained line-level graph construction.** Table 1 and Section 3.1 detail how REPOGRAPH operates at line granularity (nodes per code line) with definition/reference typing and invoke/contain edges, going beyond file-level indexing of prior methods.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Statistical significance of the core results is not addressed.** The absolute improvements on SWE-bench-Lite are +2.00–2.66 percentage points (6–8 additional solved issues out of 300). The paper provides no confidence intervals, standard errors, or statistical tests — not even a simple bootstrap or McNemar test. Given the modest effect size and the inherent variance from prompt randomness and execution environment, it is unclear whether these gains are robust or could be within noise. This is the most consequential gap in the paper's evidence.

- **The state-of-the-art claim is not directly substantiated.** The paper states (Section 4.2, Observation i) that Agentless+REPOGRAPH at 29.67% "achieves the state-of-the-art performance on the benchmark among all open-source methods," citing the leaderboard. However, it does not state what the previous best open-source result was, list which methods were considered, or provide any direct comparison table. While the leaderboard citation is a valid reference, the paper should contextualize this claim with the relevant numbers for readers to assess it directly.

- **No evaluation on the full SWE-bench set.** Experiments are limited to SWE-bench-Lite (300 instances). Given the modest absolute gains, reporting results on the full 2,294-instance set would be important to confirm the findings are not an artifact of the particular subset chosen.

- **No analysis of search term quality.** REPOGRAPH's effectiveness depends entirely on the quality of search terms generated by the LLM/agent (e.g., "separability_matrix"). The paper does not evaluate how often the generated terms are present in the graph, how well baselines generate these terms, or what fraction of search_repograph calls return useful vs. empty/noisy results. Without this, it is unclear whether the graph is being used effectively or merely adding context that may be ignored.

### Minor

- **Modest technical novelty.** The individual components (AST-based code line parsing via tree-sitter, built-in/third-party call filtering, ego-graph retrieval) are all well-established techniques. The contribution lies in the *integration strategy* — how the graph is incorporated into existing procedural and agent pipelines — rather than in novel algorithmic design. This is a legitimate engineering contribution but with limited technical depth.

- **No ablation of the third-party library filtering step.** Section 3.1 excludes all third-party library calls from the graph with the motivation of reducing noise. However, bugs involving third-party libraries (e.g., incorrect `numpy` usage) would lose critical relations. The paper does not compare performance with and without this filtering, so its impact cannot be assessed.

- **No analysis of graph construction overhead.** The paper reports inference costs but not the upfront cost of scanning, parsing, and building the graph for each repository. Practitioners need to know how long this takes, especially for large repositories.

- **Python-only evaluation.** The method relies on tree-sitter grammars and is evaluated only on Python. This is acknowledged as future work but limits the scope of the contribution.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting insight from the reviews — and one that the paper itself surfaces but does not fully articulate — is the asymmetry between REPOGRAPH's performance on SWE-bench (small, consistent gains of ~2pp) and on CrossCodeEval (dramatic gains of 10–18pp EM). This suggests that the value of structured graph context depends heavily on the nature of the task: it is highly beneficial for code completion where the correct function/class needs to be identified from cross-file dependencies, but provides more marginal benefit for issue resolution where deeper reasoning beyond dependency retrieval is required. The paper's error analysis showing that "incorrect localization" errors decrease with REPOGRAPH while "contextual misalignment" remains dominant further supports this interpretation — the graph helps find the right location but does not help the LLM construct the right fix once located.

## Suggestions

1. **Provide confidence intervals or a statistical test** for the SWE-bench-Lite resolve-rate improvements. Even a simple McNemar test (paired by instance) or bootstrap confidence interval would substantially strengthen the credibility of the reported gains.
2. **Either substantiate the SOTA claim with explicit numbers** (previous best, which methods were considered) or soften it to "competitive with state-of-the-art open-source methods."
3. **Add an ablation of the third-party filtering step** — a simple with/without comparison on a subset would clarify a key design choice.
4. **Report results on the full SWE-bench set** to confirm the Lite results generalize.
5. **Add an analysis of search term quality** — what fraction of generated terms exist in the graph, and what is the precision/recall of the retrieved ego-graphs relative to ground-truth patches?
