2. **The reframing of alignment failures as a principal-agent problem of misunderstanding is productive.** Section 4.2 identifies that even a perfectly aligned-utility AI can produce "helpful harm" because different innate knowledge causes identical symbols to project differently in Z-space, creating misunderstanding rather than conflict. This is a genuine conceptual contribution that reframes a well-known economics framework for the AI alignment context and invites productive disagreement.

3. **The distinction between thinking language and tool language is discussion-worthy.** Section 2.5 introduces this distinction, noting that natural language serves as both for humans but primarily as tool language for AI—a lens that explains why fluent output does not entail shared conceptual grounding, going beyond standard symbol grounding arguments.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The position is clear and debatable, not incoherent. The paper is a genuine position paper, not a literature review.

### Major

1. **The central claim does not follow from its own premises.** The paper establishes that symbols are ambiguous (semantic underdetermination), that AI conceptual spaces differ from human ones (Triangle Problem), and that fluent communication does not entail shared understanding. From these premises, it could legitimately argue that symbolic rules alone are *insufficient* for robust alignment. But the paper repeatedly claims the far stronger conclusion that symbolic systems "fundamentally cannot constrain" learning systems (Conclusion, line 580) and that "we cannot constrain AI through rules" (line 519). Imperfect, partial, or probabilistic constraint is still constraint—the paper's own argument does not establish that symbolic mechanisms have *zero* constraining force. This logical gap is not a complaint about provocative language; it is a gap in the argument itself, because the premises support a weaker conclusion than the one stated. The paper would be substantially stronger if it argued for what it actually demonstrates—that symbolic systems alone are fundamentally insufficient for ensuring alignment—rather than overclaiming to impossibility of constraint.

2. **Section 5 dismisses counterarguments by reassertion rather than engagement.** The paper acknowledges neuro-symbolic AI, formal verification, and rule-based reward modeling as alternatives, then dismisses them by restating its thesis (AI lacks symbol adhesion, learning systems modify meanings). But the strongest counterposition is not that symbols perfectly constrain, but that formal/symbolic components can provide *partial, scoped, enforceable* constraints—restricting action spaces, verifying subsystems, enforcing interfaces—even when meanings are underdetermined. The paper never confronts this scoped-constraint version of the objection. A position paper with this thesis needs a genuine response to "symbolic constraints can be materially useful despite not fully determining meaning," not just a restatement of why they cannot fully determine meaning.

3. **The paper equivocates between natural language and all symbolic systems.** Its strongest arguments (class-based semantics, context-dependence, lack of referential precision) target natural language specifically. But its conclusion targets "laws, regulations, and programmatic constraints" (Introduction), "laws, regulations, procedures" (line 519), and formal verification (Section 5)—all of which can operate with formal semantics, type systems, and machine-interpretable specifications that sidestep the natural language ambiguities the paper documents. The paper does not explain why the specific failures of natural language generalize to formal languages and verification systems.

### Minor

1. **Key constructs are suggestive but underdefined.** "Symbol adhesion," "value knowledge," "innate knowledge" (beyond a brief enumeration as "organs"), and "thinking language" vs. "tool language" carry substantial argumentative weight but are defined imprecisely or deferred to appendices. This makes the Triangle Problem's four verification contents difficult to operationalize or test, undermining the paper's claim to have provided a framework for empirical verification.

2. **Section 4.1 overextends "context jailbreak" as the root cause of diverse failure modes.** Attributing all prompt jailbreaks to context manipulation is too sweeping; some attacks exploit reward misspecification, objective function loopholes, or training data artifacts rather than context reframing. This overgeneralization weakens what is otherwise a compelling connection between context theory and jailbreak vulnerabilities.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel contribution is the principal-agent reframing: alignment failures can arise not from misaligned incentives (the standard principal-agent frame) but from *misunderstanding*—identical symbols projecting to different regions of Z-space because the agent lacks human innate knowledge. This transforms "the AI is being deceptive" into "the AI is being helpful on different conceptual foundations," which has different policy implications and invites a genuinely different research direction. The Triangle Problem's geometric formalization of conceptual divergence, while underdeveloped, also provides a structure for future empirical work.
