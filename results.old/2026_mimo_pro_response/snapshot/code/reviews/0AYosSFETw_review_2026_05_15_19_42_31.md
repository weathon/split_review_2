## Summary

CHATS is a discrete-token system that transforms written dialogues into spoken dialogues with human-like backchannels, laughter, and natural turn-taking. It conditions a dual-tower Transformer language model on phoneme sequences from the speaker's utterance while generating listener-side reactions (backchannels, laughter) unconditionally. A novel turn-taking mechanism (TTM) handles both overlapping speech and pause durations by modifying utterance time boundaries during training. Human evaluation shows CHATS significantly improves Dialogue Naturalness over a turn-based TTS baseline (3.30 vs. 3.01, p=0.038), and objective metrics demonstrate it closely reproduces ground-truth distributions of backchannel frequency and turn-taking event durations.

## Strengths

- **Text-conditioned generation of both speaker and listener channels**: The system conditions the speaker channel on phoneme sequences while generating listener-side backchannels and laughter unconditionally, filling a gap between turn-based TTS (no listener reactions) and textless models like dGSLM (no content control). Table 1 shows Proposed achieves PER (13.03) close to Baseline TTS (12.13) while simultaneously generating backchannels and handling turn-taking; Table 2 shows backchannel proportions closest to Ground Truth (23.02% vs. 19.61%).

- **Novel turn-taking mechanism (TTM)**: The mechanism handles both overlap and no-overlap scenarios by modifying time boundaries and conditioning on next-utterance phonemes, enabling human-like turn-taking including overlapping speech. Table 4 shows Proposed achieves the best MAE across IPU (0.24), Pause (0.08), and Overlap (0.10) durations, and removing TTM degrades pause duration correlation to -0.09.

- **Human evaluation demonstrating practical improvement**: The proposed system significantly outperforms both dGSLM and the turn-based Baseline across all subjective metrics. Table 5 shows Proposed achieves Dialogue Naturalness of 3.30 vs. 3.01 for Baseline (p=0.038) and 2.68 for dGSLM, while dGSLM's Meaningfulness collapses to 1.18, highlighting the value of text conditioning.

- **Effective data augmentation by context reduction**: The augmentation strategy addresses the cold-start problem when context units are unavailable at inference start. Table 1 shows removing augmentation catastrophically degrades PER from 13.03 to 59.35.

- **Practical backchannel exclusion pipeline**: The hybrid rule-based and ML-based method for converting spoken dialogue transcriptions to written dialogues (Section 2) is a practical contribution enabling training on existing spontaneous dialogue corpora.

- **Online inference capability**: The system requires only current and next utterance phonemes plus recent context units, supporting continuous sequential generation (Section 3.4).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

None.

### Minor

- **Private dataset limits external reproducibility**: Training and evaluation rely on an internal 74h spoken dialogue dataset. While the CSJ corpus provides public pre-training data, the core dialogue experiments cannot be independently reproduced. This is common in industry systems papers but should be acknowledged as a limitation.

- **Human evaluation improvement is modest and sound quality gap is large**: The Dialogue Naturalness improvement over Baseline is 0.29 on a 5-point scale (3.30 vs. 3.01, p=0.038), which is statistically significant but practically modest. Sound Quality for Proposed (3.38) falls substantially below Resynthesized (3.82) and Ground Truth (4.75), indicating the s2u/u2s pipeline introduces significant degradation that may limit practical adoption.

- **IPU classifier accuracy not reported**: The binary classifier used to identify listener IPUs (Section 2) is central to both training data preparation and potentially evaluation. Its accuracy is never quantified. Errors in this step propagate into the "written dialogue" input and could affect backchannel metrics. A small-scale manual validation would substantially strengthen confidence.

- **PER trade-off not fully justified within the TTS evaluation**: In the utterance-level TTS setting, Proposed (13.03) underperforms Baseline (12.13), and the paper rightly notes the degradation is small considering the additional tasks. However, those additional tasks (listener speech, turn-taking) are not exercised in the TTS setting, so the trade-off argument relies entirely on separate dialogue-level evaluations. A dialogue-level evaluation of the "w/o next sentence" variant—which achieves better PER (12.79) but allegedly produces unnatural overlaps—would complete the ablation story.

### Trivial

- The "Proposed w/o next sentence" variant achieves better PER than Proposed (12.79 vs. 13.03) but no dialogue-level evaluation of this variant is shown to demonstrate the claimed unnatural overlaps; this is a small gap in an otherwise thorough ablation study.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Re-analysis of backchannel metrics using a held-out, manually annotated subset of dialogues would eliminate any residual concern about classifier circularity, even at small scale.
- Evaluation on longer dialogues beyond the two-sentence context window would strengthen the claim of "continuous spoken dialogue generation."
- Reporting per-speaker confidence intervals or effect sizes in the human evaluation would provide richer information beyond the single p-value.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The paper's central insight—that conditioning a dual-tower discrete-unit language model on phoneme sequences enables simultaneous generation of text-controlled speaker speech and unconditioned listener reactions with natural turn-taking—is genuinely novel and well-motivated by the gap between turn-based TTS and textless models.

## Suggestions

- Provide IPU classifier accuracy metrics and, ideally, a small-scale manual verification of backchannel identification to strengthen the evaluation's reliability.
- Include a dialogue-level evaluation of the "Proposed w/o next sentence" variant to demonstrate the claimed unnatural overlaps and justify the PER trade-off.
- Explicitly acknowledge the private dataset as a limitation and discuss what public benchmarks or released model weights could enable community comparison.

**Originality**: The paper addresses a clear gap—no existing system combines text-conditioned speaker generation with unconditioned listener backchannels/laughter and explicit turn-taking in a single model. The TTM is a simple but clever mechanism.

**Importance**: Generating human-like spoken dialogues from text has clear practical value for voice assistants, entertainment, and agent-to-agent communication. The work moves beyond turn-based TTS toward more natural multi-party spoken interaction.

**Claim support**: Claims are generally well-supported by the combination of utterance-level PER, dialogue-level distribution matching, and human evaluation. The human evaluation shows a real but modest improvement; the paper is appropriately measured in its claims about remaining gaps.

**Soundness**: The experimental design is thorough with appropriate baselines (dGSLM, turn-based TTS, ablations). The main soundness concern is the unreported IPU classifier accuracy and the private dataset, both of which are addressable limitations rather than fatal flaws.

**Clarity**: The paper is well-written and well-structured. The TTM, system architecture, and evaluation protocol are clearly described (with figures). Some implementation details are deferred to the stripped appendix, which is standard practice.

**Value to community**: The CHATS approach provides a blueprint for combining text conditioning with discrete-unit spoken dialogue modeling. The backchannel exclusion pipeline and TTM are transferable ideas. The private dataset limits direct reproducibility but the methodology is clearly described and could be adapted to other corpora.
