## Summary

The paper uses ECoG recordings from 9 participants listening to a 30-minute narrative and contextual embeddings from all 48 layers of GPT2-XL to show that the *layer index* of the model correlates positively with the *lag of peak encoding performance* in higher-order language areas (IFG, aSTG, TP), with the strongest result in IFG (Pearson r = 0.85, p < 10e−13). The authors argue that the spatial layer hierarchy of GPT2-XL maps onto the temporal hierarchy of language processing in cortex and use the within-ROI spread of peak lags to recover the canonical "increasing temporal receptive window" gradient along the ventral stream.

## Strengths
- **ECoG temporal resolution surfaces a layer-to-time mapping invisible to prior fMRI work.** Fig. 2C/E shows a temporally orderly progression across all 48 layers; the headline IFG lag-layer correlation (Pearson r = 0.85, p < 10e−13; Spearman r = 0.80; mixed-effects p < 10e−15) is well-anchored by multiple statistical tests rather than a single p-value (Sec. 4).
- **The projection-out control directly addresses the "everything is driven by the best layer" objection.** By subtracting each layer's projection onto layer 22 (the inverted-U peak) and re-running the analysis, the authors show the lag-layer ordering persists (Sec. 4 + Supp. Fig. 8). This is a substantive control, not cosmetic.
- **A linear-interpolation control rules out the simplest mixing baseline.** Sec. 5 / Supp. Fig. 9 shows that 46 pseudo-layers generated by interpolating between layers 1 and 48 yield significantly smaller lag-layer slopes (p < .01) than the real nonlinear layer sequence — addressing the "early-layer-previous-word / late-layer-current-word" alternative explicitly articulated by the authors.
- **Multi-ROI replication and quantification of the temporal-receptive-window gradient.** Effects replicate in aSTG (r = 0.92) and TP (r = 0.93), and Levene's test confirms growing variance of peak lags from mSTG to aSTG to TP (Sec. 5).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **The headline lag-layer correlation depends on argmax over per-layer-rescaled curves whose stability is never characterized.** Sec. 3.2 explicitly scales each layer's encoding timecourse so it peaks at 1; Fig. 2C shows the unscaled amplitudes differ enormously across layers (inverted-U with peak at layer 22). The argmax of a near-floor curve is dominated by the layer's noise structure, yet the paper provides no bootstrap or precision-weighted analysis of the per-layer peak lag itself (only per-electrode generalization is bootstrapped). Because the correlation is driven precisely by the early/late layers whose curves are lowest amplitude, this is a concrete reliability gap in the central quantity.
- **Controls do not separate "match to the learned GPT2-XL sequence" from "match to any progressively-contextualizing sequence."** The interpolation control is the weakest possible "trivial sequence" baseline (linear blend of endpoints). A randomly-initialized GPT2-XL or a shuffled-layer GPT2-XL would directly test the more interesting alternative that any monotone progressive contextualization (a generic property of contextual transformers, as the paper itself notes in Sec. 1) would produce a similar temporal alignment. Without that, the discussion's framing ("layer-by-layer accumulation of contextual information mirroring … neural activity") is stronger than the controls support.

### Minor
- **TP results carry much of the "hierarchy" argument but rest on 6 electrodes.** Sec. 3.1 reports 6 TP electrodes; the strong TP correlations (r = 0.93/0.96) are computed over n = 48 layers, not over electrodes, so per-ROI sample size is asymmetric across the four regions (mSTG 28, aSTG 13, IFG 46, TP 6). The Levene's test for the temporal-receptive-window claim (Sec. 5) also pools over those 6 TP electrodes. The headline TP p-values are real but they obscure how thin the cortical sampling is for the region carrying the longest-timescale conclusion.
- **Asymmetric interpretation of the mSTG negative result.** Sec. 5 reads mSTG's null (Spearman r = −0.24, p = .09) as evidence of regional specificity to higher-order areas. Since mSTG is the area where surface acoustic features dominate and GPT2 contextual encoding is expected to be weakest, an encoding-quality-matched test (e.g., showing the effect persists in mSTG electrodes selected for high overall encoding) would distinguish "regional specificity" from "low SNR."
- **Permutation test null is weaker than it appears.** The permutation in Sec. 4 shuffles layer indices while holding the observed peak lags fixed. This tests against the null that observed peak lags are exchangeable, not against a generative null on the encoding timecourses themselves; the p < 10e−5 is therefore not as evidentially strong as it sounds for ruling out artifacts of the scaling procedure.
- **Projection-out control is reported only qualitatively.** Sec. 4 / Supp. Fig. 8 states "the results held," but no quantitative lag-layer correlation is reported after projecting out layer 22. Given that this is the paper's strongest control, the post-projection correlation belongs in the main text.

### Trivial
- The abstract's framing ("layered hierarchy of DLMs may be used to model the temporal dynamics") is stronger than the body's more careful claim that the *order* of peak lags matches the *order* of layers. Tighten Sec. 1's closing language and the Discussion's closing paragraph on "paradigm shift," which overshoots an ordinal correlation result.
- A brief clarification in Sec. 3.2 / A.4 about whether PCA is refit per cross-validation fold (rather than fit on the full word set) would remove an ambiguity about a small potential leak.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Replication of the IFG effect on a second narrative within the same dataset, to show it is not story-specific.
- A precision-weighted version of the lag-layer correlation, where each layer's peak lag is weighted by its estimated SE.
- Reporting the temporal-receptive-window result with explicit per-ROI electrode counts adjacent to the F-statistics.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.
- *"Electrode selection on the same neural data biases reported encoding magnitudes."* (Harsh critic.) The critic concedes this likely does not bias the lag-layer correlation, which is the paper's central claim; the magnitude caveat is a meta-comment to readers, not a flaw in the contribution.
- *"The 'increasing temporal receptive window' claim should pool electrodes across ROIs in a single mixed model."* (Harsh critic improvement #4.) The paper *does* report a single LME with ROI as a fixed effect and electrode as a random effect (Sec. 5), so this is partially addressed; the residual concern collapses to the TP-n issue already retained as a Minor.
- *Strength: "lag-layer relationship validated with Pearson, Spearman, permutation, and LME tests"* — kept under "Major statistical anchoring" in Strengths; do not double-count.
- *Strength about ECoG temporal resolution being uniquely informative* — retained but compressed; the bare statement that "ECoG has high temporal resolution" is generic and only counts as a strength when paired with the specific layer-to-time mapping.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviewers correctly identify that the most important untested alternative is "any progressively-contextualizing model" (not just linear interpolation between endpoints) and that the headline correlation depends on argmax of rescaled curves whose reliability is uncharacterized — both of which are concrete, paper-improving observations rather than novel scientific insights.

## Suggestions
- Add an untrained-GPT2-XL or layer-shuffled-GPT2-XL baseline; compute the lag-layer correlation for these and show it is significantly lower than the trained, in-order model.
- Bootstrap each layer's peak lag across electrodes (or across resampled words) and report the lag-layer correlation as a distribution rather than a point estimate; this directly addresses the argmax-on-noisy-curves concern.
- Report the post-projection lag-layer correlation (Supp. Fig. 8) quantitatively in Sec. 4.
- For the mSTG null, control for overall encoding quality (e.g., subset electrodes by encoding rank or include encoding strength as a covariate) before interpreting the absence as regional specificity.
- Soften the closing rhetoric ("paradigm shift from a symbolic representation of language") to match the ordinal nature of the result.

## Axis-by-axis assessment
- **Originality:** Moderate-to-strong. The specific empirical observation — layer index correlating with peak encoding lag *within* a single high-order ROI — is meaningfully new relative to the fMRI literature that previously found only an intermediate-layer plateau across ROIs.
- **Importance of research question:** Strong. Whether DLM layer structure maps onto neural processing in time (not just space) is a central question for cognitive computational neuroscience.
- **Support for claims:** Mixed. The ordinal correlation is well-supported by multiple tests, but the strongest framing (DLM layers "model the temporal dynamics") is undersupported by the controls in place.
- **Soundness of experiments:** Reasonable, with the noted gap on uncertainty of the argmax-derived peak lags and on the absence of an untrained/shuffled baseline.
- **Clarity:** Good. The encoding pipeline, controls, and statistical tests are clearly laid out.
- **Value to the community:** Solid contribution to the brain-LM alignment literature; useful both for the empirical observation and for the projection-out and interpolation controls.
