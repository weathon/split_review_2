## Summary

The paper proposes LOGIT, a server-side framework that learns to generate surrogate gradients for unavailable clients in federated learning by training a lightweight gradient generation network (GGN) per client. The GGN is conditioned on each client's gradient history and updates from available clients, aiming to preserve statistical diversity during aggregation. The authors provide a convergence bound (O(1/√T)) and report experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and IMAGENETTE showing accuracy gains over FedAvg, MIFA, and WS baselines.

## Strengths

- **Addresses a practically relevant problem.** Client unavailability due to stragglers, battery, and connectivity is a well-known challenge in federated learning, and the paper targets this directly with a generative approach rather than discarding missing updates.
- **Convergence analysis is attempted.** The authors derive a convergence bound that explicitly includes terms for participation probability, gradient staleness, and generator approximation error, offering theoretical grounding for the proposed method.
- **Consistent empirical improvement.** LOGIT achieves higher test accuracy and requires fewer communication rounds to reach target accuracy across all three datasets and heterogeneity levels, with reported gains up to ~5% under high heterogeneity.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Unrealistic unbiasedness assumption (Assumption 3).** The paper assumes that the surrogate gradient generated by the GGN is *unbiased* (𝔼[𝑔̃_𝑛(𝐱)] = ∇F_𝑛(𝐱)) with bounded variance. This directly contradicts the paper’s core motivation—if the generator can produce an unbiased gradient for an offline client, then the problem is essentially solved. Moreover, the generator is trained only on available clients and uses stale inputs (g_n(𝐱_{t−τ_n^t})), so unbiasedness cannot hold under distribution shift or prolonged outages. The bound then introduces an extra "epsilon" term (ε̄*) to account for generator approximation error, but its definition (line 460–465) is vague and no bound on ε̄* is provided, making the convergence result weaker than claimed.

2. **Incremental contribution relative to prior work.** The paper claims "the first generative approach in FL to address the challenge of intermittent client unavailability," yet cites Ji et al. (2019) which learns server-side RNN/ARNN aggregators, and Deng et al. (2025) which uses a meta gradient generator (MGG) for test-time adaptation. The GGN design (coordinatewise LSTM) is directly taken from Andrychowicz et al. (2016) and applied per client. The main novelty—conditioning on available clients' updates via an alignment loss—is a small delta over existing L2O-based gradient estimation. The paper does not clearly delineate what distinguishes LOGIT from these works beyond the application setting.

3. **Limited experimental scale.** All main experiments use only 10 clients (N=10), and the largest ablation has N=50. Real-world FL deployments often involve hundreds or thousands of clients, and the scaling behavior shown (Fig. 3c) extrapolates only to 50 clients. The paper does not evaluate under practical unavailability patterns (e.g., correlated dropouts, long-tail participation) or compare against more recent FL methods that address partial participation (e.g., FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedBuff, MIME). The baselines (FedAvg, MIFA, WS) are narrow and do not reflect the current state of the art.

4. **Inconsistent metric in ablation study.** Fig. 3 reports "Top-3 accuracy" while the main results (Table 1) report top-1 accuracy. The switch is unexplained and makes the ablation results not directly comparable to the main table. Additionally, the target accuracy thresholds in Fig. 2 (75% on CIFAR-10, 55% on CIFAR-100, 65% on IMAGENETTE) seem chosen ad hoc; no justification is given for why these thresholds are meaningful.

### Minor

- The GGN training uses only available clients, but if a client stays offline for many rounds, its generator is not updated and must synthesize from a stale input. The paper does not analyze how performance degrades with prolonged absence or how the staleness τ_n^t is bounded in practice.
- No ablation or sensitivity analysis on the balancing parameter λ_n in Eq. (3). The value of λ_n used in experiments is not reported.
- The paper does not discuss the additional memory cost of storing per-client GGN parameters (10 clients × LSTM weights) nor the computational overhead of per-coordinate forward passes at the server per round.
- The convergence bound (Theorem 1) contains an irreducible error floor ε̄* that is not quantified; practitioners cannot know whether this term dominates the rate.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Comparison against asynchronous FL methods (e.g., FedAsync, FedBuff) and variance-reduction methods (SCAFFOLD) would strengthen the experimental evaluation.
- A study breaking down accuracy by client participation rate (e.g., high vs. low availability) to show whether LOGIT helps the worst-off clients most.
- More thorough analysis of the GGN’s computational overhead on the server (both train and inference time) relative to the communication savings.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The idea of using a learned per-client generator conditioned on stale history and current available updates is plausible but not fundamentally surprising, given prior work on learning to optimize and gradient estimation in FL.

## Suggestions

- Remove or rephrase the "first generative approach" claim; acknowledge that Ji et al. (2019) and Deng et al. (2025) are closely related and clearly state how LOGIT differs.
- Relax the unbiasedness assumption (Assumption 3). Replace it with a bounded-error assumption that does not require unbiasedness, and analyze how the generator error propagates through the bound in a more explicit way.
- Run experiments with larger client counts (e.g., N=100, 500) to demonstrate scalability, and include at least one comparison with a state-of-the-art partial-participation method (e.g., FedBuff or MIME).
- Consistently report top-1 accuracy throughout (including ablation studies) or justify why top-3 is used.

## Score and Decision

**Score:** 4  
**Decision:** Reject

Weaknesses (2, 3, and 4) are major and individually sufficient to reduce confidence in the paper’s claim of broad effectiveness. The theoretical grounding is undermined by unrealistic assumptions, the experimental validation is limited in scale and baseline coverage, and the novelty relative to existing L2O and gradient-estimation works is modest.
