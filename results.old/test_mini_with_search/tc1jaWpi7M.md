## Summary

MaskComp proposes an iterative framework for object completion that alternates between a generation stage (CompNet, a modified ControlNet that recovers complete objects) and a segmentation stage (SAM-based mask extraction and voting). The key insight is that each generation+segmentation cycle acts as a mask denoiser, progressively refining a partial occlusion mask into a complete one, thereby providing better shape guidance for final generation. Experiments on AHP and DYCE datasets show large FID gains over ControlNet, Kandinsky 2.1, and Stable Diffusion 1.5/2.1, supported by extensive ablations across architectural choices.

## Strengths

1. **Novel and well-motivated framework**: The iterative mask denoising (IMD) idea — framing partial mask refinement as alternating sampling between generation and segmentation — is conceptually clean and the paper provides clear evidence that mask quality directly drives generation quality. Table 3(a) shows FID improving from 16.9 (partial mask) to 12.7 (complete mask), validating the core premise.

2. **Extensive, well-designed ablation studies**: The paper systematically ablates segmentation model choice (SAM vs. CLIPSeg vs. SEEM, Table 1(a)), IMD step count (FID 24.7 → 16.1 from 1→7 steps, Table 1(b)), number of sampled images (Table 1(c)), gating operation (Table 1(d), +1.3 FID), mask loss (Table 4(e), +0.8 FID), voting strategies (Table 4(d)), occlusion rates (Table 3(b)), occlusion types (Table 4(b)), and availability of complete data during training (Table 4(c)). This provides a thorough understanding of each component's contribution.

3. **Clear robustness demonstration**: Table 5 shows that injecting mask noise (5–15% area perturbation) increases the convergence iteration count but does not significantly degrade final FID (15.9 at no noise vs. 16.5 at 15% noise after 9 iterations), supporting the claim that errors are not propagated through the iterative process.

4. **Substantial FID improvements over baselines**: On AHP, MaskComp achieves FID-G 16.9 vs. 30.8 for the next best baseline (SD 2.1); on DYCE, 20.0 vs. 30.0. These are large margins, even acknowledging the baseline limitations discussed below.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Baseline comparisons do not include dedicated object completion or inpainting methods**. The paper compares against ControlNet, Kandinsky 2.1, and Stable Diffusion 1.5/2.1 — all generic conditional generation models not designed for this task. The only task-relevant baseline is the amodal segmentation+ControlNet pipeline (Table 3(d)), which the paper beats by a large margin (29.4 vs. 16.9 FID). However, the absence of comparisons with diffusion-based inpainting pipelines (e.g., Stable Diffusion inpainting) or recent inpainting methods (e.g., LaMa, MAT) makes it unclear whether the iterative refinement adds value beyond a single-pass inpainting approach. The paper would be substantially strengthened by adding the SD inpainting pipeline as a baseline, which is a natural comparison point for a method that generates from a masked partial input. This is the most significant weakness — it does not invalidate the results but limits the strength of the SOTA claim.

2. **Training data generation for the conditioned mask is underspecified**. Section 3.3 states that "the interpolated mask $M$ is generated by using one of the occlusions" but does not specify how the two occlusion processes relate, what occlusion rates are used, how interpolation is performed (random or fixed), or any algorithm for constructing the training pairs. Since the entire method hinges on the model's ability to handle masks at varying completeness levels, this vagueness is a reproducibility barrier. A precise algorithm or pseudo-code is needed.

### Minor

3. **User study lacks standard reporting details**: The paper reports "Rank" and "Best" percentages (Table 1) but omits the number of participants, number of image sets evaluated, and any measure of inter-annotator agreement or statistical significance. While the large FID gains make this less critical for the paper's core claims, the user study is presented as evidence for object completeness — a dimension not captured by FID — and the lack of reporting limits its evidentiary value.

4. **No limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss limitations. Key omissions include: (a) the computational cost of multiple diffusion passes per object (acknowledged only briefly in the inference time ablation), (b) reliance on SAM, which may struggle with novel categories or fine structures, (c) dependence on BLIP-generated text prompts and what happens when the prompt is inaccurate, and (d) the restriction to single-object-centric completion rather than multi-object scenes.

5. **Gibbs sampling analogy in Sec 3.5 is disconnected from experiments**: The discussion frames the iterative process as Gibbs-like sampling from a joint distribution $p(I,M)$, but this framing is never validated experimentally — e.g., by testing whether multiple chains converge to consistent masks, or whether the marginal distributions actually justify the MCMC interpretation. This makes the section feel more like speculation than analysis.

### Trivial

6. **OpenImage subset selection criteria unspecified**: The paper trains on a subset of OpenImage with 429,358 objects but does not describe the filtering criteria (categories, occlusion synthesis method), affecting reproducibility.

7. **Voting threshold $\tau=0.5$ is used without sensitivity analysis**: While typically reasonable, a brief ablation on this hyperparameter would strengthen the work.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Quantify mask improvement across iterations**: Showing IoU or mask completeness against the ground-truth mask as a function of IMD step would directly substantiate the "mask denoising" claim beyond qualitative visualizations (Fig. 7).
- **Compare with SD inpainting pipeline**: As noted in Weakness 1, this is the most natural additional baseline and would help position the work relative to standard inpainting methods.
- **Test the Gibbs-sampling interpretation**: Running multiple chains from different initial subregions and measuring mask convergence would give empirical support to the theoretical framing.
