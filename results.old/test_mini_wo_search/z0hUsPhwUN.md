## Summary

Control-GIC is a VQGAN-based generative image compression framework that achieves variable bitrate control from a single model. It introduces a granularity-informed encoder that allocates fine/medium/coarse representations to image patches based on information density (entropy), a non-parametric statistical entropy coding module, and a probabilistic conditional decoder that reconstructs hierarchical features. The key contribution is enabling fine-grained, continuously adjustable bitrate adaption across a broad range without retraining separate models for each rate-distortion point.

## Strengths

- **Fine-grained bitrate control at sub-0.001 bpp resolution**: Figure 6 demonstrates that adjusting the medium-grained ratio \(r_2\) from 32.9% to 32.88% changes bpp by only 0.0001 (0.4171→0.4172), a precision well beyond prior variable-rate or progressive methods.

- **Single unified model with competitive perceptual quality**: The R-D curves on Kodak (Figure 2) and DIV2K (Figure 3) show Control-GIC matching or exceeding MS-ILLM and MRIC on LPIPS, DISTS, and NIQE — despite those methods training separate models per R-D point. The ablation in Figure 7 cleanly demonstrates that both the medium- and fine-grained conditional replacements improve reconstruction realism, with the full model achieving the best scores.

- **Fastest encoding/decoding among compared methods**: Figure 5 reports that Control-GIC achieves 7× faster encoding than MS-ILLM and 3× faster decoding, with only 0.6M training steps for a single model covering a wide bitrate range — whereas per-point methods require n independent trainings and SCR requires 15× more steps.

- **Statistical entropy coding outperforms simple baselines**: Table 1 shows the proposed frequency-table-based Huffman coding achieves 6.4–8.2% bit savings over uniform-frequency Huffman and 59.0–68.4% over ZIP across three different granularity configurations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing quantitative comparison with prior variable-rate generative methods.** The paper mentions (line 20) that "several methods introduce scalable [Iwai et al. 2024] or variable-rate [Guo et al. 2023] designs into generative models" and claims these are "constrained by finite compression rates," yet provides no quantitative comparison against them. The paper's repeated "first" claim (abstract, contribution list) hinges on outperforming or extending these prior works. Without direct comparison, the novelty advantage is unsubstantiated. This gap weakens the central positioning of the paper.

### Minor

- **Mechanism for per-patch entropy computation is underspecified.** Section 3.1 (line 95) states that masks are generated by retaining "the proportion of elements with the lowest entropy" from each granularity, but never defines how per-patch entropy is computed. Is it the entropy of codebook assignment probabilities? The entropy of local pixel values? At what spatial scale? The phrase "lowest entropy" is also ambiguous without context — if entropy measures information content, retaining lowest-entropy elements (most predictable) while discarding high-entropy ones (most informative) is an unconventional design choice that requires explicit justification and formula. This omission makes the core masking procedure non-reproducible.

- **Ratio-to-bitrate mapping for R-D curve points is unspecified.** The R-D curves in Figures 2 and 3 show Control-GIC spanning ~0.1–0.6 bpp across multiple points, and Table 1 confirms that different ratio configurations (0%/50%/50%, 10%/80%/10%, 50%/40%/10%) produce very different bitrates (0.132–0.505 bpp). However, the paper never states which specific ratio configurations generated each point on the R-D curves. Without this mapping, one cannot assess whether performance degrades at extreme ratios far from the training configuration (50%/40%/10%), which is critical to the "broad spectrum" claim.

- **"Nearly lossless reconstruction" claim is overstated.** Line 135 states the method "effectively avoids the information loss between the encoder and decoder features except the quantization, thereby achieving nearly lossless reconstruction." While the decoder's conditional replacement mechanism does preserve the transmitted features, the overall pipeline is inherently lossy due to both the masking operation (discarding features) and quantization. The claim conflates decoder-side feature preservation with overall reconstruction fidelity and should be qualified.

- **Notation inconsistency for granularity ratios.** Section 3.1 defines \(r_1, r_2, r_3\) as ratios for fine, medium, coarse granularities respectively. However, the Figure 6 caption (line 470) refers to "the coarse-grained feature ratio \(r_1\)" — swapping \(r_1\) to mean coarse. The main text on line 476 correctly uses \(r_3=50\%\) for coarse. This inconsistency is confusing and should be harmonized.

### Trivial
- The \(\arg\min\) notation in Eq. (1) for VQ does not mention the straight-through estimator used for gradient flow; this is standard in VQ literature but worth a brief note for clarity.
