## Summary

This paper proposes a methodological framework for evaluating unsupervised anonymous record linkage without labeled training data. The key insight is that when a structural constraint limits positive outcomes per individual (e.g., at most one originated first-lien mortgage per property), observable lower bounds on precision and relative recall can be derived using only the fraction of clusters with multiple originated loans and the global origination probability. The authors instantiate this framework using agglomerative hierarchical clustering on the HMDA mortgage dataset (65.5 million applications) to detect "cross-applicants"—individuals who submit multiple applications for the same property. At their preferred specification, the estimated lower bound on precision is 92.3% with a corresponding recall lower bound of approximately 90%.

The paper makes two main contributions: (C1) a theoretical evaluation framework with observable performance bounds applicable to any label-generating algorithm under the specified structural constraint, and (C2) an applied instantiation demonstrating the framework on a large-scale mortgage dataset. The theoretical framework is the stronger contribution; the clustering algorithm itself is standard. The paper is generally well-structured and the core idea—using origination multiplicity to bound precision—is clever and clearly explained. However, the strength of the headline precision claim depends on assumptions (independent origination across borrowers, weakly increasing origination probability) that are acknowledged but not critically examined, and the conclusion over-extends into speculative applications without adequate discussion of limitations. External literature verification was unavailable in this run; novelty conclusions are deferred to manual verification.

## Strengths
1. **Clever and practical methodological contribution.** The core idea of using a structural constraint (at most one originated loan per property) to derive observable bounds on precision and recall without labeled data is genuinely clever and practically useful for the many domains where individual-level identifiers are unavailable. The bounds are computed from only two observable quantities—the global origination probability and the fraction of clusters with multiple originations—making them easy to implement.

2. **Domain agnosticism with clear applicability.** The paper correctly identifies that the same structural constraint arises in multiple domains: secured loans, insurance policies, college admissions, and job applications. The framework's method-agnostic nature (applicable to any label-generating algorithm) further broadens its potential impact. The authors explicitly discuss three downstream applications (fairness measurement, lending standard monitoring, shopping behavior analysis), demonstrating awareness of the method's practical significance.

3. **Large-scale empirical demonstration.** The application to 65.5 million HMDA mortgage applications is a realistic, high-impact test case. The authors use a confidential version of HMDA with detailed application-level information, and the data filtering steps are clearly documented (2018–2023, first-lien mortgages, approved/denied applications, 50 states + DC). The use of 96 combinations of distance functions and tolerance parameters shows systematic tuning.

4. **Rigorous mathematical framing.** Theorem 1 and Corollaries 1-2 are clearly stated with explicit assumptions, and the derivations are delegated to appendices for readability. The bound in Equation (2) (after dropping clusters with multiple originations) is a useful refinement that improves the practical precision estimate. The paper correctly handles the distinction between lower bounds and direct measurements.

5. **Simulation validation.** The simulation study (Section 3) with known ground truth allows the authors to validate their bound against actual precision (Figures 3a vs 4a), showing close correspondence. This is a strong check that the bound is informative in a controlled setting, and the code availability (via fastcluster) supports reproducibility.

## Weaknesses
### W1. Over-claimed novelty and unsupported "first work" statement (Major)
**Page 1 - Introduction, paragraph 2.** The paper states: "To our knowledge, this is the first work to derive observable lower bounds on both precision and relative recall in unsupervised classification settings." This is an empirical priority claim that the paper does not—and cannot—substantiate without a thorough literature review. The unsupervised classification literature is vast, and the paper provides no references to prior work it distinguishes from. The claim should be scoped to the specific context (anonymous record linkage with a one-outcome-per-individual constraint) and qualified more cautiously. In this run, external literature verification was unavailable; this claim requires manual verification before acceptance.

### W2. Assumption 1 (independent origination across borrowers) is strong and unvalidated (Major)
**Page 1 - Section 2.2, Assumption 1.** The independence assumption is critical for the bound derivations but is unlikely to hold in mortgage data. Local housing market conditions, macroeconomic shocks (e.g., COVID-era policies), and shared lender underwriting practices introduce positive correlation in origination outcomes across borrowers. The paper dismisses this assumption as "not appear very strong to us" (Remark 1 context) without any sensitivity analysis. While the inequality direction in Theorem 1 still holds under positive correlation, the bound tightness degrades, and the headline 92.3% precision estimate may become overly conservative. The authors should add: (a) a limitations paragraph discussing the direction of potential bias, (b) an empirical check of within-partition origination correlation, and (c) a sensitivity analysis showing bound stability under varying correlation assumptions.

### W3. Conclusion over-extends into unvalidated applications (Major)
**Page 8 - Conclusion.** The conclusion devotes most of its space to three forward-looking applications (fairness measurement, lending standard monitoring, shopping behavior) that are not validated or even tested in the current paper. The fairness application is particularly sensitive: it requires that identified cross-applicant clusters reliably contain marginal applicants (one approved, one rejected), but the paper has not validated this property. A paper claiming 92.3% precision should not use its conclusion to pitch applications that depend on an entirely different quality criterion (detecting near-boundary decisions rather than matching duplicates). The conclusion should be restructured to: summarize validated findings, state limitations explicitly, and briefly mention future directions in one paragraph.

### W4. Precision bound tightness depends on unexamined selection effects (Major)
**Page 4 - Remark 1, Lemma 1.** The bound Pr[False] ≤ Pr[Mult]/p² relies on Pr[Mult|False] ≥ p². The "random pair" p² benchmark assumes that applications from different individuals in a false-positive cluster are independent random draws from the population. However, these applications are specifically selected to share nine categorical variables plus near-identical continuous attributes—they are far from random pairs. The probability of two originations within such selected pairs could differ substantially from p², affecting bound tightness. The authors should discuss this selection effect and ideally bound or estimate Pr[Mult|False] more carefully using within-partition origination rates rather than the global p̂.

### W5. Recall bound is purely relative, but paper implies absolute recall (Major)
**Page 4 - Corollary 1 and Page 6 - Simulation Results.** Corollary 1 correctly derives a recall bound that depends on P_tot (true cross-applicant count), which is unobserved. The paper then correctly notes that ranking by α̂(θ)·N⁺(θ) is equivalent to ranking by the recall bound because P_tot is fixed across θ. However, in the simulation section (line 160) the paper states "we achieve a recall of 92%" without clearly stating that this is an absolute estimate obtained by imputing P_tot. The distinction between relative ranking and absolute estimation should be made explicit, and the imputation method for P_tot should be described in the main text.

### W6. "State-of-the-art clustering" overstatement (Minor)
**Page 1 - Contributions paragraph.** The clustering algorithm is described as "state-of-the-art agglomerative clustering algorithm," but it is the standard fastcluster implementation of complete-linkage hierarchical clustering (Müllner 2011, 2013). The second contribution (instantiating the framework) should not overstate the algorithmic novelty—it is an application of an existing off-the-shelf method. The actual contribution is the evaluation framework (C1) and its instantiation (C2), not the clustering algorithm per se.

### W7. Variable scaling and ε interpretability not documented (Minor)
**Page 1 - Simulation Results, Page 7 - Application.** The distance threshold ε = 0.06 is reported without specifying how variables were scaled before computing Euclidean distances. The continuous variables (income in $thousands, loan amount in $thousands, FICO score ~300-850, LTV ratio, date in days) have very different units and ranges. Without standardization details, the ε threshold is not interpretable and the tuning process is not reproducible. The authors should report the preprocessing (e.g., z-score normalization) and explain what ε = 0.06 means in terms of the original variable scales.

### W8. Missing limitation on scope: same-property only (Minor)
**Page 2 - Methodology, notation definition.** The paper defines "individuals" as borrower-property pairs, meaning that an applicant who applies for mortgages on two different properties is treated as two distinct individuals. This limits the method to detecting cross-applicants for the same property only, which should be explicitly stated in the title and abstract scope. The fairness and shopping behavior applications discussed in the conclusion implicitly assume broader coverage than the method can deliver.

### W9. Figure caption duplication and formatting issues (Minor)
**Page 1.** The Figure 1 caption appears three times in the markdown rendering. This is likely a PDF extraction artifact, but the authors should ensure the submission file does not contain duplicated captions that could confuse readers.

### W10. "First-lien mortgage" constraint may limit generality more than claimed (Minor)
**Page 1 - Introduction.** The paper gives examples beyond mortgages (insurance, college, jobs) but the distance clustering approach relies heavily on continuous matching variables (date, income, amount, credit score, LTV). For settings like college admissions or job offers, the available continuous variables may be very different, and the distance-based ε-clustering approach may require significant adaptation. The domain-agnostic claim is about the bounds framework, but the empirical instantiation is mortgage-specific.

## Score
**Final Score: 6.5/10**

The paper presents a clever and practically useful framework for evaluating unsupervised record linkage under a structural constraint. The core theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) is sound and the simulation validation confirms the bound tracks actual precision closely. The HMDA application demonstrates scalability and produces a reasonable precision lower bound.

However, the score is moderated by several factors: (1) the "first work" novelty claim and broader contribution framing cannot be verified without external literature (deferred to manual verification), (2) Assumption 1 (independent origination) is strong and unexamined, (3) the conclusion over-extends into speculative applications without adequate limitations discussion, (4) the recall bound's absolute interpretation is ambiguous, and (5) the empirical contributions rely on a standard clustering algorithm rather than a novel method. The paper would benefit from a more measured framing of its contributions, explicit limitations, and sensitivity analysis for its key assumptions.

**Scoring rationale:**
- Research value: 7/10 — The evaluation framework fills a practical gap in unsupervised record linkage. The problem is well-motivated and the solution is elegant. However, the scope (same-property only, two-application clusters) limits the breadth of impact.
- Novelty: 6/10 (deferred) — The core insight is novel within the stated scope, but the first-claim is unverifiable. The clustering instantiation uses existing methods. Final novelty assessment requires manual literature verification.
- Validity/Soundness: 6/10 — Theorem 1 and corollaries are correctly derived under stated assumptions. However, the assumptions' plausibility in real data is not critically examined, and several claims (recall, SOTA clustering) are overstated relative to evidence.
- Reproducibility: 7/10 — The data source is well-described, the filtering steps are clear, and the fastcluster package is standard. However, the variable scaling and 96-option search space are not fully specified, limiting exact replication.
