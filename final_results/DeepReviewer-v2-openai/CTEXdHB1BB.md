## Summary

This paper introduces Conditional Advantage Estimation (CANON), a modification to the GRPO/DR.GRPO advantage estimation framework for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in LLM reasoning. CANON splits a group of sampled responses into two equal-sized subgroups based on a training metric (entropy or response length), then computes two separate advantages: an *inter-group* advantage (comparing a response to the opposite group's average reward) and an *intra-group* advantage (comparing a response to its own group's average reward). These are combined via a mixing coefficient $\mu$.

The core idea is that inter-group advantage reveals which metric direction (e.g., high or low entropy) correlates with better accuracy, enabling the model to exploit beneficial metric trends without a fixed prior. Conversely, intra-group advantage favors correct responses from the lower-performing group, encouraging exploration. A dynamic scheduling variant (CANON-Dynamic) tunes $\mu$ during training to balance exploitation and exploration.

Experiments on three LLMs (Qwen2.5-Math-7B/1.5B, Llama3.1-8B) across six math benchmarks and three complexity levels of ZebraLogic show that CANON-Inter (entropy-based, $\mu=1$) achieves modest accuracy improvements on math tasks (~1.9 points over DR.GRPO), while CANON-Intra ($\mu=0$) improves complex logic reasoning (~5.2 points on hardest subset). CANON-Dynamic achieves competitive results on both task types when the scheduling strategy is chosen per model. A length-weighted variant (CANON-Eff) reduces token consumption by ~26% with minimal accuracy loss, yielding a favorable efficiency-performance trade-off.

**Novelty assessment (deferred):** External literature verification was not available in this run; novelty comparisons against prior advantage-shaping methods cannot be independently verified here. The manuscript claims that CANON is the first advantage estimation method that does not presuppose the direction of metric influence — this claim requires manual verification against prior work on adaptive reward shaping and conditional advantage methods.

## Strengths
**1. Clean and principled methodological modification.** The core idea of CANON — splitting sampled responses into two groups by a metric and computing inter/intra-group advantages — is conceptually elegant and minimally invasive. It requires only a regrouping step and a mixing coefficient $\mu$ on top of existing GRPO/DR.GRPO implementations, making it practical and easy to integrate into current RLVR training pipelines. The connection shown in Eq. (7), where DR.GRPO emerges as a special case of CANON with $\mu=0.5$, is a nice theoretical unification that helps position the contribution clearly.

**2. Well-motivated distinction between exploitation and exploration.** The paper provides a clear behavioral interpretation of the two advantage components: inter-group advantage drives exploitation of the beneficial metric trend, while intra-group advantage encourages exploration by favoring correct responses from the lower-reward group. This dual-component framing is intuitive and gives practitioners a meaningful knob ($\mu$) to control the exploration-exploitation balance during training.

**3. Comprehensive empirical evaluation.** The experiments cover a reasonable range of settings: three model families (Qwen2.5-Math 7B and 1.5B, Llama3.1-8B), six math benchmarks with varying difficulty, and a complex logic reasoning benchmark (ZebraLogic) with three complexity tiers. The inclusion of both math (answer-verifiable) and logic (search-space-based) reasoning provides a more complete picture than many RLVR papers that focus solely on math.

**4. Demonstration of practical efficiency gains.** The CANON-Eff variant for length-weighted advantage achieving ~26% token reduction with less than 1% accuracy drop is a practically useful result. The Pareto frontier analysis, despite some concerns about selective baselines, convincingly shows that CANON-Eff provides a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than simple clipping or reward-based length penalties, especially in low-token-budget regimes.

**5. Transparent analysis of training dynamics.** Section 6 provides useful insights into how different $\mu$ values affect entropy, response length, and reflection patterns over training steps. The analysis of "Gain of Rethinking" (Figure 6) helps explain why CANON-Dynamic can achieve both high training reward and positive reflection gains, while pure intra-group advantage sacrifices reward for reflection and vice versa.

## Weaknesses
**W1 (Major) — Missing statistical rigor: single-run results without variance estimation.** All experimental results in Tables 1–4 are reported as point estimates from single training runs. No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests are provided. This is a critical omission because: (a) RL training for LLMs is known to have high variance across seeds; (b) many reported gains are small (e.g., 1.9-point average improvement on math, 0.4-point accuracy drop for 26% token reduction); and (c) some comparisons show near-tie results where variance could reverse the ranking (e.g., CANON-Inter entropy at 57.6% vs Entropy Adv at 56.3% on math — a 1.3% gap that could easily be noise). Without variance estimates, the reader cannot assess whether differences are statistically meaningful. **Fix:** Report all main results as mean ± std over ≥3 random seeds; add a paired bootstrap significance test for key comparisons (CANON vs DR.GRPO on each task group).

**W2 (Major) — CANON-Dynamic scheduling is model-specific and post-hoc selected.** The paper tests four scheduling strategies and selects different ones per model (Cosin-First-Inter-Later-Intra for Qwen-7B and Llama-8B, First-Inter-Later-Intra for Qwen-1.5B). This selection is done post-hoc based on which strategy "achieves strong performance in both scenarios" (Section 5.2). While acknowledged, this practice undermines the claim that CANON-Dynamic "consistently yields leading results across both mathematical and logical reasoning tasks" — the consistency is achieved through oracle model-specific tuning rather than a single principled rule. A practitioner applying CANON to a new model would not know which scheduling strategy to choose without running all four and picking the best. **Fix:** (a) Propose a single default scheduling strategy with a principled decision rule (e.g., based on model size or training accuracy trajectory); (b) report results for all four strategies across all models in the appendix to show sensitivity; (c) clarify that model-specific tuning is an optional extension, not the recommended default.

**W3 (Major) — Causal claims for exploration/reflection are only correlational.** The Analysis section (Section 6) claims that CANON-Inter "exploits existing features," CANON-Intra "encourages effective exploration," and CANON-Dynamic "promotes beneficial behaviors such as effective exploration and reflection." The evidence for these claims is observational: Figure 5 shows entropy trends under different $\mu$ values, and Figure 6 shows correlations between "Gain of Rethinking" and training reward. No controlled experiment isolates whether CANON *causes* these behaviors or merely *selects* for responses that exhibit them. For example, the "Gain of Rethinking" metric is computed by splitting responses into more-reflection and less-reflection groups — this grouping is correlated with the CANON metric-based split itself, creating a potential circularity. **Fix:** (a) Add a direct behavioral measurement: for a fixed checkpoint, compare CANON models against DR.GRPO on metrics like number of unique reasoning paths, self-correction frequency, or backtracking rate; (b) rephrase causal claims as correlational observations; (c) add an ablation where the reflection split is controlled independently of the metric split.

**W4 (Major) — Pareto frontier claims overstate dominance over baselines.** The paper claims that "all the frontier from baselines are dominated by the frontier of CANON-Eff's" (Section 5.3). This claim is weakened by: (a) the exclusion of Length Reward (*) configurations that produce very short but low-accuracy outputs — these points are excluded because they are "difficult to fairly compare," but their inclusion could show that the frontier is less one-sided; (b) CANON-Eff ($\alpha=0.96$) achieves 56.2% accuracy at 822 tokens, while DR.GRPO achieves 56.6% at 1115 tokens — this is a trade-off, not strict dominance (DR.GRPO has higher accuracy); (c) the performance metric used for the Pareto frontier is not clearly defined (is it the math average across all six benchmarks?). **Fix:** (a) Include ALL baseline configurations in the Pareto plot, including low-performance ones; (b) replace "dominates" with "achieves a more favorable trade-off in the evaluated range"; (c) clarify the exact performance metric used in the frontier plot; (d) add interpolated matched-accuracy comparisons (e.g., token cost at 56.0% accuracy).

**W5 (Major) — Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) has an ambiguous condition.** Theorem 1 states that inter-group advantage magnitude exceeds DR.GRPO's "only when $|C_q^+| = |C_q^-|$ if $|C_q^+|$ is a constant." The phrase "if $|C_q^+|$ is a constant" is unclear: does it mean constant across queries, constant across training steps, or simply that $|C_q^+|$ is a deterministic function of the sampled responses? The intended meaning appears to be that amplification occurs only for equal-sized splits, but the qualification about constancy is never defined or discussed. This ambiguity weakens the theoretical foundation that justifies the equal-split design. **Fix:** Restate Theorem 1 precisely: "For a fixed group size $G$, the ratio $|\hat{A}^{\text{inter}}|/|\hat{A}^{\text{DR.GRPO}}| > 1$ only when $|C_q^+| = |C_q^-| = G/2$." Remove the ambiguous "if $|C_q^+|$ is a constant" clause or explain it with a concrete example.

**W6 (Minor) — Notation inconsistency in PPO clipping.** Eq. (1) defines $\text{clip}_1^{1+\varepsilon}(r_{o,t}(\theta))$ but the clipping function definition uses $\text{clip}_1^b(x) = \max(\min(x, a), b)$, mixing subscript and superscript conventions. Standard PPO uses $\text{clip}_{1-\varepsilon}^{1+\varepsilon}$, not $\text{clip}_1^{1+\varepsilon}$. This inconsistency could confuse readers familiar with PPO. **Fix:** Use standard notation $\text{clip}_{1-\varepsilon}^{1+\varepsilon}(r_{o,t}(\theta))$ with the definition $\text{clip}_a^b(x) = \max(\min(x, b), a)$ (or simply describe the clipping in text).

**W7 (Minor) — Abstract overclaims without scope boundaries.** The abstract states "CANON based on entropy consistently outperforms prior methods across three LLMs on both math reasoning and high-complexity logic tasks." This is not consistent with Table 1, where CANON-Intra (entropy) underperforms DR.GRPO on overall math accuracy (54.7 vs 55.7), and CANON-Inter (entropy) underperforms on logic (25.7 vs 26.2). Only specific CANON variants outperform in specific task categories. **Fix:** Qualify the abstract claim with task-specific boundaries and note that the best variant differs by task.

**W8 (Minor) — Introduction lacks a concrete failure scenario for directional priors.** The paper motivates CANON by arguing that hand-crafted directional priors can be "overly biased and may lead to failure," but never provides a concrete example of such a failure. A reader unfamiliar with the specific failure mode (e.g., a scenario where a fixed entropy bonus helps on hard problems but hurts on easy ones) may not appreciate why CANON's non-directional approach is necessary. **Fix:** Add one concrete example in the introduction: "For a training batch containing both easy and hard problems, a fixed high-entropy bonus may help exploration on hard problems but penalize confident correct answers on easy ones, reducing overall accuracy."

**W9 (Minor) — Weighted advantage asymmetry (Eq. 9) not explained.** The $\alpha$ coefficient in Eq. (9) is applied asymmetrically: scaling the baseline for short responses but scaling the reward for long responses. This design choice is not discussed, and its potential bias (creating a double incentive for shorter responses) is not acknowledged. **Fix:** Add a brief explanation of the asymmetry and its intended effect, along with a note about potential side effects on hard problems that require longer solutions.

**W10 (Minor) — Related work is organized as a paper list, not by comparison axes.** The two related work subsections list methods in chronological order with one-sentence descriptions, rather than organizing them by design choices (baseline type, group structure, metric handling). This makes it harder for readers to understand where CANON fits in the landscape. **Fix:** Reorganize as a comparison table or paragraph grouping by methodological axes.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** The paper presents a methodologically clean and practically relevant modification to advantage estimation in RLVR. The core idea (conditional regrouping with inter/intra-group advantage) is well-motivated, technically sound, and demonstrates practical benefits in both accuracy and token efficiency. The theoretical unification of DR.GRPO as a special case adds rigor, and the empirical coverage across three models and two task types is commendable.

However, the score is constrained by several significant weaknesses that affect confidence in the reported results:

1. **Missing statistical evidence (W1).** Single-run results without variance estimates mean that most reported gains (1-5 points) cannot be assessed for statistical significance. This affects every core claim in the paper.
2. **Model-specific scheduling selection (W2).** CANON-Dynamic's reported success depends on oracle post-hoc selection of the scheduling strategy per model, weakening generalization claims.
3. **Correlational analysis (W3).** The claimed causal link between CANON and exploration/reflection behaviors is not supported by controlled experiments.
4. **Incomplete Pareto frontier comparison (W4).** The efficiency dominance claim is partially overstated due to selective baseline inclusion.
5. **Theoretical ambiguity (W5).** Theorem 1's condition is imprecisely stated, reducing confidence in the theoretical grounding.

The paper's strengths (clean method, practical efficiency gains, broad evaluation) are real, but the evidence is not yet robust enough to fully support all the claims made. With multi-seed experiments, a principled scheduling rule, and tighter claim bounding, the contribution would be considerably stronger.

**Novelty verdict (deferred):** External literature verification was unavailable in this run. The manuscript's claim of being the first advantage estimation method without directional priors requires independent verification against prior work.
