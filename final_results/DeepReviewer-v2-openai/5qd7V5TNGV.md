## Summary
This paper presents CP4D, a compositional framework for physics-aware 4D (dynamic 3D) scene generation from text prompts. The key idea is to decompose 4D generation into three stages: (1) separate 3D reconstruction of a static background and dynamic foreground objects using pre-trained image-to-3D models, (2) hybrid motion synthesis combining physics simulators (MPM for deformable, rigid-body for solid, PBD for fluid) with SDS-based refinement using video diffusion models to correct VLM-estimated material parameters and spurious collision artifacts, and (3) automated composition via monocular depth estimation and a depth-aware heuristic for foreground-background integration, refined through sequential scale-then-position optimization.

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of current 4D generation methods—their tendency to produce physically implausible motion. The compositional formulation and the hybrid physics+SDS strategy are reasonable design choices. However, the manuscript has several weaknesses that limit its current impact: the evaluation is conducted on only 17 curated examples without variance reporting, the SDS gradient computation requires differentiable physics solvers that are not described or cited, ablation studies are purely qualitative, and the paper makes strong "significantly outperforming" claims that are not fully supported by the evidence. Novelty verification against prior physics-aware generation methods is deferred as external literature retrieval was unavailable in this run.

## Strengths
**1. Meaningful problem formulation.** Decomposing 4D scene generation into static background + dynamic foreground with explicit physics simulation is a sensible design choice that addresses a real limitation of existing methods. The compositional formulation enables flexible editing of scene elements, which is a practical advantage.

**2. Hybrid physics-video diffusion strategy.** The idea of using physics simulators to guarantee basic physical laws (gravity, collision, material deformation) and then refining with video diffusion priors via SDS is conceptually appealing. It addresses two concrete failure modes: (i) VLM-estimated material parameters are numerically imprecise, and (ii) grid-based physics solvers produce spurious collision artifacts.

**3. Automated composition mechanism.** The depth-aware heuristic for initializing foreground position and scale, followed by sequential S-then-P optimization, is a practical solution to the cross-coordinate-space alignment problem between independently generated 3D representations.

**4. Multi-material support.** Supporting elastic (MPM), rigid, and fluid (PBD) objects within a unified pipeline, with automatic solver selection, is a step forward compared to prior work that handles only one material type.

**5. Controllable editing capability.** The compositional design naturally supports zero-shot editing of backgrounds, foreground objects, and motion trajectories, which is demonstrated qualitatively in Figure 6 and is a useful feature for content creation.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Differentiable physics requirement is unaddressed (Major).** The SDS-based refinement in Equations (4)-(5) requires computing gradients of the rendered video V with respect to material parameters Θ and displacement variables ΔΓ. The gradient ∂V/∂Θ must backpropagate through the physics simulator (MPM, rigid-body, or PBD solver) and the differentiable renderer. However, the paper does not describe how this gradient flow is implemented. Standard MPM and rigid-body solvers are not natively differentiable; recent differentiable simulation frameworks (e.g., DiffTaichi, ChainQueen, or Warp) enable this but are not cited or discussed. Without differentiable simulators, the SDS optimization as written may not be practically realizable. This is a reproducibility-critical issue. **Fix**: Explicitly state whether simulators are differentiable, cite the differentiable simulation framework used, or describe the gradient approximation method (e.g., finite differences or zeroth-order optimization). Provide a clear gradient computation graph in the appendix.

**W2. Small-scale evaluation without statistical rigor (Major).** The entire quantitative evaluation is conducted on only 17 curated examples, with no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests reported. With n=17 and no error bars, the observed differences between CP4D and the strongest baseline (PhysGen3D) on metrics like WorldScore Photo Consistency (97.42 vs 93.07, Δ=4.35) cannot be assessed for statistical reliability. Additionally, the VBench Motion metric is saturated above 0.99 for all methods, making it non-discriminative. The CP4D paper claims "significantly outperforming" and "consistently outperforming" but the evidence is insufficient to support this level of certainty. **Fix**: Report mean±std across ≥3 independent runs, include 95% confidence intervals or bootstrap estimates, and perform paired significance tests (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank) between CP4D and the strongest baseline. Expand evaluation to at least one external benchmark for cross-method comparability.

**W3. Quantitative ablation missing (Major).** Section 5.3 presents ablation studies in purely qualitative terms ("unstable or non-physical object motion") with only one visual example (Figure 5). No quantitative metrics are reported for the ablated variants. Without ablation tables showing VBench/WorldScore/GPT-4o metrics for w/o material optimization, w/o position optimization, and w/o both, readers cannot assess the marginal contribution of each component. **Fix**: Add a dedicated ablation table with the same metrics as Table 1 for each ablation condition, run on all 17 examples. Include an additional ablation for the Stage III composition mechanism to verify the depth-aware heuristic.

**W4. Inconsistent performance claims (Moderate).** Table 1 shows CP4D is second-best on VBench Imaging quality (0.641 vs Runway's 0.644), yet the paper repeatedly claims "consistently outperforming existing methods" and "significantly outperforming prior methods" (Abstract, Conclusion, contribution list). These global performance claims are not supported when CP4D does not lead on all metrics. **Fix**: Qualify performance claims with specific metric breakdown. Use bounded wording: "CP4D achieves the best overall performance on 5 of 6 metrics in Table 1, with competitive imaging quality."

**W5. Related work lacks structured positioning (Moderate).** Sections 2.1 and 2.2 read as chronological paper lists rather than organized comparison axes. The paper does not explicitly state for each prior work category (a) what the shared limitation is, (b) how CP4D addresses it, and (c) what residual novelty remains. This makes it difficult for readers to assess the paper's contribution placement. **Fix**: Restructure into thematic sub-sections (e.g., "SDS-based 4D generation," "Data-driven 4D generation," "Physics simulation for 3D dynamics," "Single-image-to-physics-video") with explicit per-group comparison tables or bullet points showing CP4D's differentiating features.

**W6. Editing model reliability unexamined (Moderate).** Stage I relies on an image editing model (Qwen-Image-Edit) to produce a composite image I_{b,f} containing both background and foreground. Errors in this editing step (inconsistent lighting, incorrect placement, boundary artifacts) propagate through segmentation, depth estimation, and 3D reconstruction. The paper does not analyze failure modes, editing success rates, or implement quality gates. **Fix**: Add a failure analysis paragraph discussing editing model limitations, include a supplementary figure with successful and failed composites, and propose a CLIP-similarity quality gate for automatic regeneration.

**W7. Introduction narrative buries technical novelty (Minor).** The introduction describes all three stages in one dense paragraph without explicitly separating supporting infrastructure from core technical novelty. Stage 2 (hybrid motion synthesis with SDS refinement) is the paper's main technical contribution, yet it receives only two sentences in the introduction. **Fix**: Split the introduction's solution description into two parts: (A) stages 1 and 3 as enabling infrastructure, and (B) stage 2 as the core novel contribution with clear motivation for why SDS refinement is needed.

**W8. Conclusion lacks limitations and future work (Minor).** The conclusion restates contributions and claims consistent outperformance but includes no discussion of limitations, failure cases, or future directions. This omission can be perceived as lacking scientific self-awareness. **Fix**: Add a limitations paragraph covering: (i) dependence on editing model quality, (ii) limited material types (elastic, rigid, fluid — excluding fracture, granular, coupled physics), (iii) small-scale evaluation, and (iv) future work including differentiable simulators and larger benchmarks.

**W9. Novelty verification deferred (Note).** Due to external literature retrieval being unavailable in this run, novelty claims regarding the hybrid motion synthesis strategy and automated composition mechanism could not be verified against prior work. Manual verification against recent physics-aware 4D generation papers (e.g., PhysGen3D, DreamPhysics, OmniPhysGS, PhysGen) is recommended before submission. The paper's claims of being "novel" and "first" should be qualified until verified.

**W10. Typographical and structural issues (Minor).**
- Page 1, Challenge 1: "foreground objects and foreground objects" — duplicate phrase suggests copy-paste error.
- Eq. (8): The nested min(min(...), min(...)) simplifies to a single min over four terms; the complex notation is unnecessary and may confuse readers.
- Section 5.1: "We curate a dataset of 17 examples" — it is unclear whether these are novel prompts created by the authors or selected from existing datasets. Selection criteria are not specified.
- The "differentiable simulators" phrase in the contribution list (Page 2, Line 21) is inconsistent with the method section, which does not mention differentiability.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale.** The paper addresses a relevant problem (physics-agnostic 4D generation) with a conceptually reasonable compositional approach. The hybrid simulation+SDS refinement strategy is a worthwhile design idea. However, the current manuscript has significant evidentiary gaps that prevent a higher score:

- **Research value (7/10):** The compositional formulation and hybrid motion synthesis are valuable directions, but the paper does not provide sufficient empirical validation to establish their practical impact.
- **Novelty (deferred):** Without external literature verification, novelty cannot be definitively assessed. The paper's claims of being "novel" are plausible but require verification against recent physics-aware generation methods.
- **Validity/soundness (5/10):** The SDS gradient computation through physics simulators is unexplained, the evaluation is underpowered (n=17, no variance), ablation lacks quantitative support, and performance claims are partially inconsistent with reported numbers.
- **Reproducibility (5/10):** Missing details about differentiable simulation, incomplete specification of the SDS optimization procedure, and unreported hyperparameters limit reproducibility.

The paper's strengths (clear problem framing, compositional design, multi-material support) are offset by the weaknesses above. A revision addressing the differentiable physics gradient issue, adding rigorous statistical evaluation, quantitative ablations, and more cautious performance claims could raise the score to 7-8/10.
