## Summary
This paper presents ScaPre (Scalable-Precise Concept Unlearning), a closed-form framework for large-scale concept unlearning in text-to-image diffusion models. The key technical components are: (1) a conflict-aware stable design combining a spectral trace regularizer (which penalizes directions prone to inter-concept conflicts via SVD-based gating) with a geometry alignment term (Bures distance that matches covariance structures rather than element-wise weight differences), and (2) an Informax Decoupler that uses mutual information between channel activations and concept labels to identify concept-relevant parameters and restrict updates to those subspaces. The method produces a closed-form solution via a Sylvester equation, followed by a proximal refinement for the non-quadratic geometry alignment term.

The paper evaluates ScaPre on Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5 across object unlearning (Imagenette, custom ImageNet-Diversi50), precise unlearning (ImageNet-Confuse5 with visually similar concepts), artistic style unlearning (50 artists), and explicit content (I2P dataset). The results show that ScaPre achieves low unlearning accuracy (0.8% on Imagenette, 3.9% on ImageNet-Diversi50) while maintaining competitive CLIP scores and the highest UQ (Unlearn & Quality) metric among all compared methods. The method completes the unlearning of 50 concepts in approximately 120 seconds on an RTX A6000, making it significantly more efficient than training-based baselines.

## Strengths
1. **Well-motivated technical problem.** The paper identifies three concrete challenges in large-scale concept unlearning (conflicting weight updates, imprecise targeting, and computational overhead) that are clearly relevant to deploying diffusion models at scale. Each challenge is grounded in observations about existing methods' failure modes, making the motivation compelling.

2. **Technically grounded method design.** The conflict-aware stable design (spectral trace regularizer + Bures geometry alignment) and Informax Decoupler are grounded in established mathematical concepts (SVD, trace regularization, mutual information, Bures distance, Procrustes adjustment). The closed-form derivation via the Sylvester equation is elegant and avoids the complexity of iterative fine-tuning. The combination of these components into a unified objective is technically sound.

3. **Comprehensive evaluation across diverse benchmarks.** The paper evaluates on object unlearning (Imagenette, ImageNet-Diversi50), precise disentanglement (ImageNet-Confuse5 with visually similar categories), artistic style unlearning (50 artists), and explicit content (I2P). This breadth covers the main application scenarios for concept unlearning and provides reasonable evidence of the method's versatility.

4. **Strong empirical results on key metrics.** On Imagenette, ScaPre achieves the lowest unlearning accuracy (0.8%) while maintaining competitive CLIP scores, yielding the best UQ (64.09) by a wide margin over baselines. On ImageNet-Confuse5, ScaPre achieves 84.3% overall accuracy (harmonic mean of unlearning and preservation), substantially exceeding the next best method (SP at 50.3%). These results suggest a clear advantage on the evaluated settings.

5. **Efficiency advantage.** The closed-form solution eliminates iterative fine-tuning, completing 50-concept unlearning in ~120 seconds with ~5 GB peak memory. This is a genuine practical advantage over training-based methods (SPM: ~4.5 hours, ESD: ~4.0 hours) and positions the method well for real-world deployments where rapid concept removal is needed.

## Weaknesses
1. **[Major] Unsupportable "first" and SOTA claims without literature verification (Page 0 — Title & Page 8 — Conclusion).** The paper claims "the first closed-form framework specifically designed for large-scale concept unlearning" and "new state of the art for large-scale unlearning." Since prior closed-form multi-concept methods exist (UCE, RECE), whether ScaPre is truly "first" depends on the definition of "large-scale." External literature retrieval was unavailable in this run, so these claims cannot be independently verified. The "×5 more concepts than best baseline" claim also lacks a concrete anchor (which baseline, under what quality threshold). These claims should be qualified or supported with explicit comparison boundaries. **Fix:** Replace "first" with scoped wording (e.g., "first closed-form framework designed for 50+ concept unlearning, to our knowledge") and provide the exact threshold definition for the ×5 claim.

2. **[Major] Missing statistical significance and variance reporting (Page 6 — Table 1, Page 7 — Table 3).** All experimental results are reported as point estimates without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests. ScaPre's reported 0.8% average unlearning accuracy on Imagenette — while impressive — cannot be assessed for statistical reliability without multi-seed variance. The UQ metric itself is method-pool-dependent (normalized by mean/std of the evaluated set), making it non-portable across papers. **Fix:** Report means and standard deviations over at least 3 random seeds for all key metrics. Add a note clarifying that UQ scores are relative to the specific baseline pool.

3. **[Major] Undefined symbol $\mathcal{M}$ and potential factor discrepancy in the Sylvester equation derivation (Page 5 — Section 4.3).** In Equation (8), $A = \mathcal{M} + \mathbf{S} + \mathbf{R}$, but $\mathcal{M}$ is never defined. If $\mathcal{M} = \lambda I$ from Equation (3), this must be stated explicitly. More critically, the derived Sylvester equation $BW + WA = V^*C_E^\top$ (Equation 9) appears to omit a factor of $1/2$ from the derivative of the quadratic terms. A careful derivation gives $2WA + 2BW - V^*C_E^\top = 0$, which would produce $BW + WA = \frac{1}{2}V^*C_E^\top$. The paper should clarify whether the coefficients absorb this factor or whether there is an error. **Fix:** Define $\mathcal{M}$ explicitly and verify the derivative derivation against Appendix B.1, correcting any factor errors.

4. **[Major] Informax Decoupler lacks implementation-critical details (Page 4–5 — Section 4.2).** The MI computation requires an adaptive threshold $\tau_i$ for activation binarization and a total sample size $K$, neither of which is specified. Without these, the method is not reproducible. The binarization itself ($z = \mathbb{1}\{a_i(s) > \tau_i\}$) discards activation magnitude information, which may reduce discriminative power. The max-aggregation $\text{MI}_i = \max_k \text{MI}_i^{(k)}$ is not justified; this conservative choice may over-update channels that are concept-specific but globally important. **Fix:** Specify $\tau_i$ and $K$ explicitly. Briefly discuss the trade-offs of binarization and max-aggregation.

5. **[Major] Benchmark fairness concerns (Page 5 — Section 5.1).** The custom ImageNet-Diversi50 benchmark's class selection criteria are not stated, raising potential cherry-picking concerns. Baseline hyperparameters are not reported as being re-tuned for the 50-concept setting, which could disadvantage training-based methods that have capacity limits. The paper states "all results of compared methods are derived from their official open-source implementations" but does not confirm whether hyperparameter adjustments were made for the larger-scale setting. **Fix:** Document the class selection procedure, baseline hyperparameter configuration, and any tuning performed for the large-scale experiments.

6. **[Major] Conclusion lacks limitation discussion (Page 8 — Section 6).** The conclusion contains promotional language ("establishes a new and effective paradigm", "forward-looking direction") without acknowledging any limitations of the method. Every method has boundary conditions — for example, the R matrix models only target-concept interactions, not cross-category target–non-target interference; the Bures geometry alignment is computationally expensive and must be handled via approximation; the Informax Decoupler requires forward passes through the model. Including a concrete limitations paragraph would significantly strengthen scientific credibility. **Fix:** Replace promotional claims with a brief summary of validated findings, bounded limitations, and specific future work directions.

7. **[Minor] Abstract overclaims without bounded scope (Page 0 — Abstract).** The abstract claims "achieving a new state of the art for large-scale unlearning" and "can forget up to ×5 more concepts than the best baseline" without specifying the reference baseline, generative quality threshold, or benchmark setting. This makes the abstract read as promotional rather than scientifically precise. **Fix:** Bound all quantitative claims with explicit comparison conditions and thresholds.

8. **[Minor] Introduction paragraph 1 is a citation dump (Page 1 — Introduction).** The first paragraph contains 20+ inline citations without differentiating their individual relevance. This reduces readability and fails to establish a clear narrative progression from T2I progress to unlearning challenges. **Fix:** Reduce citations to a representative subset and add a clear transition sentence linking T2I concerns to the specific gap addressed by the paper.

9. **[Minor] UQ metric is method-pool-dependent (Page 6 — Section 5.2).** The UQ metric normalizes by mean and standard deviation of the evaluated method pool, making scores non-portable and incomparable across papers. The sigmoid scaling also compresses differences between leading methods. **Fix:** Report standard metrics (accuracy, CLIP, FID) alongside UQ, and add a caveat about the metric's dependence on the baseline pool.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** ScaPre addresses a practically important problem (scalable concept unlearning) with a technically coherent closed-form framework. The spectral trace regularizer, geometry alignment via Bures distance, and Informax Decoupler represent reasonable engineering contributions, and the empirical results on the evaluated benchmarks are strong — with ScaPre achieving the best UQ across all four benchmarks by substantial margins.

However, the score is constrained by several factors:

- **Novelty (primary scoring dimension)**: The novelty claims ("first closed-form framework," "new state of the art," "×5 more concepts") cannot be independently verified due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode. Prior closed-form multi-concept methods (UCE, RECE) exist, and whether ScaPre's additions constitute a genuinely new contribution depends on how "large-scale" is scoped. The paper would benefit from downgrading its novelty language to verified, scoped claims.

- **Research value (primary scoring dimension)**: The practical value of a closed-form, efficient, multi-concept unlearning method is clear. However, the missing statistical rigor (no variance reporting, no significance tests) reduces confidence in the results' reliability. The method's dependence on multiple hyperparameters (β for geometry alignment, the gating function's sigmoid scaling, the MI binarization threshold) without a systematic sensitivity analysis also weakens the reproducibility argument.

- **Technical verification**: The identified symbol inconsistencies in Section 4.3 (undefined $\mathcal{M}$, potential factor-$1/2$ omission in the Sylvester equation) and missing implementation details in the Informax Decoupler ($\tau_i$, $K$) need correction before the method can be considered fully specified.

The core technical ideas are promising and the evaluation breadth is commendable. With a round of revisions addressing the reproducibility gaps, statistical rigor, and claim bounding, the paper could warrant a higher score.
