## Summary

This paper addresses the training-inference mismatch in tree-based speculative decoding (SpD) for LLM inference acceleration. The authors identify that existing draft model training methods (EAGLE, HASS) use linear sequence objectives that do not reflect the tree-structured inference performed during deployment. They propose two complementary contributions: (1) **TALF (Tree-Aware Loss Function)**, which computes cross-entropy losses across all branches of a draft tree generated by the target LLM, aligning the draft model's full distribution with the target's; and (2) **SALF (Stopping at Low Further Gains)**, a dynamic tree construction algorithm with a provably monotonic early-stopping criterion that balances tree quality against drafting overhead. Combined, SALF & TALF achieve 2.16-3.48x end-to-end speedups on Llama-based models across five benchmarks, improving over EAGLE-2 by 15.6-39.4% and over HASS by 6.5-24.4%.

The paper is technically sound, the motivation is clearly articulated, and the experimental evaluation is comprehensive across multiple model families and task types. The isolated analysis of TALF and SALF contributions (Table 2) is a strength. However, several methodological details and evaluation practices deserve attention: the fixed-tree training approximation is not fully validated, variance reporting is absent for hardware-timed measurements, and the training protocol varies asymmetrically across models. These issues are addressable and do not undermine the core findings but should be resolved before publication.

## Strengths
**1. Clearly identified and well-motivated problem.** The training-inference mismatch in tree-based SpD is a genuine and previously underappreciated issue. The paper provides a clean empirical demonstration (Figure 2) showing that existing draft models perform poorly when conditioned on lower-ranked tokens, which constitute over 10% of draft tree nodes. This diagnostic experiment is both insightful and reproducible.

**2. Technically sound and principled solutions.** TALF's tree-aware loss is a natural extension of knowledge distillation to the tree-structured setting. The idea of having the target LLM construct a reference tree and computing per-node cross-entropy losses is methodologically clean. SALF's early-stopping criterion with the monotonicity guarantee (Theorem 1) is theoretically grounded and practically important for reducing drafting overhead without sacrificing tree quality.

**3. Rigorous ablation and isolation analysis.** Table 2 is a highlight of the evaluation: by testing all combinations of three loss functions (EAGLE-2, HASS, TALF) and three tree construction methods (beam search, optimal tree search, SALF), the paper cleanly isolates the contribution of each component. This goes beyond the typical "ours vs. baseline" comparison and provides genuine insight into which improvements come from training vs. inference optimization.

**4. Consistent gains across diverse settings.** The results span three target LLMs (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B), five benchmarks (MT-bench, HumanEval, GSM8K, Alpaca, CNN/DM), and two temperature regimes (0 and 1). SALF & TALF consistently outperform both baselines, with relative improvements of 15.6-39.4% over EAGLE-2 and 6.5-24.4% over HASS.

**5. Strong practical relevance.** The methods require no changes to the draft model architecture and are compatible with existing EAGLE-based deployments. The SALF threshold is intuitive and the sensitivity analysis (Table 4) provides practical guidance for deployment tuning. The reproducibility statement is comprehensive, including training scripts and hyperparameter details in the supplementary materials.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Fixed-tree training approximation not fully validated (Major).** The TALF training procedure uses a precomputed, fixed tree generated by the target model, which is reused across training epochs. The paper acknowledges this computational necessity but does not analyze whether these target-generated trees align with the trees the trained draft model would produce during inference. If the draft model's inference-time tree structure diverges substantially from the training-time fixed tree, the tree-awareness benefit may be partially lost. 

*Impact:* This creates a gap between the stated motivation (aligning training with tree-structured inference) and the actual training procedure (using target-generated trees rather than draft-generated trees). 

*Suggested fix:* (a) Measure the node overlap rate between target-generated training trees and draft-generated inference trees after TALF training. (b) Consider iterative tree refreshing where the tree is periodically regenerated using the updated draft model during training (at a small computational cost). (c) Add an ablation comparing TALF trained with fixed vs. periodically refreshed trees to quantify the approximation's impact.

**W2. Variance and statistical reliability not reported (Major).** All speedup measurements in Tables 1, 2, and 4 are reported as single values without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or indications of the number of repeated runs. GPU-based latency measurements are inherently noisy due to thermal throttling, memory bandwidth contention, and system scheduling. When improvements are as modest as 6.5-8.1% (HASS vs. SALF&TALF on Llama2-7B at temperature 1), run-to-run variance could affect the ranking.

*Impact:* Without variance estimates, readers cannot assess the statistical significance of the reported speedup improvements.

*Suggested fix:* (a) Repeat all end-to-end timing experiments at least 3-5 times and report mean ± std. (b) For the key SALF&TALF vs. HASS comparisons, provide paired bootstrap confidence intervals or statistical significance tests. (c) Report raw wall-clock times (in milliseconds) alongside speedup ratios to enable direct comparison with other systems.

**W3. Asymmetric training protocol across models (Major).** The paper uses different training protocols for Llama models (10-epoch EAGLE pre-training + 3-epoch HASS/TALF fine-tuning) vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (24-hour equal-time training for all methods). The Llama protocol does not control for total training epochs: EAGLE baselines have 10 epochs while HASS/TALF benefit from 13 total epochs (10 pre-training + 3 fine-tuning). The DeepSeek protocol controls for wall-clock time but introduces a different confound (convergence rate differences within a fixed time budget).

*Impact:* This asymmetry makes it difficult to attribute observed gains solely to the loss function rather than to differential training budgets.

*Suggested fix:* (a) For Llama models, report EAGLE baselines at 13 total epochs to match the HASS/TALF training budget. (b) Provide training loss curves for all methods to show convergence behavior. (c) Report wall-clock training time and inference overhead for each method to enable cost-benefit analysis.

**W4. SALF stopping criterion only considers top-B batch probability (Moderate).** The SALF early-stopping check (Algorithm 2, line 13) examines only the sum of probabilities of the next B nodes to be expanded, not the total probability mass of all remaining unexpanded nodes in the priority queue Q. While Theorem 1 proves this sum is monotonically decreasing, the monotonicity of the top-B sum does not guarantee that the *total* remaining probability mass is low. Nodes with individually low probabilities could collectively contribute meaningful acceptance gains if expanded in sufficient quantity.

*Impact:* The stopping threshold may be overly aggressive, potentially truncating branches where the joint probability of many low-probability nodes is high.

*Suggested fix:* (a) Compare the top-B-based stopping criterion against a cumulative criterion that stops when the sum of all remaining probabilities in Q falls below the threshold. (b) Provide a theoretical bound linking the excluded probability mass to the maximum possible loss in expected tau.

**W5. Missing wall-clock time breakdown (Moderate).** The paper's key insight for SALF is that it reduces drafting overhead while preserving tree quality. However, no empirical breakdown of total latency into "drafting time" vs. "verification time" is provided. Without this decomposition, it is unclear whether SALF's speedup comes primarily from reduced drafting overhead (as claimed) or from improved tree composition that enables faster verification.

*Impact:* Weakens the causal narrative around SALF's mechanism of action.

*Suggested fix:* Report the average number of drafting iterations per verification step and the average drafting time per iteration for SALF vs. optimal tree search vs. beam search. This data would directly validate the claim that SALF reduces drafting overhead.

**W6. SALF threshold selection rationale (Minor).** The paper selects th=0.6 as the default based on "more consistent performance improvements across the tested LLMs," but Table 4 only reports sensitivity for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, where th=0.5 actually achieves higher speedup (2.62x vs. 2.59x). The "more consistent" claim is not substantiated with sensitivity data for the other two target models.

*Suggested fix:* Report SALF threshold sensitivity for all three target models in the main paper (or appendix), and clarify the selection criterion (e.g., lowest worst-case speedup, or smallest variance across models).

**W7. Related Work is list-style rather than comparative (Minor).** The Related Work section (§5) reads as a chronological enumeration of prior methods without clear comparison axes. A table comparing methods along dimensions such as "training objective," "tree construction," and "draft model architecture" would much more effectively position the paper's contributions.

*Suggested fix:* Reorganize around 2-3 thematic axes with a comparison table, and explicitly state for each category how SALF/TALF differs from the most relevant prior work.

## Score
**Final Score: 7/10**

**Rationale:** The paper presents a well-motivated, technically sound contribution to tree-based speculative decoding. The core ideas—tree-aware training loss and early-stopping for dynamic tree construction—are both novel and practically relevant. The experimental evaluation is broad (3 models, 5 benchmarks, 2 temperatures) and the ablation analysis (Table 2) is exemplary in isolating the contributions of each component. 

The score is moderated by the following considerations (in order of importance):
- **Research value** is high but bounded: the improvements are meaningful (15-39% relative speedup) but are applied within the existing EAGLE framework rather than establishing a new paradigm. The paper refines existing techniques rather than opening a fundamentally new direction.
- **Novelty** is assessed as partially overlapping (see deferred verification note below): TALF extends HASS's feature-misalignment solution to tree structures, and SALF improves upon SpecExec's optimal-search approach with an early-stopping criterion. These are solid incremental contributions with clear differentiation from prior work, though the novelty claim should be verified against complete literature.
- **Validity** is generally strong but has gaps: the fixed-tree training approximation is not validated, variance is not reported for latency measurements, and the asymmetric training protocol creates comparison confounds. These issues are fixable but currently weaken the strength of the empirical claims.
- **Reproducibility** is good: code and hyperparameters are provided in supplementary materials.

**Novelty/comparison conclusions are deferred for manual literature verification** (external paper search was unavailable in this run). The paper's claims of improvement over EAGLE-2 and HASS are empirically validated within the paper's own experiments, but broader positioning against the full landscape of tree-based SpD methods should be independently verified by reviewers with access to the complete literature.
