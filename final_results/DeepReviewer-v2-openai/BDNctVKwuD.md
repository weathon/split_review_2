## Summary
This paper identifies a theoretical limitation of HiResCAM—that its explanations are not uniquely determined because they depend on logits rather than softmax probabilities, allowing an arbitrary shared matrix M to be added to all class-level CAMs without changing the prediction. The authors propose ContrastiveCAMs, which use pairwise differences between HiResCAMs of different classes to produce M-invariant attention maps, and further leverage ContrastiveCAMs to design a modified cross-entropy loss called Core-Focused Cross-Entropy (CFCE) that penalizes model reliance on non-core (background) image regions. Experiments on Hard-ImageNet, Oxford-IIIT Pets, and PASCAL VOC show that CFCE substantially improves the overlap between model attention maps and ground-truth core regions (ContrastiveCAM IoU increasing from ~30% to ~89% on Hard-ImageNet), at a modest cost in raw classification accuracy. The method also transfers to downstream segmentation tasks.

**Novelty assessment (deferred — external literature verification unavailable):** Because external paper search is disabled for this run, I cannot verify the novelty of ContrastiveCAMs or CFCE against the broadest set of prior work. The core ideas—removing softmax redundancy from CAM explanations and using explanation maps as training regularizers—appear technically sound, but their absolute novelty relative to prior saliency-based regularization and contrastive explanation methods should be manually verified by the authors or reviewers.

## Strengths
1. **Theoretically motivated problem identification.** The paper identifies a concrete, formally stated limitation of HiResCAMs—their non-uniqueness due to softmax shift invariance (Theorem 3.2)—and provides a clean solution (ContrastiveCAMs) that removes this redundancy. The mathematical reasoning linking softmax invariance (Proposition 3.1) to CAM-level non-uniqueness (Theorem 3.2) is sound and provides a principled foundation for the proposed method.

2. **Practical alignment improvement.** The empirical results on Hard-ImageNet are striking: CFCE improves ContrastiveCAM IoU from 30.27% (CE with the same architecture) to 89.22% (CFCE), a nearly 60 percentage point gain. Qualitative visualizations (Figure 3) convincingly show that CFCE-trained models suppress non-core attention. These results suggest that the proposed training objective meaningfully changes model behavior toward core-region focus.

3. **Practical use of approximate masks.** The demonstration that SAM-generated masks or bounding boxes can substitute for ground-truth core-region masks (Oxford-IIIT Pets results) significantly increases the practical applicability of the method, since pixel-perfect masks are often unavailable at scale. The finding that CFCE with SAM masks achieves IoU within 3-4 points of ground-truth-mask variants is practically useful.

4. **Transfer to downstream tasks.** The positive transfer of CFCE-trained backbones to semantic segmentation (PASCAL VOC bar chart) shows that the alignment improvement is not merely a metric artifact but translates to better feature representations for dense prediction tasks, strengthening the claim that CFCE improves learned features rather than just overfitting to the mask regularization.

## Weaknesses
### W1. Scope of Theorem 3.2 depends on a single-layer classifier assumption (Major)
The proof that HiResCAMs are not uniquely determined relies on Eq. (3), which relates CAM^{HiRes} to logits specifically for single-layer classifiers of the form h(z) = Wz + b (Eq. 1). The paper does not discuss whether this non-uniqueness holds for deeper classifier heads or architectures with different pooling mechanisms. Since the stated generality of Theorem 3.2 ('HiResCAM explanations corresponding to probability predictions are not uniquely determined') appears to apply to all HiResCAMs, the assumption should be explicitly disclosed in the theorem statement. **Fix:** Add the single-layer classifier assumption directly to Theorem 3.2 and discuss the extent to which the non-uniqueness generalizes.

### W2. CFCE loss design uses |·| function without gradient analysis (Major)
Eq. (15) uses the absolute value |CAM| for non-core regions while using a negative sign for core regions. The absolute value introduces non-differentiability at zero and sign-flip gradient behavior that could cause optimization instability. The paper provides no discussion of how this is handled (subgradient, smoothing, etc.) nor an ablation comparing alternative penalty forms (squared, smoothed absolute, etc.). **Fix:** (a) Provide gradient analysis of the non-core penalty; (b) add an ablation comparing |·| vs (·)² vs smoothed absolute value; (c) discuss practical handling of the non-differentiability.

### W3. KL regularization term uses softmax on a binary mask H, which is methodologically questionable (Major)
Eq. (18) applies softmax to a binary mask H (with values in {0,1}). The resulting σ(λ₂H) is a spatial probability distribution where core locations (H=1) receive equal mass and non-core locations (H=0) receive negligible mass, but the sharpness depends critically on the temperature λ₂. The paper does not discuss how λ₂ is chosen, how sensitive results are to this choice, or why softmax(H) is an appropriate target distribution for matching CAM shape. **Fix:** Either remove the softmax on H and use H directly as the target distribution (with appropriate scaling), replace the KL with a simpler shape-matching loss (e.g., Dice or binary cross-entropy), or provide a sensitivity analysis over λ₂ and λ₃.

### W4. Incomplete baseline reporting in Hard-ImageNet experiments (Major)
In Table 2, the first four baseline rows (Cross-Entropy, CORM, DFR, CORM+DFR) report point estimates without variance, while CE w/ Arch and proposed methods report ± values. ContrastiveCAM IoU is reported only for CE w/ Arch and proposed methods (marked '—' for baselines). Additionally, the architectural modifications ('w/ Arch') are described only in Appendix C, leaving readers of the main text unable to judge their impact. The fact that CE w/ Arch has *lower* GradCAM IoU (16.25) than unmodified CE (18.44) suggests the architecture changes themselves may hurt alignment, which raises questions about whether CFCE is compensating for an architecture-induced degradation. **Fix:** (a) Add variance estimates to all baselines; (b) report ContrastiveCAM IoU for all methods; (c) summarize architectural modifications in the main text; (d) explicitly state the CFCE vs CE w/ Arch delta as the fair comparison.

### W5. PASCAL VOC multilabel results terminology and interpretation issues (Major)
The loss function for multilabel classification is named 'CFBCE' (not CFCE) without a clear definition in the main text. The paper claims 'Pareto improvement' but AP drops from 99.75 (CE) to 97.92 (CFBCE+KL) on training, while validation AP is essentially flat (87.32 CE vs 87.19 CFBCE+KL). The improvement is on IoU, not a joint improvement in AP and IoU. Additionally, the CE baseline has extremely high IoU variance (±16.57%) compared to CFBCE (±0.60%), which is not explained. **Fix:** (a) Define CFBCE explicitly in the main text; (b) replace 'Pareto improvement' language with a precise description of the trade-off; (c) analyze why CE has such high IoU variance.

### W6. Discussion section lacks specific limitations (Minor)
The discussion (Section 6) is generic and does not enumerate concrete limitations of the proposed approach—such as sensitivity to mask quality, computational overhead of ContrastiveCAMs for many classes, or potential over-suppression of useful contextual features. A more specific limitations paragraph would help readers assess when the method can be applied and what its failure modes are. **Fix:** Replace the current discussion with a structured limitations paragraph as suggested in the corresponding annotation.

### W7. Missing statistical rigor for alignment claims (Minor)
While Table 2 reports ± values for proposed methods, there are no statistical significance tests comparing the alignment improvements (IoU, RFS) across methods. Given the dramatic improvements claimed (e.g., +59pp IoU), p-values or confidence intervals would substantially strengthen confidence that the gains are robust and not due to random seed variation.

### W8. Novelty position is unclear (Deferred — manual verification required)
Because external literature search is unavailable in this run, I cannot assess whether ContrastiveCAMs or CFCE overlap with prior work on contrastive explanations, saliency-based regularization, or core-region learning. The paper does not compare against the most closely related interpretability-guided training methods, which would be essential to establishing its novelty boundaries. **Fix:** Authors should explicitly discuss how ContrastiveCAM differs from other contrastive explanation approaches (e.g., XGradCAM, ScoreCAM, or any prior pairwise explanation method) and how CFCE compares to prior work on using saliency maps as regularizers.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** The paper presents a theoretically grounded observation (HiResCAM non-uniqueness) and a practical method (CFCE) that produces impressively large alignment improvements on standard benchmarks. The strengths—sound theoretical motivation, striking IoU gains, practical applicability with approximate masks, and positive downstream transfer—are meaningful. However, the paper has several weaknesses that collectively reduce confidence and impact. The CFCE loss design (absolute value penalty, KL divergence on a softmax-transformed binary mask) raises optimization and methodological concerns that are not adequately addressed. Experimental reporting is incomplete (missing variance for baselines, missing ContrastiveCAM IoU for comparisons, architectural modifications not described in main text). The theoretical claim of non-uniqueness depends on a single-layer classifier assumption that is not explicitly scoped. These issues are fixable with additional analysis, experiments, and rewriting, which makes the paper promising but not yet ready for acceptance at a top venue without substantial revision.

**Key factors influencing this score:**
- **Positive:** Strong empirical alignment improvements (IoU +59pp), theoretically motivated problem, practical mask approximations work.
- **Negative:** CFCE/KL loss design needs better justification and gradient analysis; baseline reporting is incomplete; terminology inconsistency (CFBCE); discussion lacks specific limitations.
- **Deferred:** Novelty relative to prior contrastive explanation and saliency regularization methods cannot be assessed in this run and should be manually verified.
