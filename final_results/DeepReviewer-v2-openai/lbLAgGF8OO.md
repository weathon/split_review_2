## Summary
This paper studies the Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) framework for reinforcement learning in both stochastic and adversarial (hybrid) environments. The authors introduce **Dig-DEC**, a new model-free complexity measure that removes the optimism principle used in prior work [FGQ+23] and drives exploration purely through information gain (KL divergence + estimation error). The key theoretical contributions are threefold: (1) In the stochastic setting, Dig-DEC is always no larger than optimistic DEC and can be arbitrarily smaller in constructed instances; (2) For hybrid MDPs (stochastic transitions, adversarial rewards) with bandit feedback and linear reward features, the paper establishes the first model-free sublinear regret bounds, resolving an open problem from [LWZ25]; (3) The authors refine online function estimation procedures, improving regret bounds from T^{3/4} to T^{3/5} (average estimation error, on-policy) and from T^{5/6} to √T (squared estimation error in Bellman-complete MDPs). The analysis generalizes the Algorithmic Information Ratio (AIR) framework and provides a unified treatment via a Bregman-divergence-based regret analysis that connects to mirror descent.

## Strengths
1. **Theoretically clean complexity measure.** The Dig-DEC formulation is conceptually elegant: it replaces the optimism mechanism with pure information-gain regularization. The decomposition into KL regularization (which enables regret bounding without optimism) and an additional KL information-gain term (which provides strict improvements in some settings) is well-motivated and leads to a clean analysis that connects naturally to mirror descent via Bregman divergences.

2. **Unified framework for stochastic and hybrid settings.** The paper provides a single algorithmic framework (Algorithm 1) that covers both stochastic and adversarial/hybrid MDPs with the same core mechanism. This is a significant generalization over previous work, which required separate designs. The ability to handle bandit feedback in hybrid MDPs without explicit reward estimators is a notable advance.

3. **Improved regret rates.** The paper delivers concrete improvements over prior DEC-based methods: √T for Bellman-complete MDPs (matching optimism-based approaches), and T^{3/5}/T^{7/8} for average estimation error settings. These improvements are substantiated with refined analysis techniques (unbiased split-sample estimator, two-timescale posterior updates) that are likely of independent technical interest.

4. **Resolution of an open problem.** Establishing the first model-free regret bounds for hybrid MDPs with bandit feedback under linear rewards and general transition structures (bilinear classes, coverable MDPs) directly addresses the open question posed in [LWZ25].

5. **Methodological novelty in the analysis.** The Bregman-divergence-based regret analysis departs from the "constructive minimax theorem" approach used in prior AIR frameworks [XZ23, LWZ25], making it more flexible and applicable to a wider range of divergence measures. The analysis framework itself is a solid contribution that may facilitate future developments.

6. **Honest limitation disclosure.** The paper explicitly acknowledges the limitations of its framework: Assumption 3 does not capture all learnable hybrid MDPs (e.g., low-rank MDPs with unknown reward features), and the hybrid setting requires known linear reward features (Assumption 4). This transparency is commendable.

## Weaknesses
1. **Absence of empirical validation (Major).** This is a purely theoretical paper with no experiments, simulations, or even synthetic/illustrative examples (apart from the 3-armed bandit instance referenced in Theorem 14, which is only stated without experimental demonstration). While theoretical RL papers can be acceptable without experiments, the absence of any empirical verification—even a simple numerical illustration of the claimed improvements—reduces the paper's ability to convince readers that the theoretical improvements translate to practical relevance. The regret bounds are expressed in terms of constants (H, d, |Φ|, etc.) that are never instantiated or compared numerically against prior bounds in a concrete MDP instance. **Recommendation:** At minimum, include a simulation study on a simple tabular MDP or linear MDP to illustrate the scaling of the regret bounds and to demonstrate the claimed improvement over optimistic DEC in a concrete setting (e.g., the 3-armed bandit example of Theorem 14).

2. **Computational practicality is not addressed (Major).** Algorithm 1 requires solving a minimax optimization over distributions over infosets Φ and policies Π at each round. The infoset partition size |Φ| can be very large (potentially exponential in the state/action space or feature dimension). The paper does not discuss how this optimization can be implemented efficiently, nor does it provide any computational complexity analysis. The minimax problem in Eq. (3) involves distributions over potentially enormous spaces, which may be intractable. **Recommendation:** Add a section discussing computational tractability—whether the minimax problem can be solved in closed form for the considered MDP classes, or what approximation methods are viable. If the algorithm is primarily of theoretical interest, state this explicitly.

3. **Assumption 3 severely limits applicability (Major).** The paper acknowledges that Assumption 3 (unique reward-to-value mapping given φ) does not capture all learnable hybrid MDPs—specifically, hybrid low-rank MDPs with unknown reward features cause log|Φ| to scale polynomially rather than logarithmically. This limitation means the framework cannot handle the practically important case where the reward feature is unknown, for which prior work [LMWZ24] achieves near-optimal regret. The reliance on known linear reward features (Assumption 4) further restricts applicability. **Recommendation:** Explicitly characterize which hybrid MDP classes remain outside the current framework and discuss potential extensions (e.g., integrating reward-free exploration phases).

4. **High-probability guarantees are incomplete (Moderate).** The paper remarks (end of Section 5.1) that high-probability bounds are achievable in the stochastic setting by replacing a KL term, but states that "this variant cannot handle the hybrid setting." This leaves a significant gap: for hybrid MDPs, only expected regret bounds are provided, and it is unclear whether high-probability bounds are possible under the same framework. **Recommendation:** Either provide high-probability bounds for both settings, or clearly explain why the hybrid setting precludes such guarantees.

5. **Key technical details are deferred to the appendix, making the main text incomplete (Moderate).** The paper repeatedly references Appendices A, B, C, F, H, I, J for the proofs of Dig-DEC bounds, the estimation procedures, and the comparison table. Since the appendix is removed from the provided manuscript, the reader cannot verify the correctness of the claimed bounds or understand the estimator constructions. This is a substantial barrier to evaluation. **Recommendation:** Include more technical sketches in the main body (at least for the key estimation procedure and the Dig-DEC bound derivation), or ensure the appendix is available to reviewers.

6. **Writing clarity issues (Minor).** Several sentences are grammatically awkward or incomplete, which can hinder comprehension in a technically dense paper. For example, on Page 1 line 12: "a parallel line of research seeks to relax the assumption that the environment remains stationary" could be clearer. On Page 2 line 24, the sentence "For simplicity, we $|\Pi|$ is finite" appears to have a grammatical error (missing verb). The paper uses many acronyms (DEC, E2D, AIR, DMSO, Dig-DEC, etc.) without a consistent glossary, making it hard to follow for readers not deeply familiar with the DEC literature. **Recommendation:** Perform a thorough language editing pass, fix grammatical errors, and add a notation/acronym table at the beginning of the paper.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** The paper makes genuinely novel theoretical contributions to the DEC/AIR framework, introducing Dig-DEC as a cleaner, more general complexity measure that unifies stochastic and hybrid settings. The improved regret rates for Bellman-complete MDPs (matching √T) and the first model-free bounds for hybrid MDPs with bandit feedback are solid theoretical advances. However, the score is tempered by several significant concerns: (1) the complete absence of empirical validation means the practical relevance of the theoretical improvements is unverified; (2) the computational tractability of the minimax optimization at the core of Algorithm 1 is not addressed, raising questions about whether the framework is realizable in practice; (3) restrictive assumptions (Assumptions 3, 4) limit the scope of applicability and leave important settings (e.g., unknown reward features) uncovered; (4) the deferred technical content (appendices not included) prevents full verification of the claimed bounds; and (5) novelty comparisons with prior work cannot be independently assessed in this run due to retrieval unavailability. The paper represents meaningful progress within the DEC research program but would benefit substantially from experimental validation and a discussion of computational feasibility to strengthen its impact.
