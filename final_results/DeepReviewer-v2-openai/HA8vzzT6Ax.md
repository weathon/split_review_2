## Summary

This paper addresses the tension between watermark strength and speculative sampling efficiency in LLM inference. The authors make three contributions: (1) a quantitative measure of watermark strength as expected KL divergence between watermarked and original distributions, which governs statistical detectability; (2) a formal characterization of the trade-off as a Pareto frontier constrained optimization problem, with explicit curves for existing watermarking schemes; and (3) an algorithm (Algorithm 1) that replaces the random acceptance coin flip in speculative sampling with a pseudorandom one, making generation a deterministic function of pseudorandom variables. The authors prove that this mechanism achieves maximal watermark strength while preserving speculative sampling efficiency, and empirically demonstrate improved detectability on Llama-68M/7B and Gemma-2B/7B pairs with Gumbel-max and SynthID watermarks.

**Strengths:** The paper is theoretically well-grounded, provides a clean information-theoretic definition of watermark strength, and offers a constructive algorithm with formal guarantees. The trade-off characterization as a Pareto front is a meaningful formalization that moves beyond the prior binary treatment.

**Core weaknesses:** (1) The "plug-and-play" generality claim for the trade-off framework overstates current results; (2) the temperature choice in experiments (0.5/0.7) is a confound that may amplify results; (3) the Ars-τ detector uses a fixed threshold τ that approximates a token-dependent acceptance probability without error analysis; (4) the independence assumption for pseudorandom components in Theorem 4.1 is not discussed in relation to practical PRNG implementation; (5) novelty claims cannot be verified due to retrieval unavailability in this run.

## Strengths
**1. Quantitative watermark strength definition.** Defining watermark strength as $\mathbb{E}_\zeta[D_{KL}(P_\zeta \| P)]$ is a principled and theoretically well-motivated choice. The connection to mutual information under unbiasedness ($I(w;\zeta) = \mathbb{E}_\zeta[D_{KL}(P_\zeta\|P)]$) grounds the definition in information theory, and Theorem 3.1 provides a direct link to detection sample complexity via p-value decay. This continuous measure is a genuine improvement over the binary preserved/lost definition in prior work (Hu & Huang, 2024) and enables the central contribution of the paper.

**2. Pareto frontier formulation.** Casting the trade-off as a constrained optimization problem (Definition 3.2) is a clean formalization that captures the essential tension. The authors show that the frontier can be derived for specific decoder families (linear, Hu's class, Google's class) and provide explicit expressions (Eq. 10). This formulation is conceptually elegant and provides a template for analyzing future watermark-speculative sampling combinations.

**3. Constructive algorithm with formal guarantees.** Algorithm 1 is clearly presented and comes with three strong theoretical properties (unbiasedness, maximal sampling efficiency, maximal watermark strength) in Theorem 4.1. The key insight—replacing the external acceptance coin flip with a pseudorandom variable to make generation a deterministic function of pseudorandomness—is simple yet effective, and well-motivated by the theoretical analysis of why prior approaches fail to achieve both objectives simultaneously.

**4. Empirical validation on realistic model pairs.** The experiments use established model pairs (Llama-68M/7B, Gemma-2B/7B) on a standard QA dataset (ELI5), with both Gumbel-max and SynthID watermarking schemes. The AATPS results confirm that speculative sampling efficiency is preserved, and the ROC-based detectability analysis shows tangible improvements over prior detection methods (Ars-Prior, Bayes-Prior). The inclusion of per-token time and log-perplexity metrics further strengthens the empirical support.

**5. Honest discussion of limitations.** Remark 3.1 correctly distinguishes between watermark strength (ideal detectability with known distributions) and detection efficiency (practical detection with unknown distributions). The conclusion openly acknowledges the scope of the current work (unbiased degenerate watermarks) and identifies concrete directions for extension. This intellectual honesty improves the paper's credibility.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Overclaimed generality of the trade-off framework (major, fixable).** The second contribution statement claims the trade-off formulation "can be applied in a plug-and-play manner to any watermarking schemes." In reality, the analysis focuses on specific decoder families (linear interpolation, Hu's class, Google's class) and requires specifying $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{draft}}$ and $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{target}}$ explicitly. For an arbitrary watermark, deriving the Pareto frontier may be intractable or require non-trivial adaptation. The term "plug-and-play" overstates what is demonstrated and invites justified reviewer skepticism. **Fix:** Replace "plug-and-play" with a more precise description: the formulation provides a template that can be instantiated for any decoder family, with explicit solutions shown for linear, Hu's, and Google's classes. (See annotation on Page 1 — Contribution C2.)

**W2. Temperature confound in experiments (major, fixable).** The paper reports results at temperature 0.5 (Gumbel-max) and 0.7 (SynthID), chosen "to make the results more pronounced." Lower temperatures sharpen probability distributions, which simultaneously increases watermark strength (degenerate watermarks are easier when distributions are already peaked) and increases acceptance rates (draft and target distributions become more similar). This confound means the reported improvements may not generalize to standard temperature 1.0 sampling. The paper does not report temperature 1.0 results, nor does it justify why different temperatures are used for the two watermarking schemes. **Fix:** Add results at temperature 1.0 for both watermarks; use the same temperature for both to enable fair comparison; explicitly discuss temperature as a boundary condition. (See annotation on Page 1 — Experimental Setup.)

**W3. Missing error analysis for Ars-τ (major, fixable).** The Ars-τ detector (Eq. 11) uses a fixed threshold $\tau$ (calibrated on validation data) to decide whether a token was generated by the draft or target model. However, Algorithm 1's actual acceptance condition is $u_t < \min\{1, P_{w'}/Q_{w'}\}$, which depends on the specific token and the two model distributions. The paper does not analyze the approximation error introduced by replacing a token-dependent threshold with a fixed $\tau$, nor does it characterize the conditions under which this approximation is reliable. **Fix:** Add analysis (theoretical or empirical) of the mismatch between $\tau$ and the true threshold $\min\{1, P_w/Q_w\}$. Report sensitivity analysis over $\tau$ values. (See annotation on Page 1 — Section 4.2.)

**W4. Unexplained "repeated context masking" dependence (major, fixable).** Algorithm 1 relies on "repeated context masking" to ensure unbiasedness, but this mechanism is mentioned only in passing without explanation in the main text. A reader cannot assess (a) what triggers context masking, (b) how it interacts with pseudorandom acceptance, or (c) whether Theorem 4.1's unbiasedness proof accounts for masking. If masking is non-trivial, the algorithm's behavior in repeated contexts is opaque. **Fix:** Add 2-3 sentences explaining context masking, and explicitly confirm that the proofs in Appendix D handle the masking case. (See annotation on Page 1 — Algorithm 1 description.)

**W5. Independence assumption without practical guidance (moderate, fixable).** Theorem 4.1 assumes $\zeta^D, \zeta^T, \zeta^R$ are independent, but Algorithm 1 derives all three from a single pseudorandom generator. The paper does not discuss what PRNG properties are needed to approximate independence (e.g., seed length, cryptographic quality) or whether identifiable failure modes exist. **Fix:** Add a note on safe PRNG usage; clarify that independence holds to computational indistinguishability for cryptographic-quality generators. (See annotation on Page 1 — Theorem 4.1.)

**W6. Theorem 3.1 assumptions not verified for example watermarks (moderate, fixable).** The p-value decay result assumes log-likelihood ratios are uniformly bounded and have finite MGFs in a neighborhood of zero. For degenerate watermarks where $P_\zeta$ is a point mass, $Z_t = \log(1/P_w)$ can be unbounded as $P_w \to 0$. The paper does not verify these assumptions for Gumbel-max or SynthID. **Fix:** Add a remark discussing whether the conditions hold, or interpret the result as an asymptotic guideline under regularity conditions. (See annotation on Page 1 — Theorem 3.1.)

**W7. Bayes-MLP improvement source unclear (moderate, fixable).** The SynthID detection improvement (Bayes-MLP vs Bayes-Prior) attributes gains to the extra $u_t$ feature from pseudorandom acceptance, but the MLP architecture is also more expressive than weighted averaging. An ablation comparing MLP with and without $u_t$ is needed to isolate the contribution of pseudorandom acceptance. (See annotation on Page 1 — SynthID detection.)

**W8. AATPS vs wall-clock speedup (minor, fixable).** The paper claims efficiency is "preserved" based on AATPS, but does not explicitly confirm that maintained AATPS translates to maintained wall-clock speedup (accounting for pseudorandom acceptance overhead). The PTT results in the appendix partially address this, but the main text's efficiency claim could be stronger if it explicitly referenced wall-clock results. (See annotation on Page 1 — Experimental results.)

**W9. Novelty verification deferred (unfixable in this run).** Due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode (external paper search unavailable), the novelty and positioning claims in this paper cannot be independently verified against the literature. The paper's claims about overcoming the Hu & Huang (2024) impossibility result appear technically sound based on the presented analysis, but a complete novelty assessment requires external literature comparison that was not possible in this review run. The authors should ensure their related work coverage is complete, particularly for recent watermarking+speculative sampling combinations. **Manual verification is required before publication decisions.**

**W10. Overclaiming scope in abstract and conclusion (minor, fixable).** The abstract claims the paper "uncover[s] a principle that unites speculative sampling and watermarking," which overstates the contribution as a general theory rather than a specific mechanism. The conclusion introduces tree-based methods, biased watermarks, and editing robustness as future directions without grounding them in the paper's analysis. **Fix:** Use more measured language; bound claims to what is demonstrated. (See annotations on Abstract and Conclusion.)

## Score
**Final Score: 6.5/10**

**Rationale:** This paper makes a technically solid contribution to the watermarking-speculative sampling intersection. The quantitative watermark strength definition, the Pareto frontier formulation, and the pseudorandom acceptance mechanism are well-motivated and supported by theoretical analysis. The experiments, while limited in scope (one QA dataset, two model pairs, low-temperature sampling), provide reasonable evidence that the method works as intended.

The score is constrained by the following factors:
- **Research value (primary dimension):** The core insight—making acceptance decisions pseudorandom to restore the token-$\zeta$ determinism—is genuine but incremental relative to the Hu & Huang (2024) impossibility result. The paper resolves a specific limitation (binary watermark strength definition) rather than proposing a fundamentally new approach to watermarking or speculative sampling. The practical value depends on deployment scenarios where both traceability and inference speed are critical, which is a genuine but bounded use case.
- **Novelty:** The quantitative WS definition and the trade-off curve formulation are novel contributions. However, novelty cannot be fully assessed in this run due to retrieval unavailability. The empirical demonstration of improved detectability is a valuable validation but does not by itself constitute a breakthrough.
- **Validity risks:** The most significant concerns are the temperature confound (W2) and the missing error analysis for Ars-τ (W3), both of which are fixable. The theoretical results appear sound under their stated assumptions, though some assumptions need better justification (W5, W6).
- **Reproducibility:** The paper provides code and detailed algorithmic descriptions. The main reproducibility gap is the unexplained "repeated context masking" (W4), which could hide implementation-specific behavior.

**Recommendation:** This paper is suitable for acceptance at a venue that values theoretical analysis of practical deployment challenges (e.g., NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR). The recommended revision priorities are: (1) address the temperature confound, (2) clarify the Ars-τ approximation, (3) explain context masking, and (4) temper the generality claims. With these fixes, the paper's score could rise to the 7-7.5 range.
