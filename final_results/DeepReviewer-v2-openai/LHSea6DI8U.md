# Final Review Report

## Summary

This paper proposes STBP, a framework for continual spatio-temporal forecasting (CSTF) that integrates a general-purpose spatio-temporal backbone with a scalable contextual pattern bank. The backbone uses a Frequency-Domain Network (FreNet) for stable temporal representations and a Dual-Stream Linear Graph Attention (DLGA) module for efficient spatial modeling. The contextual pattern bank is an expandable set of node-specific trainable parameters that capture evolving node-level heterogeneity and relevance. During incremental training, the backbone is frozen after the first period to preserve general knowledge, while the pattern bank expands and adapts to new nodes. The method is evaluated on three real-world streaming datasets (PEMS-Stream, CA-Stream, AIR-Stream) against eight baselines spanning conventional STGNNs and CSTF methods. STBP achieves consistent improvements, with average MAE reductions of 21.44%, 21.93%, and 2.35% respectively. Additional experiments on few-shot forecasting, ablation studies, pattern bank visualization, and efficiency comparison support the core claims.

The paper addresses a relevant and timely problem (continual learning for evolving spatio-temporal graphs) and introduces a thoughtful combination of frequency-domain processing, linear attention, and parameter-expansion-based prompting. The architecture is technically sound in its high-level design, with several novel elements in how the pattern bank interacts with the backbone. However, several aspects require substantive revision: (1) key mathematical formulations (Eq. 5 gating mechanism, Eq. 9 linear attention approximation) have dimensional ambiguities or missing normalization that affect reproducibility; (2) the experimental setup lacks critical details about incremental period construction, baseline adaptation protocols, and compute budget; (3) the FreNet's FFT dimension is not specified, making the core frequency-domain claim untestable; (4) the performance claims are partially overstated without external literature verification (deferred in this run). The contributions are promising but need tightened presentation and evidence.

## Strengths
1. **Well-motivated problem and clear research gap.** The paper clearly articulates the limitations of static STGNNs in evolving urban environments and the weaknesses of existing CSTF backbones. The four challenges enumerated in Section 1 (handling distributional drift, modeling dynamic correlations, alleviating catastrophic forgetting, and designing efficient incremental strategies) provide a useful framework for evaluating CSTF methods. The motivation for a general (node-count-independent, adjacency-free) backbone is well-justified.

2. **Thoughtful architectural design combining frequency-domain stability with linear-attention efficiency.** The FreNet module's goal of extracting stable periodic components via frequency-domain processing is conceptually sound for handling distributional drift. The DLGA module's use of linear attention (O(N) complexity) with a dual-stream structure that incorporates pattern bank embeddings as an additional key is a novel and principled way to integrate prompted knowledge into spatial modeling. The overall design choice of freezing the backbone and updating only the pattern bank is a clean stability-plasticity decomposition.

3. **Comprehensive experimental evaluation against diverse baselines.** The paper evaluates against eight baselines covering two categories (conventional STGNNs and CSTF methods) on three real-world datasets with different domains (traffic and air quality) and sampling rates (5-minute and hourly). The inclusion of few-shot forecasting (Table 2) and the ablation study (Figure 4) strengthen the empirical claims. The t-SNE case study (Figure 6) provides compelling qualitative evidence that the pattern bank autonomously discovers node clusters without explicit supervision.

4. **Practical efficiency considerations.** The efficiency study (Figure 8) directly compares training time, GPU memory, and accuracy across methods. The toy dataset analysis isolating linear vs. quadratic attention complexity helps validate the scalability claims. The paper also releases code (GitHub), which supports reproducibility.

5. **Promising results on traffic datasets.** On PEMS-Stream and CA-Stream, STBP achieves substantial improvements (21.44% and 21.93% MAE reduction over the best baseline). These gains are practically significant for real-time traffic forecasting systems, where even small percentage improvements translate to operational value.

## Weaknesses
**W1 (Major — Mathematical Correctness). DLGA linear attention formulation (Eq. 9) omits the normalization denominator, creating a mathematical error.** The standard linear attention mechanism (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) computes attention as $\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \phi(Q)(\phi(K)^\top V) / (\phi(Q) \sum_j \phi(K_j))$, where the denominator ensures row-wise normalization. Eq. (9) in the paper omits this normalization entirely. As a result, the output scale of $\mathbf{H}_\tau^{s'}$ depends on the node count $N_\tau$, which changes across incremental periods in CSTF. This could cause training instability and evaluation inconsistency as the graph grows. The authors must either add the normalization term or provide an alternative justification for why it can be omitted without affecting optimization dynamics. *See annotation ID 073e2ab6-489e-46d5-b4f4-348c2d03f738 (Page 1 - Section 4.3: DLGA).*

**W2 (Major — Reproducibility). The FreNet's Fast Fourier Transform dimension is not specified, making the core frequency-domain claim unverifiable.** The paper states that input $\mathbf{X}_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times T_h}$ is linearly projected to $\mathbf{H}_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times d}$ and then transformed via FFT. If FFT is applied along the feature dimension $d$, the "frequency components" correspond to feature-space frequencies rather than temporal frequencies, contradicting the paper's claim of extracting "periodicity and trends" (temporal concepts). If FFT is applied along the time dimension, the linear projection $T_h \to d$ must be invertible or the FFT must be applied before projection. The paper must specify exactly which dimension the FFT operates on, clarify how the frequency-domain features correspond to temporal periodicity, and provide the exact tensor operations in the forward pass. *See annotation ID ad6c5ea3-8195-4fba-98f2-5fb258e94da6 (Page 1 - Section 4.3: FreNet).*

**W3 (Major — Reproducibility). The prompt-based gating function (Eq. 5) has dimensional ambiguity between element-wise and matrix multiplication.** The notation "·" in $\mathbf{H}_\tau \cdot (1 + \mathbf{P}_\tau^{(0)})$ and $\mathbf{P}_\tau^{(1)} \cdot h_\theta(\cdot)$ does not distinguish between element-wise and matrix multiplication. Given $\mathbf{P}_\tau^{(1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times d}$ and $h_\theta(\mathbf{H}_\tau \cdot (1 + \mathbf{P}_\tau^{(0)})) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times d}$, using matrix multiplication would produce $\mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times N_\tau}$, which is dimensionally inconsistent with the expected output. If element-wise multiplication is intended, the operations reduce to simple gated linear modulation, which should be explicitly stated. The paper must clarify the operation types and preferred tensor operation ordering. *See annotation ID 68bba95e-5f81-4403-ba51-f137ecb77a6d (Page 1 - Section 4.2: Contextual Pattern Bank).*

**W4 (Major — Experimental Sufficiency). The experimental setup lacks critical reproducibility details: incremental period structures, baseline adaptation protocols, hyperparameter tuning procedures, and compute budget are not reported.** This makes it impossible for other researchers to reproduce the results or assess the fairness of baseline comparisons. Specifically: (a) The number and structure of incremental periods per dataset are not defined — how many periods $\tau$ exist? What triggers a new period? How many nodes arrive at each period? (b) How were baselines adapted for incremental training? "Adapted specifically" is vague. Were hyperparameters retuned per period or fixed globally? (c) Hardware (GPU type, training time, total compute) is not reported, undermining the efficiency claims in Section 5.5. *See annotation ID f3c5705e-918f-4cd5-8763-446d4d5542ba (Page 1 - Section 5.1).*

**W5 (Major — Claim-Evidence Alignment). STBP's advantage on AIR-Stream is marginal (2.35% MAE reduction) and may not be statistically significant, yet the paper claims consistent state-of-the-art performance across all datasets.** The improvement on AIR-Stream (MAE 23.64 vs. baseline 24.21) is within the reported standard deviation range for some baselines. The paper does not report statistical significance tests (paired t-test, bootstrap test) for any comparison. Moreover, the discussion does not analyze why gains are dramatically smaller on AIR-Stream (2.35%) than on traffic datasets (21.4-21.9%). Possible explanations (different sampling rate, weaker periodicity, different domain characteristics) are not explored, leaving the claim of general effectiveness partially unsupported. *See annotation ID d9558339-1662-4ae5-9451-1999bee09cd6 (Page 1 - Section 5.2).*

**W6 (Moderate — Novelty Verification). Novelty claims cannot be verified in this run due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode (external paper_search unavailable).** The paper claims "state-of-the-art" performance and a "novel framework" but without external literature verification, these claims are deferred for manual verification. The authors should independently verify that: (a) the combination of frequency-domain processing and linear graph attention for CSTF is not already present in recent work (e.g., EAC, STRAP, or other 2024-2025 CSTF papers); (b) the "dual-stream" attention (incorporating pattern bank as additional key) is novel compared to existing prompt-based adaptation methods; (c) no prior CSTF method has used a similar frozen-backbone + expandable-embedding architecture. The authors are encouraged to add a detailed related-work comparison table (categorized by strategy type, backbone architecture, and forgetting-mitigation mechanism) to strengthen positioning.

**W7 (Moderate — Ablation Completeness). The ablation study confounds multiple changes in the "w/o Backbone" variant and does not isolate the pattern bank's contribution cleanly.** Removing the backbone and replacing it with CNN+GCN changes both the temporal and spatial modules simultaneously. A cleaner ablation would separately test "w/o FreNet" (replace with temporal CNN, keep DLGA) and "w/o PatternBank" (remove pattern bank entirely, keep backbone frozen with only prediction layer fine-tuned). Additionally, evaluating "w/ Random Pattern" (non-trainable random pattern bank embeddings) would isolate whether the pattern bank's value comes from learned representations or merely from providing additional input dimensions. *See annotation ID c0167eb7-c01d-4bbd-9716-9deb343c8678 (Page 1 - Section 5.3).*

**W8 (Moderate — Presentation). Table 1 contains rendering/parsing artifacts in the extracted text (some columns appear empty; GWNet cells show merged values).** While this may be an extraction artifact, the authors should verify that all columns (especially STRAP, EAC, and STBP values) are correctly rendered in the PDF. The text mentions "STRAP performs notably poorly on CA-Stream" but STRAP's column values are not visible in the extracted table, making cross-referencing difficult. *See annotation ID d9558339-1662-4ae5-9451-1999bee09cd6 (Page 1 - Section 5.2, Table 1).*

**W9 (Minor — Writing Quality). The Related Work section reads as a chronological list rather than being organized by comparison axes.** The CSTF related work paragraph goes method-by-method without grouping them by strategy type (replay vs. pattern-matching vs. prompt-tuning vs. federated). The Spatio-Temporal Forecasting paragraph follows the same chronological pattern. Organizing by design axes (e.g., fixed vs. adaptive adjacency, predefined vs. learnable embeddings) would improve readability and help position STBP's contribution. *See annotation IDs a048b0c1-39f1-4887-8377-fa42be4dd9d0 and 7566a791-8a4c-4268-bce4-299de5536389 (Page 1 - Section 2).*

**W10 (Minor — Limitation Specificity). The conclusion's limitation paragraph is too generic ("single-task setting") and does not reflect the paper's actual observed limitations.** The paper's experiments reveal important limitations that should be explicitly acknowledged: (a) the approach is substantially less effective on hourly air quality data than on high-frequency traffic data; (b) the pattern bank memory grows linearly with node count; (c) the method has only been evaluated within-domain. The "foundational spatio-temporal model" claim in the future work sentence is speculative and not supported by current evidence. *See annotation ID 1721636c-32ad-4a51-95ee-58bace215f4c (Page 1 - Section 6).*

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** The paper addresses an important problem (continual spatio-temporal forecasting) with a thoughtfully designed architecture that combines frequency-domain processing, linear attention, and an expandable pattern bank. The empirical results on traffic datasets are strong, and the qualitative analysis of emergent clustering in the pattern bank is compelling. However, the score is constrained by three factors: (1) **Mathematical incompleteness**: The DLGA attention formulation (Eq. 9) omits the normalization denominator, which is a non-trivial error that could affect training dynamics and reproducibility. The FreNet's FFT dimension is unspecified, making the core technical claim untestable. (2) **Experimental transparency**: The lack of critical reproducibility details (period boundaries, baseline adaptation protocols, hyperparameter tuning, compute budget) prevents full confidence in the results. (3) **Claim-evidence calibration**: The paper claims general state-of-the-art performance, but the gains on AIR-Stream are marginal (2.35%) without statistical significance testing, and the reasons for this discrepancy are not analyzed. Additionally, novelty claims could not be externally verified in this run due to retrieval constraints. The paper has clear strengths (motivation, architectural insight, traffic-domain results) but the above weaknesses are substantive enough that major revisions are required before the contributions can be fully validated.

**Post-Revision Target: [7, 8]/10** — achievable if the mathematical corrections (Eq. 5, Eq. 6, Eq. 9) are made, experimental details are fully documented, additional ablation variants isolate component contributions, and the discussion is calibrated to acknowledge the observed limitations.
