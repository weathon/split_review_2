# Final Review Report

## Summary

This paper introduces and systematically studies a new problem setting in machine unlearning: decoupling the class label from the target concept. While prior class-wise unlearning assumes the target concept to be forgotten exactly matches a training class, the authors identify three mismatch scenarios (target mismatch, model mismatch, data mismatch) where the label domains of forgetting data, model output, and target concept diverge. Through analysis of forgetting dynamics at the representation level, they propose TARF (TARget-aware Forgetting), which combines annealed gradient ascent on the forgetting data with selective gradient descent on hard-to-affect retaining data in a three-phase procedure. Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K demonstrate that TARF consistently achieves the best composite Gap metric across all four forgetting settings, outperforming prior methods like FT, GA, SCRUB, and L1-sparse.

**Novelty-comparison note:** External literature verification was unavailable in this run; novelty and positioning conclusions are deferred for manual verification. The core conceptual novelty — introducing and formalizing label-domain mismatch in class-wise unlearning — appears to be a genuine extension of the existing unlearning taxonomy, but the extent of overlap with prior concept-level unlearning work (e.g., in the bias mitigation or concept erasure literature) requires independent verification.

## Strengths
**S1. Novel problem taxonomy.** The paper identifies and formalizes a meaningful gap in the class-wise unlearning literature: the assumption that the target concept coincides with the class label. By introducing three mismatch settings (target, model, data mismatch), the authors provide a systematic framework for studying a class of real-world unlearning scenarios that prior work has largely overlooked. This conceptual contribution is the paper's strongest aspect and has the potential to influence how future unlearning research defines evaluation protocols.

**S2. Clear theoretical intuition backed by empirical analysis.** The "representation gravity" concept (Theorem 3.2) provides a clean, intuitive explanation for why certain mismatch scenarios cause existing methods to fail. The connection between representation distance in latent space and co-movement of loss during gradient ascent is well-motivated, and the t-SNE visualizations (Figure 3) effectively illustrate the entanglement vs. under-coverage issues across different label domain configurations.

**S3. Comprehensive evaluation across settings.** The experimental evaluation is thorough: four forgetting tasks, two benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10/100) plus ImageNet-1K, multiple backbone architectures (ResNet-18, VGG-16, WideResNet), and eight baseline methods. The fine-grained evaluation on superclass-level unlearning (Table 2) adds a useful layer of analysis that goes beyond aggregate metrics.

**S4. Consistent comparative advantage.** TARF achieves the best or second-best composite Gap in every setting across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. In the most challenging mismatch settings (target mismatch, data mismatch), the improvement over the next best baseline is substantial (e.g., Gap 0.21 vs. 8.86 for GA on CIFAR-100 target mismatch), demonstrating the practical value of the proposed approach.

**S5. Real-world application demonstrations.** The extension to concept removal in Stable Diffusion (Figure 6) and personal information removal in LLaMA-3.2 (Table 5) shows that the mismatch framework and TARF generalize beyond image classification to generative models and LLMs, significantly broadening the paper's impact scope.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Strong practical assumptions limit real-world applicability (Major).** The method assumes that (a) the number of classes belonging to the target concept is known in target mismatch forgetting (Section 2, Preliminaries), and (b) a coarse-to-fine label hierarchy is available for constructing the four mismatch scenarios. In practice, unlearning requests often involve free-form concepts not aligned with any predefined label taxonomy, and the developer would not know how many sub-classes fall under a target super-concept. This assumption creates a circular dependency: evaluating TARF requires knowing the very structure the method is supposed to discover in practical deployment. While the Phase I target identification step can partially address this, the paper does not evaluate performance when the number of affected classes is misspecified.

**W2. The theoretical derivation (Theorem 3.2) has imprecise notation and limited practical predictive power (Major).** The Jacobian term \(\lambda_{\max}(J_\theta(\cdot) x_1)\) is not well-defined: \(J_\theta = \partial h(x)/\partial \theta\) is a third-order tensor, not a matrix, and the product with \(x_1\) is dimensionally ambiguous. The \(O(\eta^2)\) term is uncharacterized, making the bound effectively loose without additional smoothness assumptions on the Hessian. Furthermore, the bound depends on \(\lambda_{\max}(J_\theta)\), which is architecture-dependent and can be arbitrarily large for deep networks — this weakens the theorem's ability to predict forgetting dynamics across different models. The grammatical error "we can the following dynamics" also indicates the derivation may need careful proofreading.

**W3. Systematic retaining accuracy degradation in mismatch settings (Major).** While TARF achieves the best composite Gap, individual metrics reveal a consistent pattern of retaining accuracy (RA) loss. In CIFAR-100 data mismatch, TARF's RA = 95.01 vs. Retrained RA = 98.50 (a 3.5-point drop). TA drops from 80.15 to 78.98. In model mismatch (CIFAR-100), UA drops below the Retrained reference (86.67 vs 88.22), indicating over-forgetting. The composite Gap metric, which averages UA, RA, TA, and MIA equally, can mask these utility costs because MIA differences dominate the average. This trade-off between forgetting efficacy and model utility is not adequately discussed.

**W4. Phase II/III temporal overlap and binary mask staleness in TARF algorithm (Major).** The definition of \(k(t)\) in Eq. (5) causes k(t) to decay to zero at epoch \(T - t_0\), while Phase III (pure retaining) is described as starting at \(t_0\). For typical parameter choices (\(t_0 < T/2\)), these two phases overlap: the forgetting loss is still active when the retaining phase has already begun. The binary mask \(\tau(x,y,t)\) is estimated once at time \(t_1\) and never updated, so if the representation changes during Phase II, the hard-to-affect data selection may become stale. A soft weighting or periodic refresh would improve robustness. The beta estimation procedure ("lowest value of top-10% data with in a descending order") is underspecified.

**W5. Evaluation gaps in experimental methodology (Minor).** (a) Ablation studies (Figure 7) lack error bars and significance testing — each condition appears run once, making it impossible to assess stability. (b) The comparison of k(t) schedules does not control for total forgetting budget (integral of k(t) over time), so the observed advantage of decreasing k may simply reflect lower total forgetting pressure. (c) The MIA metric, which measures membership inference attack success, has a fundamentally different scale (~0-100%) from accuracy-based metrics, and the composite Gap equally weights all four indicators; this design choice needs justification. (d) The ImageNet-1K experiments only unlearn 1-3 classes; forgetting more classes (e.g., 10-50) would better test scalability.

**W6. Missing limitation section in conclusion (Minor).** The conclusion summarizes contributions but does not discuss any limitations of the proposed approach. For a paper introducing new problem settings, this omission is notable. Key limitations to acknowledge include: the reliance on known label hierarchies, the RA utility cost in mismatch settings, the sensitivity to hyperparameters (k, t₀, t₁, β), and the restriction to vision classification benchmarks for main results.

**W7. Storyline and writing polish issues (Minor).** (a) The Introduction paragraph 1 reads as a reference dump rather than an engaging problem statement. (b) Abstract claims a "general framework" but the method makes several specific assumptions. (c) The term "under-entangled" in Section 3.1 is confusing and should be replaced with more precise terminology. (d) Multiple minor grammatical issues (e.g., "we can the following dynamics", "we also constructing") should be corrected.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

*Scoring rationale:* The paper presents a well-motivated conceptual contribution — identifying and formalizing label-domain mismatch in class-wise unlearning — which constitutes meaningful research value and has the potential to influence future evaluation protocols in machine unlearning. The empirical evaluation is comprehensive across four settings, two benchmarks, and multiple architectures. The proposed TARF consistently outperforms baselines in composite metrics.

However, the score is constrained by: (1) strong practical assumptions (known class count, predefined label hierarchy) that limit real-world applicability; (2) imprecise theoretical derivation that weakens the claimed formal contribution; (3) systematic retaining accuracy degradation in mismatch settings that is under-analyzed; (4) algorithmic underspecification (phase overlap, stale binary mask) affecting reproducibility; and (5) limited evaluation scope for multi-class unlearning and non-vision domains. External novelty verification could not be conducted in this run; the conceptual contribution's full positioning relative to concept-erasure and bias-mitigation literature requires manual validation.
