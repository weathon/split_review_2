## Summary
This paper addresses the problem of distributional equivalence in linear non-Gaussian (LiNG) causal models with latent variables and cycles. The authors' central claim is that they provide the first general equivalence characterization for latent-variable causal models without structural assumptions in any parametric setting. They introduce edge rank constraints as a new graphical tool, develop a graphical criterion (Theorem 2) and a transformational characterization (Theorem 3) for equivalence, and propose glvLiNG, an algorithm to recover the equivalence class from data.

The theoretical contributions are substantial: (1) a formal definition of distributional equivalence for latent-variable LiNG models, (2) an irreducibility condition to eliminate trivial unidentifiability, (3) edge rank constraints with a duality theorem connecting path ranks and edge ranks, (4) a localized graphical criterion for equivalence based on "children bases," and (5) a complete transformational characterization via admissible cycle reversals and edge additions/deletions.

However, the paper has significant weaknesses that limit its current impact. The glvLiNG algorithm relies on oracle OICA, whose practical instability is acknowledged but not adequately mitigated. The empirical evaluation in the main text is extremely sparse — all critical metrics are deferred to the appendix, and no concrete numbers (F1, SHD, confidence intervals) appear in the main body. The paper lacks a dedicated Related Work section, making its strong novelty claims difficult to verify. The conclusion is unusually brief and discusses limitations too narrowly. Additionally, external literature verification was unavailable in this run, so all novelty/comparison conclusions are deferred to manual verification.

## Strengths
**1. Theoretically clean equivalence characterization.** The paper establishes the first general distributional equivalence theory for latent-variable linear non-Gaussian causal models without structural assumptions on graph topology. Theorem 2 provides a localized graphical criterion (children bases) that reduces exponentially many rank checks to singleton checks, and Theorem 3 gives a complete transformational characterization analogous to the Meek conjecture. These results are mathematically elegant and fill a recognized gap in the causal discovery literature.

**2. Edge rank constraints as a new tool.** The introduction of edge ranks (operating on local edge structure) with a duality theorem (Theorem 1) connecting them to classical path ranks is a genuine methodological contribution. Edge ranks complement the well-studied path rank perspective, and the paper correctly notes that this duality has long been known in matroid theory but underappreciated in causal discovery. The matching rank formulation (Definition 6) provides an algebraic handle that is easier to compute than path-based constraints.

**3. Clear problem framing and motivation.** The paper does an excellent job of motivating why equivalence characterization is a prerequisite for structural-assumption-free methods. The analogy with the historical development of PC/CPDAG and FCI/MAG is effective. The catalog of structural assumptions that existing methods require (pure measurement models, hierarchical constraints, pure children counts, acyclicity) is comprehensive and well-supported by citations.

**4. Clean handling of cycles.** Lemma 6 (admissible cycle reversals) combined with the observation that at most one cycle reversal is needed shows that cycles do not introduce additional complexity in the linear non-Gaussian setting, which is a non-obvious and valuable insight.

**5. Reproducibility infrastructure.** The provision of code and an interactive demo at https://equiv.cc is commendable and supports the paper's reproducibility goals.

## Weaknesses
**W1. OICA dependency undermines practical applicability (Major/Fixable).** The glvLiNG algorithm assumes oracle access to over-complete ICA (OICA) for mixing matrix estimation. The paper correctly acknowledges this limitation in the "Final remarks" paragraph, noting that "OICA's known inefficiency in practice" is a concern and that the algorithm "serves more as a proof of concept." However, this admission sits in tension with the paper's strong contribution claim of being "the first structural-assumption-free discovery method." The disconnect between theoretical guarantee (under oracle OICA) and practical utility is a significant weakness that the paper does not adequately bridge. While the authors suggest future integration with OICA-free methods, they provide no concrete evidence that such integration is feasible or what the performance loss might look like.

*Evidence:* Page 1 - Section 5 (Algorithm), "Final remarks" paragraph.  
*Impact:* The core algorithmic contribution's practical value is uncertain. This directly affects the paper's claim to be a "discovery method" rather than just a theoretical characterization.  
*Repair Path:* (a) Add a quantitative analysis of glvLiNG's sensitivity to OICA estimation errors in the main text (currently all in appendix). (b) Explicitly discuss what OICA-free alternatives exist and how they could replace Step 1. (c) Reduce the strength of the "first discovery method" claim, or add a caveat about the oracle assumption in the contribution list itself.

**W2. Evaluation section lacks statistical rigor and transparency (Major/Fixable).** The main-text evaluation (Section 5) reports qualitative summaries without a single concrete performance number. All five evaluation aspects are described in prose without any quantitative metrics, confidence intervals, or statistical tests in the main body. While page limits may force some details to the appendix, a responsible evaluation section must include at least the key summary metrics (F1, SHD, precision/recall) and sample sizes to support the main claims. The claim that baselines "misidentify over half of the edges" (Evaluation Aspect 3) and that glvLiNG "stays more robust to latent dimensionality" (Aspect 4) are too vague to evaluate without corresponding numbers and standard deviations.

*Evidence:* Page 1 - Section 5, "Evaluation" sub-paragraphs (lines 164-169).  
*Impact:* Readers cannot assess the empirical strength of the proposed method or reproduce the findings from the main text alone. The paper's key performance claims are unverifiable from the presented evidence.  
*Repair Path:* Add a compact results table in the main text with at least: (a) F1 scores for glvLiNG vs. baselines on synthetic data across at least 3 settings, (b) runtime comparisons with error bars, (c) a brief statement of the real-data validation metric (e.g., stability across subsamples, edge recovery rate). Move some low-priority background material to appendix to free space.

**W3. Missing dedicated Related Work section (Major/Fixable).** For a paper whose central claim is "first equivalence characterization with latent variables in any parametric setting," the absence of a dedicated Related Work section is a significant structural omission. The closest prior results (Lacerda et al. 2008; Ghassami et al. 2020 for cycles-only; Adams et al. 2021 for acyclic latent-variable identifiability; the broader equivalence literature including Nowzohour et al. 2017 and Evans 2018) are scattered across the Introduction rather than being systematically compared. This makes it unnecessarily difficult for readers to verify the novelty claim and understand precisely how this work extends beyond prior results.

*Evidence:* The paper jumps from Introduction directly to Problem Setup (Section 2) without any dedicated Related Work section.  
*Impact:* Reviewers may question whether the "first" claim is justified without a dedicated comparison to prior work. This structural gap weakens the paper's positioning.  
*Repair Path:* Add a Related Work section between the Introduction and Problem Setup (or at the start of Section 2) with three focused paragraphs: (1) Distributional vs. Markov equivalence in causal discovery, (2) Linear non-Gaussian methods taxonomy, (3) Identifiability vs. equivalence — explicitly contrasting Adams et al. (2021) identifiability conditions with this paper's equivalence characterization.

**W4. Conclusion is too brief and limitations are too narrowly scoped (Major/Fixable).** The conclusion (Section 6) is only 4 lines long. It acknowledges one limitation (OICA reliance) and one future direction (extending to linear Gaussian systems). It does not recap the paper's theoretical contributions, discuss boundary conditions of the results (e.g., faithfulness violations in cyclic models, near-Gaussian noise), or provide a structured research outlook. A conclusion this thin weakens the paper's closure and leaves the impression that the authors have not fully considered the limitations of their approach.

*Evidence:* Page 1 - Section 6 (lines 172-174).  
*Impact:* The paper's overall completeness is compromised. Readers cannot easily extract a concise summary of what was achieved and what remains open.  
*Repair Path:* Restructure the conclusion into three parts: (1) validated theoretical findings (what was proven and under which assumptions), (2) bounded limitations (OICA reliance, linearity, non-Gaussianity, faithfulness, cycle-handling scope), (3) prioritized future work (OICA-free algorithms first, then extension to linear Gaussian, then nonlinear).

**W5. Informal and over-claiming language in key statements (Minor/Fixable).** Contribution 3 states "Results are cleaner than expected," which is subjective and not standard scientific language. The abstract's phrasing "fills a missing piece in the toolbox" is promotional. Additionally, the "to our knowledge" qualifier on novelty claims, while appropriate, is not backed by a related-work section that would allow verification. The paper's tone sometimes shifts between confident technical exposition and defensive hedging ("serves more as a proof of concept"), creating an uneven authorial voice.

*Evidence:* Page 1 - Contribution 3 (line 24), Abstract (line 8), Final remarks (line 170).  
*Impact:* Such language can reduce reviewer confidence in the objectivity of the claims. It may also invite aggressive scrutiny of the novelty claims from reviewers skeptical of "first" language.  
*Repair Path:* Replace "cleaner than expected" with objective phrasing (e.g., "admits a concise characterization"). Remove "fills a missing piece" from the abstract. Add a dedicated Related Work section (see W3) to provide evidence for the novelty claims.

**W6. Over-complete ICA is assumed but not discussed in depth (Minor/Fixable).** The paper invokes OICA's identifiability results (Eriksson & Koivunen, 2004) for the irreducibility condition (Proposition 1) and for the algorithm's mixing matrix estimation step. However, over-complete ICA (where sources outnumber observed variables) is a known difficult problem with practical instability issues, especially when the number of sources is unknown. The paper does not discuss how the number of latent variables is determined from data, nor does it address the sensitivity of the overall pipeline to misspecification of the latent dimensionality.

*Evidence:* Page 1 - Section 2.2 (OICA reference in Proposition 1 discussion), Section 5 (oracle OICA assumption).  
*Impact:* These practical challenges are glossed over, creating a gap between the theoretical framework and its deployment.  
*Repair Path:* Add a brief discussion in Section 5 or the appendix about how the number of latent variables is estimated in practice, including failure modes and diagnostic checks.

**Novelty Note (Deferred):** Because external literature retrieval is unavailable in this run, all novelty and comparison conclusions are explicitly deferred to manual verification. The "first" claims (first equivalence characterization, first structural-assumption-free method) should be verified against the relevant literature before final publication.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Rationale:** This score reflects the paper's strong theoretical contributions (the first general equivalence characterization for latent-variable LiNG models, the edge rank tool, and the complete transformational characterization) weighed against significant empirical and structural weaknesses. The theoretical work is mathematically solid and fills a recognized gap in the causal discovery literature. However, the practical impact is substantially limited by: (1) the oracle OICA assumption that is acknowledged but not mitigated, (2) an evaluation section that lacks concrete metrics and statistical rigor in the main text, (3) the absence of a dedicated Related Work section that would allow verification of the strong novelty claims, and (4) an overly brief conclusion that discusses limitations too narrowly.

The primary scoring dimensions are research value and novelty. The research value is moderate-high for the theoretical characterization, but low for the algorithmic contribution given the practical feasibility concerns. Novelty cannot be fully assessed without external literature verification (deferred), but the theoretical framing appears novel within the stated scope. The score is not inflated by potential future extensions; it reflects the manuscript as currently presented.
