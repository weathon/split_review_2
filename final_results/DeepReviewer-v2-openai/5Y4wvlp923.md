## Summary
This paper addresses the problem of semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker but semantically important components (e.g., seasonality). The authors propose the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) — a directional metric that quantifies the linear recoverability of individual components from composite embeddings — and an asymmetric perceptual weighting mechanism that dynamically reweights view-specific contrastive losses in the CoST framework based on SDE-derived imbalance.

The core technical novelty is the SDE-based dynamic weighting: when SDE reveals that one component is underrepresented, the corresponding contrastive loss is upweighted to encourage balanced representations. The method is designed as a pluggable module for existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes.

**Critical assessment:** While the diagnosis of semantic imbalance is sound and the SDE metric provides a useful diagnostic tool, the paper has several material weaknesses. The primary claim of "consistently improved forecasting accuracy" is contradicted by the reported experimental results (Table 3): CoST+APW underperforms vanilla CoST on long horizons and on Electricity/Weather datasets, often substantially. The asymmetric weighting formula can produce negative loss weights under realistic Δ values, which would invert optimization. Several hyperparameters critical for reproducibility are not reported. The framing overstates novelty by presenting limitations that existing work (CoST, Barlow Twins, VICReg) already partially addresses. The experimental evaluation lacks variance reporting and selectively highlights favorable results while omitting failure cases from the narrative.

**Novelty verdict (deferred):** External literature verification was not available in this run. The core idea — detecting and adaptively reweighting contrastive losses based on a semantic imbalance metric — appears to have some novelty, but the extent of overlap with existing adaptive weighting methods for contrastive learning cannot be fully assessed without targeted literature retrieval.

## Strengths
1. **Well-motivated problem.** The semantic imbalance problem in time-series contrastive learning — where trend dominates seasonality in learned embeddings — is clearly identified and empirically demonstrated using synthetic signals in Table 1. The SDE metric cleanly quantifies this imbalance and the asymmetry factor Δ provides an intuitive scalar measure.

2. **Pluggable design.** The asymmetric weighting mechanism is designed as a lightweight addition to existing frameworks (CoST) without requiring architectural changes. This plug-and-play property, if validated, could make the method easy to adopt.

3. **Honest negative result on direct SDE regularization.** Section 4.3 reports that adding SDE as a direct regularizer to TS2Vec does not improve performance. While the interpretation of this result needs refinement (as discussed in Annotation 9), reporting a negative finding is scientifically valuable and distinguishes the paper from works that only report positive results.

4. **Synthetic analysis is informative.** Table 1 provides a clean demonstration that TS2Vec embeddings exhibit semantic imbalance across a wide range of amplitude ratios (r = 0.1 to 10), and the asymmetry factor Δ correctly tracks which component is underrepresented. This diagnostic analysis is a useful contribution independent of the proposed mitigation.

5. **Clear identification of three limitations.** The paper explicitly lists three limitations of current contrastive approaches (no decomposition bias, time-domain-only objectives, isotropic collapse), providing a structured framework for discussing the problem. This structuring helps readers understand the design space even if the claimed novelty is overstated.

## Weaknesses
Listed in descending order of severity:

### Critical

**1. Central claim of "consistent gains" is contradicted by reported data.** The Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion state that the proposed CoST+APW achieves "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and "superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines." However, Table 3 shows that CoST+APW underperforms vanilla CoST on multiple horizons and datasets: ETT1 horizon 720 (0.152 vs 0.148), ETT1 horizon 48 (0.07 vs 0.06), Electricity horizon 168+ (significant degradation), and Weather horizon 336+ (0.266 vs 0.256). On Electricity, CoST+APW at horizon 720 has MSE 1.010 — substantially worse than CoST (0.911), TS2Vec (0.863), and TNC (0.859). TS2Vec and TNC also outperform CoST+APW on most Weather horizons. The paper selectively highlights the favorable ETT results while omitting these failures from the narrative, which constitutes selective reporting and undermines the paper's objectivity. (Page 1 — Abstract; Page 6 — Table 3; Page 6 — Section 4.4.4 narrative)

*Remediation:* Rewrite all claim statements to reflect the actual mixed results. Add statistical tests (multi-seed variance) to establish significance of claimed improvements. Include explicit discussion of when the method fails and why.

**2. Loss weighting formula can produce negative weights, inverting optimization.** The asymmetric weighting L = (1 + γ·Δ)L_season + (1 + γ'·(-Δ))L_trend can become negative when |Δ| > 1/γ or |Δ| > 1/γ'. Table 1 shows Δ values as extreme as -1.34, which with γ=1 would give a weight of -0.34 — a negative coefficient that would invert the gradient for that contrastive objective. No clipping, positivity constraint, or discussion of this issue is provided. (Page 2 — Section 3.4)

*Remediation:* Add clipping (max(ε, 1+γ·Δ)) or reparameterize with exponential weights (exp(γ·Δ)). Report the range of Δ values observed during training and specify the safeguard used.

### Major

**3. Missing hyperparameters and reproducibility details.** The paper omits: γ and γ' values, λ for SDE regularization, MLP architecture for g_φ (layers, hidden dimensions), frequency of Δ computation (batch-level or epoch-level), input sequence length, embedding dimensions d_T, d_S, d_C. Without these, the method cannot be reproduced. (Page 3 — Section 4.1 Implementation)

*Remediation:* Add a complete hyperparameter table. Provide official anonymous code repository for verification.

**4. SDE regularization experiment has flawed setup.** Section 4.3 adds SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec, finds no improvement, and concludes SDE "fails to provide constructive optimization gradients." However, SDE requires embeddings of a-only and b-only signals through an encoder (TS2Vec) that was never trained on such inputs. The negative result likely reflects architecture mismatch rather than SDE's inadequacy as a regularizer. This conflates the diagnostic with the architecture incompatibility. (Page 4 — Section 4.3)

*Remediation:* Reframe the experiment as an ablation of encoder architecture compatibility, not as a failure of SDE as a regularizer. Test SDE regularization on a decomposition-aware encoder (CoST) for a fair comparison.

**5. MLP composite embedding creates circular dependency.** The MLP g_φ produces v(a+b) that is used to compute SDE and Δ, which then weights the losses that train g_φ. This creates a feedback loop where g_φ could learn to collapse the composite toward one component to minimize SDE artificially, nullifying the weighting signal. No regularization or analysis addresses this. (Page 5 — Sections 4.4.2-4.4.3)

*Remediation:* Add diversity regularization to prevent g_φ from collapsing, or decouple the Δ computation from the MLP by using a fixed additive composite (v(a+b) = v(a) + v(b)). Report correlation of Δ values with vs. without the MLP.

**6. Overstated novelty relative to existing work.** The paper frames three limitations of "current contrastive approaches" as unsolved, but Related Work 2.2-2.4 shows that CoST already addresses limitation 2 (frequency-domain alignment) and Barlow Twins/VICReg already address limitation 3 (embedding collapse via regularizers). The residual gap — dynamic weighting based on a component-level imbalance metric — is narrower than the paper's framing suggests. (Page 1 — Introduction; Page 1 — Related Work)

*Remediation:* Reframe contributions precisely: the novelty is the SDE-based diagnostic and its use for dynamic weighting, not the identification of frequency-awareness or collapse as problems. Clearly position CoST and prior regularizers as partial solutions.

**7. Conclusion contains unsupported claims and untested speculation.** The conclusion says "consistently lower SDE values and improved forecasting accuracy across multiple benchmarks" — contradicted by the paper's own data. The future directions speculate about low-pass-only decomposition and MLP analysis without any supporting experiments. (Page 6 — Section 5)

*Remediation:* Accurate conclusion summarizing validated findings, bounded limitations, and clearly stated open questions (not implied next steps from positive results).

**8. Name inconsistency for the core metric.** The same metric is called "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3). This is confusing for a metric central to the paper's contribution. (Throughout manuscript)

*Remediation:* Choose one name (e.g., "Semantic Disentanglement Error") and use it consistently from title through references.

### Minor

**9. Missing variance/statistics.** All results are reported as point estimates without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given the small and inconsistent margins (many cases where CoST+APW is within 0.01-0.02 MSE of baselines), variance reporting is essential to assess reliability. (Page 6 — Table 3)

**10. SDE interpretation is incomplete.** Values > 1.0 (negative cosine, indicating active suppression) appear in Table 1 but the paper does not discuss this geometric meaning. SDE ∈ [0,2] range is not explained. (Page 2 — Section 3.2)

### Novelty (Deferred)

External literature verification could not be performed in this run. The SDE-based adaptive weighting mechanism appears to have some novelty, but a definitive assessment requires comparison against the adaptive weighting literature in contrastive learning (e.g., automated loss balancing, uncertainty weighting, gradient surgery methods). This judgment is deferred for manual verification.

## Score
**Final Score: 4/10**

**Rationale:** The score prioritizes research value, novelty, and empirical validity as primary dimensions. The paper identifies a genuine problem (semantic imbalance in time-series contrastive learning) and proposes a sensible diagnostic metric (SDE). However, the empirical evaluation does not support the central claim of consistent improvement — the proposed method frequently underperforms baseline CoST on longer horizons and key datasets. The loss weighting formula contains a mathematically unaddressed flaw (potential negative weights). Missing hyperparameters prevent reproducibility. The novelty is partially overlapping with existing work (CoST for frequency-awareness, Barlow Twins/VICReg for collapse prevention), and the distinctive contribution — SDE-based dynamic weighting — is not convincingly shown to be effective given the contradictory results. The paper would benefit from a major revision focusing on honest reporting of mixed results, addressing the negative-weight issue, adding variance statistics, and tightening the novelty claims to match what is actually demonstrated.

**Scoring breakdown:**
- Research value (problem importance): 7/10 — semantic imbalance is a relevant problem
- Novelty: 3/10 — SDE diagnostic is somewhat novel but building heavily on CoST; core claims partially overlapped
- Empirical validity: 2/10 — central claim contradicted by own data; no variance; selective reporting
- Reproducibility: 2/10 — critical hyperparameters missing; no code provided
- Presentation clarity: 5/10 — well-structured but name inconsistency and overclaiming hurt credibility

**Post-Revision Target: 6/10** achievable if the contradictory results are honestly discussed, the negative-weight issue is fixed, hyperparameters are reported, and statistical significance is established with variance.
