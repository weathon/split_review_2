## Summary

CaTS-Bench is a large-scale, multimodal benchmark for time series captioning (TSC) built from 11 real-world datasets across 7 domains. It provides 20k samples (465k training / 105k test timestamps), each pairing numeric series with metadata, line-plot images, and captions generated by a Gemini 2.0 Flash oracle and validated through factual checks, human indistinguishability studies, and diversity analyses. The benchmark additionally includes 460 Q&A questions across five reasoning tasks. The paper evaluates leading VLMs in zero-shot and finetuned settings, finding that finetuning significantly improves open-source models and — most strikingly — that VLMs largely fail to leverage visual inputs, with plot matching emerging as the most difficult task.

## Strengths

1. **Scale and diversity of real-world data.** CaTS-Bench draws from 11 source datasets across 7 domains (health, climate, border crossing, safety, agriculture, sales, demography), yielding 20k triplet samples from 570k time steps. This substantially exceeds prior TSC benchmarks in breadth — TRUCE covers 2 domains with 34k timesteps; TADACap and TACO are more restricted in modality or realism.

2. **Multimodal design is genuinely novel for TSC.** As Table 1 shows, every prior TSC benchmark is missing at least one modality (numeric-only, visual-only, no metadata, no Q&A). CaTS-Bench is the first to pair numeric series + rich metadata + line-plot images + expressive captions + Q&A tasks. This design is what enables the paper's most impactful finding (Section 4.3: VLMs fail to leverage visual inputs), which would be invisible in unimodal benchmarks.

3. **Quality validation is thorough and honestly reported.** The paper validates LLM-generated captions through (a) manual factual checking of ~2.9k captions (98.6% accuracy), (b) a 35-participant human detectability study (41.1% — essentially random), (c) diversity/bias analyses across nine embedding models, and (d) a paraphrasing robustness check (mean Spearman 0.9266 across stylistically varied ground truths). This is the right level of rigor for a benchmark whose ground truth is semi-synthetic.

4. **The visual modality ablation is the paper's most impactful result.** Finding that most VLMs perform no better (and often worse) when given a plot versus text-only input, and that attention maps show models fixating on axis labels rather than line trends (Section 4.3), is a clear and actionable diagnosis that justifies the benchmark's existence.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Ground truth circularity from the single-oracle design.** The oracle (Gemini 2.0 Flash) generates all semi-synthetic (SS) reference captions. Gemini 2.0 Flash is also evaluated as a model in Table 3 and scores competitively (0.688 DeBERTa on SS). The concern is that the SS split measures stylistic similarity to a single LLM at least as much as it measures time series understanding. The paper's mitigations — a human-revisited (HR) subset of 579 captions (14.5% of the test set, covering 4 of 11 domains) and a paraphrasing robustness check (Spearman 0.9266) — are constructive but incomplete: the paraphrasing check tests sensitivity to *style* variation, not to the *content* biases of the oracle. A model that describes time series differently from Gemini 2.0 Flash but equally correctly would be penalized on SS. The rankings on the SS split are plausibly reliable for broad comparisons but should not be treated as definitive.

2. **Q&A filtering protocol and small test sizes.** The paper generates 4k questions per type, then discards any that Qwen 2.5 Omni answers correctly (Section 3.4, lines 144–150), leaving ~7k "challenging" questions from which 460 are sampled. Filtering by a single model risks selection bias: questions that are easy for all other models but Qwen happens to answer correctly are discarded; questions that all models get wrong but have ambiguous answers may be retained. The final test sizes are small — 100 per task for matching tasks and 40 per task for comparison tasks. With 40 questions, the 95% confidence interval around a model's accuracy is approximately ±15.5 percentage points, making it difficult to draw reliable distinctions between models. The filtering methodology and statistical uncertainty should be addressed explicitly.

### Minor

3. **Metric weights without sensitivity analysis.** The Numeric Score (Section 3.5) uses λ_A = 0.3, λ_R = 0.7, justified by the claim that "omitting critical numbers is more severe than minor numeric rounding imprecisions." No analysis is provided for how rankings change under different weightings. A sensitivity analysis over λ_A ∈ [0,1] would strengthen or bound the claim. (This is partially mitigated by the paper's use of multiple other metrics — DeBERTa, BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR, SimCSE, Statistical Inference — so the Numeric Score is not the sole ranking determinant.)

4. **Undiscussed risk of pretraining contamination.** The temporal partitioning strategy (Section 3.1, line 90) prevents leakage within the benchmark but does not address the risk that public datasets (COVID-19 data from ECDC, Walmart sales, Air Quality data) may have appeared in VLM pretraining corpora. If so, zero-shot results may partly reflect memorization rather than reasoning from provided inputs. The paper does not discuss or attempt to measure this.

5. **Table 2 balancing mechanism unclear.** Air Quality contributes 286M of 287M source timesteps (≈99.7%) but only 4.4k of 20k samples (≈22%), indicating a cap was applied. The main text states sampling "depends on its total time steps, ensuring fair representation" (line 67) without explaining the balancing mechanism, which is deferred to Appendix C.

### Trivial

6. **Missing entry in Figure 4.** The Numeric column for Phi-4 M.I. is blank (line 270). This should be explained (data availability issue or typesetting error).

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Demonstrate SS–HR ranking consistency on shared samples.** The most impactful single improvement would be to show that model rankings on the human-revisited subset correlate highly with rankings on the semi-synthetic subset for the same 579 samples (Spearman > 0.9), directly addressing the circularity concern.
- **Add confidence intervals or bootstrap estimates** to the Q&A results, especially for the 40-question sub-tasks.
- **Conduct a contamination analysis** checking whether model performance on specific datasets correlates with those datasets' prevalence in common pretraining corpora.
- **Reframe the "scalable captioning pipeline"** (Contribution 1) as a practical design choice rather than a standalone algorithmic contribution. The pipeline mechanism itself is straightforward prompt engineering; the genuine novelty lies in the benchmark, validation, and diagnostic findings.

## Removed Points
These points were raised in the input review but removed during consolidation. Treat with caution:

- **Abstract "timestamps" phrasing**: The numbers (465k training, 105k test timestamps) are numerically consistent with Table 2 (16k × 29.1 ≈ 465k; 4k × 26.1 ≈ 104k). This is a clarity nitpick, not a substantive issue.
- **Oracle receives statistics that models must infer**: The paper's evaluation metrics explicitly handle this — Statistical Inference Accuracy "does not penalize omission" (line 172). The design is transparent and the metrics are tailored for this asymmetry.
- **External knowledge enforcement not verified**: The manual validation (98.6% factual accuracy across statistical and trend claims) inherently checks that captions are data-grounded. This partially addresses the concern.
- **Human detectability study size**: 35 participants is within the normal range for this type of study; the finding (41.1%, effectively random) is clear.
- **PAL model categorization**: The paper already discusses PAL's code-execution capability separately (line 211, Table 4 text) and does not treat it as equivalent to other VLMs in the analysis.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviewer's primary insight is that the visual modality ablation (Section 4.3) is the paper's strongest and most actionable finding — a point the paper itself makes clearly. The ground-truth circularity concern is a standard and well-recognized issue in LLM-as-oracle benchmarks; the paper's mitigations (human-revisited subset, paraphrasing robustness check) are reasonable but incomplete.

## Suggestions

1. Report ranking consistency (Spearman correlation) between SS and HR captions on the 579 overlapping test samples.
2. Add confidence intervals to all Q&A results, especially for sub-tasks with 40 questions.
3. Include a brief discussion of pretraining contamination risk and, if feasible, a simple diagnostic (e.g., checking whether performance correlates with dataset popularity in common corpora).
4. Clarify the sampling/balancing mechanism for Table 2 in the main text.
5. Fill or explain the blank cell for Phi-4 M.I. in Figure 4.

## Score and Decision

This is a solid, well-executed benchmark paper that fills a genuine gap. The scale and multimodality of CaTS-Bench are clear advances over prior TSC benchmarks, and the quality validation is more thorough than is typical for dataset papers. The visual modality ablation yields a genuinely useful and actionable finding about VLM limitations. The main concerns — ground-truth circularity and Q&A methodology — are real but bounded; they do not invalidate the contribution because the human-revisited subset and multiple robustness checks provide converging evidence, and the Q&A suite is secondary to the core captioning benchmark. The paper should be accepted with minor revisions.
