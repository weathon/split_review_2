## Summary

The paper identifies a genuine problem in contrastive time-series representation learning — when semantic components (trend, seasonality) differ in amplitude, dominant components suppress weaker ones in the learned embedding. It introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a diagnostic metric to measure this imbalance, and proposes CoST+APW, an asymmetric weighting mechanism added to the CoST framework that dynamically reweights trend and seasonal contrastive losses based on an SDE-derived asymmetry factor Δ. The paper includes a synthetic experiment demonstrating the imbalance in TS2Vec and forecasting evaluations on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets.

## Strengths

1. **The SDE diagnostic metric is well-motivated and interpretable.** The idea of using vector arithmetic in embedding space — measuring whether v(a) can be linearly recovered as v(a+b) − v(b) — provides a clean, principled way to quantify which semantic components a representation preserves. This is the paper's strongest conceptual contribution, and it stands independently of the proposed mitigation method.

2. **The synthetic experiment in Table 1 clearly demonstrates the existence of semantic imbalance.** Using TS2Vec on controlled signals (trend + sinusoid at varying amplitude ratios r = std(A)/std(B)), the paper shows that SDE values become highly asymmetric when one component dominates (Δ = +1.107 when r=10; Δ = −1.293 when r=0.1). At r=1 (balanced), Δ is near zero (−0.0276). This cleanly validates the problem. This is the paper's strongest empirical evidence.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Central empirical claim is contradicted by the reported results.** The paper asserts "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines" (line 300–301) and "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" (abstract). Table 3 does not support this. Key contradictions:
   - **Electricity**: CoST+APW is worse than CoST at horizons 168 (0.566 vs 0.425 MSE, a 33% degradation), 336 (0.677 vs 0.576), and 720 (1.010 vs 0.911). It is worse than *all* baselines at these horizons.
   - **Weather**: CoST+APW is never the best method at any horizon. It is worse than all baselines at horizons 336 (0.266 vs TNC's 0.215) and 720 (0.299 vs TNC's 0.219).
   - The method wins clearly on ETTm1, is mixed on ETT1 and ETT2, and decisively underperforms on Electricity and Weather. The paper does not acknowledge or attempt to explain these failures. This is the most serious weakness — the headline claim is contradicted by the data on 2 of 5 datasets.

2. **Abstract claims "no architectural changes" but an MLP fusion layer is introduced.** The abstract states: "Our approach can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes" (line 10). Yet Section 4.4.2 introduces "a small MLP g_φ that maps the concatenated embeddings [v(a)||v(b)] to a composite embedding" — this is an architectural addition to CoST. The paper itself flags this as unresolved in the "looking ahead" section (line 306: "we aim to analyze the role of the fusion MLP in greater depth"), confirming that the MLP is part of the current method.

### Minor

1. **SDE acronym has three different full names.** The title uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (line 5), Section 3.2 uses "Semantic Separability Error (SDE)" (line 69), and Sections 4.1 and 4.3 use "Semantic Decomposition Error (SDE)" (lines 150, 178). These are three distinct names for the same quantity, suggesting the paper was assembled from pieces without integration.

2. **Loss formulation inconsistency.** Section 3.4 (line 101) and Section 4.4.3 (line 257) define the loss using separate hyperparameters γ and γ' for seasonal and trend weighting: L = (1+γ·Δ)L_season + (1+γ'·(−Δ))L_trend. The Figure 1 caption (line 128) uses a single γ for both, writing the trend weight as "1−γΔ" (implying γ = γ'). The paper never clarifies which formulation is actually used in experiments.

3. **No SDE values are reported in the main experiment.** Line 263 states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance." Yet Table 3 only shows MSE/MAE. The paper claims CoST+APW achieves "consistently lower SDE values" but never presents this evidence. Since the core mechanism is designed to reduce SDE, this omission is significant.

4. **Critical experimental details missing.**
   - **Δ computation during training**: The paper does not specify whether Δ is recomputed every step, every epoch, as a moving average, or on what data (training minibatch vs. held-out set). This affects training dynamics and reproducibility.
   - **γ, γ' hyperparameter values**: These control the entire weighting mechanism but are never reported.
   - **No ablation separating the MLP fusion layer from the asymmetric weighting**: The method introduces two new components (MLP fusion + weighting). Without ablating them separately, any observed improvement cannot be attributed to the SDE-based weighting — the MLP alone could account for it.

5. **Disconnect between problem diagnosis and solution validation.** The paper diagnoses semantic imbalance using TS2Vec (Section 4.2, Table 1), then attempts and fails to fix TS2Vec via direct SDE regularization (Section 4.3, Table 2). The proposed method is built on CoST — a different architecture with separate trend/seasonal encoders that already partially addresses the diagnosed limitation. The paper never validates that *CoST itself* suffers from semantic imbalance (the Table 1 analysis is only run on TS2Vec), nor does it show that the method can fix TS2Vec. The logical chain from diagnosis to solution is incomplete.

### Trivial

- **ETT dataset labeling is confused in Table 3.** The paper's experimental setup (line 140) lists four subsets: ETT1h, ETT2, ETTm1, ETTm2. Table 3 labels the first block "ETT1" (horizons 24,48,168,336,720 — ETT1h) and a later block also "ETT1" (horizons 24,48,96,288,672 — should be ETTm1). These are distinct datasets with a shared label, causing confusion.

- **Section 2.4 citation inaccuracy.** The paper groups Barlow Twins (Zbontar et al., 2021) and VICReg (Bardes et al., 2021) under "asymmetric weighting" (line 51), but those works use redundancy-reduction objectives, not asymmetric weighting as defined in this paper.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Error bars or confidence intervals for Table 3, given that many comparisons show small margins where statistical significance is unknown.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for γ, γ'.
- Analysis of Δ dynamics during training (e.g., does Δ decrease under CoST+APW vs. vanilla CoST?).
- Comparison to simpler reweighting strategies (e.g., fixed upweighting of the seasonal loss by a constant factor) to show that the *adaptive* SDE-based weighting is genuinely better.

## Suggestions

1. **Tone down all claims to precisely match the evidence.** Remove "consistent" from all descriptions of forecasting improvements. Acknowledge and analyze the failures on Electricity and Weather — e.g., does the reweighting destabilize learning when periodic components are already well-captured? Does it harm long-horizon forecasting?

2. **Report SDE values in the main experiment.** Since the method is claimed to reduce SDE, this metric must appear alongside forecasting metrics. Show that Δ actually decreases under CoST+APW relative to vanilla CoST.

3. **Add an ablation study** separating the MLP fusion layer from the asymmetric weighting (i.e., compare CoST+MLP-only vs. CoST+APW vs. CoST+weighting-only without the MLP).

4. **Report γ, γ' values** and describe how Δ is computed during training (frequency, data source, batch size).

5. **Fix the SDE naming inconsistency** — choose one name and use it throughout.

6. **Resolve the loss formulation inconsistency** between Section 3.4 (γ, γ') and Figure 1 (single γ).

7. **Fix the dataset labeling** in Table 3 so ETTm1 is distinguished from ETT1h.

## Score and Decision

The paper identifies a genuinely important problem and proposes an interpretable diagnostic metric. However, the central empirical claim — consistent improvement in forecasting accuracy — is contradicted by the paper's own data on key datasets (Electricity, Weather). The paper overclaims throughout, omits critical experimental details (γ/γ' values, Δ computation procedure, ablation of components), and contains inconsistencies that undermine precision (three SDE names, conflicting loss formulations, contradictory statements about architectural changes). The SDE diagnostic contribution has value, but the evaluation of the proposed mitigation method (CoST+APW) does not support the advertised conclusions. Major revisions are needed.
