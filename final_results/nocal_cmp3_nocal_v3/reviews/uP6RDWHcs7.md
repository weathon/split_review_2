## Summary

This paper proposes Marginal Flow, a density estimation framework where the density is defined as a Monte Carlo average q_θ(x) = (1/N_c) Σ_i q(x|w_{θ,i}) with w_{θ,i} sampled from a learnable distribution q_θ(w). The parameters w are generated by passing base-distribution samples through an unconstrained neural network. Key claimed advantages: exact density evaluation, efficient sampling, no bijective constraints, flexible choice of parametric family q(x|w), and natural support for lower-dimensional manifolds. Experiments span 2D synthetic data, simulation-based inference, Wishart distributions on positive-definite matrices, and VAE latent-space manifold learning.

## Strengths

1. **Elegant and well-motivated core idea.** The core mechanism — marginalizing mixture-component parameters by resampling them from a learnable distribution — is conceptually clean and clearly communicated in Section 2.1. It avoids the structural constraints of normalizing flows (bijections, Jacobians), ODE solvers of flow matching, and variational bounds of VAEs, all of which are genuine limitations in existing methods.

2. **Demonstrated speed advantage.** Figure 3 shows Marginal Flow is orders of magnitude faster than Normalizing Flows, Flow Matching, and Free-form Flows for both sampling and density evaluation across dimensions 10²–10⁵, with NF and FM hitting OOM errors at high dimensions. This advantage is a genuine consequence of the method's design (no Jacobians, no ODEs) and is well-supported by the runtime measurements.

3. **Flexibility of the parametric family q(x|w).** The Wishart experiment (Section 4.3) cleanly demonstrates that swapping q(x|w) to match the data domain (positive-definite matrices) is straightforward and effective. Marginal Flow achieves test KL ~0.0088 vs. NF's ~0.82, and the 100×100 matrix setting (d=5050) is naturally handled while NFs are computationally prohibitive.

4. **Natural support for lower-dimensional manifolds.** By setting m < d for the base distribution, Marginal Flow learns densities on lower-dimensional manifolds without extra machinery. The spiral example (Figure 4) illustrates this clearly, a capability that Normalizing Flows and Flow Matching lack without additional approximations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Experimental scope does not match the generality of the claims.** The paper positions Marginal Flow as a general-purpose density estimation framework that "overcomes these limitations altogether" (abstract) and "alleviates altogether the common shortcomings of current approaches" (line 35), with Table 1 comparing against GANs, VAEs, diffusion models, NFs, and Flow Matching. Yet the experimental validation is confined to: 2D synthetic data (d=2), simulation-based inference (results deferred to appendix), Wishart distributions on structured positive-definite matrices (d=55, d=5050), and VAE latent spaces (d=10–20). There are **no experiments on standard unstructured density estimation benchmarks** — no UCI tabular data (POWER, GAS, HEPMASS, MINIBOONE, which are routine in the NF/flow-matching literature), no raw image likelihoods, no CIFAR-10 or MNIST pixels. The Wishart 100×100 experiment is the highest-dimensional case but involves specially structured data (positive-definite matrices on a 1D manifold) and has no baseline comparison (NF is "computationally prohibitive"). This gap between the paper's framing and its evidence substantially undermines the claim that Marginal Flow is a general-purpose framework competitive with the full range of deep generative models. The method's interesting properties (speed, manifold support) are demonstrated, but the paper does not show it works well in the regimes that motivate the comparison.

### Minor

1. **"Perfectly learn" overclaim is not supported by the evidence.** Section 4.1 states Marginal Flow "can perfectly learn all densities without needing any fine-tuning," and the conclusion repeats "perfectly reconstruct synthetic datasets." The Figure 6 description characterizes the learned densities as "more diffuse, blurred versions" of the ground truth, and the Checkerboard and Pinwheel densities appear noticeably smeared. While the method clearly captures the main structure and achieves strong log-likelihood numbers (Figure 7), the "perfect" characterization is overstated and the mismatch between claim and evidence weakens trust.

2. **N_c values never reported; finite-approximation analysis absent.** The model definition (Eq. 2) uses N_c Monte Carlo samples of w_i, which are "sampled again for each evaluation or sampling of q_θ(x)" (Section 2.2). The paper states N_c is "not required to be fixed" and "modeling capacity is not directly linked to N_c," but never reports what N_c was used in any experiment, nor analyzes how test log-likelihood varies with N_c, nor discusses the Monte Carlo variance of the density estimate when w_i are resampled per test point. This is a reproducibility gap that also makes it hard to assess the practical behavior of the finite-sample approximation.

3. **Image latent-space experiments are purely qualitative with an unsupported "disentanglement" claim.** Section 4.4 provides only visualizations with no quantitative metric (reconstruction error, latent coverage, or any standard disentanglement measure). The claim of "disentanglement of digits and writing style" (Figure 10 caption) and "disentanglement of faces and emotions" (Section 4.4) is asserted based on visual inspection of a handful of images without any supporting metric.

4. **No sample quality evaluation.** The paper shows density heatmaps on synthetic data and manifold traversals on images, but never evaluates sample quality using any standard metric (FID, IS, or even visual inspection on held-out real data). For a method positioned alongside generative models (Table 1), this is a notable omission.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An analysis of how gradient variance from the Monte Carlo sampling of z_i (and hence w_i) scales with N_c and affects training stability.
- A discussion of the well-known difficulty of Gaussian mixtures in high dimensions (concentration of measure) and whether Marginal Flow's resampling mechanism mitigates this issue.
- Reporting of the number of mixture components N_c used in each experiment.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution:
- **KDE/GMM baselines not included for 2D data** — Requesting classical non-deep baselines (KDE, GMM) for a deep generative modeling paper is scope creep. The paper compares against appropriate deep generative baselines (NF, FM, FFF), which is standard.
- **SBI "state-of-the-art" claim unverifiable from main text** — The paper states results are in Appendix Figure 14 due to space constraints. Deferring detailed benchmark tables to an appendix is standard practice; the appendix exists in the original submission.
- **Section 2.1's 10-component GMM motivation is a straw man** — This is a pedagogical illustration of the marginalization concept, not a competitive experimental comparison. The conceptual point about resampling vs. fixed optimization is valid regardless of the specific GMM configuration shown.
- **Various formatting nitpicks and criticisms about missing appendix content** — These reflect parser artifacts or the stripped appendix; they do not apply to the original submission.

## Novel Insights
The harsh critic's central observation — that the experimental scope is mismatched to the breadth of the claims — is accurate and has been retained as the primary weakness. This is the most actionable finding across the reviews.

## Suggestions
1. Add at least one standard unstructured density estimation benchmark (e.g., a UCI tabular dataset from the NF/flow-matching literature such as POWER, GAS, or MINIBOONE) to support the claim of generality. This single addition would substantially strengthen the paper.
2. Report N_c values for all experiments and include an analysis of how test log-likelihood varies with N_c. Clarify the evaluation protocol (fixed vs. resampled w_i per test point) and discuss the Monte Carlo variance.
3. Tone down the "perfectly learn" / "overcome limitations altogether" language to accurately characterize the empirical results (e.g., "competitive with baselines" / "effectively captures the main structure").
4. Add a quantitative metric to the image manifold experiments or remove the "disentanglement" claim.
