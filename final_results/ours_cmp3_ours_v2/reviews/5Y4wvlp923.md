## Summary

This paper identifies a "semantic imbalance" problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality) in learned embeddings. The authors propose the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a diagnostic metric for measuring component recoverability, and integrate it into an asymmetric weighting strategy that dynamically reweights view-specific contrastive losses within the CoST framework. The paper includes synthetic analysis diagnosing the problem in TS2Vec, a negative result for direct SDE regularization, and a CoST+APW method evaluated on forecasting benchmarks.

## Strengths

1. **The problem is real and well-motivated.** The paper clearly articulates that semantic imbalance — dominant components suppressing weaker ones in contrastive embeddings — is a genuine limitation of methods like TS2Vec. The synthetic experiment in Table 1 provides concrete evidence for this phenomenon.

2. **The synthetic SDE analysis in Table 1 is informative.** The controlled experiment varying amplitude ratio *r* shows a clean monotonic relationship between imbalance and Δ, demonstrating that TS2Vec produces asymmetric SDE values when components have different magnitudes. This is the paper's cleanest empirical result.

3. **The negative result in Table 2 is honestly reported.** Reporting that direct SDE regularization on TS2Vec fails rather than omitting this finding adds credibility and motivates the more sophisticated asymmetric weighting approach.

4. **The SDE diagnostic is a novel conceptual contribution.** Defining a metric to quantify how recoverable one component is from a composite embedding, and using the asymmetry between directional SDE values as a training signal, is an interesting idea that could be useful beyond this specific instantiation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The empirical results do not support the claimed "consistent gains."** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and Section 4.4.4 claims "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy." Table 3 shows a genuinely mixed picture:
   - On **Electricity**, CoST+APW degrades dramatically at longer horizons: MSE at horizon 168 is 0.566 vs. 0.425 (CoST) and 0.429 (TS2Vec) — a 33% increase. At horizon 720, MSE is 1.010 vs. 0.863 (TS2Vec) and 0.911 (CoST).
   - On **Weather**, CoST+APW underperforms TS2Vec and TNC at horizons 336 and 720 (0.266 vs. 0.231/0.215 at 336; 0.299 vs. 0.233/0.219 at 720).
   - On **ETT1 hourly**, APW underperforms CoST at horizons 48 (0.07 vs. 0.06) and 720 (0.152 vs. 0.148).
   - On **ETT2**, APW underperforms both CoST and TS2Vec at horizon 48 (0.129 vs. 0.128 vs. 0.124).
   
   The method does show wins in other settings (ETT1 at 168, 336; ETT2 at 24, 168, 336, 720; all ETTm1 settings; Electricity at 24, 48; Weather at 24, 168). However, "consistent gains" and "superior forecasting accuracy" are inaccurate characterizations when the method produces substantially worse results on key benchmarks. The paper offers no explanation for these failures, nor does it qualify its claims.

2. **Missing critical hyperparameter values for the core mechanism.** The asymmetric weighting is controlled by hyperparameters γ and γ' (Equation in Section 3.4) that determine how aggressively losses are reweighted. Neither their values, a sensitivity analysis, nor the tuning procedure is reported anywhere. Since the method sometimes helps and sometimes hurts, the reader cannot assess whether the reported wins are robust or cherry-picked from a hyperparameter sweep.

3. **No statistical significance or variance reporting.** All results in Table 3 are reported as point estimates without error bars, confidence intervals, or the number of random seeds. With 25 separate evaluation settings, the observed pattern of wins and losses could be noise. This is particularly concerning for the marginal wins (e.g., ETT2 at 168: 0.199 vs. 0.201; ETT2 at 336: 0.200 vs. 0.206; ETTm1 at 96: 0.037 vs. 0.038).

### Minor

1. **The "pluggable" framing is overstated relative to the paper's scope.** The paper diagnoses semantic imbalance in TS2Vec (Sections 4.2–4.3) but the proposed solution builds entirely on CoST, which already has separate trend/season encoders and frequency-aware contrastive learning. The asymmetric weighting is never applied to TS2Vec or TNC. The paper should either demonstrate pluggability into a non-CoST architecture or adjust the framing to "improving CoST."

2. **The SDE linearity assumption is unexamined.** The SDE metric computes cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)), which assumes additive compositionality in the embedding space of nonlinear encoders. The paper cites word-embedding arithmetic as inspiration, but word2vec embeddings are linear by construction while time-series encoders are not. This assumption is neither validated nor discussed.

3. **No ablation isolating MLP fusion from asymmetric weighting.** The proposed method has two components (MLP fusion layer + asymmetric weighting), but the experiments only compare CoST+APW (both) against CoST (neither). An ablation separating the two would reveal which component drives improvement.

4. **SDE computation procedure during training is underspecified.** It is not stated whether Δ is computed per batch, per epoch, or on a held-out set, or how stable it is during training. This matters for reproducibility.

### Trivial

1. **Inconsistent naming of the core metric.** The acronym SDE is expanded as "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3, Section 4.1).

2. **Equation/figure inconsistency on hyperparameters.** Section 3.4 uses two hyperparameters (γ, γ') for seasonal and trend losses separately, while the Figure 1 caption uses a single γ (1+γΔ for seasonal, 1−γΔ for trend).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (γ, γ') to demonstrate robustness.
- An explanation for the failure cases on Electricity and Weather long horizons — these are the paper's most informative data points.
- Application of the asymmetric weighting to at least one non-CoST architecture to validate the "pluggable" claim.
- A discussion of limitations, explicitly acknowledging the settings where the method underperforms.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface one observation not foregrounded in the paper: the systematic failure pattern on Electricity long horizons (strong periodicities) suggests the asymmetric weighting may overcorrect — boosting the seasonal loss so aggressively that it harms forecasting when seasonal patterns are strong. The paper could test this hypothesis by examining the learned weighting dynamics on Electricity vs. ETT. However, this remains speculative without access to the missing hyperparameter values and training dynamics.

## Suggestions

1. **Calibrate the claims.** Replace "consistent gains" and "superior forecasting accuracy" with an honest assessment of where the method helps and where it fails. This would actually strengthen the paper by demonstrating scientific integrity.
2. **Report the γ and γ' values used in Table 3**, and include a sensitivity analysis showing how performance varies with these hyperparameters.
3. **Add error bars** (at minimum, 3 random seeds with standard deviation) to the main results table.
4. **Ablate the two components** (MLP fusion vs. asymmetric weighting separately) to isolate what drives improvement.
5. **Discuss the Electricity and Weather failures.** These are the paper's most informative results — understanding when the method fails is as valuable as understanding when it succeeds.
6. **Unify the SDE naming** throughout the paper.
7. **Clarify the SDE computation procedure during training** (batch-level vs. epoch-level, held-out vs. training data).
8. Either test the method on a non-CoST architecture or reframe the contribution as "improving CoST."
