## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework that operates entirely within SAM's architecture. It introduces two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that samples prompts from a similarity-based distribution, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. On FSC-147, the method achieves MAE 16.25 (best among SAM-only training-free methods), and on CARPK it achieves MAE 4.68, outperforming all training-based methods in the comparison.

## Strengths

- **Genuinely novel use of SAM's output tokens for verification.** The core idea — using SAM's decoder output tokens directly as verification criteria rather than relying on image embedding similarity — is original and well-motivated. The paper correctly identifies that output tokens encode both semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, making them naturally suited for verification. Most prior work (TFOC, C-Anything, OmniCount) computes similarity in the image embedding space, which conflates multiple visual features. (Sections 3.2, Figure 1)

- **Strong CARPK results.** MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13 on CARPK outperform all training-based methods in Table 1 (FamNet 18.19, BMNet+ 5.76, SAFECOUNT 5.33, LOCA 9.97). Only TFCAC (MAE 4.39), which uses an additional DINO encoder, does better — and the paper correctly notes TFCAC's higher computational cost. This is the paper's strongest empirical result. (Table 1)

- **Clean, self-contained method.** The entire framework operates within SAM's architecture without additional models, fine-tuning, or architectural modifications. The probabilistic prompt generation with iterative probability updates is a principled alternative to simple grid or superpixel prompting. (Sections 3.1, 3.2)

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Abstract substantially overstates the results.** The abstract states: "Experimental results show our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is contradicted by Table 1: LOCA (MAE 10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62), and TFCAC (12.26) all achieve substantially lower MAE. The body of the paper accurately describes the method as achieving "the best performance among training-free SAM only methods" (Section 4.2), but the abstract's blanket claim of superiority over all existing methods is inaccurate and must be revised. (Abstract, line 9; Table 1)

- **High RMSE on FSC-147 is not adequately discussed.** The method's RMSE on FSC-147 is 133.11 — second-worst after plain SAM (137.50), and actually higher than TFOC (132.16), C-Anything (131.24), and TFCounter (130.59), all simpler baselines. This is more than double that of LOCA (56.97) and TFCAC (56.33), indicating very high prediction variance. The paper focuses on MAE in its qualitative discussion and does not explain why RMSE is so high or whether the method is reliable for deployment. (Table 1, Section 4.2)

- **Core claimed contribution — the TS-SS metric on output tokens — shows only marginal improvement.** The TS-SS metric (MAE 16.25) improves over cosine similarity (MAE 16.48) by only 0.23 MAE (1.4%), and output tokens (16.25) improve over image embeddings (16.94) by 0.69 MAE (4%). No error bars or multiple-run statistics are reported despite the stochastic sampling procedure. The paper then uses strong language claiming cosine similarity has reached a "performance plateau" requiring a "new hybrid metric," which overinterprets this small gap. (Tables 3, 5; Section 4.4)

### Minor

- **No variance or significance reporting despite a stochastic method.** The method samples prompts from a multinomial distribution, making results inherently stochastic. Yet no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multiple-run averages are reported for any metric. The reader cannot determine whether the reported MAE of 16.25 is a single run or averaged, or whether the 0.23 MAE gap between TS-SS and cosine similarity is meaningful. (Tables 1–5)

- **Probability update rule is underspecified.** The paper states that for negative regions, "the value of the probability distribution at that mask region is decreased" but provides no precise update rule (fixed decrement? multiplicative factor? by how much?). This makes exact reproduction difficult. (Section 3.1, lines 147–148)

- **Key hyperparameters lack sensitivity analysis.** The token verification threshold (300) and t-scale factor (0.066) are stated based on "validation experiments" but no analysis of how performance varies with these parameters is provided. Combined with the TS-SS formula's use of degrees (θ/360°), the threshold value is essentially uninterpretable without distributional context. (Section 4.1, line 180; Equation 2)

- **Non-monotonic iteration behavior is unexplained.** Increasing iterations at a fixed prompt count can degrade performance: (16 iter, 128 prompts) → MAE 16.25 vs. (32 iter, 128 prompts) → MAE 16.65. The paper claims performance "consistently improves," which is misleading across iteration counts. (Table 4, Section 4.4)

- **Exemplar count on CARPK not ablated.** The CARPK evaluation uses 12 exemplars from the training set. The paper does not specify how many exemplars comparison methods use on CARPK, nor does it ablate the impact of exemplar count on performance. (Section 4.1, line 174)

## Suggestions
1. Revise the abstract to accurately reflect comparative results (e.g., "competitive performance among SAM-only training-free methods" or "best performance among training-free methods using SAM alone").
2. Add a discussion of the high RMSE on FSC-147, including analysis of failure cases or variance sources.

4. Provide a sensitivity analysis of the token verification threshold.
