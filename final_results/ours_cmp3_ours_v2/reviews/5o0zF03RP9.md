## Summary

This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL rewards with a KL-divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s), weighted by a coefficient β. The paper's claimed central novelty is a Bayesian scheme that adapts β online, removing the need for manual tuning. Experiments are reported on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8.

## Strengths

- **Clean toy-MDP demonstration.** Section 5.2 shows that in a minimal 2-state MDP where the preferred outcome aligns with the reward-yielding state, adding the KL penalty (β > 0) accelerates convergence and reduces variance (Table 1). This provides a useful didactic illustration of the core idea.

- **Cognitively motivated framing.** The connection to dopamine-based reward prediction error and the Free Energy Principle (Sections 2.3–2.4, 6.3) is clearly articulated and provides a coherent intellectual narrative for the shaping term.

- **Honest discussion of failure modes.** Section 6.2 candidly identifies preference misalignment, KL dominance, and latent mismatch as potential failure modes, which is a strength relative to papers that only discuss benefits.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

1. **Bayesian β adaptation — the paper's central claimed novelty — is entirely unspecified.** The abstract (line 9) calls this "the central novelty," and the contributions (line 21) label it "Bayesian adaptation (novelty)." Yet the paper never describes the Bayesian model: no prior distribution on β, no likelihood model, no inference procedure (exact, variational, MCMC, or otherwise), no description of what data the posterior conditions on, and no definition of what a "round" is (Figures 3–4, Table 3). The paper shows posterior concentration plots and summary statistics, but a reader has no way of knowing what computation produced them. This is not a matter of insufficient hyperparameter detail — the mechanism itself is absent. A paper whose core contribution cannot be implemented or evaluated from the text does not meet the bar for publication.

### Major

2. **p(o|s,a) is not concretely specified for the non-toy environments.** The shaping term — the method's foundation — requires a predicted outcome distribution p(o|s,a). For the toy MDP this is simply the transition function (line 166). For MountainCar, the paper states only that "p(o|s,a) is the predicted outcome distribution" (line 199), and for MiniGrid no specification is given at all. No forward model, network architecture, training procedure, or loss function is described. Since the entire method depends on this component, its absence for the two main empirical demonstrations means those experiments are not reproducible.

3. **No comparison against any existing intrinsic motivation method.** The paper positions IncentRL as addressing limitations of curiosity-driven exploration, empowerment, VIME, and related approaches (lines 9, 37, and throughout Section 2). Yet every experiment compares only against β = 0 — i.e., standard RL without any intrinsic motivation. No comparison is made against ICM (Pathak et al. 2017), RND (Burda et al. 2018), or any other existing method. Without this, the paper cannot support its claim of improving upon prior intrinsic motivation approaches.

4. **Bayesian β adaptation is not validated on actual task performance.** Figures 3–4 and Table 3 show that the posterior over β concentrates over rounds, but the paper never directly compares the task performance (success rate, episode length, or reward) of the adaptive-β agent against the best fixed-β agent. The only mention is a single sentence (line 228) stating "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" — with no data, no number of seeds, and no variance. The central claim that Bayesian adaptation "removes the need for manual trade-off tuning" (lines 9, 289) requires a direct task-performance comparison to be supported.

### Minor

5. **Internal inconsistency about the value β converges to.** The text and Figure 3 caption state that the posterior mean concentrates "near the effective region (β≈0.1)" (line 239), but Table 3 shows Round 4 mean β = 0.0173 — an order of magnitude lower. The MiniGrid fixed-β sweep found β = 0.01 to be the best tested value, so a convergence at 0.0173 is consistent with that, but the figure's "target effective β = 0.1" line is inconsistent with both the data and the MiniGrid results.

6. **The "theoretical analysis" (Section 4) consists of basic continuity/limit properties.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimality) and Proposition 2 (large β makes KL dominate) are standard properties of any bounded additive penalty with a weight parameter. They do not distinguish IncentRL from any reward-shaping approach and provide no actionable insight into when the method helps or why.

7. **Limited experimental scale and statistical rigor.** Two of three experiments use only 3 seeds (MountainCar, MiniGrid). No statistical significance tests are reported. The MiniGrid text reports point estimates (98% vs. 90.5% success rate) without variance while the figure includes error shading; the MountainCar standard deviations are large (472–909 for mean goal reaches of 1654–4135). An ablation of the KL mechanism against a different divergence or a simpler bonus is not conducted.

## Suggestions

1. **Fully specify the Bayesian β adaptation mechanism.** A paper whose core novelty is a Bayesian procedure must describe the prior, likelihood, inference algorithm, and the data the posterior is conditioned on. Without this, the contribution cannot be evaluated.
2. **Specify p(o|s,a) for each non-toy environment.** Describe the architecture and training procedure used to obtain predicted outcome distributions in MountainCar and MiniGrid.
3. **Add at least one intrinsic-motivation baseline.** Compare against ICM or RND on the same benchmarks to support the claim of improving upon existing approaches.
4. **Directly compare adaptive-β task performance against the best fixed β.** Show learning curves for the adaptive variant on the same axes as the fixed-β=0.01 and β=0 baselines for MiniGrid.
5. **Resolve the β≈0.1 vs. 0.0173 inconsistency.** Clarify whether the "target effective β" in Figure 3 refers to MountainCar or MiniGrid, and ensure the text matches the data in Table 3.
