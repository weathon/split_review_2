## Summary
The paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a cosine-similarity metric measuring whether one semantic component (e.g., trend or periodicity) is linearly recoverable from a composite time-series embedding, and an Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) mechanism that reweights CoST's seasonal and trend contrastive losses based on SDE-derived asymmetry. The core motivation — that dominant components suppress weaker ones in contrastive time-series learning — is well-diagnosed through controlled synthetic experiments, but the empirical evaluation shows mixed results with substantial unacknowledged regressions on two of five datasets.

## Strengths
- **Well-designed diagnostic experiment (Table 1):** Synthetic experiments varying amplitude ratio r = std(A)/std(B) clearly demonstrate that TS2Vec suffers from semantic imbalance — Δ smoothly transitions from negative to positive across ratios, with near-zero value at perfect balance (r=1). This provides concrete, quantitative evidence of the diagnosed problem and is the paper's strongest contribution.
- **Honest reporting of failed direct regularization (Table 2):** The paper transparently shows that adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec uniformly worsens performance (e.g., ETTm1 H288: 0.101 → 0.126). This negative result strengthens the narrative arc and demonstrates scientific integrity.
- **Clear wins on ETTm1 (Table 3):** CoST+APW outperforms all baselines across all five horizons (24, 48, 96, 288, 672), with notable gains at longer horizons (H672 MSE: 0.132 vs. CoST 0.154 vs. TS2Vec 0.142).
- **Pluggable design requiring no architectural modifications:** The method modifies only the loss weighting of CoST, making adoption straightforward.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **"Consistent gains" claim is contradicted by the paper's own Table 3.** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and the conclusion reiterates "consistently lower SDE values and improved forecasting accuracy." However, Table 3 shows: (1) Electricity at long horizons — CoST+APW achieves MSE 0.566/0.677/1.010 at H168/336/720, vs. best baselines TNC at 0.412/0.548/0.859, a 37%/24%/18% degradation; (2) Weather — CoST+APW is worst or near-worst at H24, H48, H336 (MSE 0.266 vs. TNC 0.215, a 24% gap), and H720 (MSE 0.299 vs. TNC 0.219, a 37% gap). The paper does not acknowledge or analyze any of these failures. This directly undermines the core contribution claim.

- **SDE values claimed but never reported.** Section 4.1 lists SDE as an evaluation metric, and Section 4.4.4 states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance." Yet Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE columns. The conclusion also claims "consistently lower SDE values" without any supporting data. For a paper whose primary contribution is an SDE-based mechanism, the absence of SDE results makes it impossible to verify whether the reweighting actually reduces semantic imbalance.

- **No ablation isolating the MLP contribution.** Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP that maps concatenated trend and seasonal embeddings to a composite embedding, adding new parameters. Table 2 ablates SDE regularization on TS2Vec, but there is no corresponding ablation for CoST+APW separating the MLP's capacity contribution from the Δ-based reweighting. Without this, the reader cannot determine whether observed gains come from extra MLP capacity or from semantic rebalancing.

- **Diagnostic analysis on TS2Vec, method applied to CoST — unjustified transfer.** Sections 4.2–4.3 analyze semantic imbalance in TS2Vec. Section 4.4 states (line 225): "To address the weakening of secondary semantic components observed in ts2vec, we build upon CoST's multi-view contrastive learning framework." The paper never justifies why findings from TS2Vec would transfer to CoST (a different architecture with frequency-aware multi-view contrastive learning), or whether CoST's existing decomposition already mitigates the diagnosed problem.

### Minor
- **SDE assumes linear composability in embedding space without justification.** The metric SDE_{a,b} = 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)) assumes v(a+b) − v(b) should approximate v(a) — a linearity assumption that holds for word2vec-style embeddings but not generally for deep encoders. SDE values in Table 1 frequently exceed 1.0 (e.g., 1.378 at r=0.2), meaning compared vectors point in opposite directions — a regime where SDE reflects encoder nonlinearity rather than semantic imbalance. The paper does not discuss this.

- **Hyperparameters γ and γ' are never specified or analyzed.** These control the entire mechanism (Section 4.4.3) but their values are never stated and no sensitivity analysis is provided. It is unclear whether the setting that works on ETT causes the Electricity/Weather regressions.

- **Triple naming inconsistency for the core metric.** The paper uses three names: "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title, abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2 heading), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.1 metrics, Section 4.3). This creates confusion around the central contribution.

- **Implementation section reports inconsistent horizons.** Section 4.1 states forecasting horizons are {24, 48, 96, 192}, but Table 3 uses different horizons (e.g., {24, 48, 168, 336, 720} for most datasets).

## Novel Insights
The paper's genuinely novel insight is that contrastive time-series encoders exhibit systematic, quantifiable semantic imbalance where dominant components suppress weaker ones, measurable via directional cosine similarity in embedding space. The synthetic experiments (Table 1) are well-designed and convincing. The failure of direct SDE regularization (Table 2) is an instructive negative result suggesting diagnostic metrics don't necessarily translate into useful optimization signals. However, the most scientifically interesting unexplored finding is the Electricity/Weather regressions, which suggest the reweighting mechanism can overshoot — but the paper does not analyze this.

## Suggestions
- Acknowledge and analyze the failures on Electricity and Weather; these are the most scientifically interesting results in the paper.
- Report SDE values in Table 3 for all methods to validate whether the reweighting reduces semantic imbalance.
- Add ablation separating MLP contribution from Δ-based reweighting.
- Justify the TS2Vec→CoST transfer or add corresponding SDE analysis on CoST's own embeddings.
- Specify γ and γ' values and add sensitivity analysis.
