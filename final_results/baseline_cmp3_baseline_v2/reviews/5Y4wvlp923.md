## Summary
This paper identifies a semantic imbalance problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress minor ones (e.g., seasonality). The authors propose the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric to quantify component recoverability, and integrate it into an adaptive weighting strategy for view-specific contrastive objectives within the CoST framework. Experiments on forecasting benchmarks show modest improvements over baselines, particularly under semantic skew conditions.

## Strengths
- The paper identifies a genuine and under-explored problem: semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, where dominant temporal components can suppress weaker but semantically important ones.
- The SDE metric provides a principled way to quantify component-level recoverability, and the asymmetry factor Δ offers a clear diagnostic tool for detecting imbalance.
- The proposed method is pluggable into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes, which is practical for adoption.

## Weaknesses
### Fatal
- **The core claim that SDE-based weighting improves performance is not convincingly supported by the experimental results.** In Table 3, CoST+APW (the proposed method) does not consistently outperform vanilla CoST. On ETT1 at horizon 720, CoST achieves 0.148 MSE vs. 0.152 for CoST+APW. On Electricity at horizons 168, 336, and 720, CoST+APW is substantially worse than CoST (e.g., 0.566 vs. 0.425 at horizon 168). On Weather, TS2Vec and TNC often outperform both CoST variants. The paper claims "consistent gains" but the evidence shows mixed results at best.

### Major
- **The paper conflates two different methods without clear separation.** Sections 3.2-3.3 define SDE as "Semantic Separability Error" but Section 4.3 redefines it as "Semantic Decomposition Error" with a different formulation. The paper appears to present two different approaches (direct SDE regularization in Section 4.3 and asymmetric weighting in Section 4.4) but the relationship between them is unclear. The direct regularization attempt fails (Table 2), yet the paper still claims SDE as a contribution.
- **The experimental setup is incomplete and lacks statistical rigor.** No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multiple seeds are reported. Given the small and inconsistent improvements, it is impossible to determine whether the observed differences are statistically significant or simply noise.
- **The method is evaluated only on forecasting tasks.** The paper motivates the problem with representation learning and mentions classification and anomaly detection, but provides no experiments on these tasks. This limits the generality of the claims.
- **The ablation study is insufficient.** The paper does not ablate the MLP fusion layer, the asymmetric weighting mechanism, or the hyperparameters γ and γ'. Without these ablations, it is unclear which component drives any observed improvements.

### Minor
- The paper claims three contributions (diagnosing semantic imbalance, systematic ablations, and a rebalancing framework), but the "systematic ablations" are limited to one failed regularization experiment (Table 2) and one comparison table (Table 3).
- The SDE metric definition changes between sections (Sections 3.2 vs. 4.3), which is confusing.

### Trivial
- The paper uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error" in the title but "Semantic Separability Error" and "Semantic Decomposition Error" in the body.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Evaluate on classification and anomaly detection tasks to demonstrate generality.
- Report results with multiple random seeds and statistical significance tests.
- Ablate the MLP fusion layer and the asymmetric weighting separately.
- Include a sensitivity analysis for hyperparameters γ and γ'.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The observation that dominant temporal components suppress weaker ones is not new—it is a known issue in time-series decomposition and representation learning. The SDE metric is a reasonable diagnostic tool, but its utility as an optimization signal is not convincingly demonstrated.

## Suggestions
- Clearly separate the diagnostic SDE metric from the optimization method. The paper currently mixes these two ideas.
- Provide statistical significance tests (e.g., confidence intervals over multiple runs) to support claims of improvement.
- Include experiments on classification and anomaly detection to demonstrate that the method improves representation quality, not just forecasting.
- Ablate the key components (MLP fusion, asymmetric weighting) to identify which part of the method is responsible for any gains.

## Score and Decision
The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a reasonable diagnostic metric, but the experimental evidence does not support the core claim that the proposed method consistently improves performance. The results are mixed, with the proposed method often underperforming baselines. The lack of statistical rigor and incomplete evaluation further weaken the contribution.
