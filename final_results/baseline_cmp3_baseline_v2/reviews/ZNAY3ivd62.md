## Summary

The paper introduces GUI-Spotlight, a visual grounding model for GUI agents that iteratively invokes a set of specialized tools (crop, extract, find_color) to progressively narrow focus to a target element. The model is trained in three stages: supervised fine-tuning on 2.5K multi-turn demonstrations (generated by a 72B teacher), followed by reinforcement learning with a modified GSPO objective that includes a stabilizing cross-entropy loss on valid/correct samples, and a final high-resolution RL stage. With only 18.5K training samples, GUI-Spotlight achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, outperforming all 7B open-source models and competing with much larger models. The paper also documents negative results and ablation studies on RL algorithms and reward design.

## Strengths

- **Strong empirical results with high data efficiency.** GUI-Spotlight (initialized from UI-TARS-1.5-7B) reaches 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, surpassing the previous best 7B model (UI-Venus-7B at 50.8%) while using only 18.5K training samples—orders of magnitude less than competing approaches (e.g., 9.6M for V2P-7B).
- **Comprehensive ablation and negative-result documentation.** The paper systematically compares RL algorithms (GRPO, GSPO variants) and reward formulations, clearly reporting which modifications helped or hurt. This practical guidance is valuable for future work on tool-augmented grounding.
- **Generalizable approach across backbones.** GUI-Spotlight improves the non-UI-specialized Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +11.9 points on ScreenSpot-Pro (+7.4 on UI-Vision), demonstrating that the multi-tool RL recipe transfers beyond UI-specific base models.
- **Well-designed training stability mechanism.** The auxiliary cross-entropy loss on format-valid and correct samples (tool-filtered positives) prevents the RL collapse observed with vanilla GRPO/GSPO in multi-turn tool-use settings, a nontrivial practical contribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Data efficiency claim is partly attributable to teacher supervision.** The 2,561 multi-turn trajectories used for SFT warm-up are generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B, a much larger and stronger model. The low total sample count is achieved in part by distilling knowledge from a 72B teacher, which should be acknowledged more prominently when contrasting with methods that use millions of *unaided* training samples.
2. **Improvements on OSWorld-G are marginal.** GUI-Spotlight (UI-TARS) improves only +0.8% over its UI-TARS-1.5-7B baseline (61.9%→62.7%) and underperforms GTA1-7B (67.7%). The paper’s narrative of “substantially outperforming” fits ScreenSpot-Pro and UI-Vision but does not hold uniformly across all benchmarks.
3. **Limited discussion of failure modes and scope.** The three tools (crop, extract, find_color) are hand-designed. The paper does not analyze when these tools are insufficient (e.g., when the target has no distinctive color or when coarse quadrant extraction fails) or the computational overhead of multi-step inference in latency-sensitive GUI agents.

### Minor

- The “think-with-image” terminology is used repeatedly but never formally defined. Its meaning (reasoning with image context across turns) becomes clear from context, but a concise definition would aid clarity.
- Reward weights (α) are reported but no ablation on the overall weight distribution is provided beyond the Crop/Extract ratio. The sensitivity to other weights (e.g., Format reward) is underexplored.

### Trivial
- Figure 2’s caption appears twice due to a formatting artifact; this does not affect understanding.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Evaluate GUI-Spotlight on additional dynamic or more challenging GUI benchmarks (e.g., Mind2Web, WebArena) to test generalization beyond static screenshots.
- Compare the per-step inference speed of GUI-Spotlight against single-step grounding models to quantify the latency trade-off.
- Provide a qualitative analysis of cases where the model fails after tool invocation, e.g., due to incorrect tool selection or ambiguous color matching.

## Novel Insights

The key insight is that iterative visual spotlighting with a small set of well-chosen tools can be effectively learned through a carefully stabilized RL training pipeline, achieving competitive grounding accuracy with dramatically fewer training samples than end-to-end fine-tuning on millions of examples. The finding that a cross-entropy auxiliary loss on tool-valid and result-correct trajectories prevents policy collapse in multi-turn RL is practically significant and likely reusable in other tool-augmented RL settings beyond GUI grounding.

## Suggestions

- In the data efficiency discussion, separately report the cost of generating demonstrations from the 72B teacher (API calls, compute) so readers can compare total resource expenditure, not just sample count.
- Add a paragraph on limitations, covering what types of GUI elements or visual clutter the current tool set handles poorly, and how inference latency scales with the number of turns.

## Score and Decision

**Score:** 8  
**Decision:** Accept

The paper makes a solid empirical contribution by demonstrating a practical, data-efficient method for improving GUI visual grounding through iterative tool use and stabilized reinforcement learning. The experiments are thorough, the ablation studies are informative, and the documentation of negative results adds value. While some claims are nuanced (data efficiency depends on teacher distillation, not all benchmarks show large gains), the overall contribution is strong enough for acceptance at ICLR.
