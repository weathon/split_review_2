## Summary

This paper proposes a unified transformer-based sequence-to-sequence framework that jointly models expressive piano performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content and global performance style representations. A diffusion-based performance style recommendation (PSR) module is introduced to generate style embeddings directly from score content, enabling automated and stylistically appropriate rendering without requiring manual control or composer labels. The framework is trained using sequence-aligned data without fine-grained note-level alignment and evaluated on both objective metrics and subjective listening tests, achieving competitive performance on both tasks and demonstrating effective content-style disentanglement and style transfer.

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated unified framework**: Jointly modeling EPR and APT as inverse tasks is an underexplored direction in music information retrieval. The paper clearly draws motivation from ASR/TTS joint modeling and provides a principled formulation leveraging the duality between rendering and transcription. This conceptual contribution is significant.

- **Thorough evaluation across multiple dimensions**: The paper substantiates claims with a comprehensive suite of experiments: objective metrics for both APT (MUSTER, ScoreSimilarity) and EPR (variance, KL divergence, MAE, alignment rates), subjective listening tests with 11 trained musicians, disentanglement verification via performer/composer identification (Table 4), style transfer evaluation, and qualitative visualizations of latent spaces. The statistical significance testing strengthens the results.

- **Convincing disentanglement evidence**: The performer/composer identification experiments (Table 4) clearly show that style embeddings carry much stronger performer and composer information (77.46% composer accuracy) compared to content representations (29.99%), while content representations still capture composer-agnostic structural information. This directly validates the claimed disentanglement. The t-SNE visualizations in Figures 3 and 4 further support this.

- **Practical contribution of alignment-free formulation**: The Seq2Seq formulation that does not require note-level alignment is a practical advance, as alignment preprocessing is a bottleneck in prior EPR systems. Despite this relaxed supervision, the model achieves competitive performance with alignment-dependent baselines.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Limited subjective evaluation scale**: The listening test involves only 11 participants. While this is acceptable for a pilot study, the subjective ratings would carry more weight with a larger participant pool. The paper does not report inter-rater reliability or statistical significance of differences between systems in the subjective results (Figure 2), making it unclear whether the observed differences are meaningful beyond these 11 raters.

- **PSR module dependency on joint model quality**: The PSR module is trained separately using ground-truth style embeddings extracted from the joint model. This means the PSR module's quality is fundamentally bounded by the joint model's style encoder. The paper does not discuss how errors in the joint model's style encoding propagate to the PSR module, nor does it provide a sensitivity analysis.

### Minor

- **Unpaired data contribution not isolated**: The paper claims joint training with unpaired data improves EPR performance (EPR-Only vs Ours-Target in Table 2), but there is no ablation study isolating the contribution of unpaired data versus the effect of joint APT training objectives. A clearer decomposition would strengthen the causal claim.

- **Style transfer evaluation limited to three samples**: The subjective style transfer evaluation (Figure 5) uses only three samples. While the results are suggestive, a broader evaluation across more pieces and styles would better demonstrate the generality of style transfer capability.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation comparing Ours-Target with a variant trained only on paired data (without unpaired losses) would isolate the benefit of unpaired data from the benefit of joint APT supervision.
- Discussion of failure cases or limitations—e.g., styles or composers where the PSR module produces less appropriate embeddings—would add depth.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, a valuable insight is that performance style embeddings learned through a joint rendering-transcription objective naturally encode both performer identity and composer-specific stylistic conventions, with composer traits dominating. This finding empirically supports the assumption that professional pianists adapt their expressive style to the composition, and it validates the design choice behind the PSR module: that stylistically appropriate embeddings can be inferred from score content alone. The smooth interpolation results in style transfer further suggest that the learned style manifold is geometrically well-structured, which is an interesting property for a globally pooled latent space in a Seq2Seq model.

## Suggestions

- Increase the participant pool in the subjective listening test and report inter-rater reliability (e.g., Fleiss' kappa) and statistical significance of pairwise system comparisons.
- Add an ablation experiment removing the unpaired losses to isolate their contribution to EPR performance improvement over the EPR-Only variant.
- Provide failure analysis for the PSR module: on which styles or score types does the generated embedding deviate most from ground-truth style embeddings?
