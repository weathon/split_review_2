## Summary

This paper proposes **DIPOLE**, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm for stable and controllable fine-tuning of diffusion/flow policies. The key idea is to reformulate a greedified KL-regularized objective, whose closed-form solution decomposes the optimal policy into a pair of dichotomous policies: a positive policy that maximizes return and a negative policy that minimizes it, both trained with bounded sigmoid weights. During inference, actions are generated by a linear combination of the score functions of these two policies, analogous to classifier-free guidance, enabling adjustable greediness. The method is evaluated on 39 tasks from ExORL and OGBench (offline and offline-to-online) and on a real-world autonomous driving benchmark (NAVSIM) using a billion-parameter vision-language-action model, consistently outperforming strong baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel theoretical framing** – The greedified KL-regularized objective and the resulting dichotomous policy decomposition are original and elegant. The connection to classifier-free guidance (CFG) is insightful and provides a theoretically grounded way to control greediness via the ω parameter.
- **Stable and practical training** – By replacing the unbounded exponential weight with bounded sigmoid functions, DIPOLE avoids loss explosion and sample dominance, making large-scale diffusion policy optimization feasible without unstable gradient propagation or crude likelihood approximations.
- **Strong empirical validation** – The paper demonstrates clear improvements across a wide range of RL tasks (39 tasks) and scales to a real-world autonomous driving benchmark with a 1B-parameter model, showing that the method is not only effective in controlled settings but also applicable to complex, high-stakes domains.
- **Controllable inference** – The inference-time linear score combination gives practitioners a simple knob (ω) to directly adjust the greediness of the policy, which is both practically useful and theoretically well-motivated.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Computational overhead** – Training two separate diffusion models (positive and negative) doubles the memory and compute relative to a single policy. While the paper uses LoRA in the large-scale experiment to mitigate this, the overhead is not quantified or discussed in the main text, and it could be a practical barrier for many users.
- **Dependence on a good reference policy** – The method is fundamentally a fine-tuning approach that builds on a pre-trained reference policy μ. Its success likely depends on the quality of μ, but the paper does not analyze failure modes when the reference policy is poor or when the dataset lacks high-return trajectories.

### Minor
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity** – The method introduces two hyperparameters (β and ω) for controlling greediness, and their interaction is only briefly addressed in the appendix. A more systematic study of how these parameters affect performance and stability would strengthen the practical guidance.
- **Limited comparison in autonomous driving** – The autonomous driving benchmark compares with many methods but does not include recent diffusion-based RL fine-tuning baselines (e.g., DDPO, DPPO) on the same VLA model; only one DPPO variant is shown, and its performance is slightly lower than DIPOLE, but the gap is small. A broader comparison would be more convincing.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A figure or table comparing the computational cost (FLOPs, memory, wall-clock time) of DIPOLE against a single-policy weighted regression baseline on the same tasks.
- An ablation on the quality of the reference policy (e.g., fine-tuning from a weaker pre-trained policy) to understand the robustness of the approach.
- A discussion or visualization of how the inferred positive and negative score functions differ qualitatively (e.g., in an illustrative 2D action space).

## Novel Insights

The central insight—that the optimal policy under a greedified KL-regularized objective can be expressed as the ratio of a positive and a negative policy, each trained with bounded weights, and that inference reduces to a CFG-like linear score combination—is genuinely novel. It bridges two previously separate ideas: weighted regression for RL policy extraction and classifier-free guidance for diffusion models. This synthesis not only solves the instability issue of prior weighted regression methods but also provides a principled way to control the degree of optimization, which could inspire future work on safe and adjustable policy improvement in other domains.

## Suggestions

- Add a comparison of training time and GPU memory for DIPOLE vs. a single-policy weighted regression baseline (e.g., on ExORL tasks) to make the computational overhead transparent.
- Include experiments where the reference policy is deliberately weakened (e.g., by using only a subset of the data for pretraining) to test the robustness of the method.
- Provide a sensitivity analysis of ω and β jointly, perhaps as a heatmap of final performance.
