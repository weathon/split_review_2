

Transcormer: Transformer for Sentence Scoring with Sliding Language Modeling

Abstract Sentence scoring aims at measuring the likelihood score of a sentence and is widely 1 used in many natural language processing scenarios, like reranking, which is to 2 select the best sentence from multiple candidates. Previous works on sentence 3 scoring mainly adopted either causal language modeling (CLM) like GPT or 4 masked language modeling (MLM) like BERT, which have some limitations: 1) 5 CLM only utilizes unidirectional information for the probability estimation of 6 a sentence without considering bidirectional context, which affects the scoring 7 quality; 2) MLM can only estimate the probability of partial tokens at a time and 8 thus requires multiple forward passes to estimate the probability of the whole 9 sentence, which incurs large computation and time cost. In this paper, we propose 10 Transcormer – a Transformer model with a novel sliding language modeling (SLM) 11 for sentence scoring. Specifically, our SLM adopts a triple-stream self-attention 12 mechanism to estimate the probability of all tokens in a sentence with bidirectional 13 context and only requires a single forward pass. SLM can avoid the limitations of 14 CLM (only unidirectional context) and MLM (multiple forward passes) and inherit 15 their advantages, and thus achieve high effectiveness and efficiency in scoring. 16 Experimental results on multiple tasks demonstrate that our method achieves better 17 performance than other language modelings. 18

Inspired by these successes of CLM and MLM in NLG and 36 NLU tasks, some works [18, 19, 20, 21, 22] have tried to use these pre-trained LMs for reranking on 37 machine translation or speech recognition tasks and achieved some promising results. 38 However, we notice that both CLM and MLM still remain some deficiencies in calculating sentence 39 scores. For instance, some works [22] utilized GPT-style model for ASR reranking within single 40 inference, yet GPT model can only extract unidirectional information due to the limitation of CLM, 41 without considering the whole sentence semantics, and thus affect the sentence score. To utilize 42 bidirectional context, some works [19, 20, 21] applied BERT model for rescoring. However, the 43 nature of MLM needs model to mask some tokens in the sentence for prediction, which means it 44 requires BERT model to forward multiple times that each forward only masks one token for prediction. 45 As a result, it is time-consuming to directly adopt MLM for scoring sentence. Overall, we find that for 46 calculating sentence score, CLM only needs once inference but only use unidirectional information 47 and MLM is costly in computing sentence score although it can use bidirectional context. Therefore, 48 we raise a natural question: is it possible to design a language modeling to use bidirectional context 49 for sentence scoring with only once inference pass. 50 To address the above issues, in this paper, we introduce Transcormer , a Transformer model designed 51 for sentence scoring. More specifically, our Transcormer leverages a novel language modeling, named 52 as sliding language modeling (SLM), that allows us to produce the probability of all tokens within 53 single inference pass, and simultaneously utilizes bidirectional context. To fulfill this target, we 54 innovatively design a triple-stream self-attention mechanism, which consists of two content streams (a 55 forward stream and a backward stream) and one query stream. By employing some specific-designed 56 mask strategies on the attention matrix, our method allows each token in the query stream to leverage 57 all token information except itself for estimating probability (the tokens before and after it) to avoid 58 any information leakage. To the best of our knowledge, SLM is the first language modeling tailored 59 for sentence scoring. We pre-train our SLM on large-scale corpus, and then evaluate it on multiple 60 datasets for reranking. Experimental results demonstrate that our Transcormer improves the baseline 61 by up to + 0.8/0.6 BLEU score on low-resource/rich-resource machine translation tasks and gives 62 20% relative improvements on ASR datasets. 63 The contributions of our paper can be summarized as: 64 • We analyze the pros and cons of CLM and MLM when using them for scoring sentences, and thus 65 propose Transcormer with a new sliding language modeling, which uses bidirectional context for 66 probability estimation within a single pass. 67 • We introduce a novel triple-stream self-attention mechanism in SLM, which has two content 68 streams to collect forward/backward semantics, and a query stream to estimate the probability of 69 each token in a sentence. 70 • Experimental results on multiple datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of our SLM 71 for sentence scoring. 72

2 Background 73 2.1 Sentence Scoring 74

 Sentence scoring has a long-term development history in NLP applications, especially in reranking 75 tasks ( e.g. , reranking for machine translation [23, 24, 25, 26] or speech recognition [27]). Generally, 76 given n -best candidates generated by text generation models, reranking aims at scoring each candidate 77 to select the best answer. Early works [23, 24, 25, 26] mainly used statistical LM to calculate the 78 sentence scores and some works [28, 29] tried to combine statistical LMs with RNN-based LM for 79 sentence scoring. Recently, directly using end-to-end neural network based LM has became the de 80 facto approach for scoring and has been widely used in many NLP tasks [2, 1, 30]. Specifically, 81 2 causal language modeling (CLM) and masked language modeling (MLM) are the most representative 82 language modeling methods, which are famous as GPT [8, 9, 10] and BERT [5] respectively. The 83 details of CLM and MLM are described in below. 84 CLM CLM is to model sequence based on the previous tokens in a chain-style rule. Assuming 85 the target sequence is y = { y 1 , · · · , y | y | } , where y i is the i -th token, so the probability estimation 86 function of CLM is (cid:80) | y | i =1 log P ( y i | y <i ) . Hence, CLM can obtain the probability of each token of 87 the sentence in a single pass, but can only capture unidirectional information. 88 MLM Unlike CLM, MLM use bidirectional context for prediction. Specifically, MLM replaces a 89 random subset of tokens as special symbol [MASK] for prediction. Assuming the masked subset as 90 y S and the corrupted sequence as y \S , the objective of MLM is to optimize (cid:80) |S| i =1 log P ( y S ⟩ | y \S ) . 91 However, MLM only supports partial prediction as each time we can only mask a subset for pre- 92 diction. To calculate the sentence score, a kind of solution [19, 20, 21] is to forward multiple times 93 and only mask one token each time. Therefore, the MLM for sentence scoring is formulated as: 94 (cid:80) | y | i =1 log P ( y i | y \ y i ) . We can find that the cost of MLM for scoring needs | y | inference passes, 95 which is too time-consuming. To alleviate this problem, some works attempted to use stochastic 96 estimation [19] or distillation [18] to approximately estimate the probability of each toke produced 97 by MLM, but the approximated score cannot precisely estimate the token probability. Therefore, how 98 to calculate sentence score efficiently is the main challenge in MLM. 99 Overall, CLM only needs a single forward pass to estimate the probability of all tokens but cannot 100 extract bidirectional context, while MLM leverages bidirectional information but needs multiple 101 inference passes. Consequently, we raise a natural question: is it possible to design a pre-trained lan- 102 guage model to support all token prediction in a single pass and simultaneously leverage bidirectional 103 information? This is exactly the motivation of our method. 104

2.2 Multiple-Stream Self-Attention

 105 The pioneer of multiple-stream self-attention is XLNet [11], which introduces two-streams self- 106 attention to incorporate autoregressive pre-training for language understanding. It pre-trains Trans- 107 former [17] in an autoregressive manner via maintaining a content stream and a query stream. In 108 details, for the t -th step, the content stream is able to capture the dependency from the tokens before 109 the t -th step and itself ( i.e. , x ≤ t ), while the query stream is only allowed to view the tokens before 110 the t -th step ( i.e. , x <t ) to avoid information leakage. Besides, there also remain some other variants 111 of two-stream self-attention [12, 31, 32]. For example, MPNet [12] used two-stream self-attention to 112 build masked and permuted pre-training. ProphetNet [31] designed multiple query streams to predict 113 N-gram future steps for sequence-to-sequence tasks, and ERNIE-GAN [32] proposed a multi-flow 114 generation model, which includes two query streams for span and word prediction. We observe these 115 works shared some similarities, that they used a single content stream and then used one or many 116 query streams to predict more information. Different from these works, we introduce a triple-stream 117 self-attention mechanism, which enables query stream to leverage two content streams for prediction, 118 and thus enjoys the benefits of additional bidirectional context for estimating token probability. 119

3.1 Sliding Language Modeling

 132 Considering the pros and cons of CLM and MLM for scoring, we notice: 1) CLM can produce 133 probability of all tokens within one forward pass, and thus obtain the unidirectional information of 134 the whole sentence; 2) MLM for sentence scoring needs multiple inference passes, so that many 135 context has been repeatedly calculated and cause a waste of computation. So, is it possible to reuse 136 token information to build bidirectional context for token prediction? 137 Therefore, we propose sliding language modeling (SLM) to address the inherent flaws in previous 138 LMs ( i.e. , CLM and MLM) for sentence scoring. As a result, our SLM enforces each token to only 142 capture the dependency from its past tokens and its future tokens concurrently, so that each token can 143 utilize the whole sentence information (except itself) to estimate token probability. 3.2 Triple-Stream Self-Attention

 153 Based on the Eqn 1 of our proposed SLM, model needs to maintain two states to collect forward and 154 backward contexts for prediction, and we call these two states as the forward stream and backward 155 stream respectively. To avoid information leakage, we additionally maintain an individual state for 156 prediction and control it to only capture the dependency from the forward and backward streams. We 157 name this state as the query stream. Therefore, we propose a novel Triple-Stream Self-Attention to 158 update each stream, and the detailed design is described as following. 159 To fulfill our target, we choose Transformer [17] as our basic model, due to its flexibility in cap- 160 turing global dependency. Assume the input sequence as { w 1 , w 2 , · · · , w n } and its positions as 161 { p 1 , p 2 , · · · , p n } , where w i and p i represents i -th token and its position in the sentence, and n is 162 the token number. For the query stream, we only use the positions { p 1 , p 2 , · · · , p n } as the input. 163 For the forward and backward streams, we also maintain two individual states and both of them use 164 the tokens plus its position ( i.e. , { w 1 + p 1 , w 2 + p 2 , · · · , w n + p n } ) as the input. 3.3 Discussion

 182 To better understand our SLM, we analyze the advantages of our SLM over other LMs. The 183 comparisons are listed in Table 1. We select three representative LMs for comparisons, which are 184 CLM (BERT), MLM (GPT) and bidirectional LM (Bi-LM, used in ELMO [34] 2 ) respectively. From 185 Table 1, we have the following observations: 186 1. When compared with CLM, our SLM requires 3 × computations. However, our SLM can fully use 187 the whole sentence information for prediction while CLM can only use unidirectional information. 188 Even scaling CLM as 3 × parameters, it still can not use bidirectional context for prediction. This 189 also demonstrates the effectiveness of our SLM in using context. 190 2. MLM is powerful at extracting bidirectional context but it needs n × inferences to calculate 191 the whole sentence information limited to its masked prediction. Our SLM just needs a single 192 inference and uses bidirectional information for prediction with only 3 × computations. Especially, 193 our SLM shows higher efficiency compared with MLM when n is large. 194 3. Bi-LM can also extract forward and backward contextual information, but it just simply concate- 195 nates the forward and backward features for the final prediction, without any interactions. Instead, 196 our SLM can iteratively fuse the bidirectional information thanks to our triple-stream self-attention 197 mechanism. 198 Overall, the design of SLM is dedicated for sentence scoring, while CLM prefers NLG tasks and 199 MLM/Bi-LM prefer NLU tasks. 200

5.2 Varying Numbers of Forward Passes in MLM 257

 As mentioned above, MLM needs to forward n passes as each time only mask one token. So what 258 will happen if we allow each pass to mask more tokens? Therefore, we design experiments that 259 enforces MLM to forward k tokens for prediction so that it only needs ⌈ n/k ⌉ passes, and investigate 260 the effect of different k . For a certain k , we randomly split the sentence as ⌈ n/k ⌉ sets, and each time 261 8 masks one subset for prediction. The comparisons are listed in Table 5. We find that using larger 262 k will severely harm the performance, even if it can reduce the number of inference passes. When 263 k is set as n 3 , which is equal to the cost of our SLM, it can hardly give any improvements over the 264 baseline. We think that masking more tokens in the sentence will make it more difficult to estimate 265 the token probability at a time. These comparisons also highlight the efficiency and effectiveness of 266 our SLM for sentence scoring. 267

6 Conclusion

 285 In this paper, we propose Transcormer, a Transformer with a novel sliding language modeling for 286 sentence scoring. Specifically, our SLM is able to produce the probability of each token over the 287 whole sentence within a single forward pass, and utilizes bidirectional context for prediction, and 288 thus inherents the advantages of CLM and MLM and avoids their deficiencies. To the best of our 289 knowledge, the proposed Transcormer is the first pre-trained language model tailored for sentence 290 scoring. Experimental results on multiple datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our Transcormer 291 in computing sentence score for reranking tasks . 292 Besides , we summarize some potential directions of our Transcormer and SLM as the future works: 293 • Currently our Transcormer is only conducted on the English domain under the base setting, due to 294 limited computation. We expect to develop large-scale Transcormer and use different language 295 domains or multilingual data for training in the future. 296 • We design sliding language modeling for sentence scoring, and our experiments are mainly on 297 reranking task. However, based on the characteristics of our SLM, we believe our model can 298 also be used for other scenarios ( e.g. , correction, data selection), and we will explore the specific 299 fine-tuning techniques when applying our SLM on different downstream tasks. 300 • Besides, our Transcormer mainly pre-trains SLM on an encoder framework. However, our SLM 301 is not limited to model structure. For example, SLM can be easily extended to encoder-decoder 302 framework based on paired data. Therefore, we also expect to explore the possibility of using SLM 303 on different frameworks. 304 • Although our paper mainly focuses on text data, we want to highlight that SLM can also be extended 305 to other different modalities with sequential characteristic ( e.g. , image, speech and time series data). 306 Consequently, how to apply SLM to other modalities is also a valuable topic in the future. 307 9
