### Summary

This paper introduces Arenabench, a framework for automatically updating benchmarks to prevent data leakage and maintain relevance as models advance. Arenabench identifies core abilities in test cases, generates new question-answer pairs, and validates them using an LLM judge. It iteratively refines benchmarks based on feedback from multiple models, ensuring that updates expose shared weaknesses without being biased towards any single model. The framework is applied to math, commonsense reasoning, and safety domains, producing more challenging and fair benchmarks that better differentiate model capabilities.

#### comment

1. The method relies on the assumption that there is a shared weakness among the models in the pool. This assumption may not always hold true, especially when the model pool is small or diverse. The paper does not adequately address scenarios where models might exhibit orthogonal failure modes, potentially limiting the effectiveness of the benchmark updates in such cases. For instance, if one model struggles with numerical reasoning while another falters on logical deductions, the method might not generate test cases that effectively target both weaknesses simultaneously.

2. The method heavily depends on the quality of the LLM-based generator and verifier. Any biases or limitations inherent in these components could propagate through the benchmark evolution process, affecting the validity of the updated benchmarks. The paper lacks a thorough analysis of how these biases might manifest and how they could be mitigated. For example, if the LLM generator has a preference for certain types of questions, the updated benchmark might become skewed towards those types, failing to provide a comprehensive evaluation.

3. The computational requirements of ARENABENCHER, particularly the multi-model feedback loop and iterative refinement, could limit its scalability to very large benchmarks or model pools. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for each step, making it difficult to assess its practical applicability. The absence of a complexity analysis makes it hard to determine the feasibility of applying this method to real-world scenarios with extensive benchmarks and numerous models.

4. Lack of ablation studies to justify the design choices, e.g., the number of candidates to generate, the size of the model subset to sample, the number of refinement rounds, etc. Without these studies, it is unclear how sensitive the method is to these parameters and whether the chosen values are optimal. The paper should include experiments that systematically vary these parameters to understand their impact on the performance of the method.

5. Lack of baselines to compare with. The paper does not compare the proposed method with existing benchmark augmentation techniques, making it difficult to assess its relative advantages and disadvantages. The paper should include comparisons with relevant methods to demonstrate the superiority of the proposed approach.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more rigorous exploration of the assumptions underlying the method. Specifically, the assumption of shared weaknesses among models needs further scrutiny. The authors should investigate how the method performs when models exhibit diverse failure modes. One approach could involve creating synthetic model pools with varying degrees of orthogonality in their weaknesses and evaluating the effectiveness of the benchmark updates in these scenarios. Additionally, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the types of weaknesses that are commonly shared among models, and how these shared weaknesses relate to the core abilities being tested. This analysis would provide a deeper understanding of the method's strengths and limitations.

Furthermore, the paper needs a more thorough investigation into the potential biases introduced by the LLM-based generator and verifier. The authors should analyze the types of questions generated by the LLM and assess whether there is a skew towards certain categories or formats. They should also explore the impact of different LLMs on the benchmark evolution process. For example, they could compare the performance of the method using different LLMs for generation and verification. Additionally, the paper should include a discussion of how the quality of the LLM impacts the overall performance of the method. This analysis would help to establish the robustness of the method and identify potential areas for improvement.

Finally, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the method. The authors should provide a breakdown of the computational resources required for each step, including the generation, verification, and multi-model feedback loops. They should also compare the computational cost of the proposed method with existing benchmark augmentation techniques. This analysis would help to assess the scalability of the method and identify potential bottlenecks. Moreover, the paper should include ablation studies to justify the design choices, such as the number of candidates to generate, the size of the model subset to sample, and the number of refinement rounds. These studies would provide a better understanding of the method's sensitivity to these parameters and help to optimize its performance.
