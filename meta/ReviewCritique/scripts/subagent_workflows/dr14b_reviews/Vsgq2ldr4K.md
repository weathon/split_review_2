### Summary

The paper proposes a new inference-time sampling method for LLMs. The authors propose a target distribution (called the power distribution) for reasoning tasks and show that sampling from this distribution is different from low-temperature sampling. Then, they use MCMC to sample from an unnormalized version of this target distribution. The authors show that their method outperforms the base model and low-temperature sampling on 3 reasoning benchmarks and is competitive with RLHF on one of the benchmarks.

### Strengths

- The paper is well written and the method is well motivated.
- The method is training-free and can be applied to any model.
- The method outperforms the base model and low-temperature sampling on 3 reasoning benchmarks.
- The method maintains diversity over multiple samples, unlike RLHF.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more thorough comparison against a wider range of inference-time methods. Specifically, the authors should include comparisons to best-of-K sampling, speculative decoding, and tree-of-thoughts. These methods represent common and effective strategies for improving the quality of LLM outputs at inference time, and a comparison would help to contextualize the performance of the proposed method. For example, best-of-K sampling, while computationally expensive, is a strong baseline for evaluating the quality of a single response, and it would be useful to see how the proposed method compares in terms of both performance and computational cost. Similarly, speculative decoding is a method that aims to reduce the computational cost of sampling, and it would be interesting to see if the proposed method can achieve similar performance with a similar computational budget. Finally, tree-of-thoughts is a method that explicitly explores multiple reasoning paths, and it would be useful to see how the proposed method compares in terms of its ability to find correct solutions. These comparisons would provide a more complete picture of the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method.

Furthermore, the paper should include a more comprehensive comparison against a broader range of RL-based methods. While the authors compare their method to RLPO, it is important to compare against other popular RL methods such as DPO, RPO, and RLHF. These methods represent different approaches to RL training, and a comparison would help to determine if the proposed method is specifically more competitive with verifiable reward-based methods or if it is broadly competitive with RL methods in general. For example, DPO and RPO are methods that do not rely on a separate reward model, and it would be useful to see how the proposed method compares to these methods. Additionally, RLHF is a widely used method that incorporates human feedback, and it would be useful to see how the proposed method compares to this method. The authors should also investigate whether the competitive performance of the proposed method is due to its training-free nature or if there is some other underlying reason. This could be done by comparing the proposed method to RL methods that also do not have training instability issues.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. The current analysis only shows that the number of tokens generated is proportional to $T^2$, but it does not provide a clear comparison to the number of tokens generated by the baseline methods. The authors should provide a table that shows the number of tokens generated by the proposed method and the baseline methods for different values of T. This would help to understand the practical implications of the computational cost of the proposed method. Additionally, the authors should investigate the relationship between the number of tokens generated and the performance of the proposed method. It would be useful to see if there is a point of diminishing returns where increasing the number of tokens does not lead to a significant improvement in performance. This would help to determine the optimal number of tokens to use for the proposed method.
