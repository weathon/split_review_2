### Suggestions

The authors should consider reframing their work as a more traditional research paper, focusing on the novel aspects of their methodology and findings, rather than solely on refuting a previous response. While the identification of flaws in existing work is valuable, it needs to be coupled with a clear presentation of new scientific contributions. For instance, if the authors have developed a new analysis technique or have identified a previously unknown phenomenon, this should be the central focus of the paper, with the rebuttal serving as supporting evidence. The current structure makes it read more like a letter to the editor, which is not the typical format for a TPAMI publication. The authors could also consider expanding their analysis to include additional datasets or experiments to further support their claims, which would strengthen the paper's contribution.

To improve the paper's suitability for TPAMI, the authors should expand on the technical details of their approach. This includes providing a more in-depth explanation of the data collection process, the specific analysis methods used, and the rationale behind their choices. For example, if they are using a specific statistical test, they should justify its selection and discuss its limitations. Furthermore, they should clearly articulate the novelty of their approach compared to existing methods. This could involve a detailed comparison with other techniques, highlighting the advantages and disadvantages of each. The paper should also include a more thorough discussion of the implications of their findings, exploring how they contribute to the broader field and what future research directions they suggest. This would help to elevate the paper beyond a simple rebuttal and position it as a significant contribution to the literature.

Finally, the authors should consider adding more quantitative results and visualizations to support their claims. While they point out flaws in the previous response, they should also provide concrete evidence of the validity of their own findings. This could include tables, graphs, or other visual aids that clearly demonstrate the results of their analysis. For example, if they are claiming that a certain method is flawed, they should provide quantitative evidence of this flaw, rather than just stating it. This would make the paper more convincing and easier to evaluate. The paper should also include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their own work, acknowledging any potential biases or uncertainties in their findings. This would demonstrate a more balanced and rigorous approach to the research.
