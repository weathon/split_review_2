### Summary

This paper introduces AtlasKV, a framework for integrating large-scale knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) with minimal GPU memory usage. AtlasKV addresses the limitations of existing retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods, which rely on external retrieval modules and long context priors, leading to high inference latency. The key innovations of AtlasKV include KG2KV, which converts KG triples into high-quality query-key-value (Q-K-V) data, and HiKVP, a hierarchical key-value pruning algorithm that reduces computational and memory overhead while maintaining accuracy. AtlasKV achieves superior knowledge grounding and generalization performance compared to existing methods, making it a scalable and efficient solution for integrating billion-scale KGs into LLMs.

1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of AtlasKV, which is important for understanding its scalability and efficiency. Specifically, the paper lacks a breakdown of the time and space complexity of the KG2KV conversion process and the HiKVP algorithm, making it difficult to assess the practical limitations of the approach, especially when dealing with very large knowledge graphs. A more rigorous analysis, including the dependence on the size of the knowledge graph, the number of layers in the HiKVP, and the dimensionality of the key-value pairs, is needed.
2. The paper does not discuss the potential limitations of AtlasKV in terms of the types of knowledge that can be integrated and the domains in which it can be applied. For example, it is unclear how AtlasKV would handle knowledge graphs with complex relationships or those that require reasoning beyond simple fact retrieval. The paper also does not address the potential for bias in the knowledge graphs and how this might affect the performance of the model. A discussion of these limitations is needed to fully understand the scope of the proposed method.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of different hyperparameters on the performance of AtlasKV, which is important for understanding its robustness and generalizability. For instance, the paper does not explore the sensitivity of the model to the number of layers in the HiKVP pruning process or the size of the key-value pairs generated by KG2KV. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to determine the optimal configuration for different datasets and tasks.

### Suggestions

To address the lack of computational complexity analysis, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the time and space complexity for each component of AtlasKV. This should include an analysis of the KG2KV conversion process, detailing the computational cost of transforming knowledge graph triples into query-key-value pairs, as well as the HiKVP pruning process, including the cost of hierarchical clustering and key-value selection. The analysis should consider the impact of the size of the knowledge graph, the number of layers in the HiKVP, and the dimensionality of the key-value pairs on the overall computational cost. Furthermore, the authors should provide empirical results demonstrating the actual runtime and memory usage of AtlasKV on different datasets and with varying hyperparameter settings. This would provide a more concrete understanding of the practical scalability of the approach and allow for a more informed assessment of its efficiency.

To address the limitations regarding the types of knowledge and domains, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the types of knowledge graphs that AtlasKV is designed to handle and the potential challenges of applying it to more complex knowledge graphs. This should include an analysis of how AtlasKV would handle knowledge graphs with multi-hop relationships, logical constraints, or those that require more complex reasoning. The authors should also discuss the potential for bias in the knowledge graphs and how this might affect the performance of the model. This could include an analysis of the sensitivity of AtlasKV to biased training data and potential mitigation strategies. Furthermore, the authors should provide examples of real-world scenarios where AtlasKV would be particularly effective and where it might face limitations, providing a more nuanced understanding of the applicability of the approach.

To address the lack of hyperparameter analysis, the authors should conduct a more thorough investigation of the impact of different hyperparameters on the performance of AtlasKV. This should include an analysis of the sensitivity of the model to the number of layers in the HiKVP pruning process, the size of the key-value pairs generated by KG2KV, and the learning rate used during training. The authors should provide empirical results demonstrating how these hyperparameters affect the knowledge grounding accuracy and generalization performance of AtlasKV. This analysis should also include a discussion of the trade-offs between different hyperparameter settings and provide guidance on how to choose the optimal configuration for different datasets and tasks. This would provide a more robust understanding of the model's behavior and allow for better generalization to new scenarios.
