### Summary

This paper proposes a method to address the issue of dominant components suppressing minor ones in contrastive learning for time-series representation. The authors introduce Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a measure of component recoverability and integrate it into an adaptive weighting strategy for view-specific contrastive objectives. The approach can be incorporated into existing frameworks without architectural changes and shows improved forecasting accuracy and representational robustness in experiments.

### Strengths

1. The paper addresses a specific and relevant problem in time-series representation learning where dominant semantic components overshadow minor ones, which has practical implications for improving model robustness and accuracy in real-world applications like forecasting.
2. The introduction of SDE as a directional measure of component recoverability is a novel contribution to the field of contrastive learning for time series.

1. The paper primarily focuses on forecasting accuracy as the evaluation metric, which may not fully capture the quality of the learned representations. Including evaluations on downstream tasks like anomaly detection or classification could provide a more comprehensive assessment.
2. The paper could benefit from more detailed ablation studies to isolate the impact of SDE and the adaptive weighting strategy from other components of the proposed method.
3. The paper lacks a thorough analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the proposed method, especially when applied to large-scale time-series datasets.
4. The paper could be improved by providing more insights into how the proposed method affects the learned embeddings and by visualizing the differences between representations learned with and without the proposed approach.
5. The paper's explanation of the SDE metric and its integration into the training process could be more detailed and clearer, especially for readers unfamiliar with the specific notations and concepts used in time-series analysis.

### Suggestions

To strengthen the evaluation, the authors should include experiments on downstream tasks beyond forecasting, such as anomaly detection or classification. These tasks would provide a more holistic view of the learned representations' quality and generalizability. For instance, the learned embeddings could be used as input features for a classifier trained to distinguish between different types of time series, or for an anomaly detection algorithm that identifies unusual patterns. This would demonstrate the versatility of the proposed method and its ability to capture meaningful semantic information beyond just forecasting performance. Furthermore, the evaluation should include a comparison with other state-of-the-art time-series representation learning methods on these downstream tasks to provide a more comprehensive benchmark.

To better understand the contribution of each component, the authors should conduct more detailed ablation studies. Specifically, they should isolate the impact of the SDE metric and the adaptive weighting strategy. This could involve experiments where the SDE metric is removed, or where a fixed weighting strategy is used instead of the adaptive one. The ablation studies should also explore the sensitivity of the method to different hyperparameter settings, such as the weighting parameters in the adaptive weighting strategy. This would provide a more granular understanding of how each component contributes to the overall performance and robustness of the proposed method. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the training process, including the convergence behavior of the model with and without the proposed components.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the SDE metric and its integration into the training process. This should include a clear definition of the SDE metric, its mathematical formulation, and its relationship to the contrastive loss function. The explanation should also include a step-by-step description of how the SDE metric is calculated and how it is used to adjust the weights of the contrastive objectives. Furthermore, the authors should provide visualizations of the learned embeddings to illustrate the differences between representations learned with and without the proposed approach. This would help to provide a more intuitive understanding of how the proposed method affects the learned representations and how it improves the disentanglement of semantic components.
