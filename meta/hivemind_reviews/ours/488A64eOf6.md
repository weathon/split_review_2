## Summary

This paper proposes DAMON (Decoding as Direct Metrics Optimization), a principled framework that formulates LM decoding as a constrained optimization problem: minimize reverse KL divergence from the base LM distribution while matching expected scores of multiple human-aligned evaluation metrics. The optimal solution takes an analytical EBM form p(x) ∝ p_θ(x) exp[-μ^T f(x)], and a key theoretical result (Proposition 2) proves this induced distribution strictly improves perplexity on human texts relative to the original LM. A practical SIR-based sampling algorithm enables tractable generation from the globally normalized distribution. Experiments on Wikipedia and News domains with GPT-2 XL (1.5B) and OPT-6.7B show consistent improvements over strong baselines on repetition, coherence, diversity, information content, and MAUVE, corroborated by human evaluation.

## Strengths

1. **Principled formulation with analytical solution (Proposition 1, §3.1).** The paper derives a closed-form solution to the constrained optimization: p_{θ,μ}(x) ∝ p_θ(x) exp[-μ^T f(x)], which scales the base LM distribution via a sequence-level energy function defined by the chosen metrics. This provides a clean, differentiable framework for multi-aspect control without heuristic trade-offs.

2. **Theoretical guarantee of perplexity improvement (Proposition 2, §3.2).** The proof shows that the optimal decoding distribution strictly improves perplexity on human texts over the base LM, and its support covers that of the human distribution. This is a formal guarantee that no prior heuristic decoding method (truncation, contrastive decoding, beam search) can provide, and it directly supports the claim that the induced distribution is a better approximation to the underlying human text distribution.

3. **Consistent and substantial empirical gains across domains and model scales (Table 1, §4.5).** DAMON is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-human scores on all five reported metrics (sr-4, tr-32, coh, div, e^ent) and the highest MAUVE score across both Wikipedia and News domains with GPT-2 XL and OPT-6.7B. For example, on Wikipedia with GPT-2 XL, sr-4=0.42 (reference 0.48) versus the best baseline at 0.81 (Typical); MAUVE=88.1 versus the next best 83.3 (CS).

4. **Rigorous human evaluation with statistically significant win rates (Table 2, §4.4).** Pairwise comparisons against CD, CS, Nucleus, and Typical on Fluency, Coherence, and Informativeness all favor DAMON, with most wins significant at p<0.005 (e.g., vs. Nucleus: 0.66 win rate in Coherence). This provides direct evidence that the automatic metric alignment translates to perceived quality improvements.

5. **Ablation studies validating the necessity of each constraint (Table 4, §5).** Removing any metric set causes substantial degradation on the corresponding aspect (e.g., sr-4 jumps from 0.42 to 3.57 when sr-n metrics are removed; coherence drops from 62.5 to 57.6 when coherence is removed), confirming that the constrained optimization actively drives alignment rather than relying on side effects.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

None. The paper's core claims are well-supported by theory and experiment.

### Minor

1. **Development set sensitivity not analyzed.** The coefficient estimation (Algorithm 1) relies on a single 512-sample development set to estimate expectations under p_d and fit the coefficients μ (lines 190, 306). For high-dimensional metrics like coherence (via SimCSE), these estimates have nontrivial variance, and the paper does not report robustness across different development splits or bootstrap confidence intervals for μ. While the human evaluation provides external validation that the method works, the stability of the estimates is an open question.

2. **WIS effective sample size for μ estimation not characterized.** Algorithm 1 samples trajectories from p_θ (no temperature modulation) for importance-weighted estimation of the target expectations. If the optimal EBM distribution p_{θ,μ} deviates sharply from p_θ (e.g., due to strong metric constraints with large μ), the effective sample size of the WIS estimator could be small, potentially biasing μ (line 222, Algorithm 1). The paper invokes asymptotic results (bias ∝ N^{-1}) but provides no diagnostic analysis (e.g., effective sample size or importance weight distribution) to verify that the proposal adequately covers the target for the specific metrics and μ values encountered.

3. **Overlap between constraint metrics and automatic evaluation metrics.** The same metrics used as constraints (repetition, coherence, diversity, information content) are also used for automatic evaluation (except MAUVE, lines 321–322). This creates a degree of circularity: the method is evaluated on how well it matches the constraints it was designed to satisfy. The human evaluation (§4.4) properly breaks this circularity, and the paper acknowledges the issue. Nevertheless, the automatic evaluation tables are less informative than they would be if held-out metrics were also reported.

### Trivial

- **Proof sketch could be more explicit.** The proof of Proposition 2 (line 130) assumes p_α = (1-α)q_opt + α p_d ∈ C for α∈[0,1]. This requires that p_d ∈ C, which holds trivially because the constraints are E_{x~p_d}[f_k(x)] = E_{x~p_d}[f_k(x)], but this is not explicitly noted.
- **No dedicated limitations section.** Limitations (development set reliance, SIR approximation error, metric quality dependence) are mentioned only briefly in future work (lines 517–520).

## Novel Insights

The two reviews converge on the same assessment: the paper's core strength is its principled optimization framing that yields both theoretical guarantees (perplexity improvement) and strong empirical results. The weakness, articulated independently by both the Harsh Critic and implied by the structure of the experiments, is that the method's practical success depends on reliable estimation of expectations from a modest development set, and the paper does not fully characterize the sensitivity of its estimates to this choice. The human evaluation is the key mitigating factor, and both reviews treat it as such. Neither review identifies a fatal flaw; both agree the contribution is significant and well-executed.

## Suggestions

- **Report robustness of μ estimates.** Run the coefficient estimation on multiple 512-sample splits of the development data and report the variance of final metric scores. This would address the primary methodological open question.
- **Add effective sample size diagnostics.** For the WIS-based μ estimation, report the effective sample size or the distribution of importance weights to confirm that the proposal p_θ provides adequate coverage of the target EBM distribution.
- **Include one held-out metric for automatic evaluation.** Even a simple additional metric (e.g., perplexity under a different LM, or sentiment polarity) would strengthen the claim that optimization on the chosen metrics generalizes beyond direct constraint satisfaction.
