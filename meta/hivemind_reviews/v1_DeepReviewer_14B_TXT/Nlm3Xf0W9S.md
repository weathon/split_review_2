## summary
This paper introduces MARWL, a novel watermarking framework specifically designed for order-agnostic language models (LMs). Unlike traditional LMs that generate text sequentially, order-agnostic LMs produce content without a fixed left-to-right sequence, posing unique challenges for watermarking. MARWL addresses this challenge by employing a Markov-chain-based key sequence generator and a statistical pattern-based detection algorithm. The key contributions of this work include a new watermarking method that does not depend on sequential generation, a Markov-chain-based key sequence generator that produces high-frequency key patterns, and a statistical pattern-based detection algorithm that can recover the key sequence and perform statistical tests to detect watermarks with a controlled false positive rate. The authors present extensive experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of MARWL in terms of detection efficiency, generation quality, and robustness against attacks. The experiments are conducted on two specific order-agnostic LMs: ProteinMPNN, used for protein generation, and CMLM, used for machine translation. The results show that MARWL outperforms baseline methods like Soft watermark, Multikey watermark, and Unigram watermark in these tasks. The core idea of MARWL involves embedding a watermark by modulating the token probabilities based on a key sequence generated by a Markov chain. During detection, the key sequence is recovered from the generated content, and statistical tests are performed on the occurrences of specific key patterns to determine the presence of the watermark. The authors also provide a dynamic programming algorithm to compute the probability distribution of pattern occurrences in the generated sequences, which is crucial for controlling the false positive rate. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of watermarking for order-agnostic LMs, addressing a previously unexplored problem with a novel and effective approach. The experimental results demonstrate the practical viability of the proposed method, highlighting its potential for real-world applications. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of computational complexity analysis, robustness evaluation, and generalizability.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First and foremost, the paper addresses a novel and important problem: watermarking order-agnostic language models. This is a domain that has not been extensively explored in prior work, and the authors have identified a critical gap in the existing literature. The traditional watermarking techniques that rely on sequential generation are not directly applicable to order-agnostic LMs, making the development of a new approach essential. The proposed MARWL framework is a creative and well-designed solution to this challenge. The use of a Markov chain to generate key sequences and a statistical pattern-based detection algorithm is a novel approach that effectively addresses the non-sequential nature of order-agnostic LMs. The Markov chain allows for the generation of high-frequency key patterns, which are then used to embed the watermark into the generated content. The statistical detection algorithm, based on hypothesis testing, provides a rigorous method for detecting the presence of the watermark with a controlled false positive rate. The authors also provide a dynamic programming algorithm to compute the probability distribution of pattern occurrences, which is crucial for the statistical detection process. The experimental evaluation of MARWL is another strength of this paper. The authors conduct extensive experiments on two different tasks: protein generation and machine translation, using ProteinMPNN and CMLM models, respectively. These experiments cover various aspects, including detection efficiency, generation quality, and robustness against attacks. The results demonstrate that MARWL outperforms baseline methods in terms of detection efficiency, while maintaining high generation quality. The robustness experiments, which include random token modification and paraphrasing attacks, further demonstrate the effectiveness of MARWL in real-world scenarios. The paper is also well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the proposed method and the experimental results. The authors provide a detailed explanation of the method, including the mathematical formulation and algorithms, and the experimental setup and results are presented in a clear and concise manner. The inclusion of an ablation study, which examines the effect of pattern length, is also a positive aspect of the paper, as it provides insights into the sensitivity of the method to different parameter settings. Overall, I believe that the paper makes a significant contribution to the field of watermarking for order-agnostic LMs, and the proposed MARWL framework is a promising approach for addressing the challenges posed by these models.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed MARWL framework. While the authors mention that the algorithms are efficient, a formal analysis of the time and space complexity is missing. Specifically, the paper does not provide a breakdown of the computational cost associated with the Markov-chain-based key sequence generator and the statistical pattern-based detection algorithm. This makes it difficult to assess the practical scalability of the method, especially for large-scale language models and datasets. For instance, the paper mentions the time complexity of the algorithm for calculating the probability distribution of pattern occurrences as $O(n^2l^m)$, where $n$ is the sequence length, $l$ is the size of the key space, and $m$ is the pattern length. However, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how the complexity scales with the size of the vocabulary, which is a crucial factor in practical applications. This lack of analysis makes it hard to determine the feasibility of using MARWL in real-time applications or with very large models. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly lacks this analysis. Second, the paper could benefit from a more in-depth discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors evaluate robustness against random token modification and paraphrasing attacks, the paper does not explore the potential vulnerabilities of the framework to adversarial attacks that specifically target the statistical patterns used for detection. For example, the method relies on specific key patterns generated by the Markov chain, which could be vulnerable to attacks that disrupt these patterns. The paper also does not discuss the sensitivity of the method to different parameter settings, such as the size of the key space or the length of the key sequences, beyond the ablation study on pattern length. A more thorough analysis of these limitations would provide a more balanced view of the method's applicability. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does touch on robustness and parameter sensitivity, but not in sufficient detail. Third, the paper focuses on two specific order-agnostic LMs, ProteinMPNN and CMLM, and it would be helpful to discuss the generalizability of the proposed method to other order-agnostic LMs and different types of tasks. The paper does not provide sufficient evidence to support the claim that the method can be easily adapted to other models with different architectures or training objectives. It is unclear if the method is equally effective across different domains or if it requires specific adjustments for each new application. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's experiments are limited to two specific models. Fourth, the method relies on a Markov chain model, which may not be suitable for capturing the complex dependencies in the output of language models. Specifically, the assumption that the next state depends only on the current state might be too simplistic for language models, where long-range dependencies are crucial. This could lead to the watermark being easily removed or altered without significantly affecting the output distribution, thus compromising the robustness of the watermarking scheme. The experimental results, which show a decrease in TPR under attacks, support this concern. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly states the use of a Markov chain and the experimental results show a decrease in TPR under attacks. Fifth, the method requires the recovery of the key sequence from the generated content for watermark detection. The effectiveness of the method depends on the accuracy of this recovery process. The paper does not provide sufficient detail on the key recovery mechanism, making it difficult to assess its reliability. If the recovery process is prone to errors, especially in the presence of noise or adversarial attacks, the overall performance of the watermarking scheme will be significantly degraded. While the paper mentions key recovery and provides a high-level description of how it's done, it lacks a detailed analysis of the error rate or reliability of this process, particularly under noisy conditions or adversarial attacks. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does describe the key recovery process, but lacks a detailed analysis of its accuracy. Finally, the method requires prior knowledge of the vocabulary split and the transition matrix of the Markov chain used for watermark generation. This could limit its applicability in practical scenarios where this information is not available. In real-world applications, the attacker might not have access to the exact vocabulary split or the transition matrix, making it challenging to implement the watermarking scheme effectively. This reliance on specific internal parameters makes the method less flexible and potentially vulnerable to changes in the model or its configuration. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly states the requirement for a vocabulary split and a transition matrix, and does not address scenarios where this information is unavailable.

## suggestions

This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and space complexity of both the watermark generation and detection processes. Specifically, the authors should analyze how the complexity scales with key parameters such as the length of the generated sequence, the size of the vocabulary, and the dimension of the Markov chain. This analysis should also consider the memory requirements for storing the key sequences and intermediate results. Furthermore, the authors should provide empirical measurements of the runtime and memory usage for different parameter settings to validate their theoretical analysis. This would provide a clearer understanding of the practical limitations of the method and guide users in selecting appropriate parameter values for their specific applications. Second, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive evaluation of the robustness of MARWL against a wider range of attacks. This should include not only token modifications but also more sophisticated attacks such as paraphrasing, reordering, and adversarial perturbations. For each type of attack, the authors should analyze how the detection rate and false positive rate are affected, and discuss the underlying reasons for these changes. For example, the authors could investigate how the statistical properties of the key patterns are altered by different types of attacks and how this impacts the detection algorithm. Additionally, the authors should explore the trade-offs between watermark robustness and generation quality, providing a more detailed analysis of how different parameter settings affect both aspects. This could involve varying the strength of the watermark and analyzing its impact on metrics such as BLEU score or protein diversity. This would provide a more nuanced understanding of the limitations of the method and guide users in selecting appropriate parameter values for their specific applications. Third, to address the generalizability of the proposed method, the authors should conduct experiments on a wider range of order-agnostic LMs and tasks. For example, the authors could evaluate the performance of MARWL on models used for time series forecasting, speech generation, or other tasks where order-agnostic LMs are commonly used. Furthermore, the authors should investigate whether any adjustments or modifications are needed to adapt the method to different types of models or tasks. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the generalizability of the method and guide users in applying it to new applications. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of the key recovery process to errors in the generated content. Finally, to enhance the practical applicability of the method, the authors should explore techniques that reduce the reliance on prior knowledge of the vocabulary split and the transition matrix. One possible approach is to use a more robust watermarking scheme that is less sensitive to variations in these parameters.
