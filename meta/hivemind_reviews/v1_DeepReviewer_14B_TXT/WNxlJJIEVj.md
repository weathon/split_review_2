## summary
This paper introduces Contrastive Diffuser (CDiffuser), a novel offline reinforcement learning (RL) method designed to effectively leverage low-return trajectories, which are often underutilized in traditional offline RL approaches. The core idea behind CDiffuser is to employ a contrastive learning framework, where states from high-return trajectories are treated as positive samples, and states from low-return trajectories are treated as negative samples. This contrastive mechanism is integrated into a diffusion-based planning module, guiding the agent's trajectory towards high-return states and away from low-return states. The method operates by first grouping states from offline trajectories into high-return and low-return categories based on the cumulative reward of the trajectory. These groups are then used to train a contrastive model that learns to distinguish between desirable and undesirable states. The planning module, based on a diffusion model, uses this contrastive model to generate trajectories that are more likely to lead to high returns. Specifically, the diffusion model is conditioned on the current state and then guided by the contrastive model to generate a sequence of states that are similar to high-return states and dissimilar to low-return states. The paper presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation of CDiffuser on 14 D4RL benchmark datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to several strong baselines, including CQL, IQL, and other diffusion-based methods. The results suggest that CDiffuser effectively leverages low-return trajectories to improve policy learning in offline RL settings. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of CDiffuser, further supporting the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Overall, the paper presents a novel and promising approach to offline RL, addressing a key challenge in the field by effectively utilizing low-return data. The empirical results are compelling, and the method is well-motivated. However, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of theoretical analysis and computational considerations.
