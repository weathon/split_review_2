## summary
This paper introduces a novel domain adaptation approach for image restoration, leveraging the unique properties of diffusion models to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world data. The core idea revolves around performing domain adaptation in the noise space of a diffusion model, rather than directly in the image space. The authors propose a diffusion loss that guides the restoration model to align the distributions of restored synthetic and real-world images with a target clean distribution. This is achieved by conditioning a diffusion model on the output of a restoration network and using the diffusion model's ability to predict noise as a proxy for the quality of the restoration. The method also incorporates channel-shuffling and residual-swapping contrastive learning to prevent shortcut learning during joint training. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on three classical image restoration tasks: denoising, deblurring, and deraining. The experimental results show that the proposed method outperforms existing domain adaptation techniques, highlighting its potential for improving the generalization of image restoration models to real-world scenarios. The paper's main contribution lies in its innovative use of diffusion models for domain adaptation in the noise space, which offers a new perspective on addressing the challenges of generalizing image restoration models to real-world data. The method is designed to be general and flexible, applicable to various image restoration tasks without requiring prior knowledge of noise distribution or degradation models. The authors present a comprehensive evaluation of their method, demonstrating its effectiveness across different image restoration tasks. The results show significant improvements over existing methods, highlighting the practical value of the approach. The paper's approach of using a diffusion model as a proxy for domain adaptation is a novel contribution to the field, and the experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach. The authors also introduce techniques to prevent shortcut learning, which is a common problem in joint training of diffusion models. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and thoroughly evaluated method for domain adaptation in image restoration, making a valuable contribution to the field.
