## summary
This paper introduces FiDeLiS, a novel retrieval-augmented reasoning method designed to enhance knowledge graph question answering (KGQA). The core idea behind FiDeLiS is to anchor responses to structured, verifiable reasoning paths, thereby improving the reliability and interpretability of large language model (LLM) reasoning in KGQA. The method consists of two primary components: Path-RAG and Deductive-Verification Beam Search (DVBS). Path-RAG is responsible for retrieving chains of entities and relations from the knowledge graph (KG), while DVBS generates multiple reasoning paths leading to the final answer. The approach begins by extracting keywords from the input query using an LLM, which are then used to retrieve relevant entities and relations from a vector-based index of the KG. This process aims to ensure high-recall retrieval of potentially useful information. The retrieved entities and relations are then used to construct candidate reasoning paths, which are scored based on a combination of relation and entity scores, as well as the scores of their neighboring nodes. The top-m reasoning steps are selected based on this score. DVBS then takes these candidate paths and employs a beam search algorithm to generate multiple reasoning paths. A key innovation in DVBS is the use of deductive verification, where an LLM assesses the validity of each reasoning step, ensuring that the generated paths are logically sound and grounded in the KG. This deductive verification process helps to avoid misleading reasoning chains and reduces unnecessary computational demand. The method also incorporates a planning step, where an LLM generates a plan for answering the query, which guides the subsequent reasoning process. The authors evaluate FiDeLiS on three datasets: WebQSP, CWQ, and CR-LT, demonstrating that it outperforms several strong baselines. The empirical results show that FiDeLiS achieves higher accuracy and F1 scores compared to existing methods, highlighting its effectiveness in improving the reliability and interpretability of LLM reasoning in KGQA. The authors also present an efficiency analysis, showing that FiDeLiS has lower average runtime and token usage compared to the ToG baseline. Overall, the paper presents a well-structured and empirically validated approach to KGQA, with a focus on grounding LLM reasoning in verifiable paths within the knowledge graph. The method's training-free nature and its ability to enhance the interpretability of LLM reasoning are significant contributions to the field.

## strengths
The paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall impact. First, the proposed method, FiDeLiS, is a training-free approach, which is a significant advantage in terms of computational efficiency and generalizability. This means that the method can be applied to different KGQA tasks without the need for extensive training, making it more accessible and adaptable. Second, the method demonstrates superior performance compared to strong baselines across three datasets, highlighting its effectiveness in improving the reliability and interpretability of LLM reasoning in KGQA. The empirical results, as shown in Table 1, consistently demonstrate that FiDeLiS achieves higher accuracy and F1 scores than existing methods, which is a strong indicator of its effectiveness. The use of a keyword-enhanced retrieval mechanism in Path-RAG is another strength, as it ensures high-recall retrieval of relevant entities and relations from the KG. This mechanism, which leverages an LLM to extract keywords from the query, helps to identify the most pertinent information for reasoning. The deductive verification process in DVBS is also a key strength, as it allows the LLM to assess the validity of each reasoning step, ensuring that the generated paths are logically sound and grounded in the KG. This process helps to avoid misleading reasoning chains and reduces unnecessary computational demand. Furthermore, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, which makes it accessible to a wider audience. The authors clearly explain the methodology and provide sufficient details for understanding the different components of FiDeLiS. The inclusion of an efficiency analysis, which compares the average runtime and token usage of FiDeLiS with ToG, is also a positive aspect, as it demonstrates the method's computational efficiency. Finally, the paper's focus on grounding LLM reasoning in verifiable paths within the knowledge graph is a significant contribution to the field, as it enhances the interpretability and reliability of LLM-based KGQA systems.

## weaknesses
Despite its strengths, the paper exhibits several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. One significant limitation is the method's reliance on the quality of the knowledge graph. As the paper states, the method directly uses entity and relation embeddings from the KG for retrieval. This means that any errors, inconsistencies, or inaccuracies in the KG could propagate through the reasoning process, leading to incorrect answers. The paper does not include any mechanisms for handling such issues, which is a major concern. Specifically, the method's reliance on a vector-based index of the KG for retrieval means that any inaccuracies in the entity and relation embeddings within this index could lead to the retrieval of irrelevant or incorrect information, thereby compromising the entire reasoning process. Furthermore, the method does not explicitly address how to handle cases where the KG contains conflicting information, which could lead to ambiguous or incorrect reasoning paths. This dependence on KG quality is a critical weakness that needs to be addressed. Another significant weakness is the method's dependence on the quality of the initial keyword extraction. The paper states that keywords are generated by an LLM to maximize the coverage of potential reasoning steps. However, if the keywords are not representative of the query's intent, the retrieval process may fail to identify the relevant entities and relations, leading to poor performance. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the keyword extraction process, including the types of keywords that are most effective and the potential for keyword ambiguity. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the method to variations in keyword quality. Moreover, the method does not consider the potential for the LLM to generate its own keywords, which could be more effective than relying on a separate keyword extraction module. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with the proposed method. While the paper provides empirical runtime and token usage comparisons, it does not include a formal analysis of the time complexity of each step of the method, including the retrieval, path ranking, and beam search components. This analysis should be compared against the computational costs of the baseline methods, with a clear breakdown of the time spent on each operation. The paper also does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of the beam size on the computational cost. This lack of a formal computational cost analysis is a significant weakness, especially for a method that relies on a beam search algorithm, which can be computationally expensive. The paper's presentation of the Path-RAG and DVBS components could also be improved. The description of Path-RAG, particularly the scoring function in Equation 3, lacks sufficient detail. The roles of the terms S_rel(r), S_ent(e), and the max operator are not clearly explained, making it difficult to understand how the method selects the top-m reasoning steps. The paper should provide a more intuitive explanation of the scoring function, perhaps with examples illustrating how different terms contribute to the final score. Additionally, the connection between the Path-RAG module and the subsequent DVBS process is not clearly established. It is unclear how the reasoning paths generated by Path-RAG are used as input to the DVBS module, and how the beam search is guided by the deductive verification process. A more detailed explanation of the interaction between these two modules is needed, including a clear description of the input and output of each module. The paper also does not provide a clear explanation of the deductive verification process, including the criteria used to determine the validity of a reasoning path. While the paper defines the criterion C(q', s^t, s^{1:t-1}), it does not provide a detailed explanation of how this criterion is implemented in the context of the proposed method. The paper also does not address the semantic richness of the entity and relation embeddings used in the method. The paper mentions that entities in KGs are often represented as machine identifiers (MIDs), which lack semantic richness. However, the proposed method also relies on entity and relation embeddings, which may also lack semantic richness, especially for unseen or rare entities and relations. The paper does not explain how the method addresses this issue. The paper also does not explicitly address the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors mention that the method is limited to multi-hop questions with a single answer, this limitation should be discussed in more detail. The paper should explore the potential challenges of extending the method to handle single-hop questions, questions with multiple answers, and questions requiring complex reasoning beyond simple deductive steps. Finally, the paper does not provide the code for their work, which makes it difficult to verify the results and reproduce the experiments. The paper states that the code will be released at a later date, but the lack of code at the time of submission is a significant limitation. The paper also lacks details on how the planning steps are generated. The paper mentions that an LLM is prompted to generate planning steps, but it does not provide the specific prompt used or details on how incorrect or incomplete plans are handled. This lack of detail makes it difficult to assess the robustness of this component. The paper also does not provide a direct comparison of the quality of paths retrieved by Path-RAG versus those generated by other methods, such as a simple beam search without the retrieval component. While the ablation study includes experiments without Path-RAG, a direct comparison of path quality is missing. Finally, the paper's novelty is somewhat limited, as the method is more like a combination of some existing methods. The Path-RAG component is similar to GraphPath, and the DVBS component combines beam search with a verification process similar to ToG. The paper does not clearly articulate how their deductive verification process differs fundamentally from existing verification methods beyond the specific prompting strategy.

## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, to mitigate the reliance on a perfect knowledge graph, the authors should explore methods for incorporating uncertainty and confidence scores into the reasoning process. This could involve assigning confidence scores to the retrieved entities and relations based on their provenance or the reliability of the source. The method could also be enhanced by incorporating techniques for detecting and resolving inconsistencies within the KG, such as using graph embedding methods to identify conflicting information. Furthermore, the authors should consider incorporating a mechanism for identifying and handling cases where the KG does not contain the necessary information to answer a query, such as using a fallback mechanism or a query reformulation technique. This would make the method more robust to incomplete or inaccurate KGs. Second, to improve the robustness of the method to variations in keyword quality, the authors should conduct a more detailed analysis of the keyword extraction process. This analysis should include an evaluation of the types of keywords that are most effective for different types of queries, as well as an assessment of the potential for keyword ambiguity. The authors should also explore the use of LLMs to generate keywords directly from the query, which could be more effective than relying on a separate keyword extraction module. This could involve prompting the LLM to generate a set of keywords that are relevant to the query, and then using these keywords to retrieve entities and relations from the KG. The authors should also consider incorporating a mechanism for filtering out irrelevant or ambiguous keywords, such as using a semantic similarity measure or a keyword ranking algorithm. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the use of techniques for expanding the initial set of keywords, such as using a knowledge graph to identify related concepts or entities. Third, to address the lack of analysis of the computational cost, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the time complexity of each step of the method, including the retrieval, reasoning, and scoring steps. This analysis should consider the impact of the beam size on the computational cost, as well as the size of the knowledge graph and the number of candidate reasoning paths. The authors should also provide empirical results on the runtime performance of the method on different datasets, including a comparison of the runtime performance of the method with different beam sizes. This analysis should also consider the trade-off between performance and efficiency, and provide guidance on how to choose the optimal beam size for different applications. The authors should also investigate the use of techniques for optimizing the computational cost of the method, such as using indexing techniques to speed up the retrieval process or using pruning techniques to reduce the number of candidate reasoning paths. Fourth, to improve the clarity of the methodological details, the authors should provide a more intuitive explanation of the scoring function in Equation 3, perhaps with examples illustrating how different terms contribute to the final score. The authors should also provide a more detailed explanation of the interaction between Path-RAG and DVBS, including a clear description of the input and output of each module. The authors should also provide a more detailed explanation of the deductive verification process, including the criteria used to determine the validity of a reasoning path. Fifth, to address the semantic richness of the entity and relation embeddings, the authors should explore methods for incorporating semantic information into the embeddings, such as using pre-trained language models or knowledge graph embedding techniques. The authors should also consider using techniques for handling unseen or rare entities and relations, such as using sub-word embeddings or character-level embeddings. Sixth, the authors should explicitly address the limitations of the proposed method, including its limitations to multi-hop questions with a single answer. The authors should explore the potential challenges of extending the method to handle single-hop questions, questions with multiple answers, and questions requiring complex reasoning beyond simple deductive steps. Seventh, the authors should provide the code for their work to improve reproducibility. Finally, the authors should provide more details on how the planning steps are generated, including the specific prompts used and the reasoning process behind them. The authors should also conduct a more comprehensive ablation study, including an evaluation of the vanilla DVBS (i.e., without Path-RAG) to isolate the contribution of the Path-RAG component. This ablation should also include a comparison of the quality of paths retrieved by Path-RAG versus those generated by other methods, such as a simple beam search without the retrieval component. The authors should also provide a more detailed comparison with existing methods, particularly GraphPath and ToG. The discussion should go beyond superficial differences in path generation direction and focus on the core algorithmic distinctions. For example, a detailed analysis of the information retrieval process, including the specific embedding models used, the retrieval metrics, and the impact of different retrieval strategies, would be beneficial. The authors should also clarify how their deductive verification process differs from existing verification methods, providing a more rigorous analysis of the verification process and its impact on the overall performance. A more detailed explanation of the prompting strategy used for deductive verification is needed, including examples of the prompts and the reasoning process.
