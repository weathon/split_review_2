## summary
This paper introduces FrAug, a novel data augmentation technique designed specifically for time series forecasting (TSF) in the frequency domain. The core idea behind FrAug is to address the challenge of data scarcity in TSF by manipulating the frequency components of time series data, thereby generating augmented samples that maintain the temporal relationships crucial for accurate forecasting. The authors propose two primary augmentation strategies: frequency masking, which randomly sets certain frequency domain representations to zero, and frequency mixing, which combines the frequency domains of two different time series samples. These techniques are applied to the concatenated look-back window and forecasting horizon, aiming to preserve the temporal dependencies between input and output sequences. The authors argue that existing data augmentation methods, often designed for classification or anomaly detection, are not well-suited for TSF due to the need to maintain fine-grained temporal relationships. They demonstrate the effectiveness of FrAug through extensive experiments on eight widely-used benchmark datasets and several state-of-the-art TSF models, including DLinear, Autoformer, FEDformer, and Informer. The empirical results show that FrAug consistently improves forecasting accuracy across various scenarios, including long-term forecasting, cold-start forecasting, and test-time training. Notably, the authors find that models trained with FrAug on a small fraction of the original training data can achieve performance comparable to models trained on the full dataset. Furthermore, they show that applying FrAug during test-time training can significantly improve forecasting accuracy when there are distribution shifts between training and test data. The paper's main contribution lies in the introduction of a frequency-domain data augmentation method tailored for TSF, which is a relatively unexplored area in the literature. The authors' focus on preserving temporal relationships during augmentation and their empirical demonstration of FrAug's effectiveness in various challenging scenarios highlight the practical value of their approach. However, the paper also has several limitations, including a lack of theoretical analysis, limited exploration of frequency domain augmentation techniques, and insufficient experimental details, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of time series forecasting, offering a novel and effective data augmentation technique that addresses the challenges of data scarcity and distribution shifts. However, further research is needed to address the identified limitations and fully realize the potential of frequency domain augmentation for TSF.
