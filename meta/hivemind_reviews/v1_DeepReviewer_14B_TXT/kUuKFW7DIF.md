## summary
This paper introduces multi-resolution HuBERT (MR-HuBERT), a novel self-supervised learning approach for speech representation that extends the original HuBERT model by incorporating a hierarchical Transformer architecture and multi-resolution masked unit prediction. The core idea is to encode speech information across multiple resolutions within a single model, aiming to capture both fine-grained and coarse-grained acoustic details. The architecture employs a convolutional encoder to extract initial speech features, which are then fed into a hierarchical multi-resolution Transformer encoder. This encoder progressively downsamples the feature representations to a lower resolution and then upsamples them back to the original resolution. The final output features from the multi-resolution encoder are used to predict masked frames at multiple resolutions, using pseudo-labels derived from a pre-trained HuBERT-base model. The model is pre-trained using a large amount of unlabeled speech data, and the authors demonstrate its effectiveness through extensive experiments on various downstream tasks. The empirical findings show that MR-HuBERT consistently outperforms the original HuBERT model across a range of benchmarks, including the LibriSpeech dataset, the Speech Universal PERformance Benchmark (SUPERB), and the Multilingual SUPERB (ML-SUPERB). Specifically, the proposed method achieves substantial performance improvements on tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR), speaker identification, emotion recognition, and speech translation. Furthermore, the authors report a reduction in computational complexity during inference, with a 9-13% reduction in Multiply-Accumulate operations (MACs) compared to the original HuBERT. The authors have made their implementation and pre-trained models publicly available, which facilitates further research and adoption of the proposed method. Overall, this work presents a significant advancement in self-supervised speech representation learning by effectively leveraging multi-resolution modeling to capture richer and more robust speech features, leading to improved performance on a variety of downstream tasks and computational efficiency during inference.
