## summary
This paper introduces EvA, an evolutionary-based algorithm designed to perform adversarial attacks on graph neural networks (GNNs). The core contribution of EvA lies in its direct approach to discrete optimization, circumventing the need for gradient information and thus enabling attacks on black-box models with non-differentiable objectives. Unlike traditional gradient-based methods that relax the adjacency matrix, EvA operates directly on the discrete graph structure, offering a more realistic attack scenario. The method employs a genetic algorithm to explore the space of possible perturbations, using a fitness function that directly measures the impact of perturbations on model accuracy. The algorithm encodes perturbations as a vector of indices representing flipped edges, which allows for a memory-efficient representation. The paper introduces two novel attack objectives: reducing the effectiveness of robustness certificates and breaking conformal prediction sets, expanding the scope of adversarial attacks on graphs. The experimental results demonstrate that EvA outperforms existing state-of-the-art attacks, achieving an average accuracy reduction of approximately 11%. The authors evaluate EvA on common graph datasets, including Cora-ML, Citeseer, and PubMed, and show its effectiveness against both vanilla and robust GNN models. The paper also explores the impact of different fitness functions and introduces targeted and adaptive mutation strategies to enhance the attack's performance. Overall, the paper presents a novel and effective approach to adversarial attacks on GNNs, highlighting the potential of evolutionary algorithms in this domain and opening new avenues for research into the robustness of graph-based machine learning models.

The method's ability to operate without gradient information makes it applicable to black-box models and non-differentiable objectives, which is a notable advantage. Furthermore, the paper's use of a sparse encoding for perturbations is a clever way to reduce memory complexity, making the method more scalable. The authors also demonstrate the impact of different fitness functions and introduce targeted and adaptive mutation strategies, which further enhance the attack's performance. The fact that the method is model-agnostic and can be applied to any black-box model broadens its applicability and makes it a valuable tool for evaluating the robustness of various GNN architectures. Finally, the paper's clear presentation of the method and its experimental evaluation makes it easy to understand and appreciate the contributions.
