## Summary
This paper introduces FAVICOMP, a novel training-free evidence compression method designed to enhance retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in large language models (LLMs). The core idea behind FAVICOMP is to make retrieved evidence more 'familiar' to the target model by integrating parametric knowledge during the compression phase. This is achieved through an ensemble decoding strategy that combines token logits from both a compression model and the target model. Specifically, at each decoding step, FAVICOMP selects the token with the highest probability from the combined set of logits, effectively biasing the compressed context towards tokens that the target model is more confident about. This approach aims to balance the integration of parametric knowledge (the model's internal knowledge) and non-parametric knowledge (the retrieved evidence). The authors argue that this method addresses a critical challenge in RAG, where retrieved evidence can sometimes be noisy, irrelevant, or unfamiliar to the target model, leading to suboptimal performance. The method is evaluated on multiple open-domain question answering (QA) datasets, including Natural Questions (NQ), TriviaQA, SQuAD, HotpotQA, and MuSiQue. The experimental results demonstrate that FAVICOMP consistently outperforms several recent evidence compression baselines, achieving accuracy improvements of up to 23.91% while maintaining high compression rates. The authors also conduct ablation studies and analyses of compression rates to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the method's performance. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel approach to evidence compression, which leverages the target model's own knowledge to generate more familiar and effective context. This training-free approach is particularly appealing as it avoids the need for additional training data or computational resources, making it easily integrable into existing RAG workflows. The authors also explore the impact of an ensemble coefficient, α, which controls the balance between the compression model and the target model, and find that a balanced approach (α=0.5) generally yields the best results. Overall, the paper presents a compelling method for improving RAG performance through a novel evidence compression technique that is both effective and practical.

## Strengths
I found several strengths in this paper, primarily centered around the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed method. The core idea of FAVICOMP, which is to make retrieved evidence more 'familiar' to the target model by integrating parametric knowledge during compression, is a significant contribution. This approach directly addresses a key challenge in RAG systems, where the retrieved evidence may not always align well with the target model's internal knowledge. The use of ensemble decoding to combine token logits from both the compression model and the target model is a clever way to achieve this familiarity. By selecting tokens that the target model is more confident about, FAVICOMP effectively biases the compressed context towards information that the model can more effectively utilize. This is a unique approach that goes beyond traditional compression methods, which often focus solely on summarizing the retrieved documents without considering the target model's perspective. Furthermore, the fact that FAVICOMP is training-free is a major advantage. This makes it easily adaptable to different RAG workflows and eliminates the need for additional training data or computational resources. The experimental results are also compelling, demonstrating that FAVICOMP consistently outperforms several recent evidence compression baselines across multiple open-domain QA datasets. The reported accuracy improvements of up to 23.91% are substantial and highlight the practical benefits of the proposed method. The authors also conduct thorough ablation studies and analyses of compression rates, providing a comprehensive evaluation of FAVICOMP's performance. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain the motivation behind FAVICOMP, the details of the method, and the experimental setup. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the key concepts and results. Overall, the paper presents a novel, effective, and practical method for improving RAG performance, making it a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses
While I appreciate the strengths of this paper, I have also identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, the paper lacks a comparison with more advanced retrieval models. The authors use Contriever-MSMARCO4 for retrieval, which is a standard method, but they do not compare against models that utilize more sophisticated retrieval mechanisms, such as graph-based methods or learned embeddings for document representation. This is a significant limitation because it restricts the assessment of FAVICOMP's performance in scenarios with more complex information needs. As the reviewer pointed out, models like [1] could provide a more challenging benchmark. Without such comparisons, it's difficult to determine how FAVICOMP would perform when integrated with state-of-the-art retrieval techniques. This limits the generalizability of the findings and makes it harder to assess the practical applicability of FAVICOMP in real-world scenarios where the retrieval component is often a critical factor in overall performance. Second, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of FAVICOMP compared to other approaches. While the authors mention that the method is slower than some baselines, they do not provide a breakdown of the time spent on different stages of the pipeline, such as retrieval, compression, and generation. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to understand the trade-offs between performance and efficiency. As the reviewers noted, a detailed analysis of the time spent on each stage, along with the memory footprint, is crucial for practical applications, especially when dealing with large-scale datasets. Without this information, it's hard to assess the feasibility of using FAVICOMP in resource-constrained environments. Third, the paper's analysis of the sensitivity of the method to different hyperparameter settings is limited. While the authors analyze the impact of the ensemble coefficient α, they do not explore the impact of other key hyperparameters, such as the temperature parameter for the target model or the compression ratio. As the reviewers pointed out, a more detailed sensitivity analysis is needed to understand the robustness of the approach and provide guidance for practitioners. The paper should explore the impact of these hyperparameters on the overall performance and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate values. This would make the method more practical and easier to use for a wider range of applications. Fourth, the paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the impact of the quality of the retrieved evidence on the performance of FAVICOMP. While the authors divide the test samples into evidence-relevant and evidence-irrelevant subsets, they do not explicitly manipulate the quality of the retrieved evidence or systematically vary the number or diversity of retrieved documents. As the reviewer noted, it is crucial to evaluate how FAVICOMP performs when the retrieved documents are noisy, irrelevant, or contain contradictory information. This analysis is essential for understanding the limitations of the approach and identifying areas for future research. Fifth, the novelty of the proposed method is somewhat limited. The core mechanism of ensembling token logits from different models is a common approach in the field of LLMs. While the specific application of this technique to evidence compression in RAG is a contribution, the paper could have better articulated the specific challenges in evidence compression that this method addresses and how the proposed approach provides a unique solution compared to existing methods. Sixth, the paper's explanation of the relationship between the ensemble coefficient α, performance, and perplexity is not entirely convincing. The authors claim that when α exceeds 0.5, performance declines as perplexity decreases due to the lack of evidential knowledge during evidence compression. However, as the reviewer pointed out, there is no clear correlation between the performance and perplexity of individual samples. This explanation is based on aggregate trends, and the underlying mechanism might be more complex than a direct causal link. Finally, the generalization of the method is limited by the choice of model families used in the experiments. The authors employ three compression and target model pairs, where two pairs use the same model for compression and target, and one pair uses different models, all from the Llama/Mixtral/Mistral family. It is not clear how the method would perform when applied to different model families, such as Qwen and LLaMA. This limits the assessment of the method's generalization capabilities and its applicability to a wider range of models.

## Suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should include a comparison with more advanced retrieval models that go beyond simple term-based methods. Specifically, models that utilize graph-based representations of documents or learned embeddings for retrieval should be considered. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of FAVICOMP's performance in scenarios with complex information needs and demonstrate its effectiveness against state-of-the-art retrieval techniques. For example, incorporating a comparison with models that use knowledge graph embeddings or transformer-based retrieval mechanisms would be beneficial. This would help to establish the practical applicability of FAVICOMP in real-world scenarios where the retrieval component is often a critical factor in overall performance. Furthermore, the authors should analyze the performance of FAVICOMP when the retrieval model is not perfect, simulating scenarios where the retrieval component might introduce noise or irrelevant information. Second, a detailed analysis of the computational cost of FAVICOMP is essential. The authors should provide a breakdown of the time spent on different stages of the pipeline, such as retrieval, compression, and generation. This would help to identify the computational bottlenecks of the method and allow for a more informed assessment of its efficiency. Furthermore, the memory footprint of FAVICOMP should also be considered, especially when dealing with large-scale datasets. The authors should compare the computational cost of FAVICOMP with other evidence compression techniques, providing a clear understanding of the trade-offs between performance and efficiency. This analysis should also include the impact of different hyperparameter settings on the computational cost, such as the compression ratio and the number of retrieved documents. Third, a thorough sensitivity analysis of the hyperparameters is crucial for understanding the robustness of FAVICOMP. The authors should explore the impact of key hyperparameters, such as the temperature parameter for the target model, the compression ratio, and the weighting of the parametric and non-parametric knowledge sources. This analysis should reveal the optimal operating range for these parameters and their influence on the overall performance. The authors should also investigate the impact of the quality of the retrieved evidence on the performance of FAVICOMP. This analysis should consider scenarios where the retrieved documents are noisy, irrelevant, or contain contradictory information. Furthermore, the authors should evaluate the impact of the number of retrieved documents on the final performance, as well as the diversity of the retrieved set. This would help to identify the limitations of the approach and guide future research directions. Fourth, to address the limited novelty concern, the authors should more clearly articulate the specific challenges in evidence compression that FAVICOMP addresses and how the proposed approach provides a unique solution compared to existing methods. For example, the authors could discuss the limitations of existing compression techniques in handling noisy or irrelevant information and how their method mitigates these issues through the ensembling process. Furthermore, a more detailed analysis of the method's behavior under different conditions, such as varying levels of noise in the retrieved documents, would be beneficial to demonstrate the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed approach. Fifth, to address the concerns about the relationship between α, performance, and perplexity, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of this relationship. The current explanation that performance declines as perplexity decreases when α exceeds 0.5 is not convincing, as there is no clear correlation between the performance and perplexity of individual samples. The authors should conduct a more fine-grained analysis of the impact of α on the generated context and how this context affects the final performance. For example, the authors could analyze the diversity and relevance of the generated context for different values of α and how these factors correlate with the final performance. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of why the optimal value of α is around 0.5 and how this value balances the contributions of the compression model and the target model. Sixth, to improve the generalization of the method, the authors should conduct experiments using a wider range of target models with varying generalization abilities, including models from different families like Qwen and LLaMA. This would help to clarify the relationship between the target model's generalization performance and the effectiveness of the proposed method. Finally, the authors should provide more insights into the selection of the ensemble coefficient α. While the authors mention that α controls the balance between the compression model and the target model, the process of choosing the optimal value of α is not clearly explained. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of how different values of α affect the performance across various datasets and tasks. It would be beneficial to include a sensitivity analysis that demonstrates the impact of α on the final results, along with guidelines for selecting an appropriate value for different scenarios. This would enhance the practical applicability of the method and provide users with a better understanding of how to fine-tune the model for their specific needs.
