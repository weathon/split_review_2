## summary
This paper introduces a novel two-stage inverse rendering framework designed to reconstruct explicit geometry, materials, and lighting from multi-view images. The method begins by extracting a coarse mesh using a neural radiance field, followed by a refinement stage that leverages physically-based rendering techniques, including multi-bounce path tracing and Monte Carlo integration. A key innovation is the incorporation of reservoir sampling to reduce noise and enhance convergence during the optimization process. The authors demonstrate that their approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in decomposition results, particularly in challenging scenarios involving complex shadows. Furthermore, the explicit mesh representation facilitates downstream applications such as scene editing, relighting, and material editing, making it compatible with modern graphics engines and CAD software. The method's core contribution lies in its combination of explicit mesh representation with advanced physically-based rendering techniques, offering a more accurate and realistic solution for inverse rendering compared to methods relying on implicit representations or simplified path tracing. The paper presents a well-structured methodology, supported by clear experimental results, and highlights the potential of the proposed framework for various applications in computer graphics and vision. The use of multi-bounce path tracing allows for a more accurate estimation of indirect illumination, including self-shadowing and internal reflections, which significantly enhances the quality of the reconstructed geometry, materials, and lighting. The incorporation of reservoir sampling further improves the optimization process by reducing noise and facilitating gradient-based optimization with low sample counts. The authors provide both qualitative and quantitative comparisons to demonstrate the performance of their method, showcasing its advantages over existing approaches. The explicit mesh representation is a significant advantage, enabling seamless integration with existing graphics pipelines and tools. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to inverse rendering that addresses several limitations of previous methods and opens up new possibilities for creating high-quality 3D assets from multi-view images.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core strength lies in the novel two-stage inverse rendering framework that combines explicit mesh representation with physically-based rendering techniques. This approach effectively addresses the limitations of previous methods that rely on implicit representations or simplified path tracing, offering a more accurate and realistic solution for inverse rendering. The use of multi-bounce path tracing is a significant technical innovation, allowing for a more accurate estimation of indirect illumination, including self-shadowing and internal reflections. This is crucial for achieving high-quality decomposition results, especially in complex lighting scenarios. The incorporation of reservoir sampling is another notable contribution, as it effectively reduces noise and enhances convergence during the optimization process. This is particularly important for gradient-based optimization with low sample counts, making the method more efficient and practical. The paper also demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in decomposition results, particularly in complex shadow scenarios, which is a testament to the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The quantitative comparisons provided in the paper, using metrics such as MAE, CD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, clearly show the superior performance of the method compared to existing approaches. Furthermore, the explicit mesh representation is a significant advantage, as it facilitates further applications in scene editing, relighting, and material editing. This compatibility with modern graphics engines and CAD software makes the method highly practical and versatile. The paper is also well-organized and clearly presents the methodology, experiments, and results. The figures and tables effectively illustrate the performance of the proposed method compared to existing approaches, making it easy for readers to understand the contributions and advantages of the work. The authors provide a detailed description of each stage of the pipeline, making it easy to follow the logic and implementation of the method. The use of a two-stage approach, inspired by NeRF2Mesh, is a logical and effective way to approach the inverse rendering problem. The first stage provides a good initialization for the second stage, which then refines the geometry, materials, and lighting using physically-based rendering techniques. Overall, the combination of technical innovations, empirical achievements, and clear presentation makes this a strong and impactful paper.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further consideration. A significant limitation is the lack of a comprehensive analysis of the computational costs associated with the proposed method. Specifically, the paper does not provide a comparison of the training time of the proposed method with existing methods. While the paper mentions that "The training process of both stages takes about 4.5 hours," it does not compare this time with other state-of-the-art techniques. This absence of comparative data makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method, especially in scenarios where computational resources are limited or real-time performance is desired. A detailed breakdown of the time spent on each stage of the pipeline, such as mesh extraction, material estimation, and lighting optimization, would be beneficial. Furthermore, the paper does not provide a comparison of the inference time of the proposed method with existing methods. While training time is important, the inference time is equally critical for practical applications. The paper should include a thorough analysis of the rendering speed of the proposed method, including the time required to generate a single rendered image or a sequence of images. This analysis should consider different scene complexities and rendering resolutions to provide a comprehensive understanding of the method's performance. The absence of this data makes it challenging to evaluate the real-time applicability of the method. Additionally, the paper does not provide a comparison of the memory usage of the proposed method with existing methods. Memory consumption is a significant factor, especially when dealing with complex scenes or high-resolution data. The paper should include a detailed analysis of the memory footprint of the proposed method, including the memory required for storing the mesh, material properties, and lighting information. This analysis should be compared with existing methods to understand the trade-offs between memory usage and performance. The lack of this information makes it difficult to assess the resource requirements of the proposed method. Moreover, the paper does not provide a comparison of the scalability of the proposed method with existing methods. It is important to understand how the method performs as the complexity of the scene increases. The paper should include experiments that evaluate the performance of the method on scenes with varying levels of geometric detail and material complexity. This analysis should consider the impact of scene complexity on training time, inference time, and memory usage. The absence of this analysis makes it unclear how the method would perform on more complex scenes. Another weakness is that the contributions of this paper are somewhat incremental, and the proposed pipeline is similar to that of NeRF2Mesh. The paper explicitly mentions NeRF2Mesh in the "Method Overview" section, stating: "Inspired by NeRF2Mesh Tang et al. (2023b), we employ a two-stage training process: initially, we train a neural radiance and signed distance field to extract a coarse mesh from the input images." While the paper introduces novel components like multi-bounce path tracing and reservoir sampling, the core idea of a two-stage process involving initial coarse mesh extraction followed by refinement is shared with NeRF2Mesh. This similarity reduces the novelty of the overall pipeline. The pipeline is also complex, requiring two separate stages and additional optimization techniques, which may be difficult to use in practice. The "Method Overview" section clearly outlines the two-stage process: "Stage 1: Radiance Field and Coarse Mesh Acquisition" and "Stage 2: Mesh Refinement and Intrinsic Decomposition." Stage 2 involves "Mesh refinement", "Material optimization", and "Lighting optimization", each with its own components and techniques like multi-bounce path tracing and reservoir sampling. This multi-stage nature and the introduction of advanced techniques could potentially increase the complexity of implementation and usage. Finally, the paper lacks sufficient quantitative and qualitative comparisons with state-of-the-art methods, such as DiffIR, PhyIR, and DIF-FRM. The "Experiments" section includes quantitative comparisons with TensoIR, GS-IR, and NVdiffrec-MC on the TensoIR dataset (Table 2) and relighting and novel view synthesis comparisons on the OWL dataset (Table 3). However, DiffIR, PhyIR, and DIF-FRM are not included in the comparisons. The lack of comparison with these methods limits the assessment of the proposed method's performance relative to these specific techniques. These limitations, while not invalidating the core contributions, highlight areas where further investigation and analysis are needed to fully understand the practical implications and limitations of the proposed method.

## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the computational cost of each stage of their pipeline, including the time spent on mesh extraction, material estimation, and lighting optimization. This should be compared with existing state-of-the-art inverse rendering techniques using a consistent hardware setup and dataset. The comparison should not only focus on the total training time but also analyze the time spent on each component of the pipeline. For example, a table could be provided that shows the time spent on mesh optimization, material parameter estimation, and lighting optimization for both the proposed method and other methods. This would allow for a more granular understanding of the computational bottlenecks and advantages of their approach. Additionally, the authors should consider providing a scalability analysis of the training time, showing how the training time increases with the complexity of the scene. Second, the authors should provide a comprehensive analysis of the rendering speed of their method, including the time required to generate a single rendered image or a sequence of images. This analysis should consider different scene complexities and rendering resolutions. The analysis should also compare the inference time of their method with existing state-of-the-art techniques, using a consistent hardware setup and dataset. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the impact of different rendering parameters, such as the number of samples used for Monte Carlo integration, on the inference time. This would provide a more complete understanding of the trade-offs between rendering quality and speed. The authors could also explore the potential for optimizing the rendering process, such as using adaptive sampling techniques or pre-computing certain aspects of the rendering pipeline. Third, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the memory usage of their method, including the memory required for storing the mesh, material properties, and lighting information. This analysis should be compared with existing methods to understand the trade-offs between memory usage and performance. The authors should also investigate the scalability of the memory usage, showing how the memory footprint increases with the complexity of the scene. This analysis should consider the impact of different scene complexities and rendering resolutions on memory usage. Furthermore, the authors should explore potential strategies for reducing the memory footprint of their method, such as using more efficient data structures or compression techniques. This would make their method more practical for use in resource-constrained environments. Fourth, to address the concern about the incremental nature of the contributions, the authors should more clearly articulate the specific advantages of their approach over NeRF2Mesh. While the two-stage pipeline is similar, the authors should emphasize the novel components, such as multi-bounce path tracing and reservoir sampling, and how these components specifically improve the inverse rendering process compared to NeRF2Mesh. A more detailed discussion of the limitations of NeRF2Mesh and how the proposed method overcomes these limitations would also be beneficial. Fifth, to address the complexity of the pipeline, the authors should provide a more detailed breakdown of the computational cost associated with each stage. Specifically, they should quantify the time required for mesh extraction in the first stage and the optimization process in the second stage, perhaps by reporting the number of iterations or the time per iteration. This would allow for a more informed assessment of the practical feasibility of the method. Furthermore, the authors should discuss the sensitivity of the method to the hyperparameters of the optimization process, such as the learning rate and the number of samples used for Monte Carlo integration. A sensitivity analysis would help to understand the robustness of the method and provide guidance for users on how to tune the parameters for different scenes. Finally, the paper needs a more comprehensive evaluation that includes comparisons with state-of-the-art methods. The authors should include comparisons with DiffIR, PhyIR, and DIF-FRM on a wider range of datasets, including both synthetic and real-world scenes. The evaluation should include both quantitative metrics, such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and qualitative comparisons of the reconstructed geometry, material, and lighting. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of their method compared to other methods, highlighting the specific scenarios where their method performs better or worse. For example, it would be useful to see how the method performs on scenes with complex geometry, highly specular materials, or challenging lighting conditions. A more thorough evaluation would provide a more complete understanding of the capabilities and limitations of the proposed method.
