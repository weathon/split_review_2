## Summary
This paper introduces LASP-2, a novel sequence parallelism (SP) approach designed to enhance the training efficiency of linear attention transformer models, particularly when dealing with very long input sequences. The core contribution of LASP-2 lies in its reimagining of the communication strategy within SP, moving away from the ring-style point-to-point communication of its predecessor, LASP-1, to a more efficient all-gather collective communication operation. This shift significantly reduces communication overhead and allows for better overlap of communication and computation, which is crucial for handling long sequences. The authors also extend LASP-2 to LASP-2H, which applies similar communication optimizations to standard attention modules, enabling efficient SP in hybrid models that combine both linear and standard attention layers. The method is motivated by the observation that existing SP methods do not fully exploit the right-product-first feature of linear attention, leading to inefficiencies. LASP-2 addresses this by reorganizing the computation and communication order, allowing for a single all-gather operation on intermediate memory states, whose sizes are independent of the sequence length. This design choice is particularly beneficial for long sequences, where communication costs can become a major bottleneck. The experimental evaluation focuses on the Linear-Llama3 model, a variant of the Llama3 model that uses linear attention instead of standard attention. The authors demonstrate that LASP-2 achieves significant throughput improvements over existing methods, including LASP-1, Ring Attention, and Megatron-SP, particularly when the sequence length is very long (e.g., 2048K tokens). Specifically, LASP-2 shows a 15.2% improvement over LASP-1 and a 36.6% improvement over Ring Attention at this sequence length on a 64-GPU system. The paper also presents a theoretical cost analysis comparing LASP-1 and LASP-2, highlighting the reduced number of communication steps in LASP-2. Furthermore, the authors evaluate the convergence performance of LASP-2 with various linear attention modules, including RetNet and GLA, showing that LASP-2 maintains comparable or better loss values while achieving higher throughput. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and effective approach to improving the training efficiency of linear attention models, with a clear focus on addressing the communication bottleneck in SP. The introduction of LASP-2H further extends the applicability of the method to hybrid models, making it a valuable contribution to the field of efficient training of large language models.
