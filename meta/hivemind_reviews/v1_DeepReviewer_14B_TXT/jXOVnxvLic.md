## Summary
This paper introduces a novel neural organoid simulation framework (NOSF) designed to model the complex interactions between neural organoids and external stimuli. The core contribution of this work lies in the development of a computational framework that aims to reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world organoid experiments. The authors propose a framework that utilizes advanced neural computing models, specifically spiking neural networks (SNNs), to simulate the behavior of neural organoids. The framework incorporates elements such as the Generalized Integrate-and-Fire (GIF) neuron model, AMPA synapses, and a small-world network architecture, which are intended to capture key aspects of biological neural systems. The framework also includes an 'intelligent expansion platform' based on SNNs, which is designed to facilitate collaborative intelligence tasks between the simulated organoid and AI models. The authors present a benchmark for evaluating the framework, which includes a set of real organoid experimental data and a series of evaluation metrics. The experimental results presented in the paper primarily focus on demonstrating the similarity between the simulation outputs and real-world organoid data, as well as evaluating the framework's performance on a basic classification task. The authors claim that their framework can realistically reconstruct the details of interaction experiments with real mature organoids, and that it can be used to perform pre-experiments, thus minimizing the need for trial-and-error in real-world experiments. The paper also suggests that the framework can be used to explore the potential of organoid-machine collaborative intelligence. However, the paper also acknowledges that the framework is not without limitations, particularly in terms of the complexity of the models used and the scope of the experimental validation. Overall, the paper presents a significant step towards the development of computational tools for neural organoid research, but it also highlights the need for further work to address the identified limitations and to fully realize the potential of such frameworks.

## Strengths
The primary strength of this paper lies in the novelty of the proposed neural organoid simulation framework (NOSF). The authors have successfully developed a framework that integrates various neural computing models to simulate the behavior of neural organoids, which is a significant contribution to the field. The use of spiking neural networks (SNNs) as the core computational model is a notable technical innovation, as it allows for the simulation of temporal dynamics and complex interactions within the organoid. The inclusion of the Generalized Integrate-and-Fire (GIF) neuron model, AMPA synapses, and a small-world network architecture further enhances the biological plausibility of the simulation. The authors' efforts to create a benchmark for evaluating the framework, which includes real organoid experimental data and a series of evaluation metrics, is another significant strength. This benchmark provides a valuable resource for assessing the performance and capabilities of the simulation framework and can serve as a basis for future research in this area. The experimental results presented in the paper, while limited in scope, do demonstrate the framework's ability to generate outputs that are similar to real-world organoid data. The authors have shown that the framework can capture key aspects of neural organoid behavior, such as spike firing patterns and network dynamics. The 'intelligent expansion platform' based on SNNs is another innovative aspect of the paper. This platform explores the potential of collaborative intelligence between the simulated organoid and AI models, which opens up new avenues for research and development. The authors have demonstrated that the framework can be used to perform basic classification tasks, and that the integration of the SNN expansion platform can improve the performance of these tasks. The paper also clearly articulates the motivation for developing the framework, which is to reduce the time and cost associated with trial-and-error experiments in real-world organoid research. The authors have presented a clear rationale for the need for a computational tool that can simulate organoid behavior and guide experimental design. In summary, the paper's strengths lie in its novel framework, its technical innovations, its benchmark, and its demonstration of the framework's potential for simulating organoid behavior and facilitating collaborative intelligence.

## Weaknesses

One major concern is the lack of a detailed cost analysis comparing the proposed framework with real-world experiments. While the authors claim that the framework can reduce the time and cost associated with trial-and-error experiments, they do not provide any concrete evidence to support this claim. The paper lacks a detailed breakdown of the computational resources required for the simulation, such as processing time, memory usage, and energy consumption. Furthermore, there is no comparison of these costs with the expenses associated with traditional organoid experiments, including personnel, equipment, and consumables. This omission makes it difficult to assess the practical benefits of the framework in terms of cost-effectiveness. The absence of such an analysis undermines the claim that the framework can alleviate the financial burden of organoid research. The authors should also compare these costs with the expenses associated with traditional organoid experiments, including personnel, equipment, and consumables. This analysis should be presented in a clear and concise manner, allowing readers to easily assess the cost-effectiveness of the framework. The authors should also consider evaluating the framework's performance on tasks such as image segmentation of organoid structures, super-resolution imaging, and target identification for drug discovery. The authors should also discuss the assumptions made in the simulation model and the potential impact of these assumptions on the results. This would address the current lack of discussion on the limitations of the chosen models and enhance the credibility of the framework.
