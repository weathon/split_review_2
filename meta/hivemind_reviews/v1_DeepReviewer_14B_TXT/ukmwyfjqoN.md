## summary
This paper introduces ReBotNet, a novel recurrent bottleneck mixer network designed for real-time video enhancement. The core contribution lies in its efficient architecture, which employs a dual-branch framework to process video frames. One branch learns spatio-temporal features by tokenizing input frames along both spatial and temporal dimensions using a ConvNext-based encoder, while the other branch focuses on improving temporal consistency by processing tokens extracted from individual frames. These features are then merged by a common decoder to predict the enhanced frame. A key aspect of ReBotNet is its recurrent training approach, where the prediction from the previous frame is used to enhance the current frame, aiming to improve temporal consistency and efficiency. The authors also introduce two new video enhancement datasets, PortraitVideo and FullVideo, which contain synthetic degradations, to evaluate their method. The empirical findings demonstrate that ReBotNet achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of computational efficiency, with lower latency and memory requirements compared to existing approaches, while maintaining competitive visual quality. The paper presents a comprehensive set of experiments, including ablation studies, to validate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and training strategy. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of real-time video enhancement by proposing an efficient and effective architecture that balances performance and computational cost. However, as I will detail, there are some limitations that need to be addressed to further strengthen the paper's claims and impact.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First and foremost, the proposed ReBotNet architecture is a novel and efficient approach to video enhancement. The dual-branch design, which combines a ConvNext-based encoder for spatio-temporal feature extraction with a bottleneck mixer for temporal consistency, is a clever way to balance performance and computational cost. The use of a recurrent training approach, where the previous frame's prediction is used to enhance the current frame, is also a significant contribution, as it allows the model to leverage temporal dependencies in the video while reducing computational overhead. The paper also introduces two new datasets, PortraitVideo and FullVideo, which, although synthetically degraded, provide a valuable resource for evaluating video enhancement methods in practical scenarios. The experimental results presented in the paper are compelling. ReBotNet consistently outperforms the baseline methods in terms of computational efficiency, achieving lower latency and memory requirements while maintaining competitive visual quality. The extensive ablation studies further demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and training strategy. The authors have also done a good job in presenting the method and results in a clear and concise manner, making the paper easy to follow and understand. The paper's focus on real-time video enhancement is also a significant strength, as it addresses a critical need in many practical applications, such as video calls and live streaming. The authors have successfully demonstrated that their method can achieve state-of-the-art performance while meeting the strict latency requirements of these applications. Finally, the paper's comprehensive evaluation, including both quantitative and qualitative results, provides a strong foundation for the claims made by the authors. The inclusion of latency and FPS metrics, in addition to standard quality metrics like PSNR and SSIM, is particularly valuable for assessing the real-time capabilities of the proposed method.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that need to be addressed. First, while the paper compares ReBotNet against several established methods, I find that the selection of baselines could be more comprehensive. The paper compares against FastDVDNet, VRT, BasicVSR++, and RVRT in the main experiments, and DeepDeblur, EDVR, TSP, PVDNet, VRT, and RVRT on public datasets. While these are relevant baselines, the field of video enhancement is rapidly evolving, and the inclusion of more recent state-of-the-art methods, particularly those focusing on low-latency or efficient architectures, would strengthen the claims of superiority. The paper does not explicitly justify the selection of these specific baselines or discuss why other recent methods were not included. This lack of comparison with the most recent advancements in the field makes it difficult to fully assess the relative performance of ReBotNet. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does compare against several baselines, but the lack of more recent comparisons is a valid concern. Second, I am concerned about the lack of evaluation on real-world datasets. The paper introduces two new datasets, PortraitVideo and FullVideo, which are synthetically degraded. While these datasets are useful for controlled experiments, they do not fully capture the complexities and nuances of real-world video degradation. The paper does not include any evaluation on datasets with real-world degradations, such as those found in low-light conditions, or with compression artifacts. This is a significant limitation, as it makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the proposed method. The paper mentions that the new datasets contain a mixture of degradations, but these are still synthetic and may not accurately reflect the types of degradations found in real-world scenarios. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly describes the synthetic degradation process for the new datasets and lacks any explicit evaluation on datasets with real-world degradations. However, the paper does not provide a more granular breakdown of the processing time for each stage of the network, which would be helpful in identifying potential bottlenecks. Additionally, the paper does not show the method's performance on a variety of hardware platforms, including those with limited computational resources. This makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method in real-world scenarios. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does provide some evidence of real-time performance, but more detailed analysis and experiments are needed.
