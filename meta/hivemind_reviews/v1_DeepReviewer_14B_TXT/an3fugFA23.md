## summary
This paper introduces Probability Distribution Estimation (PDE), a novel method designed to bridge the gap between white-box and black-box text detection methods, particularly in the context of proprietary large language models (LLMs). The core idea behind PDE is to estimate the full probability distribution of a model's output using only partial observations, specifically the top-K token probabilities, which are often accessible even when full model access is restricted. This approach enables the application of white-box detection techniques, such as Entropy, Rank, and Fast-DetectGPT, to proprietary models where only API access is available. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of PDE by applying it to several state-of-the-art proprietary LLMs, including GPT-3.5, and evaluating its performance on multiple datasets and languages. The empirical results show that PDE-enhanced white-box methods achieve high accuracy in detecting machine-generated text, often outperforming baseline methods. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of various parameters, such as the number of top-K tokens used for estimation. The paper's significance lies in its ability to extend the capabilities of white-box detection methods to proprietary models, addressing a critical challenge in the field of machine-generated text detection. By leveraging the power of large language models while working within the constraints of API access, PDE offers a practical and effective solution for identifying machine-generated content. The research contributes to the development of more robust and trustworthy AI systems by providing a means to detect and potentially mitigate the risks associated with the misuse of advanced LLMs. The authors also explore the limitations of their approach, particularly the reliance on the availability of top-K token probabilities and the potential for adversarial attacks. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field, offering a novel and practical method for detecting machine-generated text in the context of proprietary LLMs.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper's reliance on the availability of top-K token probabilities from proprietary models is a critical limitation that needs to be more clearly acknowledged. While the authors state that they assume a proprietary model provides these probabilities, this assumption is not guaranteed, as API providers may not offer this information. The paper acknowledges that not all proprietary models provide a Completion API, but it does not explicitly state that the availability of *top-K probabilities* is a critical assumption and a potential limitation if this information isn't provided by the API. The method's applicability is directly tied to this assumption, and the paper should more clearly acknowledge this dependency as a critical limitation. This is a significant concern because the entire PDE method hinges on the accessibility of these top-K probabilities, and if they are not available, the method cannot be applied. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's method description and experimental setup are entirely dependent on this assumption, and the paper does not provide any alternative approaches if this assumption is not met. Second, the paper lacks a thorough analysis of the method's robustness against adversarial attacks that directly manipulate the probability distribution. While the paper includes an experiment on 'Robustness under Paraphrasing Attack,' this focuses on modifications to the generated text, not direct manipulation of the probability distribution itself. The PDE method relies on the integrity of the probability distribution, and an attacker who can manipulate this distribution could potentially evade detection. The paper does not discuss the potential for attackers to circumvent the method by altering the probability distribution of the generated text, and this is a significant vulnerability that needs to be addressed. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the method's reliance on probability distributions makes it inherently vulnerable to such attacks, and the paper does not provide any analysis of this vulnerability. Third, while the paper includes an ablation study on the top-K parameter, the analysis could be more thorough. The paper demonstrates that increasing K generally improves the accuracy of PDE methods, but it does not explore the sensitivity of the method to different K values across different models and datasets in sufficient detail. A more granular analysis of the impact of K on the estimation quality and detection performance would be valuable. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does include an ablation study, but it could be expanded to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's sensitivity to this parameter. Finally, the paper lacks a detailed breakdown of the computational cost associated with the PDE method. While the paper provides a high-level comparison of time and cost with a baseline method, it does not provide a granular analysis of the time and resources required for each step of the method, such as the API calls, local processing, and any overhead. This information is crucial for understanding the method's efficiency and scalability, and the paper should provide a more detailed breakdown of the computational cost. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper does not provide sufficient details about the computational cost of the PDE method itself, which is crucial for understanding its practical implications.

## suggestions
Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements to this paper. First, the authors should explicitly address the reliance on the availability of top-K token probabilities from proprietary models. They should discuss the implications if this information is not provided or is limited by the API. A sensitivity analysis of the method's performance with varying levels of top-K availability would strengthen the paper. Furthermore, the paper should explore alternative approaches or modifications to the PDE method that could mitigate the impact of limited access to top-K probabilities. This could involve investigating the use of lower-ranked tokens or exploring other model outputs that might be more readily available. The authors should also consider the practical implications of relying on proprietary APIs, which may change over time, and discuss the robustness of their method to such changes. Second, the paper needs a more thorough analysis of the method's robustness against adversarial attacks that directly manipulate the probability distribution. The authors should investigate how the PDE method performs when the probability distribution of the generated text is altered. This could involve simulating different types of attacks, such as re-ranking the probabilities or using a different decoding strategy. The paper should also discuss the potential for adaptive attacks that are specifically designed to evade the PDE method. The authors should consider incorporating techniques to detect or mitigate adversarial attacks, such as using statistical checks or comparing the distribution to known patterns of human-generated text. A discussion of the limitations of the method in the face of adversarial attacks is crucial for a realistic assessment of its applicability. Third, the authors should conduct a more rigorous analysis of the PDE method's sensitivity to the parameter K, the number of top probabilities used for estimation. This analysis should include experiments across a range of K values, and should evaluate the impact on detection accuracy for different models and datasets. The authors should also investigate the trade-offs between computational cost and detection performance as K varies. For instance, a smaller K might lead to faster computation but potentially lower accuracy, while a larger K might improve accuracy but increase computational overhead. This analysis should be presented with clear visualizations, such as plots of detection accuracy versus K, and should include a discussion of the optimal K values for different scenarios. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed breakdown of the computational cost associated with the PDE method. This should include a granular analysis of the time and resources required for each step of the method, such as the API calls to the proprietary models, the local processing of the estimated distributions, and any overhead. The authors should also compare the computational cost of the PDE method with other detection methods, and should discuss the scalability of the method to large-scale datasets and models. This analysis should be presented with clear metrics, such as the average time per text sample and the memory usage, and should include a discussion of the practical implications of the computational cost.
