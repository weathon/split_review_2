## summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to image inpainting, termed Semantic-Aware Implicit Representation (SAIR), which leverages both semantic and appearance information to enhance the quality of reconstructed images. The core idea revolves around the use of a modified CLIP model to extract spatial-aware semantic embeddings, which are then integrated with appearance features within an implicit neural representation framework. The method comprises two main modules: Semantic Implicit Representation (SIR), which enhances the semantic embedding, and Appearance Implicit Representation (AIR), which utilizes the enhanced semantic information along with appearance features to reconstruct the color of pixels. The authors argue that this combined approach allows for a more robust and accurate reconstruction, particularly in scenarios with severely degraded images. The proposed SAIR method is evaluated on the CelebAHQ and ADE20K datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to several state-of-the-art inpainting techniques in terms of quantitative metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, L1 loss, and LPIPS. The paper's primary contribution lies in the specific integration of semantic information derived from CLIP into an implicit representation framework for image inpainting. The authors modify the CLIP model to output spatial-aware embeddings, which are then processed by the SIR module to enhance the semantic representation. This enhanced semantic information is subsequently used by the AIR module, along with appearance features, to guide the reconstruction process. The experimental results show that the proposed method achieves better performance than existing methods, especially in cases with larger masked regions. The authors also present an ablation study to analyze the impact of semantic information on the inpainting results. Overall, the paper presents an interesting approach to image inpainting by combining semantic and appearance information within an implicit representation framework. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to further strengthen the paper's contribution.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be commendable. The core idea of incorporating semantic information into an implicit neural representation for image inpainting is both intuitive and promising. By leveraging the rich semantic understanding encoded within the CLIP model, the authors aim to enhance the reconstruction quality, particularly in challenging scenarios with significant image degradation. The proposed SAIR method, which combines Semantic Implicit Representation (SIR) and Appearance Implicit Representation (AIR), offers a structured approach to integrating semantic and appearance information. The modification of the CLIP model to output spatial-aware embeddings, as described in Section 4.2, is a clever adaptation that allows for the integration of semantic information into the pixel-level reconstruction process. The experimental results, as presented in Tables 1 and 2, demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance compared to several state-of-the-art inpainting techniques on the CelebAHQ and ADE20K datasets. This quantitative evidence supports the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Furthermore, the ablation study, while limited in scope, provides some insight into the contribution of semantic information to the overall performance. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which facilitates understanding of the proposed method and its underlying rationale. The authors clearly articulate the motivation behind their work and the technical details of their approach. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the proposed method and the experimental results. The paper's focus on addressing the limitations of existing implicit representation methods, which often struggle with severely degraded images, is also a significant strength. By explicitly incorporating semantic information, the authors aim to overcome these limitations and achieve more robust and accurate reconstructions. The combination of a modified CLIP model with an implicit neural representation framework is a novel approach that has the potential to advance the field of image inpainting. The paper's focus on leveraging both continuous appearance and semantic mapping to enhance image restoration quality is a valuable contribution. The authors have clearly identified a gap in the existing literature and have proposed a method that addresses this gap effectively.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, the novelty of the proposed method is somewhat limited. While the specific combination of CLIP embeddings with implicit neural representations is interesting, the core idea of using a pre-trained model like CLIP for downstream tasks is not entirely new. As noted by multiple reviewers, the modification to the CLIP model, while effective, is relatively straightforward and inspired by existing work like MaskCLIP. The paper does not provide a thorough discussion on how this specific modification fundamentally advances the field beyond existing CLIP-based applications. The authors state in the Introduction that they propose a novel approach that simultaneously leverages both continuous appearance and semantic mapping, but the specific contribution of the SIR and AIR modules needs more justification to be considered a significant contribution. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of how these modifications advance the field beyond existing CLIP applications. This is a significant concern because it questions the originality of the proposed method. Second, the paper lacks a comprehensive discussion on the differences between the proposed method and existing image inpainting techniques. While the paper includes a section on 'Comparison with State-of-the-Art Inpainting Methods,' the comparison is primarily quantitative and does not delve into the qualitative differences, advantages, and disadvantages of SAIR compared to each baseline method. The paper does not explicitly discuss computational complexity, generalization capabilities, or robustness to different types of image corruptions in the comparison. This is a critical weakness because it makes it difficult to assess the unique benefits of the proposed method over other techniques. The absence of a detailed qualitative analysis and a discussion of the trade-offs with existing methods limits the paper's ability to demonstrate the true value of the proposed approach. The paper lacks a dedicated section or detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method, including sensitivity to hyperparameters, computational cost, and performance on more diverse datasets. ## suggestions
Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements to strengthen this paper. First, the authors should provide a more in-depth analysis of the novelty of their approach. They need to clearly articulate how the specific modifications to CLIP and the integration with implicit neural representations fundamentally advance the field beyond existing CLIP-based applications. This could involve a more detailed discussion of the underlying mechanisms of SIR and AIR, and how they interact with the CLIP embeddings. The authors should also discuss the potential limitations of relying on CLIP's pre-trained representations and how their method addresses these limitations. Furthermore, a theoretical analysis of the proposed method's convergence and stability would strengthen the paper's contribution. Second, the authors should include a more detailed comparison with existing inpainting techniques. This should include a qualitative analysis, highlighting the advantages and disadvantages of SAIR compared to each baseline method. The comparison should also include an analysis of computational complexity, generalization capabilities, and robustness to different types of image corruptions. The authors should also compare their method against a wider range of state-of-the-art inpainting techniques, including both traditional and deep learning-based methods. A more thorough analysis of the method's performance on different types of masks (e.g., large vs. small, regular vs. irregular) would also be beneficial. The paper should also discuss the potential limitations of the proposed method in handling complex scenes or images with significant occlusions. Third, the authors should conduct experiments on a wider range of downstream tasks, including image super-resolution, novel view synthesis, and perhaps even tasks like image editing or manipulation. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's capabilities and limitations. For image super-resolution, the authors could investigate how the semantic information helps in reconstructing high-frequency details. For novel view synthesis, they could explore how the semantic understanding of the scene can improve the generation of new viewpoints. These additional experiments would not only demonstrate the versatility of the method but also highlight its potential for broader applications. The authors should also consider evaluating the method on more challenging datasets with complex scenes and textures, as the current evaluation is limited to relatively simple images. Fourth, the authors should conduct more extensive ablation studies to validate the contribution of each component. This should include experiments that isolate the impact of SIR and AIR, and to determine the optimal configuration for each component. For example, they could vary the architecture of SIR and AIR, or remove each component entirely to assess its impact on performance. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameter choices and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate values. Fifth, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed method. This should include a discussion of the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameter choices, the computational cost of the proposed approach, and its performance on diverse datasets with varying levels of complexity and corruption. The authors should also analyze the performance of the method on images with complex structures or textures, or on images with large missing regions. The analysis should include a quantitative evaluation of performance degradation with increasing mask sizes and complexities, and a qualitative analysis of the types of structures and textures that pose challenges to the method. Sixth, the authors should include a comparison with more recent baselines, such as LSIR and MIR. This comparison should include not only quantitative metrics but also qualitative analysis, such as visual comparisons and ablation studies. Seventh, the authors should provide the number of parameters and FLOPs for all the methods, including the proposed method and the baseline methods. This information is crucial for assessing the practical applicability of the proposed method. Finally, the authors should provide the visual comparison on more diverse and challenging images, such as images with more complex scenes and textures. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the method's performance in real-world scenarios. These suggestions, which are directly linked to the identified weaknesses, would significantly strengthen the paper and enhance its overall contribution.
