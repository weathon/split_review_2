## Summary
This paper introduces a novel learning principle for designing graph filters, aiming to construct infinite-depth Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The authors propose a framework where graph filters are designed as power series with exponentially decaying weights, ensuring convergence and stability. This approach is motivated by the limitations of existing GNNs, which often struggle with over-smoothing and cannot be naturally extended to infinite depth. The core contribution is the Adaptive Power GNN (APGNN), a practical model derived from this principle. APGNN employs a graph filter that aggregates multi-order neighbor information with exponentially decaying weights, allowing for the capture of both local and global graph structures. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed learning framework, including a generalization analysis and an upper bound for the generalization error. They demonstrate that the proposed framework can be extended to an infinite-depth network, addressing a key limitation of many existing GNN architectures. The paper also presents a detailed analysis of the convergence and stability of the proposed graph filter. The empirical evaluation of APGNN is conducted on several benchmark datasets for node classification tasks, where the model demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art GNNs. The authors also provide a parameter study, exploring the impact of key hyperparameters on the model's performance.
