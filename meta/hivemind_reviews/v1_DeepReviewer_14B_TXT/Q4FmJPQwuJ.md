## summary
This paper introduces CrossTVR, a two-stage text-video retrieval (TVR) framework designed to enhance retrieval accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The core idea revolves around a multi-grained video-text cross-attention module that operates at both the frame and video levels. In the first stage, a cosine similarity network, such as TS2Net or CLIP-ViP, is used to generate initial text-video candidate pairs. This is followed by a second stage, where the proposed CrossTVR re-ranker refines these results using a multi-grained cross-attention mechanism. This mechanism involves frame-level cross-attention, which computes attention between text tokens and each frame's visual tokens, and video-level cross-attention, which captures temporal interactions by using a token selector to identify the most informative tokens from each frame. The method leverages a frozen CLIP model, allowing it to scale to larger pre-trained visual models like ViT-G with minimal additional computational cost. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CrossTVR through extensive experiments on five mainstream TVR benchmarks, showing significant improvements in retrieval performance when combined with existing cosine similarity-based methods. The paper also includes an ablation study to analyze the contribution of different components of the proposed method. The authors emphasize the efficiency of their approach, highlighting its ability to achieve substantial performance gains with marginal additional computation cost. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear motivation and good visual illustrations for the proposed method. The authors also provide a qualitative analysis of the results, which helps to understand the behavior of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a practical and effective approach for text-video retrieval, with a focus on balancing accuracy and efficiency. However, as I will detail in the weaknesses section, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of novelty, computational analysis, and experimental comparisons.

## strengths
The primary strength of this paper lies in its practical approach to improving text-video retrieval performance. The proposed CrossTVR method is simple yet effective, demonstrating substantial improvements in retrieval accuracy when combined with existing cosine similarity-based methods. The two-stage architecture, where a coarse retrieval is followed by a more fine-grained re-ranking, is a well-established strategy, and the authors have successfully applied it to the TVR task. The use of a multi-grained cross-attention module, which operates at both the frame and video levels, is a sensible approach to capturing both spatial and temporal information within videos. The authors' decision to leverage a frozen CLIP model is also a significant strength, as it allows the method to scale to larger pre-trained visual models like ViT-G without incurring excessive computational costs. This is a crucial consideration for practical applications, where computational resources are often limited. The experimental results presented in the paper are compelling, with consistent improvements observed across multiple datasets. The authors have also provided a qualitative analysis of the results, which helps to understand the behavior of the proposed method. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear motivation and good visual illustrations for the proposed method. The ablation study, while not fully comprehensive, provides some insights into the contribution of different components of the proposed method. The authors have also provided a detailed description of their method, which makes it easy to reproduce. The paper's focus on balancing accuracy and efficiency is also a significant strength, as it addresses a key challenge in the field of text-video retrieval. The authors have successfully demonstrated that it is possible to achieve substantial performance gains without incurring excessive computational costs. Finally, the paper's clear presentation and well-structured arguments make it accessible to a wide audience, which is a significant strength in terms of its potential impact.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. First, the novelty of the proposed method is limited. While the authors combine several techniques, the individual components are not novel. Specifically, the use of cross-attention, hierarchical attention, and token selection mechanisms are all well-established in the field. The paper acknowledges the use of the token selector from TS2Net, and the frame-level and video-level cross-attention mechanisms are also widely used in video-language models. The paper does not clearly articulate the specific differences and advantages of their approach compared to existing methods. This lack of novelty is a significant concern, as it diminishes the overall contribution of the paper. The authors claim a novel application of hierarchical attention, but they do not provide a detailed explanation of how their specific implementation differs from existing methods. This lack of clarity makes it difficult to assess the true novelty of their approach. Second, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. While the authors claim efficiency, they do not provide concrete metrics such as inference time and memory usage, especially when scaling to larger pre-trained visual models. The paper provides some computational cost information in Tables 8 and 9, but it lacks a detailed breakdown of the CrossTVR component's cost and a comparison with other state-of-the-art methods, especially in the re-ranking context. Table 8 shows the inference speed for indexing 1000 videos, but this is for the combined system, not a detailed breakdown of the CrossTVR component. Table 9 compares memory and training time with CLIP4Clip, but this is for the end-to-end finetuning scenario, not the re-ranking scenario. This lack of detailed computational analysis makes it difficult to assess the true efficiency of the proposed method. The two-stage process, which involves an initial cosine similarity calculation followed by a re-ranking step using cross-attention, introduces additional computational overhead. The paper does not provide a breakdown of the time spent in each stage of the inference process, nor does it compare the inference time of the proposed method with that of single-stage methods or other two-stage methods. This is a significant omission, as it makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the proposed method. Third, the paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other two-stage text-video retrieval methods. The experimental section primarily compares CrossTVR with one-stage methods like TS2Net and CLIP-ViP. While Table 8 includes comparisons with CLIP4Clip and X-pool, these are presented as base methods that CrossTVR is applied to, rather than direct comparisons of two distinct two-stage pipelines. This lack of direct comparison with other two-stage methods makes it difficult to assess the true performance gains of the proposed method in its appropriate context. The authors should have included a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method against other two-stage text-video retrieval methods, such as CLIP4clip-tight and DRL. This would have provided a more fair and accurate assessment of the proposed method's performance. Fourth, the paper does not explore the impact of different token selection strategies on the performance of the proposed method. The choice of using averaged features from the token selector for re-ranking is not sufficiently justified, and the paper lacks an ablation study to demonstrate the importance of this design choice. The paper describes averaging the tokens but doesn't provide a justification for this choice or explore alternative aggregation methods. The ablation study in Table 7 focuses on the cross-attention mechanism itself, not the token selection or aggregation. The paper should have explored alternative token selection strategies, such as max pooling or other aggregation methods, and compared their performance with that of the proposed token selector. Fifth, the paper does not investigate the sensitivity of the proposed method to the number of selected tokens. The paper mentions setting M=4 for the token selector in the implementation details, but there is no ablation study on the impact of different values of M. The paper should have investigated the sensitivity of the proposed method to the number of selected tokens and provided guidelines for choosing the optimal number of tokens for different datasets and tasks. Finally, the paper lacks a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method on the video-to-text retrieval task. The experimental results primarily focus on the Text2Video task, and the paper does not report the performance of the proposed method on the Video2Text task. This is a significant omission, as it makes it difficult to assess the overall effectiveness of the proposed method. The paper should have included a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, including the performance on the video-to-text retrieval task. These weaknesses, taken together, significantly impact the overall contribution of the paper and should be addressed in future work. My confidence in these identified issues is high, as they are directly supported by the paper's content and the lack of specific analyses or comparisons.

## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should more clearly articulate the specific novelty of their approach beyond the combination of existing techniques. They should provide a detailed comparison of the architectural differences, the training procedures, and the specific types of attention mechanisms used in their method versus those in prior work. A table comparing the proposed method with existing methods, focusing on the architectural differences, training procedures, and attention mechanisms, would be beneficial. This would help to clarify the contribution of the proposed method and justify its novelty. Second, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time spent in each stage of the inference process, such as the initial cosine similarity calculation and the subsequent re-ranking with cross-attention. This analysis should compare the inference time of the proposed method with that of single-stage methods, as well as other two-stage methods, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of its efficiency. The authors should also consider exploring techniques to reduce the computational overhead of the re-ranking stage, such as using a more efficient attention mechanism or by reducing the number of candidates for re-ranking. The paper should also include a discussion of the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency, and how their method balances these two factors. Third, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive comparison with other two-stage text-video retrieval methods, such as CLIP4clip-tight and DRL. This comparison should include a variety of metrics, such as recall and precision, and should be conducted on multiple datasets. The authors should also provide a detailed analysis of the performance differences between the proposed method and other two-stage methods, highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of each approach. This would provide a more fair and accurate assessment of the proposed method's performance. Fourth, the authors should explore the impact of different token selection strategies on the performance of the proposed method. For example, instead of averaging the selected tokens, the paper could explore using max pooling or other aggregation methods. An ablation study should be conducted to justify the choice of averaging the selected tokens for re-ranking. This study should compare the performance of the proposed method with different aggregation methods and demonstrate the importance of the chosen design choice. The paper should also investigate the sensitivity of the proposed method to the number of selected tokens and provide guidelines for choosing the optimal number of tokens for different datasets and tasks. Fifth, the authors should include a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, including the performance on the video-to-text retrieval task. This would provide a more complete picture of the method's capabilities and limitations. The evaluation should include a comparison with existing methods on both text-to-video and video-to-text retrieval tasks. The authors should also report the performance of the proposed method on different datasets, to demonstrate its generalizability. Furthermore, the authors should provide an analysis of the error cases, to identify the limitations of the proposed method and suggest potential directions for future research. This would help to improve the understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses, and guide future work in this area. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the token selector and its impact on the overall performance of the proposed method. The authors should also consider exploring alternative token selection strategies and comparing their performance with that of the proposed token selector. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the role of token selection in the text-video retrieval task. The authors should also provide a more detailed explanation of the frame-level and video-level cross-attention mechanisms, including the specific implementation details and the rationale behind their design choices. This would help the reader better understand the proposed method and its contributions. By addressing these points, the authors can significantly strengthen their paper and make a more substantial contribution to the field.
