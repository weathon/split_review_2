## summary
This paper introduces WANDA, a novel pruning method for Large Language Models (LLMs) that aims to induce sparsity without requiring retraining or weight updates. The core idea behind WANDA is to prune weights based on the product of their magnitude and the norm of the corresponding input activations, evaluated on a per-output basis. This approach is motivated by the observation that input activations play a crucial role in determining the importance of weights, and that considering them alongside weight magnitudes can lead to more effective pruning. The authors argue that this method addresses a limitation of traditional magnitude pruning, which only considers the weight magnitude and not the input activation. The paper evaluates WANDA on LLaMA and LLaMA-2 models across various language benchmarks, demonstrating that it significantly outperforms magnitude pruning and achieves competitive results compared to more complex methods like SparseGPT. The empirical findings show that WANDA can effectively prune LLMs while maintaining performance, offering a computationally efficient alternative to methods that require iterative weight updates. The paper also explores the potential of WANDA for structured N:M sparsity and discusses its connection to existing pruning methods, providing a comprehensive understanding of the method and its relationship to other pruning approaches. The authors emphasize that WANDA can be implemented within a single forward pass of the LLM, making it computationally efficient. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a simple yet effective pruning metric that incorporates input activations, and the empirical demonstration of its effectiveness on LLMs. The results suggest that WANDA offers a promising approach for reducing the computational cost of LLMs without significant performance degradation. However, the paper also acknowledges that WANDA may not be as effective as methods like SparseGPT at very high sparsity levels, and that the speedup achieved by pruning is not unique to WANDA but is a general property of sparsity. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of LLM pruning, offering a practical and efficient method that can be easily implemented and applied to various LLMs.
