## summary
This paper introduces a novel task, Layout-aware Scientific Poster Generation (LayoutSciPG), which aims to automatically generate scientific posters from corresponding academic papers. This task integrates content extraction and layout design, addressing a practical need in scientific communication. The authors have made a significant contribution by creating a large-scale dataset of over 10,000 paired scientific papers and their corresponding posters, which is a valuable resource for future research in this area. To tackle the LayoutSciPG task, they propose a multimodal extractor-generator framework. This framework first employs a multimodal extractor to retrieve key text and image elements from the input paper. Then, an interactive generator, equipped with an adaptive memory mechanism, paraphrases the extracted content and generates a structured layout for the poster. The adaptive memory mechanism is designed to handle the long-term dependencies inherent in both the input papers and the generated posters, while also mitigating GPU memory consumption. The experimental results, evaluated using a combination of automatic metrics and human evaluation, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors compare their method against several baselines, showing improvements in content extraction, text generation, and layout generation. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of the proposed framework. Overall, this work presents a significant step towards automating the creation of scientific posters, offering a practical solution to a time-consuming task faced by researchers. The introduction of the dataset and the proposed framework have the potential to stimulate further research in this area, contributing to the advancement of multimodal document understanding and generation.

## strengths
The most significant strength of this paper is the introduction of the novel task, Layout-aware Scientific Poster Generation (LayoutSciPG). This task addresses a practical and important need in the scientific community, as the creation of posters is a time-consuming task for researchers. The authors have also made a substantial contribution by creating a large-scale dataset of over 10,000 paired scientific papers and their corresponding posters. This dataset is a valuable resource for the research community and will undoubtedly facilitate further research in this area. The proposed multimodal extractor-generator framework is another strength of this work. The framework is well-designed, integrating a multimodal extractor to retrieve key text and image elements from the input paper, and an interactive generator with an adaptive memory mechanism to paraphrase the extracted content and generate a structured layout. The adaptive memory mechanism is a notable technical innovation, addressing the challenges of long-term dependencies and GPU memory consumption when dealing with lengthy inputs and outputs. The experimental results, which include both automatic metrics and human evaluation, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors have shown that their method outperforms several baselines in content extraction, text generation, and layout generation. The inclusion of ablation studies further strengthens the paper by providing insights into the impact of different components of the framework. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors have clearly articulated the problem, the proposed solution, and the experimental results. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal document understanding and generation, with the potential to stimulate further research and practical applications.

## weaknesses
While the paper presents a novel task and a promising approach, there are several weaknesses that I have identified through my analysis. Firstly, the generated posters, while effective in conveying the core content, lack the visual complexity and sophistication of professional scientific posters. This is evident in the human evaluation results, where both the proposed method and the baseline received relatively low scores in the layout aesthetics category. As the paper itself notes, "there remains significant room for improvement in this area." This suggests that the current model, while functional, does not fully capture the nuances of human design, resulting in layouts that appear simplistic and lack the visual appeal necessary for effective scientific communication. This limitation is not just a matter of aesthetics; it impacts the overall impact and engagement of the generated posters. The low aesthetic scores indicate that the model's focus is primarily on content extraction and placement, rather than on creating visually engaging layouts. This is a significant limitation, as the visual design of a poster is crucial for capturing attention and effectively communicating complex information. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is directly supported by the human evaluation results and the authors' own acknowledgment of the limitations in layout aesthetics. 

Secondly, the evaluation of the proposed method, while comprehensive, could be more robust. While the paper employs several metrics, including ROUGE scores for text, ImgP and ImgR for images, and various metrics for layout, the human evaluation reveals that these metrics do not fully capture the visual appeal and layout quality of the generated posters. The relatively low scores in layout aesthetics suggest that the quantitative metrics might not be sufficient to assess the overall quality of the generated posters. Furthermore, the paper could benefit from a more thorough comparison with existing baseline methods. While the paper compares against NeuralExt, MSMO, and AdaD2P, exploring a wider range of content extraction and layout generation techniques could provide a more robust assessment of the method's effectiveness. This would help to identify the specific strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method compared to other approaches. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does include several metrics and baselines, but the human evaluation results and the limited scope of the baselines suggest that a more comprehensive evaluation is needed. 

Finally, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the diversity of the dataset, particularly in terms of layout complexity. While the paper mentions that the dataset contains over 10,000 pairs of scientific papers and their corresponding posters, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the layout diversity within the dataset. This is a significant limitation, as the diversity of the training data can significantly impact the model's ability to generate complex and visually appealing layouts. The lack of a detailed dataset analysis makes it difficult to determine whether the model's limitations in layout generation are due to the limitations of the model itself or the lack of diverse examples in the training data. This is a crucial point, as it is possible that the model is not learning the full range of possible layout patterns due to the limitations of the dataset. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper does not include a detailed analysis of the dataset's layout diversity, and the human evaluation results suggest that the generated layouts are relatively simple. The paper also does not explicitly discuss the impact of dataset diversity on layout generation quality in the related work, which further supports this weakness.

## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I propose several concrete and actionable improvements. Firstly, to enhance the aesthetic quality of the generated posters, the authors should explore more sophisticated layout generation techniques. This could involve incorporating principles of graphic design and visual communication into the model. For example, the authors could explore the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) or variational autoencoders (VAEs) that are specifically designed for image generation and can produce more visually appealing layouts. These techniques could enable the model to generate layouts with more complex panel shapes, sizes, and arrangements, as well as integrate more complex visual elements such as custom icons, color gradients, and varied typography. The goal should be to move beyond basic grid layouts to create visually engaging and informative posters that effectively communicate complex scientific information. This could also involve exploring methods to allow for more user control over the final layout, perhaps through a set of parameters or a visual interface, allowing users to tailor the generated posters to their specific needs and preferences. 

Secondly, the evaluation of the proposed method needs to be more comprehensive. While the paper uses several metrics, the authors should include more quantitative metrics that capture the visual appeal and layout quality of the generated posters. For example, metrics that measure the balance, alignment, and contrast of visual elements could be considered. Additionally, the evaluation should include a more thorough comparison with existing baseline methods. The paper should explore a wider range of content extraction and layout generation techniques, and provide a detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method compared to these baselines. This would provide a more robust assessment of the method's effectiveness and identify areas for further improvement. It would also be beneficial to include a user study to assess the perceived quality of the generated posters by domain experts. This user study should involve participants from the target user group, such as researchers and students, and should evaluate the posters based on criteria such as clarity, readability, and overall effectiveness in communicating scientific information. The study should also compare the generated posters with human-created posters to determine how well the model performs in relation to human expertise. The authors should also consider collecting qualitative feedback from the participants to identify specific areas for improvement. 

Finally, the authors should conduct a more detailed analysis of the dataset, particularly in terms of layout diversity. This analysis should include a quantitative assessment of the range of layout patterns present in the dataset, as well as a qualitative analysis of the visual complexity of the layouts. This analysis would help to determine whether the model's limitations in layout generation are due to the limitations of the model itself or the lack of diverse examples in the training data. If the dataset is found to be lacking in diversity, the authors should consider augmenting the dataset with more diverse and aesthetically pleasing posters, potentially through data augmentation techniques or by incorporating additional datasets. The authors should also consider incorporating aesthetic evaluation metrics into the training process to directly optimize for visual appeal. This could involve using a combination of quantitative metrics, such as those based on layout symmetry and balance, and qualitative metrics derived from user feedback. By addressing these points, the authors can significantly improve the visual quality and overall impact of the generated posters.
