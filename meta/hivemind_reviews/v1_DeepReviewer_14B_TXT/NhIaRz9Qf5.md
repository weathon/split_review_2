## summary
This paper introduces SeaKR, a novel adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework designed to enhance the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) by dynamically integrating external knowledge. The core innovation of SeaKR lies in its ability to leverage the LLM's internal states to assess its own uncertainty, thereby determining when to retrieve external knowledge and how to effectively integrate it. Specifically, SeaKR extracts uncertainty estimates from the feed-forward network (FFN) of each layer corresponding to the last generated token. The consistency of these internal states across multiple generations is used to compute a self-aware uncertainty score. This score is then used to decide when to retrieve external knowledge, how to re-rank retrieved documents, and how to reason with the retrieved information. The method employs a Gram matrix to measure the correlation among different representations of the LLM's internal states, and the determinant of this matrix is used as the uncertainty score. SeaKR also introduces two adaptive integration strategies: self-aware re-ranking, which prioritizes documents that reduce the LLM's uncertainty, and self-aware reasoning, which dynamically selects between rationale-only and knowledge-only reasoning based on the uncertainty score. The authors conduct experiments on both complex and simple question-answering datasets, demonstrating that SeaKR outperforms existing adaptive RAG methods. The experimental results show that SeaKR achieves better performance on complex QA tasks, such as 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA, and IIRC, as well as on simple QA tasks like NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and SQuAD. The paper also includes ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of each component of SeaKR and explores the impact of different hyper-parameters, such as the number of retrieved documents (k) and the retrieval threshold. Furthermore, the authors investigate the performance of SeaKR with different backbone LLMs, including LLaMA-2-chat 7B and LLaMA-3 8B, showing that the method is tuning-free and generalizable. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in adaptive RAG by effectively utilizing the self-awareness of LLMs to improve their knowledge integration capabilities.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the core idea of leveraging the LLM's internal states to assess its own uncertainty is both novel and effective. This approach allows for a more nuanced and dynamic control over the retrieval process compared to existing methods that rely on superficial measures or external classifiers. The use of the middle layer's hidden representation, while not fully justified, does show promising results, and the method's ability to extract uncertainty from the internal states without requiring additional training is a significant advantage. The proposed self-aware re-ranking and self-aware reasoning strategies are also well-designed and contribute to the overall performance of SeaKR. The experimental results are comprehensive, demonstrating the effectiveness of SeaKR on a variety of complex and simple QA datasets. The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the contribution of each component of the method. The inclusion of experiments with different backbone LLMs, specifically LLaMA-2-chat 7B and LLaMA-3 8B, shows that the method is not tied to a specific model and can generalize to other architectures. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain the motivation behind their approach, the details of their method, and the results of their experiments. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the key concepts and findings. The paper's focus on adaptive retrieval, which is a critical area for improving the performance of LLMs, makes it a valuable contribution to the field. The method's ability to dynamically decide when to retrieve and how to integrate knowledge is a significant step towards more efficient and robust RAG systems. Finally, the fact that SeaKR is a tuning-free method makes it more practical and applicable to a wide range of real-world scenarios.

## weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further consideration. First, while the paper introduces a novel approach to adaptive RAG, it heavily relies on the internal states of the LLM, specifically the hidden representations from the middle layer. This reliance raises concerns about the generalizability of the method across different model architectures. As the paper itself notes, the method extracts uncertainty estimates from the feed-forward network (FFN) of each layer corresponding to the last generated token. However, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the choice of layer affects performance, nor does it explore the sensitivity of the method to different layers. The paper mentions using the middle layer, but it lacks a strong justification for this choice, especially for models with an even number of layers. Furthermore, the paper does not explore the possibility of using a combination of layers or a learned weighting scheme to extract more robust uncertainty estimates. This limitation is significant because the internal states of LLMs are not universally accessible or equally informative across all architectures. The performance of SeaKR could degrade significantly when applied to models with different layer configurations or without accessible internal states. This is a critical limitation that needs to be addressed to make the method more broadly applicable. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's method description and experimental setup clearly demonstrate this reliance on a specific internal state. Second, the paper lacks a thorough analysis of the computational cost associated with SeaKR. The method requires sampling multiple generations from the LLM and computing a Gram matrix to estimate uncertainty. These operations can be computationally intensive, especially when dealing with large language models. The paper does not provide any data or analysis on the actual time cost or memory footprint of these operations. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical feasibility of the approach, especially for resource-constrained environments. The paper should include a detailed breakdown of the time complexity associated with each step of the SeaKR pipeline, including the self-aware uncertainty estimation, retrieval, and knowledge integration. This analysis should also compare the computational overhead of SeaKR with other adaptive RAG methods. The absence of this analysis is a significant limitation that needs to be addressed to make the method more practical. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's method description and experimental results lack any discussion of computational cost. Third, the paper's explanation of the threshold selection process for determining high self-aware uncertainty scores is limited. The paper mentions that retrieval is triggered if the self-aware uncertainty exceeds an empirical threshold, but it does not provide a detailed explanation of how this threshold is optimized. The paper states that the threshold is set to -6, but it lacks details on the optimization process and the impact of different threshold values on the overall performance of the model. A more rigorous analysis of the threshold selection process is needed, including a sensitivity analysis of the threshold. The paper should also discuss the potential for adaptive thresholding, where the threshold is dynamically adjusted based on the characteristics of the input query or the current state of the model. This lack of detail makes it difficult to understand how the threshold affects the trade-off between retrieval accuracy and computational cost. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's method description and experimental results lack a detailed explanation of the threshold selection process. Finally, while the paper includes experiments with LLaMA-2-chat 7B and LLaMA-3 8B, the evaluation is still limited in terms of the diversity of LLMs tested. The paper does not include experiments on different model families, such as models with different architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder models) or different pre-training objectives. This lack of experiments makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the proposed method. The performance of the method might be highly dependent on the specific characteristics of the LLaMA models, and it is unclear how well it would generalize to other models with different parameter distributions and training procedures. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does include experiments with a different LLaMA model, but the lack of experiments on fundamentally different architectures is a limitation.
