## Summary
This paper introduces ToolLLM, a comprehensive framework designed to enhance the tool-use capabilities of large language models (LLMs). The framework encompasses three key components: ToolBench, a large-scale dataset of 16,464 real-world APIs; a novel depth-first search-based decision tree (DFSDT) algorithm to improve the reasoning capabilities of LLMs; and ToolEval, an automatic evaluator for assessing tool-use performance. The authors constructed ToolBench by leveraging ChatGPT to generate instructions and solution paths for a diverse set of APIs sourced from RapidAPI. The DFSDT algorithm is designed to allow the model to explore multiple reasoning paths, backtracking when necessary, which is a departure from traditional chain-of-thought approaches. The ToolEval evaluator provides a standardized way to measure the performance of LLMs in tool-use tasks. The authors fine-tuned LLaMA on ToolBench and equipped it with a neural API retriever, resulting in the ToolLLaMA model. The empirical results demonstrate that ToolLLaMA achieves performance comparable to ChatGPT on in-distribution tasks and exhibits strong zero-shot generalization to unseen APIs in an out-of-distribution tool-use dataset, APIBench. The paper presents a holistic approach to tool-use learning, addressing data construction, model training, and evaluation, which is a significant contribution to the field. The authors also show that the ToolLLaMA model, when combined with their neural API retriever, demonstrates strong generalization to unseen APIs, which is a crucial aspect of tool-use learning. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed framework can effectively enhance the tool-use capabilities of LLMs, enabling them to execute complex instructions and interact with a wide range of APIs. The authors also highlight the importance of a large-scale, diverse dataset for training LLMs for tool use, and the DFSDT algorithm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. The paper's contributions are significant in that they provide a complete framework for tool-use learning, including data, model, and evaluation, which can be used as a basis for future research in this area. The authors also demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach by achieving comparable performance to ChatGPT and showing strong generalization to unseen APIs, which are important benchmarks for tool-use learning. Overall, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of tool-use learning for LLMs, and the proposed framework has the potential to be used in a variety of real-world applications.
