## Summary
This paper introduces a novel decoding method, termed Permute-and-Flip (PF) decoding, designed as an alternative to traditional softmax sampling in large language models (LLMs). The core idea behind PF decoding is to randomly permute the vocabulary and then iteratively select tokens based on a biased coin-flipping mechanism, where the bias is determined by the logits produced by the LLM. This approach, as the authors demonstrate, offers a compelling trade-off between stability and quality, potentially surpassing softmax sampling by up to a factor of two in terms of this trade-off, while also ensuring that it is never worse than other decoding methods. The authors further contribute by designing a cryptographic watermarking scheme tailored for the PF decoder. This watermarking scheme, inspired by Aaronson's Gumbel watermark, allows for the injection of a watermark into the generated text with a low false positive rate and high recall, particularly when the generated text exhibits high entropy. The authors provide theoretical proofs to support the effectiveness of both the PF decoder and the watermarking scheme. Empirically, the paper presents results showing that the PF decoder achieves lower perplexity scores compared to softmax sampling, and the watermarked text generated by the PF decoder can be detected with high accuracy. The paper also includes an analysis of the stability properties of the PF decoder, demonstrating that it maintains similar stability characteristics to standard sampling methods. The authors provide the code in the supplementary materials, which is a positive step towards reproducibility. Overall, the paper presents a novel and theoretically grounded approach to decoding in LLMs, along with a practical watermarking scheme, which could have significant implications for the field.

## Strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The most significant is the introduction of the Permute-and-Flip (PF) decoder itself. This is a novel approach to decoding that, as the authors demonstrate, offers a compelling alternative to standard softmax sampling. The theoretical grounding of the PF decoder, particularly the proofs showing its improved quality-stability trade-off, is a major strength. The authors convincingly demonstrate that the PF decoder is never worse than other decoders and can be up to twice as good in terms of the quality-stability trade-off. This is a significant theoretical contribution that could have practical implications for the development of more efficient and stable decoding methods for LLMs. The design of the PF watermarking scheme is another notable strength. The authors have successfully adapted the concepts from Aaronson's Gumbel watermark to create a scheme that is naturally tailored for the PF decoder. The theoretical results showing that the watermarking scheme allows for an arbitrarily low false positive rate and high recall when the generated text has high entropy are compelling. This is a valuable contribution to the field of watermarking for LLMs, and it provides a practical method for identifying text generated by specific models. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow. The authors have clearly explained the concepts and methods, making it accessible to a broad audience. The inclusion of the code in the supplementary materials is also a positive aspect, as it facilitates reproducibility and further research. Finally, the experimental results, while not exhaustive, do provide initial evidence supporting the effectiveness of the PF decoder and the watermarking scheme. The perplexity results show that the PF decoder outperforms softmax sampling, and the watermarking results demonstrate the detectability of the watermarked text. These empirical findings, combined with the theoretical results, make a strong case for the potential of the proposed methods.

## Weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, and perhaps most significantly, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational overhead of the PF decoder compared to standard softmax sampling. While the authors claim an improved quality-stability trade-off, the practical implications of this in terms of decoding speed and memory usage are not thoroughly explored. The method description of PF decoding (Algorithm 1) involves a permutation of the vocabulary and a loop through the permuted vocabulary to flip coins. The paper does not quantify the computational cost of these operations relative to softmax sampling. The experimental section also lacks metrics for decoding speed or memory usage, focusing instead on perplexity, MAUVE score, and seq-rep-5. This omission is a critical weakness because the practical applicability of the PF decoder depends on its computational efficiency. Without a thorough analysis of the computational overhead, it is difficult to assess whether the PF decoder is a viable alternative to softmax sampling in real-world applications. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly omits any discussion or metrics related to computational cost. Second, the experimental evaluation of the PF watermark, while promising, could be more comprehensive. The paper primarily focuses on the theoretical aspects of the watermark, but lacks a thorough empirical evaluation of its robustness against various attacks. While the Appendix includes some evaluations of robustness against paraphrasing and editing, and also addresses the impact of text length, the main body of the paper does not provide a comprehensive robustness evaluation against a wider range of attacks, such as translation and adversarial modifications. The paper also does not provide a detailed analysis of detection accuracy under different noise levels. This is a significant weakness because the practical security of the watermarking scheme depends on its resilience against various attacks. Without a thorough evaluation of its robustness, it is difficult to assess the practical applicability of the proposed watermarking scheme. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the main body of the paper lacks a comprehensive robustness evaluation, and the Appendix only addresses some of the concerns. Third, the experimental evaluation of the PF decoder and watermarking scheme is limited in terms of the diversity of models and datasets used. The experiments primarily focus on models within the Llama family, specifically Llama-2-7B-chat and TinyLlama-1.1B, and two datasets, Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (C4) and Alpaca. This limited scope raises concerns about the generalizability of the results. It is unclear whether the performance gap observed in the current experiments holds across different model architectures and sizes, or whether the PF decoder and watermarking scheme can be applied to closed-source models like ChatGPT. The lack of evaluation on a wider range of models and datasets is a significant weakness because it limits the confidence in the practical applicability of the proposed methods. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the experimental section explicitly lists only two datasets and models from the Llama family, and there is no discussion about closed-source models. Fourth, the paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with other state-of-the-art decoding methods. The experimental results compare the PF decoder with softmax sampling and greedy decoding, and the PF watermark with Gumbel and Green-Red watermarks. However, the paper does not include comparisons with other popular decoding methods, such as beam search, top-k sampling, and nucleus sampling. This limited comparison makes it difficult to assess the superiority of the proposed method over existing alternatives. A thorough comparison with other decoding methods would provide a more complete understanding of the advantages and disadvantages of the PF decoder. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the experimental results tables explicitly list the baselines used, which do not include beam search, top-k, or nucleus sampling.

## Suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the PF decoder. This analysis should include a breakdown of the operations involved in the PF decoder, such as the number of comparisons, memory accesses, and arithmetic operations. The analysis should be compared to the corresponding operations in softmax sampling, highlighting the differences in computational cost. Furthermore, the authors should consider providing a theoretical analysis of the time complexity of both methods, using Big-O notation to express how the runtime scales with the size of the input. It would also be beneficial to include a practical runtime comparison, potentially by measuring the actual time taken to decode a fixed-length sequence using both methods on the same hardware. This could be presented in a table or graph, showing the runtime for different sequence lengths or model sizes. Such an analysis would provide a more complete picture of the computational efficiency of the PF decoder. Second, to improve the evaluation of the watermarking scheme, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive analysis of its robustness against various text manipulation attacks. This should include a range of attacks, such as paraphrasing, synonym substitution, sentence reordering, translation, and the addition of noise or adversarial examples. For each attack, the authors should measure the detection accuracy of the watermark, quantifying how well the watermark can be detected after the text has been modified. This could be presented in a table or graph, showing the detection accuracy for each attack scenario. The authors should also discuss the limitations of the watermarking scheme, identifying the types of attacks that it is most vulnerable to. This would provide a more complete understanding of the practical limitations of the proposed watermarking scheme and help guide future research in this area. Third, the authors should significantly expand the range of datasets and models used for testing the PF decoder and watermarking scheme. This should include datasets with varying linguistic characteristics, such as those from different domains (e.g., scientific text, news articles, social media posts) and different languages. Furthermore, the experiments should be conducted on a variety of model architectures and sizes, including both smaller and larger models, to demonstrate the scalability and generalizability of the proposed method. It would also be beneficial to include datasets that are specifically designed to test the robustness of the decoder, such as those with noisy or adversarial inputs. This would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's performance under different conditions. Additionally, the authors should investigate the feasibility of applying the PF decoder and watermarking scheme to closed-source models, such as ChatGPT. This would involve exploring methods to ensure that the watermarked text generated by these models maintains a distribution similar to the original model. Fourth, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive comparison with other decoding methods. This should include a detailed analysis of the trade-offs between the PF decoder and other methods, such as beam search, top-k sampling, and nucleus sampling. The comparison should not only focus on perplexity but also on other metrics, such as computational cost, diversity of generated text, and sensitivity to hyperparameters. It would be beneficial to include a theoretical analysis of the proposed method, comparing its properties to those of other decoding methods. This would provide a deeper understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses and help to identify the scenarios in which it is most likely to be effective. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the theoretical underpinnings of the PF decoder and its watermarking scheme. While the paper presents theoretical proofs, it would be beneficial to include a more in-depth discussion of the assumptions and limitations of these proofs. For example, the paper could explore the conditions under which the PF decoder is guaranteed to achieve a better quality-stability tradeoff compared to standard sampling methods. Similarly, the paper could discuss the theoretical limits of the watermarking scheme's robustness against different types of attacks. This would provide a more rigorous foundation for the proposed methods and help to identify potential areas for future research.
