## summary
This paper introduces MaskComp, a novel iterative method for object completion that alternates between generating complete objects and refining object masks. The core idea is to treat the partial mask as a noisy version of the complete mask and iteratively denoise it. The method leverages a modified ControlNet, termed CompNet, for object generation, conditioned on the partial object image and its corresponding mask. For mask refinement, MaskComp employs an off-the-shelf segmentation model, specifically SAM, to obtain object masks from the generated complete objects. The iterative process is controlled by a mask voting technique, which aggregates the masks from multiple generated images to produce a refined mask for the next iteration. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of MaskComp on two datasets, AHP and DYCE, showing that it outperforms several state-of-the-art methods in terms of object completion quality. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components and design choices, such as the number of iterations, the number of sampled images, and the choice of segmentation model. The authors provide a mathematical explanation of the method, framing it as an iterative process of refining the mask and generating the object. The key contribution of this work lies in its iterative approach to object completion, which leverages the interplay between generation and segmentation to progressively improve the quality of both the completed object and its mask. The paper's findings suggest that this iterative refinement process is effective in achieving high-quality object completion results, particularly in scenarios where the initial mask is incomplete or inaccurate. The authors also explore the computational cost of the method, providing inference times for the generation and segmentation stages. While the paper demonstrates promising results, it also acknowledges the computational expense of the iterative process and the reliance on pre-trained models. The paper's overall significance lies in its introduction of a novel iterative approach to object completion, which bridges the gap between image generation and segmentation, and its demonstration of the effectiveness of this approach on real-world datasets.
