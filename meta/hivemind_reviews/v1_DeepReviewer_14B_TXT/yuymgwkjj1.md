## Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance out-of-distribution (OOD) detection in normalizing flow models by addressing the tendency of these models to assign higher likelihoods to low-complexity OOD samples. The core idea revolves around incorporating synthetic outliers during training to guide the model towards assigning lower likelihoods to OOD data. The authors propose a specialized training objective that leverages the softplus function to ensure numerical stability and a smooth training process. The method involves generating synthetic outliers from in-domain data using techniques like CutMix, CutPaste, and MixUp for images, and synonym replacement and sentence length reduction for text. These augmented outliers are then blurred to further reduce their complexity. The training objective combines a maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) term for in-domain data with a softplus-based penalty for the synthetic outliers, effectively pushing down the likelihood of OOD samples. The authors evaluate their approach on a variety of image and text datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in OOD detection accuracy compared to baseline methods. The results suggest that the proposed method achieves performance comparable to models trained with real outliers, highlighting its practical utility. The paper also explores the relationship between the complexity of an input sample and its latent representation in the normalizing flow model, arguing that lower complexity inputs tend to concentrate in high-density regions of the latent space, leading to higher likelihoods. By introducing synthetic outliers, the authors aim to correct this bias and improve the model's ability to differentiate between in-domain and OOD data. The paper's main contribution lies in the specific combination of synthetic outlier generation and the softplus-based training objective, which is shown to be effective in mitigating the bias of normalizing flows towards low-complexity OOD samples. The empirical findings support the claim that the proposed method enhances OOD detection performance, but the paper also reveals some limitations, particularly in terms of the novelty of the approach, the scope of comparisons, and the analysis of computational complexity and failure cases.
