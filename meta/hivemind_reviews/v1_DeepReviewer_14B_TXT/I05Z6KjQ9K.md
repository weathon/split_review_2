## Summary
This paper introduces a novel gradient regularization technique, termed GrCPA, designed to enhance the transferability of adversarial attacks on vision-language models (VLMs) across different prompts. The authors' primary motivation stems from the observation that adversarial examples generated for VLMs often exhibit poor transferability, limiting their practical applicability. To address this, they propose a method that reduces the variance of backpropagated gradients in the attention and MLP components of the model, effectively mitigating overfitting during the adversarial example generation process. The core idea revolves around clipping the gradients of both visual and textual features during backpropagation, thereby preventing the optimization process from converging to overly specific solutions that are effective only for a narrow set of prompts. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through extensive experiments on several state-of-the-art VLMs, including Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA, and InstructBLIP. They employ a white-box setting, where they have full access to the model's architecture and weights, and focus on targeted attacks, aiming to induce the model to produce specific outputs. The experimental results show that GrCPA significantly improves the transferability of adversarial examples across different prompts, as evidenced by higher attack success rates compared to baseline methods. Furthermore, the authors present results indicating that their method enhances the stability of adversarial attacks, which is crucial for large-scale evaluations of VLM robustness. The paper's findings highlight the vulnerability of VLMs to adversarial attacks and underscore the importance of developing robust defense mechanisms. While the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field, it also reveals several limitations, particularly in terms of computational analysis, ethical considerations, and the scope of evaluation. The authors' work provides a solid foundation for future research in this area, and their insights into the challenges of adversarial attacks on VLMs are both timely and relevant. However, further investigation is needed to address the identified weaknesses and to fully realize the potential of the proposed method.

## Strengths
This paper presents a compelling approach to addressing the challenge of cross-prompt transferability in adversarial attacks on vision-language models. The core strength of the work lies in the introduction of the GrCPA method, which leverages gradient regularization to enhance the transferability of adversarial examples. By clipping the gradients of both visual and textual features during backpropagation, the authors effectively mitigate the issue of overfitting, which often leads to adversarial examples that are effective only for a limited set of prompts. This is a significant contribution, as it directly tackles a key limitation of existing adversarial attack methods for VLMs. The experimental results provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors demonstrate that GrCPA significantly improves the attack success rate across different prompts, as shown in Tables 1 and 3. Furthermore, the results in Table 2 indicate that GrCPA enhances the stability of adversarial attacks, which is crucial for reliable evaluations of VLM robustness. The authors' decision to evaluate their method on several state-of-the-art VLMs, including Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA, and InstructBLIP, further strengthens the validity of their findings. This comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that the proposed method is not specific to a particular model architecture and is therefore more broadly applicable. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a wide audience. The authors clearly articulate their motivation, methodology, and experimental results, which facilitates a thorough understanding of their work. The inclusion of a detailed threat model in Section 3.1 is also commendable, as it explicitly defines the attacker's capabilities and goals. Finally, the authors' identification of the non-stationary behavior during adversarial example generation is an important observation that highlights the challenges of this research area. Overall, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of adversarial attacks on VLMs, and the proposed GrCPA method represents a significant step forward in addressing the issue of cross-prompt transferability.

## Weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, several significant weaknesses warrant careful consideration. First, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the proposed GrCPA method. While the authors mention the number of iterations (1000) in the experimental setup, they do not provide a formal analysis of the time and memory complexity of their approach. This is a critical omission, as it makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method, particularly when applied to larger models and datasets. The absence of a breakdown of the computational overhead introduced by the gradient regularization process, such as the time spent on calculating and applying the regularization terms, further limits our understanding of the method's efficiency. This lack of computational analysis is a major weakness, as it prevents us from determining the trade-offs between performance and computational cost. Second, the paper does not adequately address the potential ethical implications of adversarial attacks on VLMs. While the introduction briefly mentions the possibility of generating malicious outputs and embedding advertising information, the paper lacks a comprehensive discussion of the broader ethical concerns. The authors do not explore the potential for these attacks to be used to generate misleading or harmful content, evade detection by security systems, manipulate the behavior of VLMs, discriminate against certain groups of people, or undermine trust in VLM-based systems. This omission is a significant weakness, as it fails to acknowledge the potential for misuse of the proposed method and the broader societal implications of this research. Third, the paper does not provide sufficient evidence to support the claim that the proposed method effectively alleviates overfitting issues in the deep Transformer blocks of visual and textual features. While the authors claim that gradient clipping mitigates overfitting and present improved attack success rates as evidence, they do not provide direct quantitative measures of overfitting. For example, they do not compare the training and validation performance on the adversarial example generation task itself. The improved stability results in Table 2 offer some indirect support, but a more direct analysis of overfitting is needed to fully validate this claim. This lack of direct evidence weakens the paper's central argument. Fourth, the paper's evaluation is limited to a few state-of-the-art VLMs and datasets. While the authors evaluate their method on Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA, and InstructBLIP, and use the MS-COCO validation set and VQA prompts, this is not sufficient to make strong claims about the generalizability of their findings. A more extensive evaluation across a wider range of models and datasets, including those with different architectures, training objectives, and pre-training datasets, is needed to fully assess the robustness of the proposed method. The limited evaluation scope raises concerns about the method's applicability to other VLMs and data domains. Fifth, the paper does not adequately address the potential for the proposed method to be mitigated by adversarial training or other defense mechanisms. The authors focus solely on the attack methodology and do not discuss how their method could be used to develop more robust VLMs or how existing defense mechanisms might counter the proposed attack. This omission is a significant weakness, as it fails to consider the broader context of adversarial robustness and the potential for future research to develop defenses against the proposed attack. Finally, the paper's claim that the Multi-P attack is directly proportional to the number of prompts is not well-supported. While the authors state in Section 3.2 that "the improvement in cross-prompt transferability is directly proportional to the increase in the number of textual prompts," this seems counter-intuitive, as adding more prompts should not necessarily make the attack stronger. The paper lacks a theoretical justification for this claim, and it is unclear why adding more prompts would lead to a proportional increase in attack success. This claim requires further investigation and validation.

## Suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct a thorough analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the proposed GrCPA method. This analysis should include a formal breakdown of the time and memory complexity of the approach, considering the number of parameters in the VLM, the size of the input data, and the number of iterations required for convergence. The authors should also provide empirical measurements of the runtime and memory usage of their method, comparing it to existing adversarial attack techniques. This analysis should also explore the scalability of the method to larger models and datasets, providing evidence of its performance on models with varying numbers of parameters and datasets of different sizes. Second, the authors should include a comprehensive discussion of the potential ethical implications of their work. This discussion should consider the possible negative consequences of adversarial attacks on VLMs, such as the generation of misleading or harmful content, the potential for these attacks to be used in malicious ways, and the broader societal implications of this research. The authors should also explore the potential for these attacks to be used to evade detection by security systems, manipulate the behavior of VLMs, discriminate against certain groups of people, and undermine trust in VLM-based systems. This discussion should be included in the main body of the paper, not just in a separate section. Third, the authors should provide more direct evidence to support their claim that the proposed method effectively alleviates overfitting. This could involve comparing the training and validation performance on the adversarial example generation task itself, or by using other metrics that directly measure overfitting. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of how the gradient clipping mechanism prevents the optimization process from converging to overly specific solutions. Fourth, the authors should expand their evaluation to include a more diverse set of VLMs and datasets. This should include models with different architectures, training objectives, and pre-training datasets, as well as datasets that cover a wider range of visual and textual content. The authors should also consider evaluating their method under different attack scenarios, such as targeted and untargeted attacks, and different levels of perturbation. This would provide a more complete picture of the method's strengths and weaknesses. Fifth, the authors should investigate the potential for their method to be mitigated by adversarial training or other defense mechanisms. This could involve training VLMs using adversarial examples generated by their method and evaluating the performance of the resulting models. The authors should also explore other defense strategies, such as input sanitization or gradient masking, to determine whether these can effectively counter the proposed attack. This analysis should include a discussion of the limitations of these defense mechanisms and the potential for future attacks to circumvent them. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed theoretical justification for their claim that the Multi-P attack is directly proportional to the number of prompts. This could involve a more rigorous analysis of the optimization process and the impact of adding more prompts on the adversarial example generation. The authors should also provide more experimental evidence to support this claim, perhaps by varying the number of prompts and analyzing the resulting attack success rates. By addressing these weaknesses, the authors can significantly strengthen their paper and make a more substantial contribution to the field of adversarial attacks on VLMs.

## Questions

First, how does the proposed method perform in terms of computational efficiency compared to existing adversarial attack methods for VLMs? The paper lacks a direct comparison of the computational cost of GrCPA with other methods, making it difficult to assess its practical applicability. A detailed analysis of the time and memory requirements of GrCPA compared to other methods would be valuable. Second, can the authors provide a more detailed analysis of the method's performance across different types of visual and textual data? The paper's evaluation is limited to the MS-COCO dataset and VQA prompts, and it is unclear how the method would perform on other types of data, such as medical images or legal documents. A more comprehensive analysis of the method's performance across diverse data types would be beneficial. Third, what are the potential limitations of the proposed method when applied to more complex or diverse datasets? The paper does not explicitly discuss the limitations of the method in terms of dataset complexity or diversity, and it is unclear how the method would perform on datasets with more complex visual or textual content. A more detailed analysis of the method's limitations would be valuable. Fourth, how does the method perform against VLMs that have been specifically trained to be robust against adversarial attacks? The paper evaluates on standard VLMs, not those specifically trained for adversarial robustness, and it is unclear how the method would perform against these more robust models. An evaluation of the method against adversarially robust VLMs would be crucial. Fifth, can the authors provide more insights into the potential defenses against the proposed attack method? The paper focuses solely on the attack methodology and does not discuss potential defenses. A more detailed analysis of the potential defenses against GrCPA would be valuable. Finally, can the authors discuss the potential for extending their method to other types of multimodal models beyond VLMs? The paper focuses specifically on VLMs, and it is unclear whether the proposed method could be applied to other types of multimodal models. An exploration of the potential for extending the method to other models would be beneficial.
