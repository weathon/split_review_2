## summary
This paper introduces AI2TALE, a novel deep learning-based method for localizing phishing attacks within email content. The core idea is to not only detect whether an email is a phishing attempt but also to identify the specific sentences that contribute most significantly to this classification. The authors frame this as a crucial step towards improving the explainability of phishing detection systems, moving beyond simple binary classifications to provide users with concrete evidence of why an email is deemed malicious. The proposed method employs a selection network and a classifier network, jointly trained using a mutual information-based objective. The selection network learns to identify the most relevant sentences, while the classifier network uses these selected sentences to predict the phishing label. The training process is guided by the principle of maximizing the mutual information between the selected sentences and the phishing label, while minimizing the mutual information between the selected and all original sentences. This aims to identify a minimal subset of sentences that maximally contribute to the phishing label. The authors evaluate their method on seven diverse real-world email datasets, comparing it against several state-of-the-art interpretable machine learning approaches. The results, measured in terms of label accuracy and cognitive true positive rate, suggest that AI2TALE outperforms the baselines. Furthermore, a human evaluation is conducted to assess the perceived relevance of the selected sentences, with a majority of participants indicating that the highlighted sentences would influence their decision to follow instructions in the email. The authors also introduce new metrics for evaluating the performance of phishing attack localization methods, which they argue is a contribution to the field. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to the problem of phishing attack localization, combining a theoretically grounded method with empirical validation and human evaluation. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this work.

## strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the paper tackles a very important and timely problem: the detection and explanation of phishing attacks. Phishing remains a significant threat, and methods that can provide both accurate detection and clear explanations are highly valuable. The authors' focus on localization, identifying the specific sentences that contribute to a phishing classification, is a crucial step towards making these systems more transparent and user-friendly. Second, the proposed method is grounded in a solid theoretical foundation, using information theory and the information bottleneck principle. This provides a good rationale for the approach, and the use of mutual information to guide the selection process is a novel and interesting idea. The authors' attempt to minimize the mutual information between the selected and all original sentences, while maximizing the mutual information between the selected sentences and the label, is a clever way to encourage the model to select a minimal yet informative subset of sentences. Third, the empirical evaluation is a strength of the paper. The authors evaluate their method on seven diverse real-world email datasets, which suggests that the method is robust and can handle different types of email data. The comparison against several state-of-the-art interpretable machine learning approaches provides a good benchmark for the performance of AI2TALE. The inclusion of a human evaluation is also a significant strength. The results of the human evaluation suggest that the selected sentences are indeed perceived as relevant and influential by users, which is a critical aspect of the method's practical utility. Finally, the introduction of new metrics for evaluating the performance of phishing attack localization methods is a valuable contribution to the field. These metrics, such as the cognitive true positive rate, provide a more nuanced way to assess the performance of these methods, going beyond simple accuracy measures. These strengths, taken together, make this paper a valuable contribution to the field of phishing detection.
