## Summary
This paper introduces ConvINT, a semi-structured framework designed to enhance conversational AI's understanding of user intentions by organizing them into four key aspects: situation, emotion, action, and knowledge. This framework aims to provide a more holistic and fine-grained approach compared to traditional structured or unstructured methods. To facilitate the large-scale application of ConvINT, the authors propose a Weakly-supervised Reinforced Generation (WeRG) method. WeRG leverages a combination of human-annotated instances, coarsely labeled data, and weak supervision signals to efficiently generate high-quality ConvINT annotations across extensive datasets. The core idea behind WeRG is to use reinforcement learning to train a model that can generate accurate ConvINT labels, guided by a reward function that encourages the model to produce annotations that align with the ConvINT framework. The authors evaluate their approach on two datasets: DuRecDial, a dataset of conversational recommendations, and ESConv, an emotional support conversation dataset. The experimental results demonstrate that integrating ConvINT with WeRG significantly improves the ability of Large Language Models (LLMs) to comprehend user intentions. This improvement is measured through both automatic metrics, such as F1 score, BLEU, and BERTScore, and human evaluations. Furthermore, the paper shows that using the generated ConvINT annotations enhances performance in downstream tasks, such as response generation and task completion. The authors compare their method against several baselines, including direct prompting and chain-of-thought prompting with zero-shot and few-shot settings. The results consistently show that the proposed method outperforms these baselines, indicating the effectiveness of the ConvINT framework and the WeRG method. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the proposed methods and experimental results. The authors provide a thorough explanation of the ConvINT framework and the WeRG mechanism, supported by detailed experimental evaluations. Overall, this work presents a novel approach to intention analysis in conversational AI, offering a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of user inputs. The proposed WeRG method addresses the challenge of scalability while maintaining precision, making it a practical solution for real-world applications. The experimental results are robust and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods, showing significant improvements in both automatic and human evaluations. The paper's contributions lie in the introduction of the ConvINT framework, the development of the WeRG method, and the empirical validation of their effectiveness in enhancing LLMs' understanding of user intentions.

## Strengths

This aspect-aware approach allows for a more fine-grained analysis of user intentions, capturing different facets of the conversational context. Second, the Weakly-supervised Reinforced Generation (WeRG) method is an innovative approach to scaling ConvINT annotations across large datasets. By combining a small set of human-annotated instances with coarsely labeled data as weak supervision signals, WeRG effectively learns to generate high-quality annotations. This addresses the challenge of scalability while maintaining precision, which is crucial for practical applications. The use of reinforcement learning to optimize the annotation generation process is a clever way to leverage weak supervision and produce high-quality labels. Third, the paper is well-written and easy to follow. The logical flow of the paper guides the reader through the motivation, methodology, and experiments, making it easy to understand the key contributions and findings. The authors conduct extensive evaluations on multiple datasets and compare their approach with several baselines, showing significant improvements in both automatic and human evaluations. The authors should provide concrete examples demonstrating how the [KNOWLEDGE] component adds value beyond what is captured by the [SITUATION] and [ACTION] components. For instance, if the conversation is about booking a flight, while the [SITUATION] might capture the intent to travel, and [ACTION] might capture the act of booking, the [KNOWLEDGE] component should clarify specific information like preferred airlines, seating arrangements, or loyalty programs. The authors should also discuss how this knowledge is represented and utilized by the model. A clear definition and examples will help to justify the necessity and utility of this component. Second, to strengthen the evaluation, the authors should expand the human evaluation by including a larger number of annotators with diverse backgrounds and expertise. The inter-annotator agreement should be reported using metrics like Cohen's Kappa or Fleiss' Kappa to ensure the reliability of the human evaluation. The evaluation should also include metrics that assess the impact of the generated annotations on downstream tasks, such as response quality or task completion rates. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the practical utility of the WeRG method. Furthermore, the authors should consider using more sophisticated evaluation metrics beyond surface-level similarity, such as metrics that evaluate the semantic quality and coherence of the generated annotations. The paper should also include an analysis of the types of errors made by the WeRG method and discuss potential strategies for addressing these errors. Third, the authors should also address potential biases in the datasets and the annotation process. A detailed analysis of the datasets should be included, discussing potential cultural biases, limitations in the range of emotions or situations represented, and any other biases that might affect the generalizability of the WeRG method. The paper should also discuss potential biases introduced by the weak supervision approach and how these biases might impact the generated annotations. The authors should also explore the performance of the WeRG method on datasets from different domains or languages to assess its robustness and generalizability. Finally, the paper should include a discussion of the computational cost of the WeRG method and its scalability to larger datasets, as well as the limitations of the framework in handling implicit intentions or conversations where the user's goal is not clearly defined.
