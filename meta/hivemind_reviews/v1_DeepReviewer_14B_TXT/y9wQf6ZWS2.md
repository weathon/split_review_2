## summary
This paper introduces a regularized Q-learning algorithm (RegQ) designed to address the instability issues that can arise when using linear function approximation in traditional Q-learning. The core idea is to incorporate an L2 regularization term directly into the Q-learning update, aiming to ensure convergence even in scenarios where standard Q-learning might diverge. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed algorithm, leveraging an ordinary differential equation (ODE) approach and a switching system model to establish its convergence properties. This analysis hinges on a key assumption regarding the regularization parameter, which must scale with the square root of the state and action space sizes. Empirically, the authors demonstrate that RegQ converges in environments where standard Q-learning is known to fail, and they claim that it exhibits faster convergence than other two-timescale Q-learning algorithms. However, the experimental evaluation is limited in scope, focusing on relatively small-scale environments and lacking a direct validation of the theoretical error bounds. The paper's primary contribution lies in proposing a simple regularization technique to stabilize Q-learning with linear function approximation and providing a theoretical analysis of its convergence. However, the practical implications of the strong theoretical assumptions and the limited empirical validation raise questions about the algorithm's broad applicability. The authors also do not provide a detailed comparison to existing methods, particularly those employing similar regularization techniques, which makes it difficult to assess the true novelty and advantages of their approach. Overall, while the paper presents a theoretically grounded approach to a challenging problem, its practical significance is limited by the strong assumptions and the lack of extensive empirical validation.
