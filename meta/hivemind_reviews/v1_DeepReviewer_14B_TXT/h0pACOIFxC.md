## summary
This paper introduces a novel meta-learning framework, termed Meta-LoRA, designed to enhance the retraining of foundation models (FMs) for downstream tasks. The core idea revolves around addressing the limitations of traditional two-stage retraining and fine-tuning approaches, which often lead to suboptimal performance on unseen tasks. The authors propose a Meta-Adapters objective function, which incorporates parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) during the retraining phase. This approach aims to produce a model that is more adaptable to new tasks, by learning a set of base weights that can be easily adjusted with task-specific adapters. The theoretical underpinnings of the method are rigorously established for linear models, demonstrating that the proposed approach can recover optimally adaptable parameters, a guarantee not offered by standard retraining methods. Specifically, the theoretical analysis shows that the Meta-LoRA objective can lead to a model that is closer to the ground truth parameters, thus facilitating better generalization. The paper presents empirical results on the ConvAI2 dataset, a dialogue dataset, using the RoBERTa-Large model, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method over conventional retraining and fine-tuning approaches. The experiments show that Meta-LoRA achieves better performance on downstream tasks, indicating that the model is more adaptable to unseen tasks. The paper's primary contribution lies in its innovative combination of meta-learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques, along with a rigorous theoretical analysis for linear models. The empirical results, while limited in scope, provide initial evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a significant step towards improving the adaptability of foundation models, with a focus on optimizing the retraining process to produce models that can generalize better to new tasks. The work highlights the potential of meta-learning to address the limitations of traditional fine-tuning approaches and offers a promising direction for future research in this area.
