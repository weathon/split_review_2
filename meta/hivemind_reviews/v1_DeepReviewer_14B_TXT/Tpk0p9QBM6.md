## summary
The paper under review addresses the problem of minimum entropy coupling (MEC) for large-support distributions, a critical issue in various applications such as steganography and causal inference. The authors propose a novel algorithm, ARIMEC, which unifies previous iterative MEC (IMEC) approaches under a single partition-based framework. ARIMEC is designed to handle arbitrary discrete distributions, overcoming the limitations of existing methods that either assume small support or factorability of the distributions. The paper also introduces a merging technique to enhance the robustness of the algorithm to suboptimal hyperparameter settings. The authors provide a thorough experimental evaluation of ARIMEC in two settings: Markov coding games and steganography. The results demonstrate that ARIMEC outperforms existing methods in terms of communication rate and robustness. However, the paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the computational complexity and practical limitations of the proposed algorithm, as well as a clearer articulation of its theoretical guarantees and the challenges in extending it to continuous distributions.

## strengths
I find the paper to be a significant contribution to the field of minimum entropy coupling (MEC). The authors have successfully developed a novel algorithm, ARIMEC, which unifies previous iterative MEC (IMEC) approaches under a single partition-based framework. This unification is a valuable contribution in itself, as it provides a clear and structured way to understand the relationships between different IMEC methods. The introduction of the merging technique is another strong point, as it addresses the issue of robustness to suboptimal hyperparameter settings, which is a common challenge in heuristic algorithms. The paper is well-written and organized, with clear explanations of the concepts and algorithms. The use of figures and examples, such as the prefix tree in Figure 2, helps to illustrate the ideas and make the paper more accessible. The experimental evaluation is thorough and convincing, demonstrating the effectiveness of ARIMEC in two different settings: Markov coding games and steganography. The results show that ARIMEC outperforms existing methods in terms of communication rate and robustness, which is a significant achievement. The authors also provide a clear motivation for their work, highlighting the limitations of existing methods and the need for a more general and robust approach. Overall, the paper makes a strong case for the importance of ARIMEC in the context of MEC for large-support distributions.

## weaknesses
Despite the paper's strengths, there are several areas where it could be improved. One of the primary concerns is the clarity and presentation of the technical sections, particularly Section 4.2, which deals with the efficient implementation of the ARIMEC algorithm. This section is quite dense and difficult to follow, lacking a high-level overview or pseudocode that would help readers understand the algorithm's logic. For example, the concept of 'touching' nodes is introduced but not clearly defined or illustrated with a concrete example, making it challenging for readers to grasp how this mechanism works in practice. Additionally, the explanation of the prefix tree and its construction is too abstract, and a more intuitive connection to the visual representation in Figure 2 would be beneficial. The introduction is also quite dense, attempting to cover too much ground in a single paragraph, including the problem definition, prior work, and the authors' contributions. A more structured introduction, with distinct sections for each of these aspects, would make the paper more accessible and easier to follow. Furthermore, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed algorithm. While the experiments demonstrate the algorithm's effectiveness in certain settings, it is important to acknowledge its potential shortcomings. For instance, the computational complexity and memory requirements of ARIMEC are not thoroughly analyzed, and it is unclear how the algorithm scales with the size of the input distributions. The paper also does not explicitly discuss scenarios where ARIMEC might fail or produce suboptimal solutions, which is crucial for a balanced and realistic assessment of the algorithm's capabilities. Another significant weakness is the lack of a formal theoretical analysis of the proposed algorithm. The paper claims that ARIMEC is the first approach to computing low-entropy couplings for large-support distributions that can be applied to arbitrary distributions, but this statement is not supported by a formal proof. It is also unclear how the approximation quality of ARIMEC relates to the approximation quality of the individual iterative MEC algorithms it encompasses. A more rigorous analysis of the approximation guarantees, even if only in specific cases or under certain assumptions, would significantly strengthen the theoretical contribution. The paper focuses on discrete distributions, but it is unclear whether the proposed approach can be extended to continuous distributions. The current formulation relies heavily on the concept of partitions of discrete sample spaces, and it is not immediately obvious how this framework could be adapted to continuous spaces. A discussion of the challenges and potential solutions for continuous distributions would be valuable. Finally, the experimental section could be strengthened by including a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of ARIMEC. While the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the algorithm in terms of communication rate and robustness, it lacks a thorough analysis of the time and memory requirements, especially as the size of the distributions increases. This analysis should also compare the computational cost of ARIMEC to that of existing methods, providing a more complete picture of the practical trade-offs involved in using the proposed algorithm.

## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete and actionable improvements. First, the introduction should be restructured to break down the dense information into smaller, more digestible sections. The introduction should start with a clear and concise problem statement, followed by a discussion of the existing literature, and then a detailed explanation of the authors' specific contributions. This would make it easier for the reader to understand the context of the work and the novelty of the proposed approach. The technical sections, particularly Section 4.2, should be revised to include a high-level overview of the algorithm, followed by a detailed explanation of each step. The use of pseudocode or flowcharts could also be helpful in clarifying the algorithm's logic. For instance, the concept of 'touching' nodes should be explicitly defined and illustrated with a concrete example, showing how this mechanism works in practice. The explanation of the prefix tree and its construction should be more intuitive, connecting the formal definition to the visual representation in Figure 2. A more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed algorithm is essential. The paper should include a section that explicitly addresses the computational complexity and memory requirements of ARIMEC, especially as the size of the input distributions increases. This analysis should also discuss potential failure cases and scenarios where the algorithm might not produce a good solution. The authors should provide a formal theoretical analysis of the proposed algorithm, including bounds on the approximation ratio and convergence time. This would help to substantiate the claim that ARIMEC is the first approach to computing low-entropy couplings for large-support distributions that can be applied to arbitrary distributions. The paper should also explore the relationship between the approximation quality of ARIMEC and the approximation quality of the individual iterative MEC algorithms it encompasses. To broaden the impact of the paper, the authors should discuss potential extensions to continuous distributions. While a full solution for continuous distributions may be beyond the scope of this paper, a discussion of the challenges and potential approaches, such as quantization techniques or adaptations using measure theory, would be valuable. The experimental section could be strengthened by including a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of ARIMEC. The authors should provide empirical results that demonstrate the runtime and memory usage of ARIMEC on distributions of varying sizes, and compare these results with existing methods. This would allow readers to better assess the practical applicability of the algorithm. Additionally, the paper should explore the sensitivity of ARIMEC to the choice of partition sets and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate partition sets for different types of distributions. A more detailed comparison to existing methods, including a breakdown of the computational cost of each step in the algorithms, would also be beneficial. Finally, the paper could benefit from a dedicated section that summarizes the key contributions, discusses potential directions for future research, and highlights the limitations of the current work. This section should provide a roadmap for future research and help to position the paper within the broader research landscape.
