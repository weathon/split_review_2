## Summary
This paper introduces DPFormer, a novel approach for training Transformer models with differential privacy (DP) guarantees, specifically addressing the challenges of long-tailed data distributions. The core contribution lies in two key techniques: Phantom Clipping and Re-Attention Mechanism. Phantom Clipping is proposed as an efficient method for per-sample gradient clipping, aiming to reduce the computational overhead associated with traditional DP-SGD. This technique leverages the structure of the Transformer's embedding layer to compute clipping bounds without explicitly calculating per-sample gradients. The Re-Attention Mechanism is introduced to mitigate the unintended attention distraction that can arise from the noise introduced during DP-SGD. This mechanism attempts to correct attention scores by tracking and adjusting for the noise propagated through the network. The authors evaluate DPFormer on two real-world datasets, MovieLens and Amazon, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining model performance while ensuring differential privacy. The empirical results show that DPFormer achieves competitive performance compared to baseline models, including vanilla Transformers, GRUs, and LSTMs, under various privacy budgets. The paper emphasizes the practical relevance of DPFormer for scenarios where data is limited, domain-specific, and requires strong privacy guarantees, such as in commercial recommender systems. The authors also highlight the efficiency gains of Phantom Clipping, showing that it significantly reduces training time compared to standard per-sample gradient clipping methods. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of differentially private deep learning, offering a practical and efficient approach for training Transformer models with privacy guarantees. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of theoretical analysis and comparisons with existing methods.
