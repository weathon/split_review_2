## summary
This paper introduces Hebbian View Orthogonal Projection (HVOP), a novel framework designed to address the challenge of catastrophic forgetting in multi-view learning scenarios. The core contribution of this work lies in its bio-inspired approach, drawing from neural mechanisms to facilitate knowledge retention and transfer when new views are introduced. The authors identify the 'view forgetting phenomenon,' where traditional multi-view learning methods struggle to maintain knowledge from previous views when new ones are encountered. To mitigate this, HVOP constructs a Knowledge Transfer Space (KTS) and employs orthogonal projection to reduce interference between old and new views. This is achieved through the use of recursive lateral connections and Hebbian learning, which are inspired by the adaptability of the human brain. The KTS is dynamically updated using Hebbian learning, specifically Oja's rule, which is intended to approximate the principal components of the input data. The model is validated primarily on node classification tasks, demonstrating superior performance in knowledge retention and transfer compared to several baseline methods. The experimental results, presented across multiple datasets, suggest that HVOP effectively mitigates the view forgetting problem, leading to improved overall performance. The authors argue that their approach provides a more robust and adaptable solution for multi-view learning, particularly in dynamic environments where new views are continuously added. The paper's significance lies in its innovative integration of neuroscience-inspired mechanisms into a machine learning framework, offering a fresh perspective on handling the challenges of multi-view learning. By mimicking the brain's ability to integrate and retain information, HVOP presents a promising approach for improving the performance and adaptability of multi-view learning models. The paper's focus on a specific problem, view forgetting, and its bio-inspired solution, makes it a valuable contribution to the field, although there are several areas that could be improved to further strengthen its impact and applicability.

The connection is stated as an inspiration and similarity, not a mathematically proven equivalence. ## suggestions
To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, to strengthen the theoretical foundation of the method, the authors should include a more rigorous mathematical treatment of the interaction between Hebbian learning and lateral connections for orthogonal projection. This should involve a clear definition of the Hebbian update rule used for the lateral connection weights, denoted as $\mathbf{R}$, and a demonstration of how this update rule, in conjunction with the recurrent application of $\mathbf{R}$, leads to the extraction of principal components. The paper should explicitly show how the update dynamics of $\mathbf{R}$ approximate the principal direction of the features, possibly by relating the Hebbian update to Oja's rule or a similar mechanism. A step-by-step derivation or a detailed explanation of the convergence properties of the update rule would significantly strengthen the theoretical foundation of the proposed method. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to include a visualization of the feature space before and after the orthogonal projection to empirically demonstrate the effect of the proposed mechanism. This would provide a more intuitive understanding of how the method works and would help to validate the theoretical claims. 

Second, to broaden the evaluation of the HVOP framework, the authors should include experiments on a wider range of tasks beyond node classification. This could include tasks such as link prediction, where the goal is to predict missing edges in the graph, or community detection, where the objective is to identify clusters of nodes with similar properties. These tasks would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the model's capabilities and its ability to generalize to different types of problems. For each task, the authors should clearly define the evaluation metrics and provide a detailed comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods. This would help to establish the practical utility of the proposed framework and its advantages over existing approaches. Additionally, the authors should consider using datasets with varying characteristics, such as different graph sizes and view numbers, to evaluate the robustness of the model. This would help to identify the strengths and weaknesses of the model and provide a more complete picture of its performance. 

Third, the paper needs a more thorough discussion of the scalability of the HVOP framework. The authors should provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the Hebbian learning and orthogonal projection mechanisms, particularly in relation to the size of the input features and the number of views. This analysis should include both time and space complexity considerations. It would be helpful to provide empirical results on the training time and memory usage of the model on datasets of varying sizes and with different numbers of views. The authors should also discuss any potential bottlenecks in the proposed method and suggest possible strategies for improving its scalability. This discussion is crucial for assessing the practical applicability of the framework in real-world scenarios. 

Fourth, to address the limitations regarding the handling of heterogeneous views, the authors should consider incorporating a mechanism that explicitly measures and mitigates the dissimilarity between feature spaces. For instance, a contrastive loss could be introduced to encourage the model to learn view-specific representations that are both discriminative and aligned in the KTS. This could involve calculating the distance between view-specific embeddings and penalizing large distances for similar instances while encouraging larger distances for dissimilar ones. Furthermore, the authors could explore the use of adaptive weighting schemes that dynamically adjust the contribution of each view based on its relevance to the current learning task. This would allow the model to focus on the most informative views and mitigate the impact of noisy or irrelevant views. A detailed analysis of the model's performance under varying degrees of view heterogeneity would also be beneficial to understand the robustness of the proposed approach. 

Finally, to enhance the analysis of HVOP's performance advantages, the authors should provide a more detailed breakdown of the results. This could include visualizing the learned representations in the KTS to understand how different views are integrated and how the orthogonal projection mechanism mitigates interference. Additionally, the authors should analyze the gradient updates in the KTS to understand how the Hebbian learning rule contributes to knowledge retention and transfer. A sensitivity analysis of the model's performance to hyperparameter settings is also crucial. This could involve systematically varying the learning rate for Hebbian updates, the dimensionality of the KTS, and other relevant parameters, and reporting the impact on performance. This would help to determine the optimal parameter settings and assess the robustness of the model. Furthermore, the authors should compare the computational cost of HVOP with other methods, providing a detailed breakdown of the time and memory requirements for each component of the framework.
