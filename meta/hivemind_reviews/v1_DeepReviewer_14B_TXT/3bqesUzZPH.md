## Summary
This paper introduces FTA, a novel backdoor attack against federated learning (FL) systems. The core idea revolves around using a generative neural network to create sample-specific triggers, which are designed to be stealthy, flexible, and adaptive. Unlike traditional backdoor attacks that rely on fixed, universal triggers, FTA generates unique, imperceptible perturbations for each data sample, making the attack more difficult to detect. The authors argue that this approach addresses the limitations of existing attacks, particularly in robust FL systems. The method involves training a generative model to produce triggers that, when applied to a poisoned sample, cause it to be misclassified as a target class. Crucially, the generated triggers are designed to ensure that the latent features of the poisoned samples are similar to those of benign samples of the target class, thereby enhancing stealth. The attack is evaluated across several datasets, including Fashion-MNIST, FEMNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet, and compared against several state-of-the-art backdoor attacks. The empirical results demonstrate that FTA achieves high attack success rates while maintaining a low impact on benign accuracy. The authors also evaluate the attack's resilience against defenses like norm clipping and FLAME, showing that FTA can bypass these defenses. The paper's significance lies in its novel approach to backdoor attacks in FL, moving away from fixed triggers and towards a more adaptive and stealthy method. The authors claim that FTA's sample-specific triggers make it more robust against defenses that rely on detecting common trigger patterns. The paper also highlights the importance of considering the stealthiness of triggers during global inference, a factor often overlooked in previous research. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to backdoor attacks in federated learning, with promising empirical results and a clear articulation of the limitations of existing methods. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened to enhance its impact and practical relevance.

## Strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be commendable. The core idea of using a generative model to create sample-specific triggers is both novel and interesting. This approach moves beyond the limitations of fixed, universal triggers, which are often easily detectable. The authors' emphasis on the stealthiness of triggers during global inference is a crucial contribution, as it addresses a gap in previous research. The design of the trigger generator, which aims to produce triggers that result in latent features similar to benign samples of the target class, is a clever technique that enhances the attack's stealth. The empirical results presented in the paper are also promising. The experiments demonstrate that FTA achieves high attack success rates across various datasets and models, while maintaining a low impact on benign accuracy. The comparison with several state-of-the-art backdoor attacks provides a clear picture of FTA's performance relative to existing methods. Furthermore, the evaluation of FTA against defenses like norm clipping and FLAME shows that the attack is resilient against these common defense mechanisms. The authors' attempt to differentiate their approach from centralized trigger generators is also a strength. They argue that centralized generators cannot ensure indistinguishable perturbations of model parameters and cannot capture the stealthiness in the feature space as effectively as FTA. The paper's focus on federated learning is also a strength, as it addresses a critical area of research with growing practical importance. The authors clearly articulate the limitations of existing attacks in the context of FL and propose a novel approach to overcome these limitations. The inclusion of an ablation study on the dataset size of the trigger generator is also a positive aspect, as it provides insights into the data requirements of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to backdoor attacks in federated learning, with strong empirical support for its claims. The combination of a novel trigger generation method, a focus on stealthiness, and a thorough evaluation makes this paper a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses
Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, while the authors claim novelty in using a generative model for sample-specific triggers, the core idea of using generative models for backdoor attacks has been explored in prior work. Although the paper attempts to differentiate itself by focusing on the FL context and addressing specific limitations of existing attacks, a more explicit and detailed comparison with relevant prior work, particularly those using generative models for triggers, is needed. The paper does not adequately discuss the specific differences in trigger generation and attack methodology compared to these existing approaches. This lack of a detailed comparison makes it difficult to assess the true novelty of the proposed method. Second, the paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art backdoor attacks in federated learning. While the authors compare FTA against several baselines, they do not include comparisons with specific attacks such as Panda and Poison the Well. This omission makes it unclear how FTA performs relative to these existing attacks in terms of effectiveness and stealthiness. The absence of these comparisons limits the paper's ability to position FTA within the broader landscape of backdoor attacks in FL. Third, the paper does not provide sufficient explanation of why the proposed method can bypass defenses, especially backdoor detection defenses. While the authors evaluate FTA against norm clipping and FLAME, they do not provide a detailed explanation of the mechanisms by which FTA evades these defenses. For example, the paper mentions that FTA can bypass FLIP, but it does not explain why its sample-specific triggers are more effective at evading detection compared to fixed triggers. The paper also does not evaluate against more advanced anomaly detection techniques or dedicated backdoor detection mechanisms. This lack of a detailed explanation and evaluation against a broader range of defenses weakens the paper's claims of robustness. Fourth, the paper's experimental evaluation is limited in scope. The experiments are conducted on a limited set of datasets and models, and the paper does not explore the attack's performance against other types of triggers, models, datasets, or federated learning settings. This limited evaluation makes it difficult to assess the generalizability of the proposed method. Fifth, the paper does not explicitly analyze the number of local iterations required for convergence or compare it to other attacks. While the paper presents results over a significant number of rounds, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the convergence speed or the computational cost of training the generative model on local devices. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical limitations of the proposed attack. Sixth, the paper relies on training a generative model, which generally benefits from more data. While the ablation study on dataset size partially addresses this concern, the paper does not fully explore the impact of varying dataset sizes on the attack's effectiveness. This reliance on a substantial local dataset for training the generative model could hinder its applicability in scenarios where data is scarce. Finally, while the paper does define most of the notations used, the presentation could be improved. The reviewer's confusion suggests that the definitions could be made more prominent or provided earlier in the text. The descriptions of the server and malicious model are present but could be more explicit. These weaknesses, taken together, highlight areas where the paper could be strengthened to enhance its impact and practical relevance. I have high confidence in these identified weaknesses, as they are based on direct examination of the paper's content and the reviewers' specific comments.
