## Summary
This paper introduces a novel perspective on the MLP-Mixer architecture, demonstrating its equivalence to a wider MLP with a sparse weight matrix, achieved through the Kronecker product. This interpretation reveals that MLP-Mixers implicitly incorporate sparsity, a concept extensively studied in deep learning, and connects them to Monarch matrices, another form of sparse parameterization. The authors establish that the implicit regularization of MLP-Mixers is related to the L1-norm of the weights, further solidifying the link between MLP-Mixers and sparsity. Through empirical evaluations, the authors show that MLP-Mixers exhibit similar performance trends to unstructured sparse-weight MLPs when increasing sparsity while keeping the number of connections fixed. They also propose a new family of MLP-Mixer-like architectures called the PK family, which generalizes the MLP-Mixer. The paper concludes by highlighting the importance of sparsity in the success of the MLP-Mixer and suggesting future research directions. The core contribution lies in the theoretical analysis that reveals the underlying sparse structure of MLP-Mixers, providing a deeper understanding of their behavior and opening avenues for more efficient architecture design. The authors' work bridges the gap between MLP-Mixers and the broader literature on sparse neural networks, offering a new lens through which to view and potentially improve these architectures. By connecting the MLP-Mixer to the Kronecker product and Monarch matrices, the paper provides a strong theoretical foundation for understanding the implicit biases of this architecture. The empirical results, while supportive, are somewhat limited in scope, focusing primarily on image classification tasks. The paper's main strength lies in its theoretical analysis and the novel interpretation it provides for MLP-Mixers. The authors successfully demonstrate that the mixing layers of MLP-Mixers can be viewed as a wider MLP with a sparse weight matrix, which is a significant contribution to the field. This interpretation not only provides a deeper understanding of the architecture but also opens up new possibilities for designing more efficient and effective neural networks. The paper's findings suggest that sparsity is a key factor in the success of MLP-Mixers, which has implications for the design of future architectures. The authors also propose a new family of architectures, the PK family, which generalizes the MLP-Mixer and achieves lower test error around C = S. The paper's overall significance lies in its ability to provide a new perspective on MLP-Mixers and connect them to the broader literature on sparse neural networks. This work has the potential to inspire new research directions and lead to the development of more efficient and effective neural network architectures.
