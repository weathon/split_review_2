## summary
This paper introduces a novel random embedding technique called Rademacher-like embedding (RLE), designed to achieve faster computation while preserving key properties of existing random embeddings. The core idea behind RLE is to implicitly generate an embedding matrix using a smaller Rademacher matrix and several auxiliary random arrays. This approach allows for the computation of the embedding through partial sums, resulting in a time complexity of O(n + k^2), which is a significant improvement over the O(nk) complexity of standard Gaussian and Rademacher embeddings, particularly when the embedding dimension k is not significantly larger than the square root of the input dimension n. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of RLE, demonstrating that it satisfies the subspace embedding property with high probability, a crucial characteristic for ensuring the accuracy of downstream algorithms. The paper also presents empirical results showcasing the effectiveness of RLE in two important applications: single-pass randomized singular value decomposition (RSVD) and the randomized Arnoldi process. In both cases, RLE demonstrates speedups of 1.5-1.7x and 1.3x, respectively, compared to Gaussian and sparse sign embeddings, while maintaining comparable accuracy. The paper's contribution lies in the development of a computationally efficient random embedding method that maintains the theoretical guarantees of subspace embeddings. The authors achieve this through a clever construction that leverages the properties of Rademacher matrices while avoiding the explicit generation of large embedding matrices. The method's linear time complexity in the input dimension n, when k is not too large, makes it particularly attractive for high-dimensional data analysis. The empirical validation of RLE in RSVD and the randomized Arnoldi process further underscores its practical relevance. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in its theoretical analysis and experimental evaluation, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of random embeddings, offering a computationally efficient alternative to existing methods. The proposed RLE method has the potential to significantly speed up a variety of applications that rely on random embeddings, making it a noteworthy advancement in the field.

## strengths

This approach allows for a significant reduction in computational complexity, achieving O(n + k^2) time complexity, which is a substantial improvement over the O(nk) complexity of Gaussian and Rademacher embeddings, especially when the embedding dimension k is not significantly larger than the square root of the input dimension n. This linear time complexity is a crucial advantage for handling high-dimensional data, making RLE a practical choice for large-scale applications. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis of RLE, demonstrating that it satisfies the subspace embedding property with high probability. This is a critical aspect of the paper, as it ensures that the proposed method maintains the theoretical guarantees necessary for accurate results in downstream algorithms. The authors prove pairwise independence of the entries of the embedding matrix, which is a key step in establishing the subspace embedding property. Furthermore, the empirical results presented in the paper provide strong support for the practical effectiveness of RLE. The authors demonstrate that RLE achieves significant speedups in single-pass randomized singular value decomposition (RSVD) and the randomized Arnoldi process, while maintaining comparable accuracy to Gaussian and sparse sign embeddings. The speedups of 1.5-1.7x in RSVD and 1.3x in the randomized Arnoldi process are compelling evidence of the practical benefits of RLE. The paper's clear presentation of the method, along with the theoretical analysis and empirical validation, makes it a valuable contribution to the field of random embeddings. The authors have successfully combined theoretical rigor with practical relevance, resulting in a method that has the potential to significantly impact a wide range of applications. The simplicity of the construction, while achieving a significant improvement in computational complexity, is also a notable strength of the proposed method.

## weaknesses
While the paper presents a promising new random embedding technique, there are several weaknesses that I have identified through my analysis. First, the paper lacks a thorough comparison with existing oblivious embedding constructions, particularly those based on fast transform techniques. Specifically, the paper omits a citation and comparison with the work of Lemanczyk et al. (2015), which presents a construction of embedding matrices with similar linear complexity under the condition k = O(n^(1/2)). This omission is significant because the construction in Lemanczyk et al. (2015) achieves a complexity of O(n^(3/2)/log n), which is comparable to the proposed RLE method. The absence of a discussion of this related work weakens the paper's positioning within the broader landscape of oblivious embeddings. The paper does mention oblivious embeddings in the background section, citing Balabanov & Grigori (2022) and Balabanov & Nouy (2019), but it fails to acknowledge or compare with other constructions achieving similar properties, particularly those using fast transforms. This omission makes it difficult to assess the true advantages and disadvantages of RLE relative to existing techniques. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the absence of this citation and comparison is clearly evident in the paper. Second, the proof of Theorem 6, which establishes the subspace embedding property of RLE, is flawed. The authors claim that the proof follows from Lemma 9 (concentration inequality for quadratic forms) and Lemma 10 (pairwise independence). However, Lemma 10 only shows that |u_i^T Θ u_i - u_i^T u_i| < ε_1 for any vector u_i, but it does not demonstrate that |u_i^T Θ u_i - u_j^T Θ u_j| < ε_1 for any two vectors u_i and u_j. This is a crucial step in the proof of Theorem 6, and the authors need to provide a more rigorous argument. The pairwise independence of entries, as established in Lemma 10, is insufficient to guarantee the required concentration for differences between quadratic forms. The counterexample provided in the review clearly demonstrates this issue: with u_1 = (1, 0, ..., 0) and u_2 = (0, 1, ..., 0), the difference |u_1^T Θ u_1 - u_2^T Θ u_2| can be 2/√k with probability 1/2, which does not satisfy the condition for a subspace embedding. This flaw undermines the theoretical guarantees of the proposed method, and my confidence in this weakness is high, as the logical gap in the proof is clearly identifiable. Third, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the constant factors involved in the complexity analysis. While the asymptotic complexity of RLE is O(n + k^2), the actual runtime performance may depend heavily on these constants. The paper compares the asymptotic complexity of RLE with Gaussian and Rademacher embeddings but does not delve into the constant factors, which can significantly impact real-world performance. A detailed comparison of these constants with existing methods, such as sparse sign embedding, would be beneficial. This weakness is evident in the lack of a detailed analysis of the constant factors in the complexity analysis. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper focuses on asymptotic complexity without a detailed analysis of the constant factors. Fourth, the numerical experiments are limited to specific datasets and parameter settings. The paper uses a fixed set of synthetic and real-world datasets for the single-pass RSVD experiments, and the parameter k is fixed at 10. Similarly, the randomized Arnoldi process experiments use datasets from the SparseSuite Matrix Collection, with the embedding dimension k fixed at 200. The lack of exploration across a wider range of datasets and parameter settings limits the ability to assess the generalizability of RLE's performance and robustness. This weakness is evident in the limited scope of the numerical experiments, and my confidence in this weakness is high. Fifth, the paper does not provide a detailed comparison of the proposed RLE method with other state-of-the-art random embedding methods, such as Count Sketch and SRHT, in terms of both theoretical properties and practical performance. While the paper mentions SRHT and Count Sketch in the background section, it does not include them in the experimental comparison. A direct comparison would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of RLE's performance relative to these widely used methods. This omission makes it difficult to fully assess RLE's strengths and weaknesses relative to these established methods. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the experimental section clearly lacks a comparison with SRHT and Count Sketch. Sixth, the paper does not provide a clear explanation of the advantages and disadvantages of the proposed method compared to existing methods. While the paper touches upon the advantages of RLE (linear complexity, robustness), it lacks a dedicated and systematic discussion of its advantages and disadvantages compared to a broader range of existing methods. This makes it harder for the reader to fully understand the trade-offs associated with using RLE. This weakness is evident in the lack of a dedicated section systematically comparing the advantages and disadvantages of RLE with existing methods, and my confidence in this weakness is high. Finally, the paper does not explore the potential limitations or drawbacks of the proposed RLE method, such as the sensitivity of RLE to the choice of parameters, such as the size of the Rademacher matrix and the number of auxiliary random arrays. It also does not address the potential for numerical instability or the impact of using finite-precision arithmetic, which could affect the practical performance of the method. This weakness is evident in the lack of a discussion of the potential limitations and drawbacks of RLE, and my confidence in this weakness is high.
