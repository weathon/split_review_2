## Summary
This paper introduces Hierarchical Side-Tuning (HST), a novel parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) method designed to adapt large Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones to a diverse range of downstream visual tasks. The core idea behind HST is to leverage intermediate activations from the pre-trained ViT backbone and process them through a lightweight, hierarchical side network (HSN). This HSN is designed to capture multi-scale features, which are then used to enhance the prediction capabilities of the model. The authors argue that this approach addresses the limitations of existing PETL methods, particularly in handling complex vision tasks like dense prediction. The HSN is structured with a series of side blocks that process intermediate features from the ViT backbone, incorporating a Meta-Register and a Transformation Bridge (T-Bridge) to facilitate the fusion of global and fine-grained information. The method is evaluated across a wide range of visual tasks, including image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation. The experimental results demonstrate that HST achieves competitive performance on the VTAB-1K benchmark and outperforms existing PETL methods on object detection and semantic segmentation tasks on the COCO and ADE20K datasets. The authors emphasize that HST significantly reduces the performance gap between PETL methods and full fine-tuning, particularly in dense prediction tasks, while maintaining a relatively small number of trainable parameters. The paper also includes an efficiency analysis, comparing the training time and inference speed of HST with other PETL methods. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to parameter-efficient transfer learning for vision transformers, with a focus on leveraging intermediate features and multi-scale representations. The authors claim that HST can serve as a versatile PETL technique applicable to various downstream tasks, potentially inspiring further research in this domain. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened through more comprehensive analysis and comparisons.

## Strengths
I find several aspects of this paper to be commendable. The core idea of leveraging intermediate features from a pre-trained ViT backbone and processing them through a hierarchical side network is both novel and effective. The design of the HSN, with its Meta-Register and Transformation Bridge, demonstrates a thoughtful approach to capturing multi-scale information and fusing global and fine-grained features. The experimental results are generally strong, showing that HST achieves competitive performance on a variety of visual tasks. Specifically, the paper demonstrates that HST significantly reduces the performance gap between PETL methods and full fine-tuning, particularly in dense prediction tasks, which is a notable achievement. The authors have also conducted a thorough evaluation of HST across a range of benchmarks, including VTAB-1K, COCO, and ADE20K, which provides a comprehensive assessment of the method's capabilities. The inclusion of an efficiency analysis, comparing the training time and inference speed of HST with other PETL methods, is also a positive aspect, although I will discuss later that this analysis could be more comprehensive. The paper is well-written and clearly presents the proposed method and experimental results. The authors have also made an effort to compare HST with a range of existing PETL methods, which helps to contextualize the contribution of their work. The focus on parameter efficiency is also a significant strength, as it addresses a critical challenge in adapting large pre-trained models to downstream tasks. The authors have clearly articulated the motivation for their work, highlighting the limitations of existing PETL methods and positioning HST as a solution to these limitations. The experimental results, particularly the performance gains in dense prediction tasks, provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method. The paper's focus on practical applicability and the potential for HST to serve as a versatile PETL technique is also a valuable contribution to the field.

## Suggestions
Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements to this paper. First, the authors should provide a more detailed and explicit discussion of the novelty of HST compared to existing methods like Side-Tuning and LST. This should include a clear articulation of the specific architectural choices and their impact on performance. A detailed ablation study could help to isolate the contributions of each component of HST, demonstrating how the hierarchical side network and the use of intermediate features lead to performance gains. Second, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive comparison with other parameter-efficient methods, including STT, AdaptFormer, and NOAH. This comparison should include not only performance metrics but also computational cost and memory requirements. The paper should also include a direct comparison with the Side-Tuning method, given the conceptual overlap. Third, the authors should provide a more in-depth analysis of the computational cost of HST, including a breakdown of the FLOPs and memory requirements for each component of the proposed method. This analysis should also compare HST with other parameter-efficient methods, such as LoRA, SSF, and Bias. Fourth, the authors should investigate the reasons behind the lower performance of HST on specific datasets like EuroSAT, Clevr/Distance, and SmallNORB/Ele. This could involve analyzing the characteristics of these datasets and identifying the specific challenges they pose for HST. Based on this analysis, the authors could propose potential solutions to improve the robustness of HST across different datasets. This could involve incorporating data augmentation techniques, adjusting the architecture of the side network, or exploring different training strategies. Fifth, the authors should explore the applicability of HST to other types of vision models, such as ConvNeXt and ResNets. This would help to establish the generalizability of the proposed method and its potential for broader application. The paper should also discuss any modifications that might be necessary to adapt HST to these other architectures. Sixth, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the broader impact and generalizability of HST. This should include a consideration of the limitations of HST and the potential challenges in applying it to more complex tasks or datasets. The paper should also explore the potential for extending HST to other modalities, such as natural language processing or audio processing. Seventh, the authors should expand the efficiency analysis to include all the PETL methods used in the performance comparisons. This would provide a more complete picture of the practical advantages and limitations of HST compared to other PETL approaches. Finally, the authors should include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of HST and suggest potential directions for future research. This would help to provide a more balanced and nuanced view of the proposed method and its potential impact on the field.
