### Summary

The paper introduces a reinforcement learning framework called Real-TG, designed to fine-tune large language models (LLMs) for explainable link forecasting on real-world temporal graphs (TGs). Real-TG leverages an outcome-based reward to encourage models to self-explore reasoning strategies from graph structures and generate explanations that justify their predictions. The authors propose a new evaluation protocol that combines ranking metrics with an LLM-as-a-Judge system to assess the quality of reasoning and the impact of hallucinations. Experiments demonstrate that Real-TG-4B, based on the Qwen3-4B model, outperforms larger models, including GPT-5 mini, on prediction accuracy and generates high-quality explanations validated by both LLM judges and human evaluations.

### Strengths

1. The paper presents a novel reinforcement learning framework, Real-TG, specifically tailored for explainable link forecasting on temporal graphs. This approach is innovative in its use of LLMs for temporal graph reasoning, addressing a gap in existing methods that often lack explainability and struggle with generalization to unseen graphs.
2. The introduction of a new evaluation protocol is a significant contribution. By combining ranking metrics with an LLM-as-a-Judge system, the paper provides a comprehensive method for assessing both prediction accuracy and the quality of reasoning traces. This dual approach is particularly valuable for evaluating the trustworthiness and reliability of LLM-generated explanations.
3. The experimental results are robust, showing that Real-TG-4B outperforms much larger LLMs on multiple real-world temporal graph datasets. The model's ability to generalize to unseen graphs and produce high-quality explanations, as confirmed by both LLM judges and human evaluations, underscores the practical impact of the proposed framework.

1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the Real-TG framework, particularly in scenarios with highly complex or noisy temporal graphs. While the authors mention the model's ability to generalize to unseen graphs, they do not delve into specific challenges that might arise in more intricate graph structures or in the presence of significant noise. For instance, how does the model perform when the temporal dependencies are long-range or when the graph exhibits rapid changes in structure? A more thorough analysis of these edge cases would strengthen the paper's claims.
2. The evaluation protocol, while comprehensive, could be further enhanced by including a wider range of baseline models, especially those that are specifically designed for temporal graph reasoning. Comparing Real-TG against a more diverse set of state-of-the-art methods would provide a clearer picture of its relative strengths and weaknesses. For example, incorporating models that utilize attention mechanisms or graph convolutional networks tailored for temporal data would offer a more robust benchmark. This would help to contextualize the performance gains achieved by Real-TG.
3. The paper's focus on explainability is a significant strength, but it could be further strengthened by providing more concrete examples of the explanations generated by the model. While the authors mention that the explanations are validated by both LLM judges and human evaluations, they do not provide specific examples of these explanations in the main text. Including such examples would allow readers to better assess the quality and interpretability of the explanations, and would provide more evidence for the model's ability to generate meaningful insights.

### Suggestions

To address the limitations regarding complex and noisy temporal graphs, the authors should consider incorporating experiments that specifically test the model's robustness under these conditions. This could involve using datasets with known long-range temporal dependencies or introducing synthetic noise into the graph structure. Analyzing the model's performance on these datasets would provide valuable insights into its limitations and potential areas for improvement. Furthermore, the authors could explore techniques for enhancing the model's ability to handle noise, such as incorporating denoising mechanisms or using data augmentation strategies. This would make the framework more applicable to real-world scenarios where data is often imperfect.

To strengthen the evaluation, the authors should include a more diverse set of baseline models, particularly those that are specifically designed for temporal graph reasoning. This could involve incorporating models that utilize attention mechanisms, graph convolutional networks, or recurrent neural networks tailored for temporal data. Comparing Real-TG against these models would provide a more comprehensive understanding of its relative strengths and weaknesses. Additionally, the authors should consider using a wider range of evaluation metrics, such as precision, recall, and F1-score, to provide a more complete picture of the model's performance. This would allow for a more nuanced comparison with existing methods and would help to identify specific areas where Real-TG excels or falls short.

Finally, to enhance the paper's focus on explainability, the authors should include concrete examples of the explanations generated by the model in the main text. This could involve providing a few representative examples of the reasoning traces produced by Real-TG, along with a discussion of their quality and interpretability. This would allow readers to better assess the model's ability to generate meaningful insights and would provide more evidence for its explainability. Furthermore, the authors could explore techniques for visualizing the reasoning process, such as using graph visualizations or attention maps. This would make the explanations more accessible and would help to build trust in the model's predictions.
