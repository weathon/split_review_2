### Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to machine unlearning (MU), specifically focusing on irreversible feature-level unlearning. The authors propose the MoRE (Mixture of Remapping Experts) framework, which aims to address the limitations of existing MU methods by ensuring that unwanted knowledge is not only removed but also made unrecoverable. MoRE achieves this through three key innovations: prototype-orthogonal projection, which decorrelates forget and remain prototypes; remapping with mixture experts, which merges forget prototypes into remain prototypes; and efficient activation-mean prototypes, which reduce the computational complexity of unlearning. The authors demonstrate that MoRE preserves the utility of the remaining data, ensures irreversibility at the feature level, and scales effectively to large models and datasets.

### Strengths

The paper presents a novel approach to machine unlearning (MU) by focusing on irreversible feature-level unlearning, which is a significant advancement over existing methods that often leave residual knowledge vulnerable to recovery. The MoRE framework introduces three key innovations—prototype-orthogonal projection, remapping with mixture experts, and efficient activation-mean prototypes—that work together to ensure that unwanted knowledge is not only removed but also made unrecoverable. This is a creative combination of techniques that addresses the limitations of previous approaches. The authors provide a rigorous mathematical foundation for their method, including proofs of irreversibility and utility preservation. The use of prototype-orthogonal projection to decorrelate forget and remain prototypes is a clever way to ensure that unlearning targets only the unwanted information. The remapping with mixture experts further enhances the irreversibility by scattering the forget features across the latent space, making recovery through fine-tuning or linear probing significantly harder. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the authors' arguments and understand the proposed method. The figures and tables are informative and effectively illustrate the key concepts and results. The authors provide a comprehensive overview of related work, placing their contribution in the context of existing research. The extensive experiments demonstrate that MoRE preserves the utility of the remaining data, ensures irreversibility at the feature level, and scales effectively to large models and datasets. The results are presented clearly, and the authors provide a thorough analysis of their findings. The paper makes a significant contribution to the field of machine unlearning by introducing a novel framework that addresses the long-standing challenges of utility degradation, irreversibility, and scalability. The MoRE framework has the potential to enable more trustworthy and privacy-preserving machine learning systems. The authors' work opens up new avenues for research in this area and provides a valuable tool for practitioners.

While the paper presents a novel and effective approach to machine unlearning, there are several areas where further investigation and clarification could strengthen the work. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the computational overhead of MoRE compared to existing methods. While the authors claim linear computational complexity, a more thorough analysis of the practical implications of this complexity would be valuable. Specifically, the paper should include a breakdown of the time and memory requirements for each step of the MoRE framework, including the prototype-orthogonal projection, the remapping with mixture experts, and the efficient activation-mean prototypes. This analysis should consider the impact of different model sizes and dataset complexities on the computational cost. Furthermore, a comparison of the computational overhead of MoRE with other unlearning methods, such as gradient-based or adversarial approaches, would provide a more comprehensive understanding of its efficiency. The paper should also discuss the potential for parallelization and optimization of the MoRE framework to further reduce its computational cost. The choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of experts in the mixture model, is not thoroughly explored. A sensitivity analysis of these parameters would provide valuable insights into the robustness of MoRE. The paper should include a detailed discussion of how the number of experts affects the unlearning performance, the utility of the remaining knowledge, and the computational cost. For example, how does the performance of MoRE change when the number of experts is varied from a small number to a large number? What is the optimal number of experts for different datasets and model architectures? The authors should also explore the impact of other hyperparameters, such as the learning rate and the number of iterations, on the performance of MoRE. This analysis should include both quantitative metrics, such as the unlearning accuracy and the remaining knowledge utility, and qualitative metrics, such as the visual inspection of the remapped features. The paper should also discuss the potential for using adaptive hyperparameter selection methods to automatically tune the hyperparameters of MoRE for different datasets and models. The paper primarily focuses on image classification tasks. While the authors demonstrate the applicability of MoRE to text-to-image generation models, a more comprehensive evaluation of its performance on other types of data and tasks would be beneficial. For example, how does MoRE perform on natural language processing tasks, such as text classification or machine translation? How does it perform on time-series data or graph data? The authors should also explore the potential for applying MoRE to other types of unlearning tasks, such as concept unlearning or membership inference unlearning. This would provide a more complete picture of the capabilities and limitations of the proposed framework. The paper could also benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach. For example, how does MoRE perform when the forget and remain data are highly correlated? Are there specific types of data or tasks for which MoRE is not well-suited? The authors should also discuss the potential for adversarial attacks on MoRE, where an attacker attempts to recover the forgotten knowledge. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the security of the proposed framework. Finally, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the ethical implications of machine unlearning. While the authors mention the importance of privacy and data protection, they could elaborate on the potential for misuse of unlearning techniques. For example, could unlearning be used to remove evidence of bias or discrimination from a model? Could it be used to censor information or suppress free speech? The authors should also discuss the potential for unintended consequences of unlearning, such as the introduction of new biases or the degradation of model performance. A more thorough discussion of these ethical considerations would help to ensure that the proposed framework is used responsibly and ethically.
