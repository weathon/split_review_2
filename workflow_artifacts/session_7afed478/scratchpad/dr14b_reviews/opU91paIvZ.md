### Summary

This paper addresses the issue of unfaithful and verbose CoT traces in language models. The authors propose a novel approach that formulates CoT monitorability as a constraint optimization problem, aiming to improve both faithfulness and conciseness. The method involves leveraging existing instruct models to transform existing CoT traces into high-quality ones that satisfy monitorability constraints. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on several benchmark datasets, showing improvements in faithfulness and reductions in CoT length while maintaining accuracy.

The comparison should not only focus on the quantitative results but also on the qualitative aspects of the generated CoTs, such as the types of errors made and the overall coherence of the reasoning. ### Suggestions

To address the limitations regarding the sensitivity of the method to initial CoT trace quality, the authors should conduct experiments where the initial traces are systematically varied in terms of their faithfulness and conciseness. This could involve introducing noise or errors into the initial traces or using traces generated by different models with varying levels of performance. By analyzing how the proposed method performs under these conditions, the authors can better understand its robustness and identify potential failure modes. Furthermore, the authors should explore the impact of different monitorability constraints on the quality of the generated reasoning. This could involve varying the thresholds for faithfulness and conciseness and analyzing how these changes affect the final CoT traces. Such an analysis would provide valuable insights into the trade-offs between different monitorability objectives and help in determining the optimal settings for the method.

To enhance the comparison with existing approaches, the authors should include a more detailed analysis of the qualitative differences between the CoTs generated by their method and those generated by other techniques. This could involve a manual inspection of the generated traces to identify common errors and patterns. For example, the authors could analyze whether their method tends to produce overly simplistic reasoning or if it struggles with complex logical inferences. Additionally, the authors should provide a more thorough comparison of the computational cost and ease of implementation of their method compared to other approaches. This should include a discussion of the training time, the number of parameters, and the hardware requirements. A detailed analysis of these aspects would help in assessing the practical applicability of the proposed method and its potential for real-world deployment.

Finally, the authors should consider exploring the use of more sophisticated evaluation metrics that go beyond simple accuracy and faithfulness scores. For instance, metrics that measure the logical consistency of the generated reasoning or the degree to which the reasoning aligns with human expectations could provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's performance. Furthermore, the authors could investigate the use of adversarial attacks to test the robustness of their method. By generating adversarial examples that are designed to mislead the model, the authors can gain a better understanding of the method's limitations and identify areas for improvement. This would provide a more rigorous evaluation of the method's ability to produce reliable and trustworthy reasoning.
