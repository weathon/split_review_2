### Summary

This paper introduces a new vulnerability in LLMs called involuntary jailbreak, which is distinct from existing jailbreak attacks. Unlike traditional attacks that target specific malicious objectives, involuntary jailbreaks can potentially compromise the entire guardrail structure of LLMs. The authors demonstrate that a simple prompt strategy can consistently jailbreak almost all leading LLMs tested, including Claude Opus 4.1, Grok 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and GPT 4.1. This vulnerability highlights the fragility of current LLM guardrails and emphasizes the need for re-evaluating the robustness of LLM safety mechanisms.

2. The discussion on potential defenses is limited, which may reduce the paper's practical utility for developers. The paper does not delve into specific strategies for mitigating the identified vulnerabilities. For instance, it lacks discussion on techniques like adversarial training, input sanitization, or output filtering, which are commonly employed to enhance model robustness. This omission leaves a gap in understanding how to practically address the vulnerabilities exposed by the proposed method.
3. The involuntary nature of the jailbreak is not thoroughly analyzed, leaving questions about the underlying mechanisms. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of why the model produces unsafe outputs when prompted in this manner. It is unclear whether this is due to a failure in the model's safety classifier, a weakness in its reasoning capabilities, or some other mechanism. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to understand the root cause of the vulnerability and to develop targeted defenses.

### Suggestions

To strengthen the paper, the authors should include a comprehensive comparison with existing jailbreaking benchmarks. This should involve not only reporting success rates but also analyzing the types of prompts and attacks used in these benchmarks and how the 'involuntary jailbreak' method compares in terms of effectiveness, stealth, and robustness. For example, the authors could evaluate their method against the top-performing attacks from the GJC or other relevant benchmarks, providing a detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of each approach. This would help to establish the novelty and significance of the proposed method and provide a clearer understanding of its practical implications. Furthermore, the authors should explore the transferability of their method to different models and datasets, which would further validate its robustness and generalizability.

The paper would also benefit from a more detailed discussion of potential defense mechanisms. The authors should explore specific strategies that could be used to mitigate the vulnerabilities exposed by their method. This could include techniques such as adversarial training, where the model is trained on examples generated by the 'involuntary jailbreak' method to improve its robustness. Another approach could be input sanitization, where the prompt is modified to remove or alter the elements that trigger the unsafe outputs. Additionally, the authors should consider output filtering, where the model's response is analyzed for potentially harmful content and modified or rejected if necessary. A thorough discussion of these and other defense mechanisms would significantly enhance the practical utility of the paper for developers and researchers working on LLM safety.

Finally, the authors should provide a more in-depth analysis of the underlying mechanisms that cause the 'involuntary jailbreak'. This could involve examining the model's internal representations and activations when processing the specific prompts used in the attack. The authors could also explore the role of different components of the model, such as the safety classifier and the reasoning module, in the generation of unsafe outputs. This analysis could help to identify the specific weaknesses that are exploited by the attack and to develop more targeted defenses. Furthermore, the authors should investigate whether the 'involuntary jailbreak' is more effective against certain types of models or datasets, which would provide a more nuanced understanding of the vulnerability and its implications.
