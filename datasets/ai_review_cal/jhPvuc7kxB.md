## Summary

This paper proposes *Look, Remember, Reason* (LRR), a framework that augments an autoregressive language model with a two-stream video encoder and trains it end-to-end on low-level surrogate tasks (object detection, re-identification, tracking) to ground high-level video reasoning in fine-grained spatiotemporal information. Surrogate tasks are used only during training via stochastic prompting; at inference the model solves the target task without surrogate overhead. Experiments on ACRE, Something-Else, CATER, and STAR show strong results, with ablations confirming that both the surrogate tasks and the two-stream encoder contribute substantially to performance.

## Strengths

1. **Surrogate-task supervision demonstrably improves grounding.** The ablation removing surrogate tasks causes accuracy to collapse across all benchmarks: from 98.2% to 38.1% on ACRE compositional (Table 1), from 62.0% to 50.1% on Something-Else compositional (Table 2), from 84.1% to 68.5% on CATER static (Table 3), and from 70.5% to 48.2% on STAR (Table 4). These large drops provide direct evidence that the surrogate tasks are essential for the model's ability to extract relevant low-level visual information, and the pattern is consistent across four diverse benchmarks.

2. **Two-stream video encoder (divided space-time attention) captures motion information critical for compositional tasks.** The ablation replacing the two-stream encoder with a plain single-stream ViT (w/o Two-stream encoder) degrades performance consistently, with an 8.4% Top-1 drop on the Something-Else compositional split (Table 2) and a 4.8% drop on CATER moving camera (Table 3). The ACRE results (Table 1) additionally show that even on a static-image causal reasoning task, the temporal attention mechanism helps with object re-identification across trials.

3. **Consistent SOTA-level results on three out of four benchmarks with clean comparisons.** On ACRE, LRR outperforms ALOE by 6.5% compositional / 5.3% systematic (Table 1), which is a fair comparison (both trained on ACRE). On Something-Else, LRR beats STIN+OIE+NL by 5.8% Top-1 on the compositional split (Table 2). On CATER, LRR outperforms ALOE by 20.7% on the moving-camera split and TFC V3D by 4.4% on the static split (Table 3). These gains are large and come from controlled comparisons.

4. **Robustness across LM backbone scale.** Performance is nearly identical when switching from OPT-125M to OPT-1.3B on ACRE (98.2% vs 99.0% systematic) and Something-Else (62.0% vs 61.3% compositional), indicating the gains come from the grounding mechanism rather than model capacity.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **STAR evaluation is confounded by additional training data not used by baselines.** For STAR (Table 4), LRR is jointly trained on Kinetics, Moments in Time, Something-Else surrogate tracking tasks, and extra text data — none of which are used by the compared baselines (SeViLA, Internvideo, BLIP-2). The paper acknowledges this (lines 409–411) but does not provide a controlled comparison. The claim of "outperforming state-of-the-art" on STAR therefore conflates the value of the surrogate-task approach with the advantage of additional training data from multiple datasets. Without a version of LRR trained only on the STAR training set, or a baseline trained with the same extra data, the 5.6% gain over SeViLA is uninterpretable as evidence for the method's grounding contribution.

2. **Quality of surrogate-task annotations is not analyzed.** All surrogate-task ground truth (detection boxes, object classes, track IDs) is generated by off-the-shelf vision models (R-CNN, etc.; line 172). The paper provides no measure of annotation accuracy on any benchmark, no analysis of how detection/tracking errors propagate to reasoning performance, and no comparison against oracle annotations where available (e.g., CATER provides ground-truth object positions). This makes it unclear whether the model learns genuine grounding or instead learns to mimic the detector's output patterns — a critical distinction for the paper's central claim.

### Minor

3. **OpenFlamingo baseline on ACRE: unclear whether fine-tuned or zero-shot.** The paper does not specify whether the OpenFlamingo baseline (Table 1) was fine-tuned on ACRE or evaluated zero-shot. The asterisk indicates "tested by ourselves" but gives no training protocol. If zero-shot, the 60% gap is inflated by this mismatch. Importantly, this does *not* undermine the paper's main ACRE result (LRR 98.2% vs ALOE 91.7%, a clean within-dataset comparison), but the text (line 257) highlights the OpenFlamingo gap prominently, which overstates the evidence.

4. **No variance or confidence intervals reported.** None of the tables report standard deviations or confidence intervals. Given the sensitivity of LM fine-tuning to random seeds and the large number of ablation comparisons, single-run results leave open the question of whether observed differences are statistically reliable.

5. **"Two-stream" terminology is imprecise.** The paper cites Simonyan & Zisserman (2014) for "two-stream" but the actual architecture is a single encoder with divided space-time attention (spatial attention over patches within a frame, temporal attention over the same patch location across τ frames), citing Bertasius et al. (2021). There are no separate RGB and optical-flow streams. This does not affect the results but the naming is technically misleading.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Include a controlled STAR experiment: train LRR on STAR alone (without Kinetics, Moments in Time, Something-Else surrogate data) and compare to baselines. This would cleanly isolate the grounding contribution.
- Report detection/tracking accuracy of the off-the-shelf annotation models on each benchmark to quantify annotation noise.
- Report standard deviations across multiple random seeds for the main results.
- Provide failure-case analysis showing when surrogate-task annotations are incorrect and how the model responds.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution:

- **"Zero-shot vs. fine-tuned for Video-ChatGPT on Something-Else"** — The paper explicitly states on line 317: "Video-ChatGPT (finetuned)." The criticism that "the text never says they were fine-tuned" is factually wrong and is removed.
- **"The Look step is misaligned with inference"** — The paper clearly states (lines 176–177) that "Random prompting forgoes the need to include surrogate tasks during inference time." The naming convention is standard for training-vs-inference distinctions. This is a misunderstanding of the paper's design.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The harsh critic's observation that the "w/o Surrogate tasks" ablation on ACRE drops to near-chance (38.1%) is noteworthy: it suggests the LM backbone alone extracts almost zero useful visual information from the video frames without the surrogate-task training signal, even though it has access to the same visual features through the cross-attention mechanism. This supports the paper's core thesis more strongly than the paper itself emphasizes — the surrogate tasks are not merely helpful but appear to be *necessary* for the LM to extract any task-relevant visual information at all on this benchmark.

## Suggestions

1. **Disentangle the STAR confound.** Add an ablation that trains LRR on STAR-only data and compares it directly to baselines trained on STAR-only data. This would cleanly isolate the grounding benefit from the benefit of additional training data.

2. **Report annotation quality.** Provide precision/recall of the off-the-shelf detectors on each dataset (especially CATER, where ground-truth object positions are available). Correlate annotation accuracy with downstream reasoning accuracy to show the method is robust to — or limited by — annotation noise.

3. **Clarify the OpenFlamingo comparison.** State explicitly whether OpenFlamingo was fine-tuned on ACRE or evaluated zero-shot. If only zero-shot results were obtainable, demote that comparison to supplementary and avoid claiming a "60% gain" without qualification.

4. **Add variance estimates.** Report results across at least 3 random seeds for the main tables, especially for the ablation comparisons.

5. **Rename "two-stream" to avoid confusion.** The architecture uses divided space-time attention within a single encoder; terms like "divided space-time encoder" or "spatiotemporal encoder" would be more precise.
