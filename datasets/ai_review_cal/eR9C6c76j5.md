## Summary

This paper proposes ARGMINN, an intrinsically interpretable neural architecture for regulatory genomics. It consists of (1) a filter-overlap regularizer that forces convolutional filters to encode distinct, non-redundant motifs, and (2) a modified attention mechanism with a memory stream that directly reveals motif instances and their positional syntax in a single forward pass. The paper presents theoretical expressivity claims (FOL-completeness) and empirical evaluations on motif discovery, motif instance calling, QTL prioritization, robustness, and interpretable sequence generation.

## Strengths

- **Filter-overlap regularization empirically produces monosemantic filters.** The core architectural contribution — a regularizer encouraging each filter to learn one distinct motif — is validated across multiple simulated and experimental datasets. Figure 1 shows ARGMINN consistently recovers more relevant motifs with higher similarity to ground truth, fewer redundancies, and fewer extraneous patterns compared to standard CNN, ExplaiNN, and MoDISco. This directly demonstrates that the learned filters encode biologically meaningful, non-redundant motifs.

- **Attention-with-memory-stream enables direct syntax extraction.** The modified attention mechanism derives keys/values directly from motif-scanner activations, allowing attention scores to trace back to specific motif instances. The recovery of REST's complex binding syntax (two half-sites with specific spacing/orientation preferences, Figure 2b) is a compelling demonstration that the architecture captures positional motif grammar without post-hoc analysis. The instance-calling comparison (Figure 2c) shows quantitatively that ARGMINN's attention-based calling outperforms traditional FIMO scanning.

- **Interpretable sequence generation validated by independent oracles.** The paper demonstrates that motifs and syntax extracted from ARGMINN can be used to construct novel functional sequences whose mechanistic basis is fully known. Validation with three independent models (including Borzoi) confirms that generated sequences are predicted to be as accessible as natural functional sequences and significantly more accessible than shuffled backgrounds (Figures 5b, Supplementary). This is a genuinely novel capability enabled by the architecture's intrinsic interpretability.

- **Biologically meaningful QTL prioritization.** ARGMINN classifies causal dsQTLs more accurately than baselines (Figure 3a), and causal mutations are shown to overlap ARGMINN-discovered motif instances (Figure 3b), providing evidence that decisions rest on interpretable motif biology rather than spurious correlates.

### Major

- **Unsupported adversarial robustness claim.** The paper claims ARGMINN "remains robust against such an attack" (Section 4.4), but provides no quantitative results for ARGMINN on the adversarial sequences — only CNN accuracy drops to 48–55% are reported. No attack is attempted on ARGMINN, no accuracy numbers are given, and no metric of robustness is shown. This claim appears in the abstract and introduction as a contribution but is entirely unsupported by evidence. The paper should either remove the claim or run the experiment and report results. *(Verified: Lines 154–155 state the claim without reporting any ARGMINN accuracy on adversarial sequences.)*

- **The argmax in the filter-overlap regularizer (Equation 3) is non-differentiable, and no mechanism for gradient-based optimization is discussed.** The loss \(\mathcal{L}_o\) uses \(\text{argmax}\{A_i\}\) to select which filter's weights to compare at each position. Argmax has zero gradient almost everywhere. While this issue can be addressed with standard techniques (straight-through estimator, softmax relaxation, or Gumbel-softmax), the paper mentions none of them and gives no implementation details. Given that this regularizer is a core contribution, the missing description is a significant gap for reproducibility. The experimental results suggest the method works in practice, so this is not fatal, but the paper must clarify how gradients are obtained. *(Verified: The paper contains no mention of any gradient-handling mechanism for the argmax; see Lines 66–73 and search results for "argmax".)*

### Minor

- **Theorem 1 (FOL expressivity) is stated without a rigorous proof.** The "sketch" (one paragraph, Lines 191) does not provide a mapping from first-order logic formulae to ARGMINN parameters, does not discuss finite-length sequences, bounded filters, or bounded layers, and does not define what "recognizable" means in terms of decision boundaries. The paper would be stronger if this theorem were either given a proper proof or scaled back to a more defensible claim (e.g., "ARGMINN can represent conjunctions of motif presence with pairwise positional constraints"). The empirical follow-up (90.9% vs 73.4% on simulated REST) provides partial support but does not prove FOL-completeness.

- **No variance reporting for most experiments.** The primary experimental figures (Figures 1–5) do not report error bars, confidence intervals, or multiple-seed comparisons. For a method involving stochastic optimization, this makes it difficult to assess whether reported advantages are reproducible. The paper reports p-values for some comparisons (e.g., Ledidi generation, sequence design validation), which is helpful, but the lack of variance information on core motif-discovery metrics weakens the evidence.

- **The attention mechanism (Equation 4) omits softmax normalization.** The expression \(a_l := K_l q_l / \sqrt{d_{q_l}}\) produces unnormalized scores. While this may be intentional (unnormalized attention), the paper does not discuss whether or how the attention scores are normalized, or what ensures they remain bounded across layers. This is a minor clarity issue.

## Novel Insights

The merge of the two reviewer perspectives reveals that the paper's core technical contribution — the filter-overlap regularizer — is broadly sound in its mathematical formulation (contrary to one reviewer's claim about ambiguous notation) but suffers from an undiscussed implementation gap (the argmax differentiability issue). This gap is not fatal (the regularizer demonstrably works in practice, as shown by the strong motif-discovery results), but it is a real omission that must be addressed for reproducibility. The more consequential weakness is the unsupported adversarial robustness claim, which appears in the abstract as a key contribution but has no supporting evidence. The paper's strengths — motif discovery outperforming baselines, direct syntax extraction, and interpretable generation validated by independent oracles — are genuine and well-supported. Overall, the paper presents a promising direction with substantial empirical validation of its main interpretability claims, but is undermined by one overclaimed result and one missing implementation detail.
