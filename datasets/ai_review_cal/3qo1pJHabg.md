## Summary
This paper proposes LRR (Language-Driven Resamplable Continuous Representation), a preprocessing defense against adversarial tracking attacks. LRR combines two modules: a spatial-temporal implicit representation (STIR) that reconstructs pixels using spatial and temporal neighborhood information, and a language-driven resample network (LResampleNet) guided by CLIP text embeddings to maintain semantic consistency with the object template. Extensive experiments on OTB100, VOT2019, and UAV123 against four SOTA attacks (RTAA, IoUAttack, CSA, SPARK) show that LRR nearly restores clean tracking accuracy and consistently outperforms adversarial fine-tuning baselines and the prior defense DISCO.

## Strengths

- **Near-complete restoration of clean accuracy under strong attacks.** Table 1 shows that on UAV123 with SiamRPN++ ResNet50, precision drops from 79.5% (clean) to 41.2% under RTAA without defense, and LRR recovers it to 77.7% — a ~89% relative improvement. On VOT2019 under SPARK, LRR achieves 0.269 EAO versus 0.079 undefended and 0.277 clean. These are striking results that directly validate the paper's central claim.

- **Language-driven resampling consistently improves over the spatial-temporal baseline alone.** Table 4 (labeled "Comparison of ResampleNet with & without language guidance") shows that adding language guidance improves over the no-language variant across virtually all settings. Gains are especially notable for CSA (e.g., OTB100 Res50: 86.0 → 89.4, +3.4%) and SPARK (87.8 → 89.3, +1.5%). This demonstrates that CLIP text embeddings contribute meaningful semantic guidance beyond pixel-level features.

- **Efficient decomposition of spatial and temporal processing enables near-real-time defense.** Equations (5–6) decompose the full STIR from O(NK²) to O(K²+N) complexity. Table 7 confirms LRR runs at ~25 fps (39ms per frame), making it practical for online tracking — a sharp contrast to DiffPure (0.04 fps, cited by the authors).

- **Transferability to a transformer-based tracker (ToMP-50) is demonstrated.** Table 5 shows LRR restores ToMP-50 under RTAA from 61.8% to 90.0% on OTB100, nearly matching clean performance (90.1%). This provides initial evidence that the defense generalizes beyond the Siamese tracker family used during training.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Training data reliance on a single tracker family limits the generality claim.** STIR and LResampleNet are trained on adversarial perturbations generated by FGSM applied to *SiamRPN++ ResNet50* (lines 182–183). The primary evaluation is on SiamRPN++ variants (ResNet50 and MobileNetV2) — the same tracker family. While attack generalization is well-supported (training attack differs from all four test attacks), the paper claims "high generalization" to "different trackers on any tracking dataset" (line 193). This is only supported by one cross-architecture experiment: ToMP-50 under a single attack (RTAA). Without evidence on additional tracker architectures (e.g., other transformer trackers, discriminative trackers) or under additional attacks, the generality claim overreaches the evidence.

### Minor

- **Text prompts used for CLIP guidance are not specified.** The paper states that "given a set of texts encompassing potential categories of the object, we compare the template's embedding with the embeddings of all the texts" (line 164), but never lists what texts were used per dataset, how they were constructed, or whether the method is sensitive to prompt phrasing. This is a reproducibility gap.

- **Language guidance provides modest, not central, improvement.** Table 4 shows that removing language guidance ("wo.Lang.") still achieves strong defense in most settings. The average gain from language is ~1–2% in precision/EAO across many settings. For example, under RTAA on OTB100 Res50, the gap is only 86.1 → 86.9 (+0.8%). While the gains are consistent, the paper's framing positions language guidance as a core innovation, but the evidence suggests the primary driver of defense is the resampling mechanism itself, with language providing a secondary refinement.

- **The three output channels of LResampleNet (Δ ∈ ℝ^{H×W×3}) are not explained.** Line 172 states the output contains "offsets of all pixels" but does not specify whether these are (δx, δy, δτ), (δx, δy, 0), or something else. Since Eq. (8) fixes the temporal coordinate τ = t, the third channel's role is unclear. This is a clarity issue that should be resolved.

- **Only one cross-tracker experiment (ToMP-50 under RTAA).** The ToMP-50 experiment demonstrates promising transferability, but testing under only one attack limits the strength of the generalization claim. Reporting a second attack would substantiate the claim more solidly.

- **No confidence intervals or standard deviations reported.** The paper reports averages from three independent trials (line 200) but provides no variance measure. Given the inherent variability in tracking and adversarial attacks, error bars would increase confidence in the reported improvements.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A diagnostic experiment showing that CLIP similarity between reconstructed frames and template text embeddings increases with LResampleNet would directly validate the claimed semantic consistency mechanism.
- Reporting DISCO's runtime alongside LRR's would help contextualize the accuracy–speed trade-off.
- A brief discussion of failure modes (e.g., heavy occlusion, fast motion where temporal neighbors are unreliable) would round out the evaluation.
- Testing on a second attack for the ToMP-50 experiment would strengthen the cross-architecture generalization evidence.
