## Summary

This paper proposes SemiCLIP, a semi-supervised method to adapt CLIP to target domains using a small number of labeled image-text pairs and many unlabeled images. The method has three components: (1) mining semantic concepts from labeled captions and training a concept classifier, (2) enforcing concept-level consistency under strong augmentation for unlabeled images, and (3) constructing learnable surrogate captions from top-\(k\) concepts and applying a trapezoidal consistency loss that constrains the geometric structure of image-text pairs without directly aligning unlabeled images to their noisy surrogate captions. Experiments on remote sensing, fashion, SciCap, and Simpsons datasets show consistent improvements over baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel trapezoidal consistency regularization**: The geometric constraint avoids forcing direct alignment between noisy surrogate captions and unlabeled images, instead relying on diagonal and leg constraints to maintain robust cross-modal alignment. Ablation results (Table 6) confirm its importance: removing the full caption-level trapezoidal consistency drops zero-shot accuracy by 5.25% and retrieval by 1.33%, far more than any other component ablation.

- **Well-designed semantic concept mining from limited labels**: Mining noun-based concepts from captions, training a linear classifier initialized with text-encoder features (Eq. 2), and using it to annotate unlabeled images is a clean and effective strategy. The qualitative examples (Figure 1a) and quantitative stage-wise analysis (Figures 2a–2b) show the supervised pre-training stage already outperforms S-CLIP on several metrics, confirming the module's standalone value.

- **Consistent gains across multiple domains and distribution shift settings**: SemiCLIP outperforms S-CLIP by 1.72%–6.58% in zero-shot classification and 2.32%–3.23% in retrieval. The L≠U setting (using out-of-distribution unlabeled data) shows a 2.94% average improvement, demonstrating robustness beyond same-distribution scenarios. Particularly large gains on fine-grained fashion subsets (+12.15% on sub-class) are well-motivated by the concept-level approach.

- **Systematic ablation study**: Table 6 decomposes the contribution of each component (concept-level consistency, diagonal constraint, leg constraint, learnable prompts), showing each contributes non-trivially. This level of decomposition provides clear evidence that the trapezoidal structure is well-motivated and not overengineered.

- **Demonstrated benefit of strong augmentation for vision-language SSL**: The paper shows a 3.1% drop when replacing strong augmentation with weak augmentation (Sec. 4.6), which is non-trivial since strong augmentation is often avoided in vision-language models. The argument that noun-based concepts are robust to such augmentation is well-supported.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Unclear whether baselines received the same unlabeled data (L≠U setting)**: The paper states it "incorporates the RESISC45 dataset as unlabeled data (L≠U)" for remote sensing experiments, and for Simpsons it "integrate[s] unlabeled images from an additional dataset called Simpsons Characters." The paper never states that the competing methods (especially S-CLIP, Hard-PL, Soft-PL) were also given these additional images. If SemiCLIP uses strictly more unlabeled data than the baselines in the L≠U setting, then the claimed gains (2.94% over S-CLIP) could be partially or entirely due to data volume rather than method quality. The authors must clarify whether all methods received identical unlabeled pools, or report results where the extra data is either included for all or excluded from all.

- **Uncontrolled backbone discrepancy with S-CLIP baseline**: The paper uses OpenCLIP (Ilharco et al., 2021) as its backbone (line 136). The S-CLIP paper (Mo et al., 2023) originally uses OpenAI's CLIP, which has different model weights and possibly different architecture variants. The paper does not state whether S-CLIP was re-implemented with the same OpenCLIP backbone and hyperparameters, or whether numbers are taken from the original S-CLIP paper directly. If the latter, architectural differences make the comparison unreliable. Since the claimed gains are large (up to +12.15% on fashion sub-class), apples-to-apples comparison is essential. The authors should explicitly describe the baseline reproduction process.

### Minor

- **Hard-PL and Soft-PL baselines are named but never defined**: The paper lists these as competing methods (line 138) but provides no description of how they operate, what thresholding or pseudo-labeling strategy they use, or how they were implemented. The reader must infer their meaning from context in the related work section. These should be explicitly defined for self-containment.

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity unexplored**: Key parameters — the frequency threshold \(\mu=5\), the top-30% frequency filter, the selection ratio \(P=30\), and the number of concepts \(k=4\) — are set to default values without any sensitivity analysis. While these choices are reasonable, the paper would be strengthened by showing that performance is stable over a range of these values.

- **Standard deviations not clearly visible in tables**: The paper states "Results are reported as the mean and standard deviation across three random seeds" (line 134), but the table captions describe parenthetical values as "performance gap from the supervised CLIP," not standard deviations. The tables are embedded as images, making it difficult to verify whether std devs are reported. This presentation choice obscures the statistical significance of the claimed improvements.

### Trivial

- The ethics statement (lines 227-228) is boilerplate text that adds no value and can be condensed or removed.

- "Extracted concepts" terminology overlaps between the concept classifier and the SCM module; the paper uses "concepts" to refer to both noun-based categories and the top-k predictions, which can cause momentary confusion.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Provide a sensitivity analysis for hyperparameters \(P\) (selection ratio) and \(k\) (number of concepts) to show the method's robustness.
- Quantify the computational overhead of the concept classifier training and pseudo-label generation relative to standard CLIP fine-tuning.
- Report the actual mean cosine distances between unlabeled images and surrogate captions with vs. without \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Trap}}\) to numerically support the claim that the lower base is adaptively lengthened (currently only shown qualitatively in Figure 2c).

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- **Computational practicality of trapezoidal loss (from Harsh Critic, "Critical Issue 3")**: Removed because it is not a real problem. The loss is defined conceptually over the combined set \(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\) but implemented per mini-batch (batch size 64, 32+32 split), which yields \(64^2=4096\) pair terms — entirely tractable. The critic's own analysis confirms this: "For a batch of size 64…this is \(64^2 = 4096\) pairs per batch, which is tractable." The "full-set" formulation versus "mini-batch" implementation is standard practice in SSL and contrastive learning papers; the critic's framing of this as a critical issue is unwarranted.

- **Ethics statement as "unnecessary boilerplate" (from Harsh Critic)**: Removed as a stylistic/presentation nitpick that carries no weight in evaluation.

- **Missing ablation on additional dataset (from Harsh Critic)**: Removed as a generic "add more experiments" request. The ablation in Table 6 is conducted on two datasets (remote sensing and fashion), which is adequate coverage given the paper's scope.

## Novel Insights

The two reviews converge on the paper's genuine novelty — the trapezoidal consistency loss — but neither review identifies a deeper limitation or opportunity beyond what the paper already states. The most interesting observation from synthesizing both reviews is that the paper's semi-supervised pipeline (concept mining → classifier training → pseudo-labeling → trapezoidal consistency) effectively bridges two previously separate literatures: semi-supervised classification (FixMatch-style consistency) and vision-language fine-tuning with limited captions. The trapezoidal consistency specifically addresses the noise in surrogate captions by relaxing direct alignment — this is a principled solution to a problem that the Harsh Critic correctly identifies as non-trivial. However, the geometric intuition (isosceles trapezoid) would benefit from a more rigorous theoretical treatment showing that the diagonal and leg constraints are necessary and sufficient conditions for the desired geometric structure, rather than being presented primarily as an analogy.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify the unlabeled data parity issue**: Explicitly state in the main paper (not just rebuttal) whether S-CLIP and other baselines in the L≠U setting were also given RESISC45 or Simpsons Characters data. If they were not, re-run experiments with equalized data and report both versions.

2. **Document the S-CLIP reproduction**: State clearly whether S-CLIP was re-implemented with the same OpenCLIP backbone, or whether numbers are cited from the original paper. If re-implemented, provide a brief description of settings.

3. **Define Hard-PL and Soft-PL**: Add one sentence describing each baseline (e.g., "Hard-PL uses hard pseudo-labels generated by…" and "Soft-PL uses soft pseudo-labels from…").

4. **Improve table presentation**: Either ensure standard deviations are clearly visible alongside the performance gaps, or separate the two metrics so readers can judge significance.
