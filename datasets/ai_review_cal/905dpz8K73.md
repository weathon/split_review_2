## Summary

This paper develops a computational model of the hippocampal–entorhinal loop using reciprocally coupled continuous attractor neural networks (CANNs), where a place-cell CANN (location coordinate) is bidirectionally coupled to multiple grid-cell CANNs (phase coordinates). The authors derive theoretically that the coupled dynamics implement gradient-based optimization of the posterior (GOP), a Bayesian-optimal integration mechanism. Simulations confirm that the model achieves optimal cue integration across noise levels and avoids the non-local error problem inherent to grid-cell phase coding.

## Strengths

- **Theoretical derivation establishing that coupled CANN dynamics implement GOP (Section 4.2, Eqs. 17–19):** The paper shows that the reduced dynamics of the bump centers are proportional to the gradients of the log posterior. The Lyapunov function analysis (Eq. 20) guarantees convergence to a local posterior maximum. This is a genuinely novel connection between attractor network dynamics and Bayesian inference in the place–grid circuit.

- **Demonstration of Bayesian optimal cue integration across noise levels (Section 5.1, Figs. 3c–d):** The decoding variance of the coupled network matches the theoretical Bayesian prediction (Ernst & Banks, 2002) across a range of input noise strengths, directly demonstrating that the model achieves optimal rather than suboptimal integration.

- **Resolution of the non-local error problem in grid-cell phase coding (Section 5.2, Figs. 4a–b):** The coupled CANN estimates remain tightly concentrated around the true location and the error stays flat as noise increases, while MAP decoding from grid cells alone produces large non-local errors that grow with noise. This is a clean demonstration of a concrete functional benefit.

- **Reciprocal connections encode the correlation prior mechanistically:** The paper derives a specific relationship \(\sigma_{\phi^i}^2 \propto 1/(\lambda_i J_{gi,p})\) (below Eq. 19), showing how the reciprocal connection strength \(J_{gi,p}\) directly implements the correlation prior \(p(z,\phi)\) from the probabilistic model, rather than requiring a separate learning or inference step.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

None. The paper's core claims (GOP implementation, Bayesian integration, non-local error avoidance) are each supported by a combination of theoretical derivation and simulation evidence. The weaknesses below are addressable matters of scope and depth.

### Minor

- **The GOP correspondence is validated at only one parameter configuration (Fig. 3b).** The scatter plot showing agreement between network decoding and explicit GOP decoding is for a single setting of coupling strength and input noise. While the Bayesian integration test (Figs. 3c–d) implicitly validates the dynamics across noise levels for the *practical consequence* of GOP, the direct correspondence between network dynamics and the gradient equations has not been tested under varying coupling strengths \(J_{gi,p}\) or numbers of grid modules. Adding a robustness scan (e.g., GOP match error vs. coupling strength) would strengthen the paper's central theoretical claim.

- **The model is evaluated only with stationary inputs in 1D.** All simulations involve relaxation to a fixed point. The paper's title and framing discuss "complementary coding" in a broader sense that naturally includes dynamic tracking during movement. The static 1D tests are a reasonable starting point, and the limitations section (6.2) acknowledges the 1D restriction and marks 2D extension as future work. However, even a simple constant-velocity simulation would demonstrate that the attractor dynamics can *track* a moving location — the paper's stated scenario of real navigation. This is a gap in scope relative to ambition.

- **No quantitative comparison with the Sreenivasan & Fieta (2011) constrained-range approach.** The paper correctly distinguishes its method qualitatively (Section 6.1): the constrained-range approach sacrifices coding efficiency to avoid non-local errors, while the coupled CANN avoids them without that sacrifice. This qualitative argument is sound, but a quantitative comparison — even on a simplified metric like non-local error probability vs. coding range — would make the claimed advantage concrete rather than conceptual.

### Trivial

- The gradient equations (Section 3.2, Eq. 7) write the derivative of the log posterior with respect to \(z\) using the unsignaled norm \(\|\phi^i - \psi^i(z)\|\) rather than the signed difference \(\phi^i - \psi^i(z)\). This is correct under linearization near the true location but technically imprecise at the wrapping boundaries of phase space. This does not affect the paper's conclusions.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A simple time-varying simulation (e.g., the animal location drifting at constant velocity while both cues are presented) would significantly expand the model's demonstrated functionality without requiring a full path-integration model.
- Sensitivity analysis of key parameters (coupling strength, number of grid modules, noise levels) on the GOP match quality would further validate the theoretical derivation.
