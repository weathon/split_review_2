## Summary

This paper proposes a two-stage method — Structure-aware Continual Pre-Training (SCPT) and Structure-aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SSFT) — for injecting domain knowledge into LLMs. The key idea is to automatically extract a hierarchical knowledge taxonomy from textbook corpora, then condition training on knowledge-structure paths. Evaluated on LongBench (free-form QA) and MMedBench (medical multi-choice QA), the method using only 76M tokens (0.3% of MMedLM's 25.5B tokens) achieves roughly 50% of the performance gain of full-data MMedLM, and consistently outperforms vanilla CPT+SFT across multiple model architectures (InternLM2-7B, Llama3-8B, Llama2-7B/13B).

## Strengths

- **Demonstrated data efficiency with concrete evidence**: Table 1 (lines 354–361) shows that with only 76M tokens (0.3% of MMedLM's data), the method achieves ~51% of MMedLM's performance gain over the base model (+4.46 vs. +8.71 on InternLM2-7B; +2.57 vs. +4.96 on Llama3-8B). These are actual, verified experimental results, not extrapolations.

- **Structure-aware training outperforms open-book retrieval on closed-book QA**: On LongBench (Table, lines 240–242), SCPT+SSFT achieves 31.5% recall in closed-book QA, surpassing even the open-book QA baseline (26.9%) and far exceeding vanilla CPT+SFT (24.2%). This directly supports the claim that structure conditioning improves knowledge memorization and retrieval.

- **Consistent gains across multiple model families and scales**: Results hold across InternLM2-7B, Llama3-8B, Llama2-7B (+8.78%), and Llama2-13B (+6.17%) (Tables 1 and 2), demonstrating that the method generalizes across architectures and model sizes.

- **Automatic knowledge-structure extraction pipeline**: The paper describes a scalable pipeline (Section 3.1) using Llama3-70B for chunk summarization and a specialized 7B model for structure identification, requiring no human annotation. This is a practical advantage for scaling to larger corpora.

- **Cross-lingual knowledge transfer**: The SSFT* ablation (Table 3, line 437) shows that structure-aware QA generated from English textbooks improves performance on Chinese, French, Russian, and Spanish subsets, indicating that the method enables knowledge transfer across languages.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None. The paper's core contribution — SCPT+SSFT as a methodology for data-efficient domain knowledge injection — is supported by actual experimental results on multiple models and benchmarks. No verified weakness invalidates the central claims.

### Major

- **The scaling law extrapolation (Eq. 3, lines 380–390) is not supported by the evidence presented.** The law is fitted from only three data points (30M, 76M, 132M tokens, corresponding to ~0.1%, ~0.3%, ~0.5% corpus ratios) using a two-parameter quadratic in log-ratio. Three points are insufficient to determine even a quadratic form reliably, let alone support extrapolation to the claim that "comparable performance (100%) would be achieved with only 5% of the total training corpus" (line 388). The paper itself admits this prediction is unverified (line 390). Yet this claim appears in the abstract ("reducing training costs to 5%", line 11) and the introduction ("potential of comparable performance with only 5% of training costs", line 61) as a headline selling point. The authors should either remove the extrapolation or clearly label it as a speculative conjecture — the current presentation overstates what the data supports.

- **The "50% improvement" phrasing in the introduction (line 61) is imprecise and risks misleading readers.** The abstract correctly says "50% of traditional knowledge injection performance" (line 5), meaning ~50% of the performance *gain* achieved by MMedLM. But the introduction states "achieve a **50%** improvement in knowledge injection compared to the SOTA MMedLM2" (line 61), which could easily be read as a 50% relative *advantage* over MMedLM2's absolute performance. The actual numbers in Table 1 (lines 354–361) support the abstract's phrasing, not the introduction's. This should be corrected for clarity.

### Minor

- **The ablation study does not fully isolate the role of structure conditioning from answer quality improvements.** In Table 3 (lines 422–449), SCPT+SSFT (49.96) outperforms SCPT+SFT (46.50). However, the SSFT answers are generated by Llama3-70B with explicit knowledge-path explanations, while the SFT answers use the original MMedBench answers. The improvement could partly come from higher-quality answers rather than from the structural conditioning per se. The paper references an additional comparison in the appendix (line 449: "randomly generate another 8K SFT pairs for further comparison"), but this is not in the main paper. A cleaner ablation — controlling for answer quality by using the same Llama3-70B generated answers with vs. without structure paths — would strengthen the attribution.

- **No evaluation of the quality of automatically extracted knowledge structures in the main paper.** The method's entire pipeline depends on the extracted knowledge taxonomy (Section 3.1), yet the main paper provides no human evaluation or quantitative assessment of whether these structures are correct, complete, or coherent. The only metric reported is "MindMap Recall" (lines 288–305), which measures whether the *model* can recall the injected mindmap — not whether the extracted taxonomy itself is of good quality. The paper references structure quality verification in the appendix (line 125: "verify that our specialized 7B model can identify sufficiently precise knowledge structure"), but this is absent from the main text.

- **Lack of statistical significance or variance reporting.** None of the main results (Tables 1, 2, 3) report confidence intervals, standard deviations, or results across multiple random seeds. Given the relatively modest absolute gains (e.g., +2.57 on Llama3-8B, Table 1), it is unclear whether the improvements are statistically reliable.

- **The OBQA vs. CBQA comparison on LongBench uses different CoT instructions.** The OBQA baseline uses "Answer the question and explain why" (line 273), while the CBQA methods use "two types of question templates" including a CoT version (line 277). This makes the comparison between OBQA (26.9%) and SCPT+SFT (26.7%) inexact, though the primary comparison of interest (CPT+SFT vs. SCPT+SFT vs. SCPT+SSFT within CBQA) is properly controlled.

### Trivial

- The RAG baseline in Table 3 (line 439) uses an unspecified retriever; the paper could clarify the retrieval method. This does not affect the main conclusions, as RAG is not the paper's focus.
