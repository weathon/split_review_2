## Summary

This paper proposes Task-Aware Learngene (TAL), a graph hypernetwork method that predicts model parameters conditioned on both the desired model scale *and* task-specific characteristics. TAL augments the standard GHN encoder-decoder pipeline with a task-specific layer (TSL) that injects task embeddings (generated by an ancestry model) into the computational graph representation before decoding into network parameters. Experiments on the Visual Decathlon benchmark show large improvements over prior GHN-based methods (LoGAH variants), and the method generalizes to unseen tasks.

## Strengths

1. **Task-conditioning mechanism yields substantial, consistent gains.** TAL introduces task embeddings into the graph-hypernetwork processing pipeline via a task hypernet and task-specific layer. The controlled comparison against LoGAH v3 (a multi-task GHN *without* task-specific conditioning) isolates this mechanism as the source of an 18.89% average accuracy improvement on untrained Decathlon models (Table 5). This cleanly demonstrates the value of the core idea.

2. **Robust results across multiple descendant model scales.** TAL is evaluated on five different descendant architectures (3-, 6-, 12-layer ViT-Tiny and 6-, 12-layer ViT-Small), all showing consistent improvements. Untrained TAL-initialized models outperform LoGAH v1 by an average of 24.39% and LoGAH v2 by 20.06% across Decathlon tasks (Table 2). The fact that the gains hold across diverse scales supports the claim of flexible, task-aware initialization.

3. **Controlled ablations confirm both design components matter.** Table 7 separates the contribution of the ancestry-model guidance (6.57% drop when removed) and the task-specific layer (12.66% drop when removed), confirming that both stages of the two-phase training (ancestry distillation + multi-task tuning) contribute meaningfully.

4. **Transfer to unseen tasks is demonstrated.** TAL shows competitive zero-shot generalization on three held-out datasets (Fashion MNIST, FER2013, HAM10000), outperforming LoGAH v1 trained only on ImageNet-1K (Table 4). Notably, the improvement is largest on FER2013 (3.15%), a visually distinct domain from the training tasks, suggesting genuine task-awareness rather than memorization.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None. The paper's central claims are supported by the evidence presented, and the weaknesses below are addressable without undermining the core contribution.

### Minor

1. **No measure of variability across runs.** Every reported result (Tables 1–5, 7) is a single number with no standard deviation, confidence interval, or indication of variance across seeds. While single-run evaluation is a common norm in the GHN literature (Knyazev et al. 2021, 2023; Zhou et al. 2024 all report single numbers), the reported gaps are large enough that even modest variance estimates would considerably strengthen confidence in the results. This is the most impactful improvement the authors could make.

2. **Task embedding computation is underspecified.** The paper states that task embeddings are "generated by the ancestry model through the average feature extraction of the task images (Vu et al., 2020)" (Section 3.2). It does not specify: (a) whether the ancestry model is frozen during this extraction, (b) whether the "average" is taken over the entire training set or per-batch, (c) which layer's output (CLS token, patch embeddings, final representation) is used, or (d) the dimensionality of the resulting embedding. This ambiguity directly affects reproducibility.

3. **Task-specific layer architecture is underspecified.** The TSL is described only as "a simple MLP" (Section 3.2) with no information about depth, hidden dimension, activation function, or initialization. While the task hypernet `h` is shown to be a linear layer (Eq. 2 explicitly gives `h(I_τ) = (W^γ, W^β)I_τ`), the MLP that constitutes the TSL itself receives no architectural details. This is a gap for replication.

4. **Compute budget for LoGAH v3/v4 not reported.** The paper introduces LoGAH v3 (multi-task from scratch) and v4 (IN-1K pre-training + multi-task tuning) as controls to disentangle multi-task learning from task-awareness (Section 4.3), and TAL outperforms both. However, the training epochs for v3/v4 are not specified, whereas the paper is careful to report 300 epochs for LoGAH v1, 100 for v2, and 100 for TAL. Without this information, the reader cannot fully assess whether the comparison is compute-fair.

5. **The "untrained descendant model" framing could be clearer.** Tables 2 and 5 report performance of "untrained" descendant models. The paper clarifies (Section 4.2) that these models receive only the predicted parameters from the hypernetwork with no further gradient updates, but the term "untrained" in a table header could mislead a casual reader into thinking no training occurred anywhere (the hypernetwork *is* trained). The paper states this clearly in prose but the table itself relies on context.

### Trivial
- Figure 5 (PCA visualization) is offered as evidence that "learngene can effectively integrate task information." The visualization does show clustering by task, which is expected behavior for a model that receives task embeddings. It is a reasonable sanity check but adds little beyond what the quantitative results already establish.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Direct test of task-awareness:** A natural ablation would be to pass constant or random task embeddings (instead of ancestry-model-derived ones) while keeping the TSL mechanism active. This would isolate whether the *content* of the task embedding matters, or merely the presence of a conditioning pathway.
- **Compute-aware comparison:** Reporting total GPU hours for each method would squarely address the compute-budget concern.
- **Limitations discussion:** The paper would benefit from acknowledging that TAL requires an ancestry model for task embedding generation, which may not be available in all deployment scenarios.

## Suggestions

1. Add standard deviations over at least 3 random seeds for the main results (Tables 1–3, 5, 7). This single change would substantially strengthen evidential credibility.
2. Clarify the task embedding computation: specify whether averaging is over the full training set or per-batch, which layer output is used (CLS token or otherwise), the embedding dimensionality, and whether the ancestry model is frozen.
3. Specify the TSL MLP architecture: depth, hidden size, activation function, and initialization scheme.
4. Report training epochs (or compute hours) for LoGAH v3 and v4 to complete the compute-budget picture.
5. Consider adding an ablation that replaces task embeddings with a constant vector to verify that the *content* (not just the presence) of task information drives the gains.
