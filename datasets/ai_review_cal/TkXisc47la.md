## Summary

DeepCircuitX is a large-scale, multimodal RTL dataset containing over 4,000 repository-level Verilog projects and 140,000 files across 77 functional categories, organized at four levels (repository, file, module, block). It is enriched with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations generated by GPT-4 and Claude, and includes synthesized netlists with PPA metrics from five technology libraries (180nm to 7nm). The paper establishes benchmarks for RTL code understanding, completion, generation, and PPA prediction, showing consistent improvements after fine-tuning across models ranging from 220M to 16B parameters.

## Strengths

1. **Multi-level repository organization.** Unlike prior datasets (RTLLM, MG-Verilog) that provide only file-level RTL code, DeepCircuitX structures data at repository, file, module, and block levels (Section 3.1, Table 1). This enables training at different scales and supports tasks (synthesis, PPA analysis) that require full project context — a genuine structural advance over existing resources.

2. **Multimodal integration bridging RTL and circuit representations.** The dataset provides synthesized netlists (Verilog + SDF), And-Inverter Graphs (AIG), and PPA metrics from five open-source technology libraries (Section 3.3.2). This bridges the gap between RTL code and downstream circuit implementations, a feature absent in RTL-only datasets. The use of commercial tools (Design Compiler, PrimeTime) alongside open-source tools (Yosys, ABC) is carefully documented.

3. **CoT annotation methodology with human validation.** The structured multi-level annotation process (Section 3.2.1) leverages GPT-4 and Claude with higher-level annotations guiding lower-level ones. Independent engineer evaluations (Table 4) rate accuracy, completeness, and clarity all above 3.5/4, providing concrete evidence of annotation quality.

4. **Scale and diversity.** With over 4,000 repositories, 140,000 RTL files, and 77 functional categories spanning chip-level, IP-level, module-level designs, and RISC-V architectures, the dataset meaningfully exceeds the scope of prior RTL datasets (which tend to focus on a single design type like processors).

5. **PPA prediction benchmark on realistic designs.** The paper establishes a benchmark using designs with >10k cells (Section 4.5.3) and shows that current learning-based models struggle on practical designs — an honest finding that identifies a concrete challenge for the community.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Fine-tuning comparisons lack baselines against other RTL datasets.** The central empirical claim — that DeepCircuitX improves performance — is supported only by comparing fine-tuned models against their non-fine-tuned originals (Tables 5, 6). This does not demonstrate whether DeepCircuitX provides value *beyond existing RTL datasets* (RTLLM, MG-Verilog, Thakur et al.'s 50K-sample collection, etc.). Since supervised fine-tuning on *any* in-domain data is expected to improve over a zero-shot baseline, the experiment design conflates "fine-tuning works" with "this specific dataset provides unique advantages." A comparative experiment (e.g., fine-tuning the same models on prior datasets and testing on a held-out set) is needed to substantiate the dataset's distinguishing value. The conclusion's phrasing — "models trained on DeepCircuitX significantly outperform existing methods" — is an overstatement given the baseline choice. This is the most significant weakness and directly affects the strength of the paper's contribution claims.

2. **PPA prediction benchmark lacks cross-dataset or non-learning baselines.** The PPA experiments (Section 4.5) train models on 156 DeepCircuitX designs and compare only against prior methods trained *on the same data*. There is no comparison to training on alternative multimodal datasets (e.g., CircuitNet, IWLS-derived data) or to simple non-learning heuristics (e.g., regression on RTL-level features). The claim that DeepCircuitX "enables cross-stage analysis" is not tested against alternatives.

### Minor

3. **No data release or access statement.** For a paper whose primary contribution is a dataset, the absence of any statement about release plans, licensing terms, or access mechanism is a significant omission. This must be clarified for the paper to serve its intended purpose as a community resource.

4. **No inter-annotator agreement reported for human evaluation.** The human evaluation (Section 4.2) reports average scores from 5 raters per annotation but provides no measure of agreement (e.g., Fleiss' kappa). Without this, the reported averages could mask high variance between raters.

5. **Test set construction and data leakage are not described.** The paper does not state how train/val/test splits were created (Section 3.2.2), whether splits are at the repository level to prevent data contamination between sibling modules, or how many examples are in each split for each task.

6. **Synthesis yield not reported.** The paper describes synthesizing RTL projects into netlists (Section 3.3.2) but does not report the fraction of projects that synthesized successfully, the common failure modes, or how failures were handled. This information is critical for assessing the utility of the multimodal component.

7. **Small PPA test set.** The PPA prediction benchmark (Section 4.5.3) uses only 10 designs for testing. While the focus on designs >10k cells is valuable, 10 test instances limit the reliability of conclusions drawn from the results.

8. **No ablation of CoT annotations.** The paper does not test whether the CoT annotations (compared to simpler descriptions or no descriptions) are responsible for the observed improvements. An ablation fine-tuning on a version of DeepCircuitX without CoT annotations would directly test the value of the annotation methodology.

### Trivial

9. **Overstatement in the conclusion.** The conclusion says models "significantly outperform existing methods," but the experiments compare only against non-fine-tuned versions of the same models, not against prior methods trained on other datasets. The abstract and introduction are more measured ("significantly improves model performance") and should be maintained throughout.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An error analysis of code generation results (e.g., control-heavy vs. datapath designs) would help interpret what kinds of RTL the models still struggle with.
- A data card with per-category statistics (token counts per level, distribution of design sizes, PPA value ranges) would help potential users assess applicability.
- Clarification on whether the synthesized data can be regenerated using only open-source tools (Yosys) or whether commercial licenses are required, to help set reproducibility expectations.

## Removed Points

- *"The paper does not report the number of annotated samples evaluated"* — The paper reports average scores per metric but does not explicitly state the count. This is implicitly available from Table 4's context ("repo-level annotation and module-level annotation") but is a minor omission that doesn't affect the core claims. Removed as too minor to merit inclusion.
- *"No analysis of distribution of designs across categories"* — Table 1 provides this breakdown across Chip/IP/Module/RISC-V levels. While a more granular listing of 77 categories would be nice, the claim is factually incorrect as stated. Removed.
- *"The paper does not report whether human raters were blind to annotation source"* — This is a methodological detail that would strengthen the evaluation but is not a standard requirement for dataset annotation quality checks. The paper states the engineers were "independent" which is sufficient. Removed.
- *"Comparison to alternative annotation methods"* — Requesting comparison to "direct description without CoT" is an ablation that belongs in the Nice-to-Haves, not a weakness. Removed from weaknesses and noted in ablation point above.
- *"No description of how reference solutions for code completion/generation were verified"* — For a dataset paper, the primary concern is data quality, and the human evaluation provides evidence of quality. The reference solutions are presumably the original RTL code from repositories. Removed as a speculation without clear evidence of a problem.
- *"The PPA experiments are too small-scale to be convincing"* — Merged into weakness #7 (small test set) above.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a tension common to dataset papers: DeepCircuitX clearly provides a richer, more structured resource than existing alternatives (multi-level, multimodal, annotated), yet the empirical evaluation does not directly test whether this richness translates to improved outcomes relative to training on prior, simpler datasets. This gap between the descriptive contribution (the dataset's features are unambiguously valuable) and the comparative evaluation (the experiments only show that fine-tuning on *some* data helps) is the central weakness. The paper would be substantially stronger not by adding more tasks or models, but by adding one focused comparison: fine-tuning on a prior dataset vs. fine-tuning on DeepCircuitX, holding all other variables fixed.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a comparative baseline.** Fine-tune the same models on an existing RTL dataset (RTLLM, MG-Verilog, or Thakur et al.'s data) and evaluate on a held-out test set from DeepCircuitX — or better, on an independent set of RTL problems. This directly tests whether DeepCircuitX's multi-level and multimodal features translate to better performance.

2. **Ablate CoT annotations.** Fine-tune on a version of DeepCircuitX without the CoT annotations (or with simple one-line comments) to demonstrate that the annotation methodology causally contributes to the observed improvements.

3. **State the data release plan.** Include a clear statement of where and under what license the dataset will be made available, even if it is "upon publication" or "upon request."

4. **Describe the train/val/test split methodology** explicitly, including whether splits are performed at the repository level to prevent contamination.

5. **Report synthesis yield** — how many of the 4,000+ repositories synthesized successfully, and how failures were handled.
