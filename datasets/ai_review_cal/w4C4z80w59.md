## Summary

This paper proposes GIE (Growth Inhibitors for Erasure), a method for suppressing inappropriate concepts (NSFW content, artistic styles, common objects) in text-to-image diffusion models without fine-tuning. The core idea is to compute attention maps for the target concept, re-weight them via a learned suppression scale, and inject these "growth inhibitors" into the prompt's attention map group during inference. An adapter (small MLP) trained with GPT-4o as a scoring oracle predicts the optimal suppression scale from cross-attention intermediate values. Experiments compare against eight baselines on NSFW erasure, style erasure, and object erasure tasks.

## Strengths

- **Fine-tuning-free concept erasure with strong quantitative results on "nude" erasure**: GIE achieves the highest NSFW removal rate (NRR ~0.9) among eight baselines on the I2P dataset for the concept "nude" (Figure 4), while maintaining the best CLIP score (0.31) on COCO-30k (Table 1), demonstrating effective erasure without degrading overall semantic alignment.

- **Effective handling of implicit unsafe prompts**: The method extracts meaningful features from synonyms, capitalized forms, and noun variants of the target concept (Figure 1). In object erasure, it achieves ORR > 0.9 even when synonyms replace the target word in prompts (Table 2), addressing a key limitation of text-filtering approaches.

- **Minimal impact on non-target concepts**: For object erasure, the ORR for non-target objects is at most 0.05 (Table 2). Style erasure qualitative results (Figure 6) show non-target styles are largely unaffected. This provides evidence that the erasure is reasonably specific.

- **Novel mechanism operating in image space rather than text space**: Unlike methods that purify text embeddings (Safe-CLIP) or fine-tune model weights (ESD, SPM), GIE operates on cross-attention features during inference, offering a complementary approach that is well-motivated for implicit unsafe prompts where text-level cues are insufficient.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The core suppression mechanism is not explained or justified.** The method injects a re-weighted version of the target concept's attention map (Eq. 7) and its text embedding into the prompt's attention pathway, claiming this *suppresses* the concept. But the paper never explains *why* this operation should suppress rather than amplify, distort, or otherwise interfere with generation in an unpredictable way. The operation is described as injection/replacement (Eq. 8: $M_{replace}$), not as subtraction, negation, or inhibition. The "growth inhibitor" analogy is metaphorical. Without a mechanistic rationale, the reader cannot assess whether the method is principled or whether its effectiveness on tested cases is coincidental. (Lines 88–106 describe *what* is done; no passage explains *why* it produces suppression.)

- **The adapter component lacks empirical validation on multiple fronts.** (a) GPT-4o is used as an oracle to score erasure quality vs. image preservation, but its reliability is never validated — no correlation with human judgment or an objective metric is reported. (b) Training uses "a few dozen images" (line 126) for a 2-hidden-layer MLP with 16 input features, raising severe overfitting concerns. (c) The paper states that "training with images containing the concept of 'cat' can be generalized to concepts such as 'dog' and 'airplane'" (line 126) but provides zero experimental support — no table, figure, or quantitative result. This generalization claim is presented as a demonstrated finding when it is, in fact, an unsupported assertion. The adapter is a central contribution (Section 4.2), yet its entire evaluation is missing.

- **No ablation isolates the contribution of the learned adapter.** The most straightforward experiment — comparing GIE with the learned adapter vs. GIE with a fixed suppression scale — is absent. The right panel of Figure 3 shows that different scales affect cross-attention intermediate values, but this does not demonstrate that the *learned* adapter improves erasure quality or reduces side effects compared to any reasonable fixed scale. Without this ablation, the adapter's value is unsubstantiated.

- **Quantitative evaluation is too narrow to support the claimed generality.** NSFW erasure quantitative results (Figure 4, NRR) are reported only for the single concept "nude." The paper mentions erasing "violence" and "horror" (Figure 5) but shows only qualitative examples. The I2P dataset contains seven categories of unsafe content (hate, harassment, violence, self-harm, sexual content, shocking images, illegal activity); only one is quantitatively evaluated. Style erasure results are qualitative only (Figure 6). While object erasure has quantitative results (Table 2), it uses simple "a photo of [xxx]" templates with only three objects. The claim of "superior erasure results with little effect on other concepts" is only partially supported by the evidence presented.

### Minor

- **No quantitative metric for style erasure.** Style erasure is evaluated only through qualitative examples (Figure 6). While style evaluation is inherently subjective, a simple classification-based metric would strengthen the evidence.

- **Multi-concept erasure is evaluated only qualitatively.** Figure 7 shows visual results for simultaneous erasure of "nude"+"horror" and "nude"+"van Gogh," but no NRR or ORR numbers are reported for the multi-concept setting.

- **Baseline configuration details are lightly reported.** The setup lists eight baselines (Section 5.1) but does not specify how fine-tuning methods (ESD, SPM) were configured for the "nude" concept or whether the same target description was used across methods, which matters for fairness.

### Trivial
- None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Evaluate image quality (e.g., CLIP score or FID) on the I2P dataset itself (not just COCO-30k) to confirm that NRR gains are not from image degradation on unsafe prompts.
- Report NRR for at least one additional NSFW category (e.g., violence) to broaden quantitative support.
- Include standard deviations or confidence intervals for key metrics (NRR, FID, CLIP score) to assess result stability.
- Analyze failure cases: prompts where GIE fails to erase or over-erases, to better characterize the method's limitations.

## Removed Points

These points were flagged by reviewers but are removed (with justification):

1. **"guide them towards more suitable ones" phrasing in abstract is misleading** — Removed: This is a semantic nitpick. The method suppresses inappropriate features, which is reasonably described as guiding features away from inappropriate content. The distinction between "suppress" and "guide towards suitable" is too minor to constitute a substantive weakness.

2. **"images without these features are immune" claim not tested** — Removed: This is a plausible property of an attention-map-injection method (if the concept features aren't present, there's nothing to inject for). While it could be tested, the absence of a dedicated experiment for this secondary claim is not a meaningful weakness.

3. **NRR does not measure semantic coherence of erased images** — Moved to Nice-to-Have: The paper does evaluate semantic alignment via CLIP score on COCO-30k, which is a standard protocol. Evaluating on I2P-specific prompts would be a worthwhile extension but is not a gap that undermines the existing evidence.

4. **Statistical significance absent** — Moved to Nice-to-Have: Single-run evaluation on large benchmarks is standard in the vision community. Requesting confidence intervals is a best-practice suggestion, not a flaw.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The two reviewers primarily converged on the same set of real issues (missing mechanistic explanation, unvalidated adapter, missing ablation, narrow quantitative scope) without introducing genuinely novel observations.

## Suggestions

1. **Add an adapter ablation** comparing GIE with the learned adapter vs. GIE with several fixed suppression scales, measuring both NRR/ORR and image quality. This is the single highest-priority addition.
2. **Provide evidence for the adapter's cross-concept generalization claim** (cat → dog/airplane) with a quantitative table showing ORR for unseen concepts with and without the adapter.
3. **Broaden NSFW quantitative evaluation** to at least one additional I2P category beyond "nude" (e.g., violence).
4. **Add a mechanistic explanation or controlled experiment** (e.g., compare injection vs. subtraction baselines) to clarify why injecting re-weighted attention maps suppresses rather than generates the target concept.
5. **Validate the GPT-4o oracle** by reporting agreement with human judgment on a small sample, or replace it with a more objective scoring criterion.
