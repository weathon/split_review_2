# Humans Coexist, So Must Embodied Artificial Agents

- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 7, 5, 8

## Abstract
This position paper introduces the concept of coexistence for embodied artificial agents and argues that it is a prerequisite for long-term, in-the-wild interaction with humans. Contemporary embodied artificial agents excel in static, predefined tasks but fall short in dynamic and long-term interactions with humans. On the other hand, humans can adapt and evolve continuously, exploiting the situated knowledge embedded in their environment and other agents, thus contributing to meaningful interactions. We take an interdisciplinary approach at different levels of organization, drawing from biology and design theory, to understand how human and non-human organisms foster entities that coexist within their specific environments. Finally, we propose key research directions for the artificial intelligence community to develop coexisting embodied agents, focusing on the principles, hardware and learning methods responsible for shaping them.

### Summary
This position paper argues that embodied agents perform well in controlled settings but fail in real-world contexts due to a design paradigm that cannot sustain situated, reciprocal, and long-term interactions with humans and the surrounding environment. The authors call for a paradigm shift toward mutable agents that learn from and influence both users and their environments. To coexist with humans, as humans do with one another by continuously adapting, agents must form reciprocal relationships that leverage and exploit situated knowledge. The behavior should increase the quality of the system. Current systems remain stagnant and generic, relying on static training data or fine-tuned datasets with fixed distributions, that limit adaptation to change. They use generic rather than adaptable general knowledge suited to nuanced contexts. Drawing on analogies to biological organisms, the authors advocate for divergence and convergence in agent design and promote research-through-design as a reflexive, continuous method for enabling reciprocal engagement. They end with a few ethical considerations and limitations to consider, such as malicious actors, and expand the appendix to include examples of coexistence as they define it.

### Strengths
The authors present a compelling critique of the current "stagnant" and "generic" approach to designing agents, clearly showing why it cannot support sustainable, long-term interactions with humans. They use this criticism effectively to strengthen their case for a fundamentally different design paradigm for embodied agents, and the argument’s timely relevance extends beyond this argument into broader machine learning, AI, and HCI contexts. Their integration of concepts from evolutionary biology and design theory demonstrates an acknowledgement that agent design requires interdisciplinary engagement. Their introduction of the concept of "steamrolling" is particularly strong, with a clear definition supported by a concrete and memorable example drawn from the prevalence of LLM-style abstracts in computer science papers.

### Weaknesses
Below I outline some points that can be improved to strengthen the authors' argument. I include section numbers where necessary.

- **Section 4.1**: The evolutionary biology analogy seems underdeveloped and does not justify why survival-driven adaptation is necessary for embodied agents whose goal is not survival.
- **Section 5.1**: This section is arguably one of the most important but the warning about harmful behavior and the need for ethical and legal frameworks is brief and lacks depth.
- **Appendix C**: I’m concerned that measurements presented for coexistence such as eye gaze and interaction duration might favor neurotypical social interactions and lead to unwanted behavior.
- More examples of meaningful interactions would be helpful.
- The argument does not fully address whether coexistence is necessary or desirable for all types of embodied agents, or why the absence of coexistence would be undesirable (for society). 
- The authors missed an opportunity to engage with counterpoints for the existence of coexistence from a critical design perspective and counter-claims that biased humans have and can influence humans and how that would affect coexisting embodied agents.

### Questions
Some of the questions below extend from the weaknesses above and would help clarify missing or confusing points:

- Is the underlying assumption that embodied agents must have a pseudo-human role for coexistence to succeed?
- Should coexistence require interpreting social cues and influencing culture, and for which types of agents?
- How does a coexisting embodied agent that can influence humans and the environment improve a system; who defines the boundaries of this influence; and how is this different from steamrolling?
- How would coexisting agents combat "steamrolling" if they are also meant to influence their environment?
- If agents can replace humans at a larger scale, how would coexistence help maintain human stability?
- Why is coexistence desirable and in what contexts would it be undesirable?
- Could mutability lead to harmful outcomes, and when might static data offer better diagnostic stability?
- What happens if coexisting agents learn undesirable behaviors from humans, and who decides what is undesirable?
- How should credit be assigned in coexistence to support continuous evaluation?
- What are the infrastructure costs of developing and sustaining coexisting embodied agents?

### Summary
This paper advocates for the need for "coexistence" as a necessary paradigm for the development of embodied artificial agents to facilitate meaningful, long-term, in-the-wild interactions with humans that avoid "steamrolling." The authors put forth that the current static state of agents are unsatisfactory since they only operate on predefined tasks and are not able to be adaptive to new stimuli. The paper provides examples across human design and biology of how coexistence is a naturally occurring phenomenon. The alternative to coexistence offered by the paper is "steamrolling," which would lead to homogeneous actions due to agents that do not adapt.  The paper's authors envision a future where embodied agents take a role in shaping and responding to an environment through coexistence.

### Strengths
The strengths of this paper are in proposing a change today that would have long-term and real-world impact on humans using embodied artificial agents. The paper is organized clearly and offers examples from biology and design to support its argument.

### Weaknesses
This paper does not offer specifics on what coexistence would look like and what that future requires of current practitioners. It makes assumptions about how humans would like to engage with embodied agents without questioning whether that is a future we would even want. The alternatives offered are of homogeneous agents and steamrolling, but do not consider a future where agents are not as fully integrated into human life as the paper assumes. The examples that it provides of coexistence do not feel satisfactory enough to fully support the argument, although the argument they make by itself is compelling. It also does not flesh out the dangers of promoting coexistence for embodied agents, and this is the greatest weakness of the paper.

### Questions
Are there any other examples that would support this call for coexistence? What are the dangers of coexistence?
