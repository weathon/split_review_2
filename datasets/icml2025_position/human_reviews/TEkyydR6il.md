## Human Reviewer 3

### Questions
1. How would you respond to regulatory frameworks like the EU AI Act, which establishes market surveillance authorities with powers to access documentation, training data, and even source code of high-risk AI systems when necessary (Article 74), while still respecting confidentiality (Article 78)? Doesn't this directly challenge your claim of fundamental incompatibility?
2. Your paper portrays trade secret protection as an absolute barrier to due process, yet Article 77 of the EU AI Act specifically empowers authorities that protect fundamental rights to request documentation and testing of high-risk AI systems. How do you reconcile this with your position?
3. You argue that AI systems cannot guarantee due process, but the EU AI Act establishes detailed procedures for risk assessment, information sharing, and corrective measures (Articles 79-80). Why do you consider these insufficient for protecting due process rights?
4. Your paper claims fundamental incompatibility between AI and due process, yet regulatory frameworks increasingly require documentation, testing, and certification of high-risk AI systems before deployment. Why couldn't these requirements, when properly implemented, address many of your due process concerns?
5. You argue that technical opacity makes it impossible for individuals to challenge AI decisions, but Article 77(3) of the EU AI Act allows authorities to request testing of high-risk AI systems. Couldn't this institutional approach provide a meaningful avenue for due process without requiring individual technical expertise?
6. The case studies you discuss involve different types of AI systems. Could you provide more technical details about these systems and how they differ in terms of interpretability challenges?
7. Finally, how do you respond to the argument that simpler, more inherently interpretable AI systems (like decision trees with limited depth) might satisfy due process requirements when deployed appropriately?
