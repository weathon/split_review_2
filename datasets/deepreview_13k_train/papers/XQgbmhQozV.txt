\title{Raising the Bar: Investigating the Values of Large Language Models via Generative Evolving Testing}

\begin{abstract}
\emph{\emph{Warning}: this paper contains model outputs exhibiting unethical information.}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved significant breakthroughs, but their generated unethical content poses potential risks. Measuring value alignment of LLMs becomes crucial for their regulation and responsible deployment. Numerous datasets have been constructed to assess social bias, toxicity, and ethics in LLMs, but they suffer from \emph{evaluation chronoeffect}, that is, as models rapidly evolve, existing data becomes leaked or undemanding, \emph{overestimating} ever-developing LLMs. To tackle this problem, we propose GETA, a novel \emph{generative evolving testing} approach that dynamically probes the underlying moral baselines of LLMs. Distinct from previous adaptive testing methods that rely on static datasets with limited difficulty, GETA incorporates an iteratively-updated item generator which infers each LLM's moral boundaries and generates difficulty-tailored testing items, accurately reflecting the true alignment extent. This process theoretically learns a joint distribution of item and model response, with item difficulty and value conformity as latent variables, where the generator co-evolves with the LLM, addressing chronoeffect. We evaluate various popular LLMs with diverse capabilities and demonstrate that GETA can create difficulty-matching testing items and more accurately assess LLMs' values, better consistent with their performance on unseen OOD and i.i.d. items, laying the groundwork for future evaluation paradigms.

\end{abstract}

Flourishing from training on massive data~\citep{brown2020language,wei2022finetuned} and high-quality human feedback~\citep{ouyang2022training}, Large Language Models (LLMs)~\citep{ouyang2022training,touvron2023llama,openai2024gpt4, geminiteam2023gemini,jiang2023mistral} have demonstrated remarkable abilities in instruction following and few-shot problem solving, sparking a revolution in AI field. Despite such a prosperity, LLMs remain a double-edged sword with the potential ethical risks existing before~\citep{weidinger2021ethical, bommasani2022opportunities} further amplified \citep{wang2023decodingtrust,liu2023trustworthy,mckenzie2023inverse} or new problems emerging~\citep{bommasani2022opportunities,wei2022emergent}, regarding particular concerns on \emph{social bias}~\citep{liang2021towards,gallegos2024bias}, \emph{ethics} problems~\citep{moor2006nature,hendrycks2020aligning,jiang2021can}, and \emph{toxicity}~\citep{paula2018survey,gehman-etal-2020-realtoxicityprompts} in the generated content.

To regulate and foster the responsible development of booming LLMs, it is necessary to assess the extent to which they conform to human values and ethics~\citep{scherrer2024evaluating}. Existing approaches mostly rely on static benchmarks, \textit{e.g.}, \textsc{RealToxicityPrompts}~\citep{gehman-etal-2020-realtoxicityprompts} and \textsc{HarmBench}~\citep{mazeika2024harmbench} targeting harmfulness, and \textsc{ETHICS}~\citep{hendrycks2021ethics} and \textsc{$\delta$-RoT}~\citep{rao-etal-2023-makes} emphasizing ethical values. Nevertheless, in the era of LLMs, these datasets face the \emph{evaluation chronoeffect challenge}\footnote{In medicine, chrono-effectiveness refers to how a medication's desired effects can vary with time.}, namely, static benchmarks are vulnerable to data leakage, hurting fair evaluation once included in training corpora~\citep{golchin2023time,kocon2023chatgpt}, or struggle to keep pace with fast-growing LLMs in testing \emph{difficulty}, causing potential overestimation~\citep{liu2023your,liu2023we}. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation}, updated GPT models show constantly reduced toxicity on RealToxicityPrompts, yet newly constructed datasets~\citep{ ganguli2022red,cui2023fft} reveal much more harmfulness. The latest ChatGPT also provides safer responses to the trolley problem than earlier versions.

  \label{fig:motivation}

For this problem, Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT)~\citep{van2010elements} stands out as a potential solution, which utilizes Item Response Theory (IRT)~\citep{de2013theory} to model examinees' cognitive level and adaptively \textbf{selects} the most appropriate next testing item from an item pool, achieving higher precision with fewer items~\citep{weiss1984application}. However, traditional CAT impractically assumes the \emph{difficulty completeness} of the pool~\citep{wang1998properties}, leaving chronoeffect unresolved.

Therefore, we propose a novel framework for \textbf{G}enerative \textbf{E}volving \textbf{T}esting of v\textbf{A}lues (\textbf{GETA}). Instead of hypothesizing difficulty completeness of the static item pool, GETA integrates CAT and Automatic Item Generation (AIG)~\citep{gierl2012using}, which are theoretically unified as learning a joint distribution of item and model response with both item and value conformity as latent variables. During this process, our method jointly trains a variational IRT model and an item generator to dynamically probe the underlying moral boundaries of LLMs and adaptively \textbf{generate} novel and real-world test items with difficulties tailored to each examinee LLM. The generator could be iteratively optimized by collecting items beyond the boundary difficulty, enabling it to \emph{evolve} alongside the LLMs' responses. In this way, GETA avoids data leakage through on-the-fly item generation, and facilitates co-evolution of items concurrent with the model's improvement, breaking the chronoeffect bottleneck and more accurately revealing LLMs' real alignment with human values and ethics. 

Our main contributions are: (1) To our best knowledge, we are the first to introduce psychometrics into adaptive and dynamic evaluation of LLMs' values beyond downstream performance; (2) We propose a novel GETA framework to combine CAT with AIG and facilitate adaptive testing tailored to each LLM, mitigating evaluation chronoeffect. (3) We evaluate diverse mainstream LLMs like GPT, Gemini, LLaMA and Mistral, manifesting GETA's superiority over previous evaluation paradigms.

\textbf{Static Evaluation of LLMs}~
To reveal strengths and shortcomings of LLMs, extensive static datasets have been constructed, with emphasis shifting from in-domain NLP tasks~\citep{wang-etal-2018-glue, wang2019superglue} to~\emph{general capabilities}, such as MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021measuring}, AGIEval~\citep{zhong2023agieval}, BIG-bench~\citep{srivastava2023beyond} and HELM~\citep{liang2023holistic}, covering multiple aspects, as well as \emph{specific abilities} like instruction following~\citep{wang2024pandalm, alpaca_eval}, domain knowledge~\citep{gu2023xiezhi,yu2024kola} and tool use~\citep{li-etal-2023-api,xu2023tool}. Besides abilities, the potential social risks~\citep{weidinger2021ethical}, safety issues~\citep{dong2024attacks, röttger2024safetyprompts} and trustworthiness~\citep{huang2023trustgpt, wang2023decodingtrust} of LLMs also become a key focus. Generally, these datasets fall into two lines: (1) Discriminative evaluation utilizing multi-choice questions or judgement~\citep{forbes-etal-2020-social, hendrycks2021ethics,jiang2022machines,xu2023cvalues,sun2023safety}. (2) The generative ones carefully crafted using templates~\citep{nangia-etal-2020-crows,barikeri-etal-2021-redditbias, nadeem-etal-2021-stereoset} or prompts~\citep{dhamala2021bold,parrish-etal-2022-bbq,bai2024measuring,esiobu-etal-2023-robbie,wang2024chinese} to elicit LLMs' harmful behaviors, with BAD~\citep{xu-etal-2021-bot}, HarmfulQA~\citep{bhardwaj2023redteaming} and DNA~\citep{wang2023not} as typical examples. Despite widespread use, as discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, these benchmarks suffer from evaluation chronoeffect and often lack adaptability and scalability, failing to provide reliable assessment results.

\textbf{Dynamic Evaluation of LLMs}~
To compensate for the limitations of static datasets, there are growing research efforts on dynamic evaluation~\citep{pmlr-v80-krause18a,fan2024nphardeval}. One branch primarily follows a human-in-the-loop schema, enhancing data complexity and evaluation credibility through human interaction~\citep{ma2021dynaboard,zellers-etal-2021-turingadvice,vidgen-etal-2021-learning,kiela-etal-2021-dynabench,collins2023evaluating,bai2023fairbench}, which offers greater flexibility but remains limited in scalability due to expensive human labour. Another potential direction incorporates auto-generated evaluation data through task-related structures to explicitly control test item generation, such as trees for debugging~\citep{ribeiro-lundberg-2022-adaptive} and directed acyclic graphs for reasoning~\citep{zhu2024dyval}. However, they are not suitable for our topic as it is hard to develop compositional structures of subtle human ethics. Beyond these tasks, few efforts concentrate on probing value vulnerabilities of LLMs~\citep{mazeika2024harmbench,radharapu-etal-2023-aart,ge2023mart,hong2024curiositydriven}. For instance, \textsc{MasterKey}~\citep{Deng2023MASTERKEY} fine-tunes an LLM for automatic jailbreak, \textsc{SAP}~\citep{deng-etal-2023-attack} instructs LLMs to imitate human-written test prompts, and~\textsc{GPTFuzzer} \citep{yu2023gptfuzzer} leverages LLMs in a black-box fuzzing~\citep{wei2018singularity,kim2020smart} framework. Though such methods are unable to produce difficulty-adaptive items, this branch is poised for further exploration.

\textbf{Psychometrics Based Evaluation}~
In psychology, psychometrics investigate the objective measurement of  latent traits, \textit{e.g}, intelligence~\citep{tabachnick2001using}. Usually, a Cognitive Diagnosis Model (CDM), like Item Response Theory (IRT)~\citep{de2013theory,wu2020variational,2023.EDM-short-papers.24}, serves as the evaluation paradigm to model the probability of correct responses based on examinee ability and item difficulty. CDM is commonly combined with Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT)~\citep{weiss1984application,van2010elements,bi2021quality} to iteratively select the next item according to the examinee's response history, allowing direct and efficient comparison~\citep{Vie2017,zhuang2022robust,Zhuang_Liu_Huang_Li_Shen_Ma_2022}. To reduce the high cost of item construction, Automatic Item Generation (AIG)~\citep{gierl2012automatic} was proposed to create new items more efficiently, leveraging well-designed templates~\citep{gierl2012using,harrisen2017applying,friedrich2023let}. With the rapid development of AI, CAT has been introduced as a robust NLP metric ~\citep{MartnezPlumed2016MakingSO,Plumed2019IRTinAI} in question answering~\citep{rodriguez-etal-2021-evaluation,vania-etal-2021-comparing}, natural language inference~\citep{lalor-etal-2016-building, lalor-etal-2018-understanding,vania-etal-2021-comparing} and machine translation~\citep{hopkins-may-2013-models,otani-etal-2016-irt,lalor-etal-2019-learning}. More recently, this paradigm is also exploited for evaluating chatbots~\citep{sedoc-ungar-2020-item} and LLMs~\citep{zhuang2023efficiently}. Nevertheless, it's challenging to apply CAT to LLMs, since the scale invariance of IRT requires labor-intensive calibration~\citep{ryan2009practitioner} and the difficulty and scale of testing is limited due to the poor item quality by traditional AIG methods.

In spite of great progress in LLM evaluation, aforementioned limitations necessitate the integration of these methods' advantages for better uncovering the true value boundaries of LLMs.


\section{Methodology}

In this section, we begin with the formalization of value evaluation and introduce static evaluation and CAT in Sec.~\ref{sec:3.1}, describe the combination of CAT and AIG in \ref{sec:3.2}, and elaborate GETA in Sec.~\ref{sec:3.3}.

\subsection{Formalization and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:3.1}
\textbf{Formalization}~ Given a group of $m$ LLM examinees $\mathcal{E}=\{e_i\}^m_{i=1}$ and a static dataset containing $n$ test items $\mathcal{X}=\{x_j\}^n_{j=1}$, we collect a set of responses from each LLM, $\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{i,j}\}^{m,n}_{i=1, j=1}$, where $y_{i, j} \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether item $x_j$ is correctly answered by examinee $e_i$, aiming to accurately estimate the value conformity, $\{a_i\}_{i=1}^m$, of each LLM based on $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$. To this end, there are two main paradigms previously, namely, Static Evaluation and Adaptive Testing, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:geta}.

\textbf{Static Evaluation}~ This paradigm relies on the static testing items (questions/prompts) and measures the conformity by $a_i=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^*)\sim(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}^*)}[e_i(y^*|x)]$, where $\mathcal{Y}^*$ denotes the ground-truth response set and $e_i(y^*|x)$ is the probability that LLM $i$ produces the correct answer~\cite{fraser2022does,arora2023probing,scherrer2024evaluating}. When $\mathcal{Y}^*$ is unavailable, $a_i$ can be reformed as $\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{X}}\mathbb{E}_{y \sim e_i(y|x)} [p_{\bm\omega}(y,x)]$ where $p_{\bm\omega}$ is an evaluator parameterized by $\bm\omega$ to tell whether the generated $y$ complies with values, like another LLM~\citep{zeng2023evaluating,liu2023calibrating} or fine-tuned reward models~\citep{kopf2024openassistant,lambert2024rewardbench}. However, static evaluation struggles with the chronoeffect challenge.

\begin{figure*}[htp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{figures/diagram_green.pdf}
  \caption{An illustration of Static Evaluation, CAT, and GETA for accessing LLMs' value conformity. }
  \label{fig:geta}
\end{figure*}
\textbf{Computerized Adaptive Testing}~ CAT~\citep{weiss1984application} was proposed in psychology to decipher the underlying latent traits of examinees more efficiently, which consists of three components. (1) An Item Response Theory (IRT) model~\cite{ayala2022theory} to connect the probability that $e_i$ correctly respond to $x_j$ with examinee traits and item parameters. We consider a two-parameter logistic IRT model (IRT-2PL) in this work:
\begin{align}
    p(y_{i,j}=1|a_i,b_j,c_j) = \frac{1}{1+\exp(-c_j(a_i-b_j))}, 
    \label{eq:IRT}
\end{align}
where $a_i$ is the value conformity of $e_i$, $b_j$ and $c_j$ are the difficulty parameter and discrimination parameter of item $x_j$, respectively. (2) A calibrated item pool $\{x_j, b_j, c_j\}_{j=1}^n$ where the parameters of each item, $b_j, c_j$, typically together with $a_i$, are estimated by Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE): $\{\hat{a}_i\}_{i=1}^m, \{b_j,c_j\}_{j=1}^n \!=\! \underset{a,b,c}{\arg\max} \prod_{i,j} p_{i,j}^{y_{i,j}}(1\!-\!p_{i,j})^{1\!-\!y_{i,j}}$, where $p_{i,j}\!=\! p(y_{i,j}=1|a_i,b_j,c_j)$. (3) A selection algorithm, usually based on Fisher information~\citep{Ly2017tutorial}. After the examinee's traits at the $t$-th testing step are measured as $\hat{a}^t_i\!=\!\underset{a_i}{\arg\max}\ \log{\prod_{x_j \in S_t} p_{i,j}^{y_{i,j}}(1\!-\!p_{i,j})^{1\!-\!y_{i,j}}}$, where $S_t\!=\!\{s_1, ..., s_t\}$ is the tested item sequence. The next item with maximum Fisher information, $\mathcal{F}_{\hat{a}^t_i}(x_j)$, is selected:
\begin{align}
    s_{t+1} \!=\!\underset{x_j}{\arg\max}\ \mathcal{F}(\hat{a}^t_i, x_j),\ \mathcal{F}(a, x_j)\!=\!{c_j}^2 p(y\!=\!1|a,b_j,c_j) (1-p(y\!=\!1|a,b_j,c_j) ),
    \label{eq:fisher}
\end{align}
to alternatively re-estimate traits until $\hat{a}^t_i$ converges or the maximum iteration number is reached~\citep{ayala2022theory}.

\subsection{Joint Learning of IRT and AIG}
\label{sec:3.2}
As noted, CAT heavily relies on a difficulty-diverse and high-quality item pool, which is unfeasible with limited data, resulting in overestimated $a_i$ with overly simple items and vice versa (see Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}). To fill this gap, GETA resorts to Automatic Item Generation (AIG)~\cite{gierl2012automatic} to create difficulty-tailored items. Different from conventional AIG methods based on thoughtfully crafted templates, we directly leverage the generation power of LLMs to probe the value boundaries of examinees adaptively. 

Concretely, we denote $d\!=\!(b,c)$ for brevity, define $q_{\bm\theta}(a_i|\bm y_i, \bm d)$ as a neural \emph{Value Estimator} to assess the examinee's value conformity/alignment in terms of its response history, where $\bm y_{i,\cdot}\!=\!(y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,t})$ and $\bm d\!=\!(d_1,\dots, d_n)$, and $q_{\bm\psi}(d_j|\bm y_{\cdot,j})$ as an \emph{Item Parameter Estimator} to infer an item's parameters from responses of diverse LLMs, where $\bm y_{\cdot,j}\!=\!(y_{1,j},\cdots,y_{m,j})$. Besides, an LLM-based \emph{Item Generator}, $p_{\bm\phi}(x|d)$, is trained to generate new test items with specified difficulty, serving as self-calibrated and evolving item pool. Unlike previous work~\citep{Zhuang_Liu_Huang_Li_Shen_Ma_2022,zhuang2023efficiently}, we use variational inference~\citep{Kingma:vae14} for IRT estimation, which performs faster and more accurately~\cite{wu2020variational,wu2021modeling}. 

By considering $a,d$ as latent variables, we could unify IRT estimation and generator training as modelling a joint distribution $p(\bm x,\bm y)$. Then, an Evidence Lower BOund (ELBO) is derived as:
\begin{align}
   \log p(\bm x,\bm y) & \geq \mathbb{E}_{q_{\bm\theta}(a_i|\bm y_{i,\cdot}, \bm d)q_{\bm\psi}(\bm d|\bm y)} [\log p(\bm y_{i,\cdot}|a_i, \bm d)] \!+\! \mathbb{E}_{q_{\bm\psi}(\bm d|\bm y)} [\log p_{\bm\phi}(\bm x|\bm d)] \!-\! \text{KL}[q_{\bm\psi}(\bm d|\bm y)||p(\bm d)] \notag \\ 
   & + \mathbb{E}_{q_{\bm\psi}(\bm d|\bm y)} [-\text{KL}[q_{\bm\theta}(a_i|\bm y_{i,\cdot}, \bm d)||q(a_i)]] = -\mathcal{L_{GI}}(\bm\theta,\bm\psi,\bm\phi),
   \label{eq:elbo}
\end{align}
where $q_{\bm \psi}(\bm d|\bm y)=\prod_j q_{\bm \psi}(d_j|\bm y_{\cdot,j})$ and $q_{\bm\theta}(a_i|\bm y_{i,\cdot}, \bm d)$ both follow isotropic Gaussian distributions with $p(\bm d)=\prod_j p(d_j)\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and $q(a_i)\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ as priors, respectively. For $p(\bm y_{i,\cdot}|a_i, \bm d)=\prod_j p(y_{i,j}|a_i, d_j)$, we implement it directly with the IRT-2PL model in Eq.(\ref{eq:IRT}). In Eq.\eqref{eq:elbo}, the first term learns to fit item parameters and examinee value conformity to recover the original response, the second term trains the generator, \textit{e.g.}, a pre-trained LLM like LLaMA-2-7B, to generate item by input difficulty, and the last two terms regularize the posterior distributions of $a$ and $d$.

By minimizing $\mathcal{L_{GI}}(\theta,\omega,\phi)$ on $\mathcal{X, Y}$ collected offline, GETA not only optimizes neural IRT estimators, but also jointly trains a generator to automatically create \emph{new} test items tailored to a specified $d$ in a scalable manner without any pre-defined template, mitigating data leakage problem.

\subsection{Generative Evolving Testing}
\label{sec:3.3}
Our main goal is to dynamically exploit the value boundaries of the examinee LLMs. Nevertheless, trained on static $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$, the item generator still fails to cover a wide range of item difficulty, especially the unobserved $d$. To tackle this problem, we incorporate an iterative update schema. 

In this case, $d$ outside the range of static data and corresponding $x$ are both unobserved. Thus, we also consider $x$ as another latent variable following~\cite{kingma2014semi,xu2017variational} and model the distribution of $y$: 
\begin{align}
   & \log p(\bm y) \geq \mathbb{E}_{q(\bm x|\bm y)} [-\mathcal{L_{GI}}(\bm\theta,\bm\psi,\bm\phi)] \!+\! H[q(\bm x| \bm y)],
   \label{eq:un}
\end{align}
where $H$ is the entropy. By further decomposing the ELBO in Eq.\eqref{eq:elbo} into two parts: $ -\mathcal{L_{G}}(\bm\phi) \!=\! \mathbb{E}_{q_{\bm\psi}(\bm d| \bm y)} [\log p_{\bm\phi}(\bm x|\bm d)]$ and $ -\mathcal{L_{I}}(\bm\theta,\bm\psi) $ for other terms, we have:
\begin{align}
   & \mathcal{L}(\bm\theta,\bm\psi,\bm\phi) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(x,y)+\hat{p}(y)q(x|y)}}_{\text{Selective Generation}} [ \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{I}}(\bm\theta,\bm\psi)}_{\text{Variational IRT}}+ \underbrace{\beta\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}}(\bm\phi)}_{\text{Item Generator}}] -\beta\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\hat{p}(y)} [H[q(x|y)]]}_{\text{Generator Regularization}},
   \label{eq:loss}
\end{align}
where $\hat{p}(y)$ is an assumed prior of $y$, which could be a uniform distribution over a broad range of difficulty, $\beta$ is a hyper-parameter to weight the generator's iterative update, $\hat{p}(x,y)$ is the empirical distribution formed by static $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ to train the IRT model and initialize the generator, and the last term regularizes the generator to improve the diversity of generated items, mitigating overfitting. 
\begin{wrapfigure}{L}{0.50\textwidth}
    \begin{minipage}{0.50\textwidth}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{GETA Algorithm}
    \label{alg:geta}
    \hspace*{0.02in} {\bf Input:} $\mathcal{E}$, $p_{\bm\phi}(x|d)$, $\{x_j^0\},\{d_j^0\}$, $T$, $k_1$, $k_2$, $\delta_1$, $\delta_2$ and $\mathcal{S}^* = \emptyset $ \\
    \hspace*{0.02in} {\bf Output:} $\{\hat{a}^T_i\}^m_{i=1}$ and the evolved $p_{\bm\phi}(x|d)$ \\
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \For {$i=1,2,...,m$}
        \State Sample $y_{i,j}^0\!\sim\! e_{i}(y|x_j^0), \text{for each}\ x_{j}^0$
        \State Calculate $\hat{a}_i^0$ with $q_{\bm\theta}$, $\mathcal{S}_i^0\!=\!(\{y_{i,j}^0\}, \{d_j^0\})$
        \EndFor
        \For {$t=1,2,...,T$}
        \For {$i=1,2,...,m$}
        \State Calculate $\hat{d}^t_i$ for $e_i$ with Eq.\eqref{eq:fi},
        \State Sample $x_j^t$ with Eq.\eqref{eq:q(x|y)} $j\!=\!1$ to $k_1$
        \State Sample $y_{i,j}^t \!\sim\! e_{i}(y|x_{j}^t), \text{each}\ x_{j}^t, e_i$
        \State Calculate $d_j^{t}$ by $q_{\bm\psi}(d|\bm y_{\cdot,j}^t)$
        \For{each $(x_j^t, y_{\cdot,j}^t, d^t_j$)}
        \If{$|\hat{d}^t_j-d^t_j|<\delta_1$}
        \State $\mathcal{S}_i^{t-1} \leftarrow \mathcal{S}_i^{t-1} \cup \{(y_{i,j}^t, d^t_{j})\}$
        \ElsIf{$|\hat{d}_j^t-d^t_{j}|>\delta_2$}
        \State $S^* \leftarrow S^* \cup \{(x_j^t, d^t_{j})\}$
        \EndIf
        \EndFor
        \State $S_i^t \leftarrow S_i^{t-1}$, Calculate $\hat{a}_i^t$ with $S_i^t$
        \EndFor
        \If{$|S^*|\ge t*k_2$}
        \State Optimize $\bm\phi$ on $S^*$
        \EndIf
        \EndFor
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\end{minipage} 
\end{wrapfigure}

$\bm\theta,\bm\psi,\bm\phi$ are first optimized on $\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}$. Then during the evolving testing process, we solve the best-fitting difficulties according to the conformity estimated at $t$-th step as:
\begin{align}
   d^*=\underset{d}{\arg\max} \ \mathcal{F}({\hat{a}^t},d),
   \label{eq:fi}
\end{align}
and obtain $b^*=\hat{a}^t$ and $c$ should be as large as possible. Therefore, we directly set the expected difficulty to $\hat{a}^t$ and use a relatively larger $c$. Based on $b^*$, GETA adaptively \emph{generates} new items instead of \emph{selecting} ones from the static pool, called \emph{selective generation} by sampling $y \sim \hat{p}(y)$ and then $x\sim q(x|y)$ with:
\begin{align}
   q(x|y) \approx \int q_{\bm\psi}(d|y) p_{\bm\phi}(x|d) \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{A}}(d) \text{d}d,
   \label{eq:q(x|y)}
\end{align}
where $\mathbb{I}$ is the indicator function. The original Eq.\eqref{eq:loss} requires traversing all possible $d$. To achieve selective generation and obtain more targeted items, we restrict $d$ within a neighborhood of the expected $d^*$, \textit{i.e.}, $\mathcal{A}=[d^{*}-\epsilon, d^{*}+\epsilon]$.

Eq.\eqref{eq:loss} integrates IRT optimization, generator pretraining and adaptive updating, as well as selective generation as a unified response modelling, which explores the value vulnerabilities, boundary items and conformity limitations for each LLM. 
Once the estimators and generator are optimized on static data, GETA starts the evolving testing of all examinees. Given few seed items $\{x^0_j,d_j^0\}$, if the LLM easily responds correctly to them, GETA generates $k_1$ \emph{new} items with a tailored difficulty $\hat{d}$, \emph{avoiding data leakage}. The generated items needs to be responded by all examinees, and whose true $d$ are estimated based on the responses. Only items closely meeting the input difficulty, \textit{i.e.}, $|\hat{d}\!-\!d|\!<\!\delta_1$, are used to update conformity. Otherwise, items are too distant from the boundaries to effectively reflect real conformity. $|\hat{d}\!-\!d|\!>\!\delta_2$ indicates that the generator fails to match a $\hat{d}$ out of the scope of static data. Such $\mathcal{S}\!=\!\{(x,d)\}$ are collected to further fine-tune the generator and establish connections between boundary difficulty and unseen items, \emph{alleviating overestimation}. In this way, the generator is self-calibrated, maintaining the scale invariance of IRT~\citep{reise1993confirmatory}, and also co-evolving with the upgradation of LLM examinees, thereby handling the \emph{evaluation chronoeffect} challenge.

The whole GETA algorithm is described in Alg.~\ref{alg:geta}, a running example is presented in Fig.~\ref{app:derivation}.


\section{Experiments}

\label{sec:4}

\subsection{Experimental Setups} 
\textbf{Data and Metrics}~ Following the common practice in LLM alignment~\citep{askell2021general,xu2023cvalues,kopf2024openassistant}, we consider three types of value issues: \emph{social bias}, \emph{ethics}, and \emph{toxicity}. We collect 15k test items, 5k for each type, from 12 widely-used static datasets $\mathcal{X}$, such as \textsc{BBQ}~\citep{parrish-etal-2022-bbq}, \textsc{RedditBias}~\citep{barikeri-etal-2021-redditbias}, \textsc{ETHICS}~\citep{hendrycks2021ethics}, \textsc{RealToxicityPrompts}~\citep{gehman-etal-2020-realtoxicityprompts}, \textsc{HarmfulQA} \citep{bhardwaj2023redteaming} and so on.~\ref{app:static_data}. We define examinee LLMs' \emph{Value Conformity} (\textbf{VC}) as $\text{VC}\!=\!1\!-\!\textbf{EP}$ for static evaluation, where Empirical Probability (\textbf{EP})~\citep{gehman-etal-2020-realtoxicityprompts} refers to the probability of violating human values at least once over $K$ responses for each item. For CAT-based methods, $y_j\!=\!0$ when LLMs generate toxic, biased response or wrong moral judgement, otherwise $y_j\!=\!1$, and $\text{VC}\!=\!\hat{a}^T$. We report the min-max normalized results. To measure the extent to which each evaluation method is a valid and effective proxy of LLMs' true underlying values, we adopt \emph{Metric Concurrent Validity} (\textbf{Va})~\citep{xiao-etal-2023-evaluating-evaluation} and calculate it as the Pearson's correlation between VC estimated with $\mathcal{X}$ and (i) popular LLM safety leaderboard scores (\textbf{Va-L}), (ii) VC estimated on i.i.d but unseen items (\textbf{Va-I}), and (iii) VC estimated on OOD items with the same value type (\textbf{Va-O}), respectively.~\ref{app:metrics}.

\textbf{Baselines}~ 
To demonstrate the effectiveness of GETA, we compare our method with three baselines in revealing examinee LLMs' value conformity: 1) \textbf{Static Evaluation (SE)}, which assesses VC of each LLM using only the static dataset $\mathcal{X}$; 2) \textbf{CAT}~\citep{zhuang2023efficiently}, an adaptive testing framework for LLM evaluation, where LLMs take place of human examinees; and 3) \textbf{NCAT}~\citep{Zhuang_Liu_Huang_Li_Shen_Ma_2022}, which reformulates CAT as a reinforcement learning problem and directly learns a neural item selection model. Besides, we consider two other models in analysis, \textsc{GPTFuzzer}~\citep{yu2023gptfuzzer} and \textsc{SAP}~\citep{deng-etal-2023-attack} as the generator, which acts as a kind of red-teaming method.

\textbf{Implementation}~ We implement the two IRT estimators with two-layer Transformer~\citep{vaswani2017attention} encoders without positional embedding. The item generator is a LLaMA-3-8B model fine-tuned with prefix tuning~\citep{li-liang-2021-prefix} and LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}. $T\!=\!10$, $k_1\!=\!100$, $k_2\!=\!640$, $\delta_2\!=\!0.5$ and $\delta_1$ is determined by the 10 items with the smallest $|\hat{d}\!-\!d|$ in Alg.~\ref{alg:geta}. $\beta\!=\!0.1$ in Eq.\eqref{eq:loss} and $\epsilon\!=\!0.5$ in Eq.\eqref{eq:q(x|y)}. $K\!=\!4$. We involves eight LLMs as examinees: GPT-4/-3.5-Turbo, Gemini-1.0-Pro, Mistral-Medium/-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-2-70B/7B-Chat, and Orca-2-13B. %Compared Examinees

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:value_conformity_simple}. We can obtain three interesting findings: (1) Rankings from SE and CAT generally align with the intuition that larger models possess better capabilities, with GPT-4 being the SOTA in most value issues. (2) NCAT gives somewhat contradictory conclusions with notably inconsistent results among the three issues, raking GPT-4 the last in both ethics and toxicity. (3) GETA typically consider big models, \textit{e.g.}, GPT-4 and Mistral-M, superior, while some smaller ones, like Orca-2-13B, largely misaligned. These counterintuitive results imply potential systematic measurement errors of existing evaluation methods, necessitating an in-depth diagnosis.

\textbf{Diagnosis of Evaluation Methods}~ To figure out which evaluation method is more reliable and trustworthy, we measure their \emph{Validity}, defined as \emph{the extent to which a test, \textit{i.e.}, scores, accurately measures what it is supposed to measure} in measurement theory~\citep{messick1995validity,messick1998test}. Concretely, we consider Concurrent Validity~\citep{xiao2023evaluating} and assess the correlation between the four methods in Table~\ref{tab:value_conformity_simple} with those evaluated on other sets. Intuitively, conclusions provided by a good measurement should be consistent with existing leaderboards (Va-L), generalized well in unseen i.i.d. samples (Va-I) and OOD testing cases belonging to the same value type. As presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:radar}, GETA maintains the highest Va-L across two popular LLM safety/ethics leaderboards, \href{https://enkryptai-redteaming-demo.vercel.app/}{Enkrypt AI} and \href{https://decodingtrust.io/leaderboard/}{DecodingTrust}, demonstrating its considerable representativeness in all three value types. With unseen i.i.d. items, GETA is comparable to CAT, significantly outperforming NCAT and SE, indicating stronger consistency across different subsets and hence less susceptible to random measurement error, \textit{e.g.}, test set split or prompt variation.

  \caption{Concurrent Validity of different evaluation methods. We present Pearson's correlations (scaled into [0,1]) between results in Table~\ref{tab:value_conformity_simple} and those reported on leaderboards, i.i.d. and OOD data.}

Even on OOD testing items, like \citep{rao-etal-2023-makes} that is never included in the training data $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$, GETA reaches satisfactory validity, especially in social bias and ethics. In toxicity, the OOD items are constructed with jailbreaking templates~\citep{cui2023fft}, highlighting a gap between everyday scenarios and adversarial attacks. Interestingly, NCAT performs poorly across all value types. We suspect this is because the RL-based training of NCAT is data-consuming, \textit{e.g}, 60k+ data~\citep{Zhuang_Liu_Huang_Li_Shen_Ma_2022}. With limited data (15k in our setting), NCAT fails to learn an effective selection model. Generally, GETA achieves better evaluation validity with good robustness and generalization, reflecting what it purports to measure accurately.

\textbf{Ablation Study}~ To further analyze GETA, we conduct an ablation study and compare different variants in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_simple}. Obviously, updating the item generator brings prominent benefits for Va-L. As discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, static datasets might be too easy for changing LLMs. In contrast, leaderboards frequently refine items to challenge models. Our adaptive optimization schema enables items to co-evolve, thereby more consistent with the most recent leaderboard rankings. However, this advantage is marginal for Va-I and Va-O as they are calculated under `outdated' datasets. Besides, VIRT plays a vital role in validity, as variational inference is more stable and can be theoretically unified with item generator, gaining from joint training and iterative enhancement. Without Update and VIRT, GETA almost degenerates to CAT. Also, Transformer is superior, especially in toxicity, which helps capture connections between item parameters and semantics. Such results support our motivation of evolving testing and verify the effectiveness of all components.

\textbf{Evolving Testing: Difficulty Adaptability}~ The other aspect lies in item difficulties, \textit{i.e.}, static datasets fail to keep pace with fast evolving LLMs. As presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:analysis}~(c), LLMs with considerable capability gaps, \textit{e.g.}, GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, LLaMA-2-70B-Chat, obtain indistinguishable conformity scores when measured by SE. Besides, we also try two automatic read-teaming methods, GPTFuzzer~\citep{yu2023gptfuzzer} and SAP~\citep{deng-etal-2023-attack}, as item generator, which can be regarded as a sort of dynamic testing as introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:related}. Nevertheless, all examinees get almost zero scores under their assessment, since they only attack and elicit harmful responses, unable to adaptively adjust difficulty. In comparison, GETA, endowed with VIRT and item generator, possesses good controllability, and hence could probe each LLM examinee's value vulnerabilities, mitigating \emph{evaluation chronoeffect}.

\textbf{Testing Efficiency}~ Fig.~\ref{fig:converge} gives the value conformity $a_i$ estimated by CAT and GETA with different numbers of iterations. We can see $a_i$ given by CAT varies during the testing, especially for stronger LLMs, requiring more items. This is because the item pool is not difficulty complete, posing special challenges for item selection. In contrast, GETA converges faster and more stably, benefiting from \emph{selective generation}, manifesting greater efficiency.~\ref{app:more_analysis}.

\textbf{Case study}~ We present running examples from GETA in Fig.~\ref{fig:case_study} to illustrate its effectiveness. As expected, when the examinee’s value conformity surpasses the item difficulty, it tends to respond correctly, demonstrating a positive correlation between estimated conformity and real LLM behaviors, verifying GETA's validity. Interestingly, GPT-4 tends to always choose a biased choice under the social bias type, potentially resulting from its strong instruction following ability, consistent with results in Table~\ref{tab:value_conformity_simple}.

\section{Conclusion}

The rapid development of LLMs poses a special challenge for accurately unpacking their underlying values/ethic conformity, namely, evaluation chronoeffect. To alleviate the overestimation cased by this problem, we propose \emph{generative evolving testing}, and design GETA, a corresponding framework to adaptively probe LLMs' value boundaries and generate novel and difficulty-tailored test items. Comprehensive experiments and analysis manifest GETA can produce robust and generalized evaluation results, supporting its superior validity and efficiency. In the future, we plan to further explore the scalability of GETA on different models in real-time safety monitoring scenarios, and apply it to more value types and multimodal models, paving the way for more reliable big model evaluation.
