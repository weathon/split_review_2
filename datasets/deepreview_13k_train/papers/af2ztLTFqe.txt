\title{Token-Aware Inference-Time Intervention for Large Language Model Alignment}

\begin{abstract}
Effectively mitigating the misalignment of large language models (LLMs) is crucial for ensuring secure AI applications. Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) technique, which applies interventions to internal representations along the probed alignment direction during inference, offers substantial alignment enhancements with minimal cost. However, previous ITI methods adopt coarse sentence-level analysis which neglects the misalignment discrepancy among varied tokens, resulting in deviant alignment direction and inflexible intervention strength.
In this work, we propose a Token-Aware Inference-Time Intervention (TA-ITI)  approach to fully utilize token-level alignment information, therefore realizing superior post-intervention performance. TA-ITI primarily consists of Mutual Information-Guided Token-level Graph Aggregation (MIG) and Misalignment-aware Adaptive Token-level Intervention (MAI). MIG develops a MI-guided graph to exploit the tokens' informative interaction for representation enrichment, thus improving alignment probing and facilitating subsequent intervention.
MAI comprehensively perceives the token-level misalignment degree from token representation and prediction to guide the adaptive adjustment of intervention strength, thereby enhancing final alignment performance. Extensive experiments on three alignment capabilities demonstrate the efficacy of TA-ITI, notably surpassing baseline by 25.8\% on the primary metric of truthfulness.
\end{abstract}

\section{1 INTRODUCTION  }


With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, large language models (LLMs) (Touvron et al., $2023\mathrm{a}$ ; Brown et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2023) have profoundly changed daily production and lifestyle. However, LLMs continue to exhibit misalignment issues and generate erroneous (Li et al., 2024a), harmful (Li et al., 2024b), and other content that deviate from human expectations (Shen et al., 2023). For example, unaligned LLMs may abet depressed people to commit suicide, causing serious hazards. This misalignment significantly impedes the safe and reliable applications of LLMs in real-world scenarios. Although training-based alignment techniques like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) (Wang et al., 2022; Taori et al., 2023) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2023) have gained considerable success, their practical applications are constrained by excessive costs and unstable effects (Casper et al., 2023).  

Benefiting from minimal resource consumption and notable alignment effectiveness, Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) methods (Li et al., 2024a; Chen et al., 2024) based on representation editing have gained significant attention. They first identify the alignment directions by training interpretable probes on aligned and misaligned samples, and then intervene in the model’s internal representations along with the directions during inference. This reduces the need for extensive training data and computational resources, lowering alignment costs and avoiding introducing new risks, such as sycophancy (Perez et al., 2022). Consequently, ITI can effectively mitigate erroneous (Li et al., 2024a) and harmful (Li et al., 2024b) contents, thereby holding substantial application value.  

However, previous ITI methods (Li et al., 2024a; Chen et al., 2024) simply adopt coarse sentencelevel analysis, and neglect the fine-grained alignment contributions of individual tokens, leading to deviant alignment direction and inflexible intervention strength. As shown in Figure 1, this coarse sentence-level analysis brings negative impacts on both probe training and representation intervention stages: (1) Sentence-level probe training simply utilizes the last token as a surrogate for the entire sentence, ignoring the informative interaction among various tokens. The extracted representation inadequately comprehends the alignment of sentence, resulting in directional deviation when probing. (2) Sentence-level representation intervention homogeneously applies identical editing strength to all tokens while overlooking the variable alignment degree of the predicted token, yielding insufficient correction of misaligned tokens. Therefore, sentence-level ITI attains suboptimal alignment performance due to the disregard for individual token’s distinct alignment contribution.

Figure 1: Comparisons between previous sentence-level ITI and proposed token-aware ITI. Tokenlevel analysis can alleviate directional deviation and realize effective intervention.  

In this paper, we propose Token-Aware Inference-Time Intervention (TA-ITI), which comprises MI-guided Token-level Graph Aggregation (MIG) and Misalignment-aware Adaptive Token-level Intervention (MAI), to fully perceive and utilize multiple tokens for alignment improvement. Aiming at mitigating the directional deviation, MIG leverages mutual information (MI) to establish the tokens’ interactions, and aggregates the graph-propagated tokens to derive more comprehensive representations, thereby refining the probed direction and facilitating subsequent alignment intervention. MI can offer a global probabilistic analysis to effectively measure information sharing, thus significantly boosting the training representations when integrated with graph. To achieve adaptive intervention across distinct tokens, MAI adjusts the editing strength with the guidance of token-level misalignment awareness. Grounded in the motivation that both token representation and prediction are closely associated with misalignment, MAI assesses the token representation using an estimator trained on token-level misalignment dataset, and quantifies the prediction uncertainty with entropy, thus accurately perceiving token misalignment to provide dependable guidance for intervention.  

To evaluate the effectiveness of TA-ITI, we conduct comprehensive experiments of three typical alignment capabilities, including truthfulness, harmlessness, and fairness. On the classic truthfulness evaluation dataset TruthfulQA (Lin et al., 2022), our method especially achieves a remarkable $87.8\%$ of primary True\*Info metric, exceeding the sentence-level ITI by $18.8\%$ . TA-ITI also significantly decreases the toxicity of baseline-generated content on RealToxicityPrompt (Gehman et al., 2020), particularly achieving nearly $90\%$ reduction on metric TP. Our performance on both fairness evaluation metrics of StereoSet (Nadeem et al., 2021) also surpasses all debiasing methods. Extensive experiments confirm the substantial improvement of TA-ITI in post-intervention alignment.  



\section{2 RELATED WORKS  }




\section{2.1 LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL ALIGNMENT  }


LLM alignment, which ensures that the behaviors or outputs of LLM systems are aligned with human expectations (Ji et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023), is crucial for guaranteeing the safe application of LLM. The alignment necessitates that LLMs acquire various human-preferred capabilities, such as truthfulness, harmlessness, fairness (Shen et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023; Ji et al., 2023), etc. To meet these intricate alignment requirements, researches have investigated a range of approaches to establish a multifaceted technical alignment framework. Prominent methodologies include Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (Wang et al., 2022; Taori et al., 2023) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2023). They acquire alignment knowledge by training on the crafted datasets containing human expectations, demonstrating considerable success. However, these methods are constrained by significant limitations like high implementation costs and instability in performance (Casper et al., 2023).  



\section{2.2 INFERENCE-TIME INTERVENTION  }


Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) is an efficient alignment technique, which posits that the interpretable internal structures related to model alignment can be utilized to reduce misaligned content. Li et al. (Li et al., 2024a) first proposed the ITI technique and demonstrated its capability to enhance the truthfulness of responses. Building on ITI, Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2024) utilized multiple orthogonal probes to model truthful directions across various dimensions, and introduced a diversified sampling method to capture a broader range of features. Additionally, Li et al. proposed DESTEIN (Li et al., 2024b) to use representation intervention for toxicity mitigation. TruthX (Zhang et al., 2024) utilized auto-encoders to decouple internal representations into semantic and truthful spaces and intervened in the truthful space to enhance truthfulness while maintaining semantic integrity. However, these methods roughly adopt sentence-level analysis, neglecting the valuable information and alignment degrees of individual tokens, leading to suboptimal intervention results.  



\section{3 METHODOLOGY  }


Sentence-level ITI ignores the significance of individual tokens to alignment analysis, leading to unsatisfactory intervention. To fully analyze and utilize all tokens for realizing optimal intervention, we explore the implementation of ITI from a token-level perspective and introduce TokenAware Inference-Time Intervention (TA-ITI). Concretely, as shown in Figure 2, we devise tailored token-level modules for both stages, i.e. MI-guided Token-level Graph Aggregation (MIG) and Misalignment-aware Adaptive Token-level Intervention (MAI). In this section, we first review the fundamental process of ITI during LLM inference in Section 3.1. We then elaborate on the proposed MIG in Section 3.2 and MAI in Section 3.3, with detailed demonstration of their contributions.  



\section{3.1 PRELIMINARY  }


Decoding-based LLMs (Touvron et al., 2023a) could possibly produce misaligned contents through language modeling head $W_{L M}$ , which have been proven to have an interpretable association with the internal representations (Li et al., 2024a). Therefore, sparse interventions on the internal representations have been designed by ITI to guide the model toward producing more aligned outputs.  

ITI first employs the widely adopted probing technique (Tenney, 2019) to discriminate between aligned and misaligned representations. Directed by (Li et al., 2024a), a sample set $\boldsymbol{S}$ comprising both aligned samples $s^{+}$ and misaligned samples $s^{-}$ is constructed. Each sample $s=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}$ is a sentence of multiple tokens, and its representation from $h$ -th head of multi-head self-attention (MHSA) within $l_{\cdot}$ -th decoding layer is $\mathbf{s}^{l,h}=\{\mathbf{o}_{1}^{l,h},...,\mathbf{o}_{n}^{l,h}\}.$ , where each $\mathbf{o}_{i}^{l,h}$ corresponds to token $x_{i}$ . Sentence-level ITI utilizes last token’s representation ${\bf o}_{n}^{l,h}$ as a surrogate for the entire sample $\mathbf{s}^{l,h}$ , and forms a dataset $\mathbfcal{D}^{l,h}=\{(\mathbf{o}_{n}^{l,h},\mathbf{y})_{i}\}$ to train probe $\ddot{P}^{l,h}$ for alignment discrimination:  

$$
\underset{\mathbf{d}^{l,h}}{\arg\operatorname*{min}}\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{o}_{n}^{l,h},\mathbf{y})\sim\mathcal{D}^{l,h}}[\mathrm{CE}(P^{l,h}(\mathbf{o}_{n}^{l,h};\mathbf{d}^{l,h}),\mathbf{y})]
$$  

In Formula 1, $\mathbf{d}^{l,h}$ denotes the learned parameter of $P^{l,h}$ , which is also determined as the editing direction of alignment. y labels each sample as aligned or misaligned, and CE is the cross-entropy loss. A sparse set of heads with the highest validation accuracy is then selected to be intervened.  

During the representation intervention stage, the editing direction $\mathbf{d}^{l,h}$ is applied to the representation with identical intensity $\alpha$ for each prediction, therefore shifting the representation towards the alignment direction and guiding the model to generate alignment outputs:  

$$
\hat{\mathbf{o}}_{k:n}^{l,h}=\mathbf{o}_{k:n}^{l,h}+\alpha\mathbf{d}^{l,h}
$$  

where ˆolk,:hn denotes the intervened model-generated token representations. Analysis reveals that both the deviant direction $\mathbf{d}^{l,h}$ and the identical intensity $\alpha$ are determined from a sentence-level

\section{3.2 MI-GUIDED TOKEN-LEVEL GRAPH AGGREGATION  }


To mitigate the directional deviation in sentence-level probes, we aim to leverage all informative tokens within a sentence to probe a universal alignment direction. However, directly averaging inadvertently neglects the impacts of token interactions on the whole sentence, potentially misguiding alignment probing. Therefore, we propose MI-guided Token-level Graph Aggregation (MIG) to model and utilize the critical interactions among tokens, finally enhancing the discrimination of alignment in training representations. It innovatively applies mutual information (MI) (Steuer et al., 2002; Gabrie´ et al., 2018) for a global probabilistic analysis of information sharing among tokens to promote the perception of their contributions, and subsequently boost the useful information in training representations by aggregating the augmented features after graph propagation.  

MI-guided Graph Propagation We first construct MI-based graph network $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ for each training sample to strengthen the analysis of token interactions. Specifically, $\mathcal{V}\,=\,\bigl\{v_{i}|i\,\in\,[k,n]\bigr\}$ represents the vertex set abstracted from all model-generated token representations $\mathbf{o}_{k:n}$ 1, and $\bar{\mathcal{E}}\stackrel{}{=}\{e_{i,j}|i,j\in[k,n]\}$ denotes the MI-based edge set. Primarily, each vertex $\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{0}$ of initial graph $\mathcal{G}^{0}$ represents the corresponding token representation $\mathbf{o}_{i}$ , and each edge $e_{i,j}^{0}$ denotes the mutual information between $\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{0}$ and $\boldsymbol{v}_{j}^{0}$ . Subsequently, we conduct $r$ rounds propagation based on the MI-based graph prior to aggregation, thereby reinforcing the beneficial information inherent in each token with the guidance of mutual information. To clarify, we formulate the graph at $r$ -th round $\mathcal{G}$ as follows:  

$$
\mathcal{G}^{r}\left(v^{r},e^{r}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}{l l}{v_{i}^{r}=\sum_{j\in\left[k,n\right]}e_{i,j}^{r-1}\cdot v_{j}^{r-1}/\sum_{j\in\left[k,n\right]}e_{i,j}^{r-1}}\\ {e_{i,j}^{r}=\mathcal{M}\mathbb{Z}\left(v_{i}^{r},v_{j}^{r}\right)=H\left(v_{i}^{r}\right)+H\left(v_{j}^{r}\right)-H\left(v_{i}^{r},v_{j}^{r}\right)}\end{array}\right.
$$  

Specifically, the mutual information is calculated based on the information theory (Shannon, 1948), where $H(\cdot)$ denotes the single Shannon entropy and $H(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the joint entropy. Following (Steuer et al., 2002), we estimate the entropy of token representation $H(v_{i}^{r})$ by discretizing the continuous representation into bins to calculate probability distribution.  

Aggregation for Alignment Probing After $r$ -th propagation, we perform graph aggregation on the enhanced tokens and obtain the final representation o˜, which contains more abundant and discriminative information. For each sample in $\boldsymbol{S}$ , we perform MI-guided graph aggregation to collect enhanced representations ˜o, and develop a preferable training set $\tilde{D}=\{(\tilde{\bf o},{\bf y})_{i}\}$ . On the basis of Equation 1, we train the universal alignment probe $\tilde{P}$ on $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ , and identify the editing direction $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$ :  

$$
\underset{\tilde{\mathbf{d}}}{\mathrm{arg}\,\mathrm{min}}\,\mathbb{E}_{(\tilde{\mathbf{o}},\mathbf{y})\sim\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}[\mathrm{CE}(\tilde{P}(\tilde{\mathbf{o}};\tilde{\mathbf{d}}),\mathbf{y})]\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\tilde{\mathbf{o}}=\sum_{i\in[k,n]}v_{i}^{r}/(n-k)
$$  

Consequently, the probe $\tilde{P}$ is more accurate and unbiased, and the direction $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$ can be applied to all tokens during inference, ensuring intervention effectively steers towards correct alignment direction.  

3.3 MISALIGNMENT-AWARE ADAPTIVE TOKEN-LEVEL INTERVENTION  

To address the imprecise edition of sentence-level intervention, we propose Misalignment-aware Adaptive Token-level Intervention (MAI), which adjusts the intensity based on misalignment awareness to achieve token-level adaptive intervention. Studies have revealed that LLM misalignment is typically manifested in token representation (Ji et al., 2024) and prediction probabilities (Varshney et al., 2023). Therefore, MAI perceives token misalignment by combining representation misalignment estimation using an estimator trained on a token-level misalignment dataset, and prediction uncertainty quantification leveraging the predictive capability of LLM. They mutually compensate for ignored misalignment aspects, thereby providing reliable guidance for intervention.  

Representation Misalignment Estimation To directly estimate the misalignment of token representation, we construct a token-level misalignment dataset assuming that specific misaligned tokens contribute to the misalignment of sample $s^{-}$ (e.g. ‘can’ leads to the misalignment of ‘Pigs can fly’). Consequently, we should label the preceding tokens (e.g. ‘Pigs’ preceding ‘can’) prone to generate misaligned predictions as 1, while other unrelated tokens as 0. Given a selected pair of aligned and misaligned samples $(s^{+},s^{-})$ , we specifically regard the different tokens between $s^{+}$ and $s^{-}$ as misaligned tokens. To clarify, we annotate the misalignment label $\mathbf{y_{j}}$ of token $x_{j}$ in $s^{-}$ as follows:  

$$
\mathbf{y}_{j}={\binom{0,}{1,}}\begin{array}{r l}{x_{j+1}\in s^{-}\cap s^{+}}\\ {x_{j+1}\in s^{-}\mid s^{+}}\end{array}
$$  

We then form the token-level misalignment dataset $\mathcal{D}_{m}\,=\,\{(\mathbf{o}_{j},\mathbf{y}_{j})|\mathbf{o}_{j}\,\in\,\mathbf{s}_{i}^{-}\}$ , where ${\bf o}_{j}$ denotes the representation of each token $x_{j}$ in misaligned sample $s_{i}^{-}$ . Based on $D_{m}$ , we train an automated misalignment estimator $E_{m}$ which is a logistic regression parameterized with $\theta$ , to assess the potential predicted misalignment degree from token representation. During inference, $E_{m}$ processes the representation $\mathbf{o}_{t}$ at $t$ -th generation and estimate the potential misalignment degree $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ :  

$$
\mathbf{m}_{t}=E_{m}(\mathbf{o}_{t};\theta)\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\arg\operatorname*{min}_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{o}_{j},\mathbf{y}_{j})\sim\mathcal{D}_{m}}\bigl[\mathrm{CE}\bigl(E_{m}\bigl(\mathbf{o}_{j};\theta\bigr),\mathbf{y}_{j}\bigr)\bigr]
$$  

Prediction Uncertainty Quantification Directly estimating the representation misalignment does not encompass analysis of prediction uncertainty, which is demonstrated to be related to the occurrence of LLM misalignment (Varshney et al., 2023). Intuitively, a higher uncertainty indicates a greater likelihood of potential misalignment. Therefore, drawing upon (Manakul et al., 2023), we obtain the probability distribution $\sigma(W_{L M}\cdot\mathbf{o}_{t})$ predicted by $W_{L M}$ , and calculate the normalized entropy with vocab size $z$ to quantify the token uncertainty $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ , assisting in misalignment awareness:  

$$
\mathbf{u}_{t}=H(\sigma(W_{L M}\cdot\mathbf{o}_{t}))/\log(z)
$$  

Adaptive Intervention Finally, we implement an adaptive intervention by jointly considering the representation misalignment estimation and prediction uncertainty quantification. We directly perform a weighted summation of $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ with balancing factor $\beta$ as the final measure of misalignment. This enables us to differentiate strength $\boldsymbol{A}(\mathbf{o}_{t})$ for various tokens. The adaptive intervention process, based on Equation 2, can be expressed by the following equation:  

$$
\hat{\mathbf{o}}_{t}=\mathbf{o}_{t}+\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{o}_{t})\tilde{\mathbf{d}}\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{o}_{t})=[\beta\mathbf{m}_{t}+(1-\beta)\mathbf{u}_{t}]\cdot\boldsymbol\alpha
$$  

Ultimately, we achieve the token-aware inference-time intervention through MIG and MAI. MIG first probes an accurate alignment direction through token-aware enhancement, and MAI intervenes adaptively guided by token-aware misalignment. TA-ITI breaks free from the constraints of conventional sentence-level approaches, thereby significantly improving alignment performance.

\section{4 EXPERIMENTS  }


In this section, we conduct comprehensive experiments on commonly acknowledged truthfulness, harmlessness, and fairness capabilities to illustrate the alignment effectiveness of TA-ITI.  



\section{4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP  }


Benchmarks and Metrics We assess the truthfulness of LLMs using widely adopted TruthfulQA (Lin et al., 2022), which encompasses open-ended generation task and multiple-choice task. The open-ended generation task is measured through the primary metric True\*Info rate. Three types of multiple-choice accuracy metrics, specifically MC1, MC2, and MC3, are used for multiple-choice task evaluation. For harmlessness, we follow (Li et al., 2024b) and use the RealToxicityPrompts (Gehman et al., 2020) dataset to examine whether the LLMs can generate harmless content under toxic prompts. We utilize the toxicity score from the Perspective API, which encompasses both Expected Maximum Toxicity (EMT) and Toxicity Probability (TP), to measure the capability for harmless alignment. To evaluate the alignment of fairness, we perform stereotype recognition task (Sun et al., 2024) based on benchmark StereoSet (Nadeem et al., 2021) with metrics Stereotype Score and Recognition Accuracy.  

Baseline and Comparative Methods We choose multiple open-source LLMs, including LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023a), LLaMA-2 (Touvron et al., 2023b), LLaMA-3 (Meta, 2024), Alpaca (Taori et al., 2023), Vicuna (Chiang et al., 2023), Mistral (Jiang et al., 2023), and Baichuan (Yang et al., 2023) as our baselines. We focus on recently released LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct for our experiments.  

We first compare the proposed TA-ITI with various ITI methods, including ITI (Li et al., 2024a), TrFr (Chen et al., 2024), TruthX (Zhang et al., 2024), LITO (Bayat et al., 2024), and DESTEIN (Li et al., 2024b). We also consider other effective methods that enhance various alignment capabilities for comparison. Other comparative methods of truthfulness involve Supervised FineTuning (SFT) performed by (Li et al., 2024a), Few-shot Prompting (FSP) achieved by (Bai et al., 2022), and decoding-based methods including DoLa (Chuang et al., 2023) and SH2 (Kai et al., 2024). Aimed at comparing the alignment effectiveness of harmlessness, additional selections include finetuning-based DAPT (Liu et al., 2021), prompt-based Self-Debiasing (SD) (Schick et al., 2021), and decoding-based DEXPERTS (Liu et al., 2021). Regarding fairness, we further consider widely recognized debiasing methods, including finetuning-based Counterfactual Data Augmentation (CDA) (Dinan et al., 2020), prompt-based Debias-Prompt (DP) (Hida et al., 2024) and Self-Debiasing (SD) (Schick et al., 2021).

\section{5.2 ANALYSIS OF MIG  }


Superiority of MI We first compete against other representation learning strategies to substantiate the superiority of our MI-guided representation aggregation approach in MIG, and the alignment performance is shown in Table 6. Methods trained using sentence-level analysis with end-ofsentence (EOS) tokens (Li et al., 2024a) or randomly selected tokens (Chen et al., 2024) only learn alignment-related information of individual tokens, thereby resulting in limited improvement over the baseline and underperforming simplest aggregation. Among compared aggregation methods (Simple averaging, similarity-based graph aggregation $\mathcal{G}_{s i m}$ and MI-based graph aggregation $g_{m i}$ ), our MIG surpasses all other methods with a notable True\*Info of $83.2\%$ . The simple averaging method reaches only $76.7\%$ True\*Info, as it fails to leverage inter-token relationships to fully comprehend alignment. While $\mathcal{G}_{s i m}$ manages to capture geometric relationships between tokens in the feature space, they lack a global understanding of the feature distributions, resulting in residual irrelevant semantics after propagation. In contrast, $\mathcal{G}_{m i}$ leverages mutual information to grasp deeper alignment connections between tokens from a probabilistic distribution perspective, resulting in the most effective enhancement of representations following information sharing.

\section{5.3 ANALYSIS OF MAI  }


Misalignment-aware Techniques Our investigations into two misalignment-aware techniques, namely representation misalignment estimation and prediciton uncertainty quantification within MAI, are detailed in Table 7. Simply using the trained misalignment estimator to produce assessment results $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ leads to a $3.7\%$ performance improvement, indicating that applying appropriate intervention intensity to tokens based on the perceived degree of misalignment significantly enhances the effectiveness of the intervention. However, without the consideration of quantified uncertainty $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ , the adaptive intervention fails to reach optimal performance. Using $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ alone as the basis for adaptive intervention yields only a $0.9\%$ improvement, underscoring the importance of developing a token-level misalignment estimator through supervised learning. Ultimately, by adjusting $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ with $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ , MAI achieves an optimal performance of $87.8\%$ , reflecting the collaborative interaction between two misalignment-aware techniques for realizing comprehensive misalignment estimation.  

Balancing Factor We further examine how to optimally balance the contributions of $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ for enhanced intervention effectiveness. The result presented in Figure 7 reveals an unimodal pattern in the relationship between performance and balancing factor $\beta$ , peaking at $\beta=0.8$ . This finding indicates that $\mathbf{m}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{u}_{t}$ indeed compensate for each other’s neglected misalignment factors, resulting in more accurate misalignment awareness. Moreover, the greater contribution of direct misalignment estimation relative to auxiliary uncertainty quantification emphasizes its essential role.

\section{6 CONCLUSION  }


In this paper, we delve into the significant fine-grained contribution of individual tokens to the overall alignment assessment and enhancement, and propose a Token-Aware Inference-Time Intervention approach. TA-ITI improves the alignment probes’ accuracy and universality by MIG, and adaptively applies appropriate intervention to diverse tokens through MAI, therefore effectively rectifying the misaligned generation. Extensive experiments on typical alignment capabilities of truthfulness, harmlessness, and fairness have confirmed that the proposed TA-ITI achieves superior alignment performance. Practical case studies also verify that TA-ITI can intervene in originally misaligned outputs to achieve alignment. In the future, we will explore more efficient token-level ITI techniques and extend them to multimodal large language models.

Analysis of Sentence-level Deficiency To explicitly illustrate that sentence-level analysis is prone to directional deviation, and fails to accurately probe alignment direction, we evaluate the accuracy of sentence-level probe trained on the last token, both on the last token and across all tokens in the validation set. Figure 8 shows a significant discrepancy between the validation results on the last token and on all tokens, with an average reduction exceeding $10\%$ across various numbers of intervention heads. This is attributed to the fact that the validation set composed of all tokens better represents the diversity of tokens encountered during practical inference by LLMs, necessitating more precise intervention directions to achieve better intervention. However, the sentence-level probe trained on the last token fails to adequately capture and integrate information among various tokens within sentence, resulting in poor performance when tested across all tokens. Therefore, we aims to leverage all informative tokens within a sentence to probe a universal alignment direction and achieve superior alignment performance.  

We also add a illustrative Figure 9 to explain this issue. We assume that aligned and misaligned samples occupy two non-overlapping regions in the feature space (represented as blue-shaded and gray-shaded areas, respectively), where each token corresponds to a point within the space (depicted as blue and gray squares of varying shades). Inference-time intervention techniques aim to identify an alignment direction that points from the misaligned region to the aligned region. As the last token’s representation cannot fully integrate the sentence information, leaving it likely positioned near the edge of the space. Consequently, the alignment direction learned from such representations deviates significantly from the desired alignment direction. In contrast, the propagation and aggregation within the MIG overcome the self-attention mechanism’s limitations in capturing token interactions, and yield more comprehensive representations (illustrated as the slanted box in the right diagram, closer to the center of the space). Consequently, the alignment direction learned is much closer to the desired alignment direction.  

Analysis of Training Data Size To investigate the impact of probe training data size on the alignment performance, we present the True\*Info results on the TruthfulQA open-ended generation task under different training data conditions, as illustrated in Figure 10. Specifically, we vary the total size of the data used for probe training and validation from $50\%$ (408 samples) to $10\%$ (80 samples). As the training data size decreases, True\*Info demonstrates a general downward trend, indicating that larger datasets facilitate better alignment probing and enhance overall model performance. Despite the reduction in training data, we also sustain a relatively high performance level. This is credited to TA-ITI’s extensive token awareness and information aggregation, highlighting the practical advantage of our approach.
