\title{Diffusion Bridge AutoEncoders for Unsupervised Representation Learning}

\begin{abstract}
Diffusion-based representation learning has achieved substantial attention due to its promising capabilities in latent representation and sample generation. Recent studies have employed an auxiliary encoder to identify a corresponding representation from a sample and to adjust the dimensionality of a latent variable $\rvz$. Meanwhile, this auxiliary structure invokes \textit{information split problem} because the diffusion and the auxiliary encoder would divide the information from the sample into two representations for each model. Particularly, the information modeled by the diffusion becomes over-regularized because of the static prior distribution on $\rvx_T$. %unidentifiable
To address this problem, we introduce Diffusion Bridge AuteEncoders (DBAE), which enable $\rvz$-dependent endpoint $\rvx_T$ inference through a feed-forward architecture. This structure creates an information bottleneck at $\rvz$, so $\rvx_T$ becomes dependent on $\rvz$ in its generation. This results in two consequences: 1) $\rvz$ holds the full information of samples, and 2) $\rvx_T$ becomes a learnable distribution, not static any further. 
We propose an objective function for DBAE to enable both reconstruction and generative modeling, with their theoretical justification. Empirical evidence supports the effectiveness of the intended design in DBAE, which notably enhances downstream inference quality, reconstruction, and disentanglement. Additionally, DBAE generates high-fidelity samples in the unconditional generation.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


Unsupervised representation learning is a fundamental topic within the latent variable generative models~\cite{hinton2006fast,KingmaW13,higgins2017betavae,goodfellow2014generative,chen2016infogan,jeff2017adversarial}. Effective representation supports better downstream inference as well as realistic data synthesis. 
Variational autoencoders (VAEs)~\cite{KingmaW13} %and generative adversarial networks (GANs)~\cite{goodfellow2014generative,karras2020analyzing} 
are frequently used because they inherently include latent representations with flexible dimensionality. Whereas generative adversarial networks (GANs)~\cite{goodfellow2014generative} originally focused on only the generation, recent advances in identifying the latent from a sample, a.k.a. GAN inversion \cite{abdal2019image2stylegan,abdal2020image2stylegan++}, are another method to find latent representations. Also, diffusion probabilistic models (DPMs)~\cite{ho2020denoising,song2021scorebased} have achieved state-of-the-art performance in terms of generation quality~\cite{dhariwal2021diffusion,pmlr-v202-kim23i}, which has naturally prompted efforts to explore unsupervised representation learning within the DPM framework~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised,yue2024exploring,wang2023infodiffusion,Wu_Zheng_2024,yang2023disdiff,hudson2023soda} since researchers hypothesized that an informative latent representation has enabled such generation performance.

As surveyed above, DPMs are a type of latent variable generative model, but its inference on latent variables is not straightforward. DPMs progressively map from data $\rvx_0$ to a latent endpoint $\rvx_T$ via a predefined noise injection schedule, which does not facilitate learnable encoding, DPMs initially would be regarded as a sample generator like GAN. DDIM~\cite{song2021denoising} introduces an invertible path between the data $\rvx_0$ and the endpoint $\rvx_T$ given a data distribution. However, the latent variable $\rvx_T$ still suffers from \textit{over-regularization problem} with a Gaussian distribution, which hinders learning meaningful encodings~\cite{takahashi2019variational}. Moreover, the inference of $\rvx_T$ is intractable and dimension cannot be reduced, which poses disadvantages for downstream applications~\cite{sinha2021d2c}.
To tackle this issue, recent DPM-based representation learning studies~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised,wang2023infodiffusion,yang2023disdiff,yue2024exploring,hudson2023soda,Wu_Zheng_2024} suggest an auxiliary latent variable $\rvz$ with an encoder used in VAEs. This encoder-generated latent variable $\rvz$ is tractable without ODE solving~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised}, and the encoder enables dimensionality reduction, as well. This paper points out the remaining problem in DPM-based representation learning methods which we refer to as the \textit{information split problem}. The information is not solely retained in the tractable latent variable $\rvz$; rather, a portion is also distributed into the intractable latent variable $\rvx_T$ evidenced by \Cref{fig:1b}. To address this issue, we introduce Diffusion Bridge AutoEncoders (DBAE), which incorporate a $\rvz$-dependent endpoint $\rvx_T$ inference using a feed-forward architecture.

The proposed model DBAE systemically resolves both \textit{over-regularization problem} and \textit{information split problem}. The endpoint $\rvx_T$ inference of DBAE is achieved through encoder-decoder propagation as the inferred endpoints shown in \Cref{fig:1d}. The diffusion endpoint $\rvx_T$ is no longer constrained to be Gaussian and it becomes tractable. Furthermore, unlike the two split inference paths in the previous approach (dotted line in \Cref{fig:1a}), DBAE encourages $\rvz$ to become an information bottleneck in inference (dotted line in \Cref{fig:1c}), leading $\rvz$ becomes more informative. In the experiments, DBAE outperforms the previous works in downstream inference quality, reconstruction, disentanglement, and unconditional generation. DBAE exhibits satisfactory results in interpolations and attribute manipulations with its qualitative benefits.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Diffusion Probabilistic Models and Representation Learning}
Diffusion probabilistic models (DPM)~\cite{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising} with a continuous time formulation~\cite{song2021scorebased} define a forward stochastic differential equation (SDE) in \cref{eq:for}, which starts from data distribution $q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0)$ and gradually perturbs it to noise $\rvx_T$. %\footnote{In previous literature, this distribution is usually notated as $p_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0)$, but we changed the notation by using $q$ to consistently represent the inference process with $q$ and the generation process with $p$.}
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = \mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_t, \quad \rvx_0 \sim q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0),  \label{eq:for}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{w}_t$ denotes a standard Wiener process, $\mathbf{f}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times [0,T] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a drift term, and $g:[0,T] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a volatility term. Let the marginal distribution of \cref{eq:for} at time $t$ be denoted as $\tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t)$. There exists a unique reverse-time SDE~\cite{anderson1982reverse} as shown in \cref{eq:back}, which traces back from noise $\rvx_T$ to data $\rvx_0$. Furthermore, the reverse-time ordinary differential equation (ODE) in \cref{eq:backode} produces an identical marginal distribution to \cref{eq:back} for all $t$, offering an alternative generative process while conferring stochasticity of the trajectory solely to $\rvx_T$.  
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log \tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t)] \mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{\bar{w}}_t, \quad \rvx_T \sim p_{\textrm{prior}}(\rvx_T),   \label{eq:back}\\ 
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-\frac{1}{2}g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log \tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t)] \mathrm{d}t, \quad \rvx_T \sim p_{\textrm{prior}}(\rvx_T),\label{eq:backode}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{\bar{w}}_t$ denotes the reverse-time Wiener process, $\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log \tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t)$ is time-dependent score function, and $p_{\textrm{prior}}(\rvx_T)$ stands for the prior distribution of the reverse process, which is known to closely resembles a Gaussian distribution with the specific form of $\mathbf{f}$ and $g$~\cite{song2021scorebased,ho2020denoising}. To construct both reverse SDE and ODE, the diffusion model estimates a time-dependent score function $\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log \tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t) \approx \mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t)$ using a neural network with score-matching objective~\cite{vincent2011connection,song2019generative,song2020improved,song2021scorebased}. %in \cref{eq:vanilla_sm}.

From the perspective of representation learning, the reverse ODE in \cref{eq:backode} (a.k.a DDIM~\cite{song2021denoising} in discrete time diffusion formulation) provides a bidirectional encoding-decoding path between data $\rvx_0$ and latent $\rvx_T$ while $\rvx_T$ is set to follow the Gaussian distribution~\cite{song2021scorebased,ho2020denoising}. %under the specific form of $\mathbf{f}$ and $g$~\cite{song2020score,ho2020denoising}. %under the assumption of $T\rightarrow\infty$. 
This mapping is both invertible and uniquely identifiable up to discretization error, indicating that the encoding is entirely determined by the data distribution $q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0)$~\cite{song2021scorebased,song2021denoising}. However, the latent variable $\rvx_T$ provided by ODE in \cref{eq:backode} presents some disadvantages. 

\textit{\textbf{Over-regularization problem}}: From the perspective of encoding, the foremost problem of $\rvx_T$ is the rigidity of the assumed distribution, the Gaussian distribution, which can be regarded as the over-regularization. For instance, \cite{takahashi2019variational} identified that the Gaussian could be distant from the optimal prior distribution, so the paper sets aggregated posterior as optimal prior, making learnable encoding more informative. DDIM has this over-regularized prior of $\rvx_T$ that cannot be adapted via the encoding. 

Some previous literature, i.e. DiffAE~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion} and PDAE~\cite{zhang2022unsupervised}, proposes auxiliary latent space utilizing learnable encoder Enc$_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{l}$, which directly maps from data $\rvx_0$ to an auxiliary latent variable $\rvz$. Unlike DDIM, these approaches tractably obtain $\rvz$ from $\rvx_0$ without solving ODE, and both DiffAE and PDAE can directly learn the latent space in reduced dimensionality. 
Consequently, the reverse ODE in \cref{eq:zbackode} becomes associated with the $\rvz$-conditional score function $\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvx_t|\rvz)$. Meanwhile, these past approaches do not solve any aspects of over-regularization in $\rvx_T$. Additionally, they introduce a new problem of information split because of the auxiliary structure.
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-\frac{1}{2}g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvx_t|\rvz)] \mathrm{d}t.\label{eq:zbackode}
\end{align}
\textit{\textbf{Information split problem}}: The generative process in \cref{eq:zbackode} initiates with two latent variables $\rvz$ and $\rvx_T$. If the framework only relies on the tractably inferred latent variable $\rvz$, the reconstruction outcomes depicted in \Cref{fig:1b} appear to fluctuate depending on the choice of $\rvx_T$. This implies that $\rvx_T$ encompasses crucial information necessary for reconstructing $\rvx_0$. To consider all information of $\rvx_0$, it is necessary to infer $\rvx_T$ by solving ODE in \cref{eq:zbackode} from input $\rvx_0$ to endpoint $\rvx_T$, enduring its intractability. Consequently, the persisting issue within the latent variable $\rvx_T$ remains an unresolved challenge in this framework. 

Therefore, this paper intends to change this endpoint to be a learnable distribution that is generated from $\rvz$, so that the inferred distribution of $\rvx_T$ becomes adapted to optimize. Furthermore, we designed $\rvz$ to be an information bottleneck structure, encouraging all information to be compressed.
\subsection{Diffusion Process with Fixed Endpoints}
\label{sec:2.2}
To control the information regarding the diffusion endpoint $\rvx_T$, it is imperative to specify a forward SDE that terminates at the desired endpoint. We employ Doob's $h$-transform~\cite{doob1984classical,rogers2000diffusions,zhou2024denoising}, which facilitates the conversion of the original forward SDE in \cref{eq:for} into \cref{eq:doobfor} to terminate at a desired point (or distribution).
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\mathbf{y},T)]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_t, \quad \rvx_0 \sim q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0), \quad \rvx_T=\mathbf{y}  \label{eq:doobfor},
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\mathbf{y},T):=\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log{\tilde{q}_t(\rvx_T|\rvx_t)}|_{\rvx_T=\mathbf{y}}$ is the score function of perturbation kernel in original forward SDE, and $\mathbf{y}$ denotes the desired endpoint. Let $q_t(\rvx_t)$ denotes the marginal distribution of \cref{eq:doobfor} at $t$. It is noteworthy that when both $\rvx_0$ and $\rvx_T$ are given, the conditional probability of $\rvx_t$ becomes identical to the original forward SDE, i.e., $q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T,\rvx_0)=\tilde{q}_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T,\rvx_0)$~\cite{doob1984classical,zhou2024denoising}. In specific formulations of original forward SDE, such as VE and VP~\cite{song2021scorebased,ho2020denoising}; $q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T,\rvx_0)$ becomes Gaussian distribution, enabling tractable sampling of $\rvx_t$ at any time $t$ with exact density function. 
There also exists unique reverse-time SDE and ODE in \cref{eq:doobback,eq:doobbackode} corresponding to $h$-transformed forward SDE of \cref{eq:doobfor}~\cite{anderson1982reverse,zhou2024denoising}.
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\mathbf{y},T)] \mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{\bar{w}}_t, \rvx_T =\mathbf{y},   \label{eq:doobback}\\ 
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-\frac{1}{2}g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\mathbf{y},T)] \mathrm{d}t, \quad \rvx_T =\mathbf{y},\label{eq:doobbackode}
\end{align}
where $q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ be the conditional probability defined by \cref{eq:doobfor}. To construct reverse SDE and ODE, it is necessary to estimate $\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q_t(\rvx_t|\rvx_T) \approx \mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T)$ through the neural network with score matching objective~\cite{zhou2024denoising} in \cref{eq:bridge_sm}. 
\begin{align}
& \frac{1}{2}\int_0^T \mathbb{E}_{q_{t}(\rvx_t,\rvx_T)}[g^2(t)||\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T)-\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q_{t}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)||_2^2] \mathrm{d}t,\label{eq:bridge_sm} 
\end{align}


\section{Method}

To resolve \textit{information split problem} with learnable endpoint in diffusion-based representation learning~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised} and its subsequent applications~\cite{wang2023infodiffusion,yang2023disdiff,yue2024exploring}, we introduce Diffusion Bridge AutoEncoders (DBAE) featuring $\rvz$-dependent endpoint $\rvx_T$ inference using a single network propagation. \Cref{fig:2} illustrates the overall schematic for DBAE. \Cref{sec:3.1} explains the latent variable inference with the encoder-decoder structure and a forward SDE utilizing Doob's $h$-transform. \Cref{sec:3.2} delineates the generative process from the information bottleneck $\rvz$ to data $\rvx_0$. \Cref{sec:3.3} elaborates on the objective function for reconstruction, unconditional generation, and its theoretical justifications. 
\subsection{Latent Variable Inference}
\label{sec:3.1}
We can access i.i.d. samples from $q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0)$. The encoder Enc$_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}:\mathbb{R}^d\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^l$ maps from data $\rvx_0$ to latent variable $\rvz$, defining the conditional probability $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvz|\rvx_0)$. To condense the high-level representation of $\rvx_0$, the latent dimension $l$ is set to be lower than the data dimension $d$. The decoder Dec$_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}^l\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^d$ maps from latent variable $\rvz$ to the endpoint $\rvx_T$, defining the conditional probability $q_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)$. Since the decoder generates endpoint $\rvx_T$ solely based on the latent variable $\rvz$, $\rvz$ becomes a bottleneck of all the information of $\rvx_0$. The encoder-decoder structure provides the endpoint distribution $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0)=\int{q_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvz|\rvx_0)}\mathrm{d}\rvz$ for the given starting point $\rvx_0$. We now discuss a new diffusion process $\left\{\rvx_t\right\}_{t=0}^{T}$ with a given starting and endpoint pair. Here, it should be noted that DBAE has a unique feature of pairing an original data $\rvx_0$ with its reconstructed endpoint $\rvx_T$ from $\rvz$ unlike the other models utilizing just a noise sample from $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$. Because this pairing is solely controlled by $\rvz$, the endpoint has to be optimized, not static as before; so $\rvx_T$ becomes the optimal prior from the diffusion model perspective.

We define a forward SDE  in \cref{eq:doobours} utilizing Doob's $h$-transform~\cite{doob1984classical,rogers2000diffusions,zhou2024denoising} to establish the relationship between the starting point and endpoint given by the encoder-decoder. The forward SDE in \cref{eq:doobours} determines the distribution of $\rvx_{t}$, where $t\in(0,T)$. Let the marginal distribution of \cref{eq:doobours} at time $t$ as $q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t)$.
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T,T)]\mathrm{d}t + g(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{w}_t, \quad \rvx_0 \sim q_{\textrm{data}}(\rvx_0),\, \rvx_T \sim q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0)  \label{eq:doobours},
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T, T):=\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log{\tilde{q}_t(\rvx_T|\rvx_t)}$ is the score function of perturbation kernel in the original forward SDE in \cref{eq:for}.
\subsection{Generative Process}
The generative process begins with the bottleneck latent variable $\rvz$, which can be inferred from input data $\rvx_0$ or randomly drawn from the prior distribution $p_{\text{prior}}(\rvz)$. The decoder Dec$_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}^l\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^d$ maps from latent variable $\rvz$ to the endpoint $\rvx_T$ with the probability $p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)$.\footnote{The two distributions $p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)$ and $q_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)$ are the same. However, to distinguish between inference and generation, they are respectively denoted as $p$ and $q$.} The reverse ODE corresponding to \cref{eq:doobours}, as described in \cref{eq:backodeours} regenerate the data $\rvx_0$. 
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-\frac{1}{2}g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T,T)] \mathrm{d}t,\label{eq:backodeours}
\end{align}
where the conditional probability $q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ is defined by \cref{eq:doobours}. However, computing the conditional probability   $q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ is intractable, so we parameterize our score model $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T):=\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log p^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ to approximate $\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$.
Our parametrized generative process becomes \cref{eq:backodeoursmodel}. Stochastic sampling with SDE is also naturally possible as shown in \Cref{sec:2.2}, but we describe only ODE for convenience.
\begin{align}
& \mathrm{d}\rvx_t = [\mathbf{f}(\rvx_t, t)-\frac{1}{2}g^2(t)\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log p^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)+g^2(t)\mathbf{h}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T,T)] \mathrm{d}t.\label{eq:backodeoursmodel}
\end{align}
\label{sec:3.2}
\subsection{Objective Function}
\label{sec:3.3}
The objective function bifurcates depending on the tasks. For downstream inference, attribute manipulation, and interpolation, the model merely requires reconstruction capability. It is adequate to approximate $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T) \approx \nabla_{\rvx_t} \log q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ and unique $\rvx_T$ encodings according to each data point $\rvx_0$. For an unconditional generation, the model must possess the ability to generate random samples from the endpoint $\rvx_T$, implying that the generative endpoint distribution $p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T)=\int{p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)p_{\text{prior}}(\rvz)}\mathrm{d}\rvz$ should closely resemble the aggregated inferred distribution $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T)=\int{q_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvz|\rvx_0)q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0)}\mathrm{d}\rvx_0\mathrm{d}\rvz$.
\subsubsection{Reconstruction} 
\label{sec:3.3.1}
For successful reconstruction, the model needs to fulfill two criteria: 1) encoding the latent variable $\rvx_T$ uniquely depending on the data point $\rvx_0$, and 2) regenerating from $\rvx_T$ to $\rvx_0$. The inferred latent distribution $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0)$ should provide unique information for each $\rvx_0$. To achieve this, we aim to minimize the entropy $\mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0))$ to embed $\rvx_0$-dependent $\rvx_T$ with minimum uncertainty. On the other hands, we maximizes the entropy $\mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T))$ to embed different $\rvx_T$ for each $\rvx_0$. Since posterior entropy $\mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_0|\rvx_T))=\mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0))-\mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T))+\mathcal{H}(q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0))$ naturally includes aforementioned terms, we use this term as a regularization. Minimizing the gap between the \cref{eq:backodeours,eq:backodeoursmodel} is necessary for regenerating from $\rvx_T$ to $\rvx_0$. This requires alignment between the inferred score function $\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log q^{t}_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ and the model score function $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T)$. Accordingly, we propose the score-matching objective function $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM}}$ as described in \cref{eq:obj_ae}, similarly as \cref{eq:bridge_sm}.  We train DBAE with the entropy-regularized score matching objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}}$ as shown in \cref{eq:obj_dae}. 
\begin{align}
&\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{SM}}:= \frac{1}{2}\int_0^T{\mathbb{E}_{q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t,\rvx_T)}[g^2(t)|| \mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T) - \nabla_{\rvx_t}\log q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)||_2^2]}\mathrm{d}t  \label{eq:obj_ae} \\
&\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}:= \mathcal{L}_{\textrm{SM}} + \mathcal{H}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_0|\rvx_T)) \label{eq:obj_dae}
\end{align}
\Cref{thm:1} demonstrates that the entropy-regularized score matching objective in $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}$ becomes a tractable form of objective, and it is equivalent to the reconstruction objective. The inference distribution $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t,\rvx_T|\rvx_0)$ is optimized to provide the best information about $\rvx_0$ for easy reconstruction.
\begin{restatable}{theorem}{thma}
   For the objective function $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}$, the following equality holds.
   \begin{equation}
       \mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_0^T{\mathbb{E}_{q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_0,\rvx_t,\rvx_T)}[g^2(t)|| \mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T) - \nabla_{\rvx_t}\log \tilde{q}_{t}(\rvx_t|\rvx_0,\rvx_T)||_2^2]}\mathrm{d}t
   \end{equation}
   Moreover, if \cref{eq:for} is linear SDE, there exists $\alpha(t)$, $\beta(t)$, $\gamma(t)$, $\lambda(t)$, such that
   \begin{equation}
   \mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_0^T{\mathbb{E}_{q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_0,\rvx_t,\rvx_T)}[\lambda(t)|| \mathbf{x}^{0}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T) - \rvx_0||_2^2]}\mathrm{d}t,
   \end{equation}
   where $\mathbf{x}^{0}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T):=\alpha(t)\rvx_t+\beta(t)\rvx_T+\gamma(t)\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_t,t,\rvx_T)$.
\label{thm:1}
\end{restatable}
The assumption and proof of \Cref{thm:1} is in \Cref{sec:A1.1}. The $\boldsymbol{\theta}$-related term in $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}$ is solely $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{SM}}$, naturally leading to the optimality specified in \Cref{cor:1}.
\begin{restatable}{corollary}{cora}
   Let $\boldsymbol{\theta}^*=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}}$. Then $\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^*}(\rvx_t, t, \rvx_T)=\nabla_{\rvx_t}\log q^t_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_t|\rvx_T)$ for all $\rvx_t$, $t$, $\rvx_T$.
\label{cor:1}
\end{restatable}
\subsubsection{Generative Modeling}
\label{sec:3.3.2}
In the \Cref{sec:3.3.1}, the discussion focused on the objective function for reconstruction. For the generative modeling, the distribution of $\rvx_T$ should be considered. This section addresses the discrepancy between the inferred distribution $q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T)$ and generative prior distribution $p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T)$. To address this, we propose the objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PR}}$ related to the generative prior.  
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{PR}}:=&\mathbb{E}_{q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0)}[D_{\text{KL}}(q_{\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvx_0)||p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T))] \label{eq:prior}
\end{align}
\Cref{thm:2} demonstrates that the autoencoding objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}}$ and prior objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PR}}$ bound the  Kullback-Leibler divergence between data distribution $q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0)$ and the generative model distribution $p_{\boldsymbol{\psi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_0)=\int{p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_0|\rvx_T)p_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvx_T|\rvz)p_{\text{prior}}(\rvz)}\mathrm{d}\rvz\mathrm{d}\rvx_T$ up to a constant. The proof is in \Cref{sec:A1.2}
\begin{restatable}{theorem}{thmb}  
$D_{\textrm{KL}}(q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0)||p_{\boldsymbol{\psi},\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\rvx_0))\leq \mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}+\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{PR}}-\mathcal{H}(q_{\text{data}}(\rvx_0))$.
\label{thm:2}
\end{restatable}
For generative modeling, we separately optimize the terms $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{PR}}$, following \cite{esser2021taming,preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised}. The separate training of the generative prior distribution with a powerful generative model is effective in reducing the mismatch between the prior and the aggregated posterior~\cite{sinha2021d2c,aneja2021contrastive}. Initially, we optimize $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{AE}}$ with respect to the parameters of encoder ($\boldsymbol{\phi}$), decoder ($\boldsymbol{\psi}$), and score network ($\boldsymbol{\theta}$), and fix the parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}$. Subsequently, we newly parameterize the generative prior $p_{\text{prior}}(\rvz):=p_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(\rvz)$ using shallow latent diffusion models, and optimize $\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{PR}}$ w.r.t $\boldsymbol{\omega}$. See \Cref{sec:A1.3} for further details.



\section{Experiment}

\label{sec:4}
This section empirically validates the effectiveness of the intended design of the proposed model, DBAE. To compare DBAE with previous diffusion-based representation learning~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised,yue2024exploring}, we follow the previous experimental settings of DiffAE~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion} as much as possible. We utilize the same encoder as well as score model architecture as described in \Cref{sec:A3}. We quantify both latent inference quality and generation quality through various tasks. We quantitatively evaluate the performance of downstream inference, reconstruction, disentanglement, and unconditional generation. We also show interpolation and attribute manipulation ability, qualitatively. We conducted experiments by dividing the encoder of the proposed model into two variations: 1) using a Gaussian stochastic encoder (DBAE) and 2) using a deterministic encoder (DBAE-d) for ablation studies. Task-specific detailed configurations and metric descriptions are elaborated in \Cref{sec:A3}.

\subsection{Downstream Inference}

To examine the quality of latent representation in the tractable latent variable $\rvz$, we perform a linear-probe attribute prediction following DiTi~\cite{yue2024exploring}. We train DBAE with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{AE}}$ on CelebA~\cite{liu2015deep} training dataset and FFHQ~\cite{karras2019style} dataset. For evaluation, we train a linear classifier on CelebA training set with 40 binary labels and examine CelebA test set using the Average Precision (AP) metric. Additionally, we evaluate using LFW~\cite{huang2007unsupervised,kumar2009attribute} dataset, which is designed for attribute regression, and we measure the Pearson correlation coefficient (Pearson's r) and MSE. \Cref{tab:main1} shows that DBAE outperforms other diffusion-based representation learning baselines~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised,yue2024exploring}. Since DiffAE~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion}, PDAE~\cite{zhang2022unsupervised}, and DiTi~\cite{yue2024exploring} suffer from \textit{information split problem}, they induce $\rvz$ less informative compared to DBAE. \Cref{fig:3} shows statistics for 100 reconstructions of the same image with inferred $\rvz$. Since PDAE's reconstruction varies depending on the selection of $\rvx_T$, it indicates that the intricacies of hair and facial features are contained in $\rvx_T$, which $\rvz$ misses. This aligns with \Cref{fig:app_0} in \Cref{sec:A4}, there is a significant performance gain of the attributes related to facial details, e.g. shadow, hair, etc. Comparing DBAE-d with DBAE reveals ablations study about the encoder's stochasticity, and the stochastic encoder exhibits slightly better. We conjecture that the stochastic encoder leverages a broader space, which has the benefits of discriminative downstream inference.

Unsupervised disentanglement of the latent variable $\rvz$ is an important application of generative representation learning, enabling controllable generation without supervision. \subsection{Interpolation}
\label{sec:4.5}
For the two images $\rvx_0^1$ and $\rvx_0^2$, DBAE can mix the styles by exploring the intermediate points in the latent space. We encode images into $\rvz^1=$Enc$_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvx_0^1)$ and $\rvz^2=$Enc$_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\rvx_0^2)$. We then regenerate from $\rvz^{\lambda}= \lambda\rvz^1 + (1-\lambda)\rvz^2$ to data $\rvx_0$ using the generative process specified in \cref{eq:backodeoursmodel}. The unique properties of DBAE offer distinct benefits here: 1) DiffAE~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion} and PDAE~\cite{zhang2022unsupervised} need to infer $\rvx_T^1$, $\rvx_T^2$ by solving the ODE in \cref{eq:zbackode} with hundreds of score function evaluations~\cite{preechakul2022diffusion,zhang2022unsupervised}. They then geometrically interpolate between $\rvx_T^1$ and $\rvx_T^2$ to obtain $\rvx_T^{\lambda}$, regardless of the correspondence between $\rvz^{\lambda}$ and $\rvx_T^{\lambda}$. 2) DBAE directly obtains an intermediate value of $\rvx_T^{\lambda}=$Dec$_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(\rvz^{\lambda})$. This does not require solving the ODE, and the correspondence between $\rvx_T^{\lambda}$ and $\rvz^{\lambda}$ is also naturally determined by the decoder ($\boldsymbol{\psi}$). \Cref{fig:6a} shows the interpolation results on the LSUN Horse, Bedroom~\cite{yu15lsun} and FFHQ datasets. The top row shows the corresponding endpoints $\rvx_T^{\lambda}$ in the interpolation, which changes smoothly between $\rvx_T^{1}$ and $\rvx_T^{2}$. The bottom row shows the interpolation results on FFHQ, which smoothly changes semantic information such as gender, glasses, and hair color.

\section{Conclusion}

\label{sec:5}
In this paper, we identify the \textit{over-regularization problem} and the \textit{information split problem} in diffusion-based representation learning, stemming from the fixed endpoint distribution $\rvx_T$. These issues hinder the representation capabilities of the tractable latent variable $\rvz$. The proposed method, Diffusion Bridge AutoEncoders, systematically addresses these challenges by constructing $\rvz$-dependent endpoint $\rvx_T$ inference. By transforming $\rvz$ into an information bottleneck, DBAE is able to extract more meaningful embeddings within the tractable latent space. The notable enhancements in the embedding quality of DBAE improve downstream inference, as well as image manipulation applications. This work lays a solid foundation for further exploration into effective representation learning in learnable diffusion inference. The usages like deep fake can potentially risk of this work.
