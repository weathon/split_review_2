\title{WizardMath: Empowering Mathematical Reasoning for Large Language Models via Reinforced Evol-Instruct}

\begin{abstract}

Large language models (LLMs), such as GPT-4, have shown remarkable performance in natural language processing (NLP) tasks, including challenging mathematical reasoning. However, most existing open-source models are only pre-trained on large-scale internet data and without math-related optimization. In this paper, we present \modelname{}, which enhances the mathematical reasoning abilities of Llama-2, by applying our proposed \REInameF{} (\textbf{\REInameS{}}) method to the domain of math. Through extensive experiments on two mathematical reasoning benchmarks, namely GSM8k and MATH, we reveal the extraordinary capabilities of our model. \modelname{} surpasses all other open-source LLMs by a substantial margin. Furthermore, our model even outperforms ChatGPT-3.5, Claude Instant-1, PaLM-2 and Minerva on GSM8k, simultaneously surpasses Text-davinci-002,  PaLM-1 and GPT-3 on MATH.} and \url{https://huggingface.co/WizardLM}.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


Recently, Large-scale language models (LLMs) have  garnered significant attention and become the go-to approach for numerous natural language processing (NLP) tasks, including open domain conversation~\citep{brown2020language,ouyang2022training,openai2023gpt4,touvron2023llama}, coding~\citep{chen2021evaluating-humaneval,Azure,PaLM,codegen,codet5,CodeT5+,CodeGeeX,li2023starcoder,luo2023wizardcoder} and math~\citep{taylor2022galactica,lewkowycz2022solving,yuan2023RFT,zheng2023php,imani2023mathprompter,wang2023planandsolve-ps}. A conspicuous example is ChatGPT, developed by OpenAI. This model uses extensive pre-training on large-scale internet data and further fine-tuning with specific instruction data and methods. As a result, it achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on various benchmarks. Subsequently, Anthropic,  Google, and Meta  also launched their competitive products one after another. Notably, Meta's series of  Llama~\cite{touvron2023llama, touvron2023llama2} models have sparked an open-source revolution and quickly narrowed the gap with those closed-source LLMs. This trend also gradually stimulates the releases of MPT\footref{fn:mpt}, Falcon~\cite{penedo2023falcon}, StarCoder~\cite{li2023starcoder}, Alpaca~\cite{alpaca}, Vicuna~\cite{vicuna2023}, and WizardLM~\cite{xu2023wizardlm}, etc. However, these open models still struggles with the scenarios which require  complex multi-step  quantitative reasoning, such as solving mathematical and science challenges~\cite{lu2023survey-mathsurvey, frieder2023mathematical, bhattacharya2017mathsurvey, wang-etal-2017-deep-mathsurvey,ling2017program-aqua,koncel-kedziorski-etal-2016-mawps,wei2022chain,kojima2022large-zeroshot-cot,zhang2022automatic-cot,wang2023selfconsistency,fu2023complexity-cot-based}.

 Chain-of-thought (CoT)~\citep{wei2022chain} proposes to design better prompts to  generate step-by-step solutions, which can lead to improved performance. Self-Consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} also achieves remarkable performance on many reasoning benchmarks, which generates several possible answers from the model and selects the correct one based on majority vote~\citep{fu2023complexity-cot-based}. In recent, \cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step} finds that process supervision with reinforcement learning significantly outperforms outcome supervision for solving challenging MATH problems.
Inspired by \EIname{} and Process-supervised Reinforcement Learning, this work aims to enhance the  mathematical reasoning abilities of the SOTA open-source LLM, Llama-2~\cite{touvron2023llama2}. As shown in the Figure~\ref{fig:reinforcement_evol_instruct_pic}, we propose a new method named \REInameF{} (\textbf{\REInameS{}}), which could firstly generate diverse math instructions data by math-specific \EIname{},  then we train an instruction  reward model (IRM) and a process-supervised reward model (PRM)~\cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step,yuan2023rrhf,dong2023raft,bai2022constitutional-claude,uesato2022deepmind-orms,yuan2023RFT,creswell2022selectioninference}, the former indicates the quality of the evolved instruction and the later receives feedback for each step in the  solution. The brand-new \EIname{} method includes two downward  evolution and upward evolution progress to produce the grade school math and  challenging math respectively. Initially, we re-generate, filter and finetune the original math instruction data from GSM8k~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} and MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring}. Immediately, we train the Llama-2 models to obtain the reward models and our \modelname{}.
We perform experiments on two mathematical reasoning benchmarks, namely GSM8k~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} and MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring}, the results demonstrate that our \modelname{} outperforms all other open-source LLMs, achieving state-of-the-art performance. Specifically, \modelname{}  observe a substantial improvement in pass@1 with an increase of +24.8 (81.6. vs. 56.8) on GSM8k, and +9.2 (22.7 vs. 13.5) on MATH. Notably, our model even also significantly surpasses OpenAI's ChatGPT-3.5\footref{fn:chatgpt},  Anthropic’s Claude Instant-1~\cite{bai2022constitutional-claude}, and Google's PaLM-2~\cite{palm2} in terms of  pass@1 on GSM8k.
The main contributions of this work are as following:

\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce \modelname{} model, which enhances the mathematical reasoning abilities for open-source pretrained large language model Llama-2~\cite{touvron2023llama2}.
\item We propose a new method,  \REInameF{} (\textbf{\REInameS{}}), alongside \EIname{} and Reinforcement Learning, for improving LLM reasoning performance.
\item \modelname{} surpasses all other open-source LLMs by a substantial margin in terms of mathematical  reasoning, including Llama-2 70B~\cite{touvron2023llama2}, Llama-1 65B~\cite{touvron2023llama}, Falcon-40B~\cite{penedo2023falcon}, MPT-30B\footref{fn:mpt}, Baichuan-13B Chat\footref{fn:baichuan} and ChatGLM2 12B~\cite{GLM-130B} on both GSM8k~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} and MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring}.
\item \modelname{} significantly outperforms various main closed-source LLMs, such as ChatGPT\footref{fn:chatgpt}, GPT-3.5, Claude Instant~\cite{bai2022constitutional-claude}, PaLM-2~\cite{palm2}, PaLM-1~\cite{PaLM} and Minerva\cite{lewkowycz2022solving} on GSM8k.
\end{itemize}




\section{Method}



In this section, we elaborate on the details of our \modelname{}. Following WizardLM and PRMs\cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step}, we propose \REInameF{} (\textbf{\REInameS{}}), which integrates the \EIname{} and reinforced process supervision method to evolve GSM8k and MATH, and fine-tune the pre-trained Llama-2 with the evolved data and reward models.

As shown in the Figure ~\ref{fig:reinforcement_evol_instruct_pic}, our methods apply three steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Supervised fine-tuning.
\item Training instruction reward model, and process-supervised reward model.
\item Active Evol-Instruct, and PPO training.
\end{enumerate}
 
\subsection{Supervised fine-tuning}

Following InstructGPT\cite{ouyang2022training}, we also firstly fine tune the base with supervised instruction-response pairs, which contains:
\begin{enumerate}
\item To make the parsing of each step easier, we few-shot re-generate 15k answers for GSM8k and MATH with an Alpha version of WizardLM 70B model to produce solutions in a step-by-step format, then find out those with a correct answer, and use this data to finetune base Llama model.
\item To enhance the model’s ability to adhere to the neural and diverse instructions, we also sample 1.5k open-domain conversations from WizardLM's training data, then merge it with above math corpus as the final SFT training data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{\EIname{} principles for math}

Motivated by the Evol-Instruct~\cite{xu2023wizardlm} method proposed by WiazrdLM and its effective application on WizardCoder~\cite{luo2023wizardcoder}, this work attempts to make math instructions with various complexities and diversity to enhance the pre-trained LLMs. Specifically, we adapt Evol-Instruct to a new paradigm including two evolution lines:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Downward evolution: It enhances instructions by making the questions easier. For example i): revising high difficulty questions to lower difficulty, or ii) producing a new and easier question with another different topic.
\item Upward evolution:  Derived from original Evol-Instruct method, it deepens and generates new and harder questions by i) adding more constraints, ii) concretizing,  iii) increasing reasoning.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{\REInameF{} (\textbf{\REInameS{}})}
Inspired by InstructGPT\cite{ouyang2022training} and PRMs\cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step}, we train two reward models to predict the quality of the instructions and  the correctness of each step in the answer respectively:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Instruction Reward Model (IRM): This model aims to judge the quality of the evolved instructions on three aspects: i) Definition,  ii) Precision, and iii) Integrity. To produce the ranking list training data of IRM, for each instruction, we firstly use ChatGPT and Wizard-E~\footnote{\quad Wizard-E named Wizard-Evol-Generator, which is an Alpha version fine-tuned Llama model specifically used to execute \EIname{} without APIs.} to generate 2\textasciitilde4 evolved instructions respectively. Then we leverage Wizard-E to rank the quality of those 4\textasciitilde8 instructions.

\item Process-supervised Reward Model (PRM): As there is no powerful open-source math reasoning LLMs before this work, there is no simple way to support highly precise process supervision without professional human-labelers and close-source ChatGPT. Therefore,  we depend on ChatGPT to provide process supervision, and ask it to assess the correctness of each step in the solutions generated by our model.

\item PPO training. We evolve the original math (GSM8k + MATH) instructions by 8 turns, increasing the data size from 15k to 96k. We use IRM and PRM to generate the instruction reward  (${r^I}$) and the answer reward (${r^A}$). Then apply a product as the final reward $r = {r^I} \cdot {r^A}$.


\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Close-Source Models.} Numerous technology companies have effectively created exceptionally proficient Large Language Models (LLMs)~\cite{GPT3,openai2023gpt4,PaLM,palm2,Chinchilla,gopher,GLM-130B,touvron2023llama,opt,touvron2023llama2}, but have opted against making them publicly available, so they are referred to as close-source models. In our research, we extensively integrate a significant number of close-source models as the foundational benchmarks. Specifically, our baselines encompass the following models: (i) OpenAI's GPT-3 ~\cite{GPT3}, GPT-3.5, ChatGPT\footnote{\quad \url{https://openai.3\footnote{\quad \url{https://www.anthropic.anthropic.

\paragraph{Open-Source Models.} Massive open-source  LLMs~\cite{gopher,touvron2023llama,opt,touvron2023llama2,alpaca,penedo2023falcon,vicuna2023,GLM-130B} have been accessible to the AI community. Nonetheless, their performance consistently tends to significantly lag behind the close-source models. 
\subsection{Evaluate Benchmarks}
We mainly evaluate WizardMath on two benchmarks (GSM8k~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} and MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring}). The GSM8k~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} dataset contains approximately 7500 training data and 1319 test data, mainly on grade school level math problems, each of which consists of basic arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division), and generally requires 2 to 8 steps to solve. The MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring} dataset collects math problems from prestigious math competitions such as AMC 10, AMC 12, and AIME. It contains 7500 training data and 5,000 challenging test data in seven academic areas: Prealgebra, Algebra, Number Theory, Counting and Probability, Geometry, Intermediate Algebra, and Precalculus. Furthermore, these problems are divided into five levels of difficulty, with `1' denoting the relatively lower difficulty level and `5' indicating the highest level.

\section{Related Work}


\paragraph{Large Language Models.}  LLMs have achieved substantial advancements within the realm of Natural Language Processing (NLP), providing a valuable and task-agnostic foundation for widespread applications. These models typically encompass parameter counts reaching into the hundreds of billions, which are trained on extensive large-scale corpuses of textual data. The prominent instances entail OpenAI's GPT3\&4~\cite{GPT3,openai2023gpt4}, Anthropic's Claude\footref{fn:claude2}, Google's PaLM~\cite{PaLM,palm2}, Bard\footnote{\quad \url{https://bard.google. However none of them have been open-sourced so far, and some of them can only be exclusively accessible through APIs.

Recently, the AI landscape has borne witness to the emergence of numerous open-source LLMs, characterized by publicly accessible model codes and weight parameters. EleutherAI has contributed GPT-NeoX-20B~\cite{GPT-NeoX-20B} and GPT-J-6B~\cite{gpt-j}. BigScience has introduced BLOOM~\cite{workshop2023bloom}. Similarly, Meta has made strides by releasing OPT~\cite{opt}, Llama 1~\cite{touvron2023llama}, Llama 2~\cite{touvron2023llama2}, and GAL~\cite{taylor2022galactica}. Tsinghua University has unveiled GLM-130B and ChatGLM~\cite{GLM-130B}. TII has facilitated the release of Falcon~\cite{penedo2023falcon}. Additionally, LLMs such as Baichuan\footref{fn:baichuan} and Qwen\footref{fn:qwen} have also surfaced. Presently, Llama assumes a pivotal role as the foundational model for supervised fine-tuning, ushering in the emergence of several extremely remarkable models, including Alpaca~\cite{alpaca}, Vicuna~\cite{vicuna2023}, Guanaco~\cite{dettmers2023qlora}, WizardLM~\cite{xu2023wizardlm}, and Orca~\cite{mukherjee2023orca}, RFT~\cite{yuan2023RFT} etc.
\paragraph{Large Language Models For Mathematical reasoning.} It's well known that complex reasoning problems are  challenging for NLP models, which include mathematical reasoning~\cite{lu2023survey-mathsurvey, frieder2023mathematical, bhattacharya2017mathsurvey, wang-etal-2017-deep-mathsurvey,ling2017program-aqua,koncel-kedziorski-etal-2016-mawps}, common-sense reasoning~\cite{talmor-etal-2019-commonsenseqa,geva-etal-2021-aristotle}, and logical reasoning~\cite{wei2022chain}. A substantial body of current research is centered around the intricate task reasoning of the Mathematical Word Problems(MWP)~\cite{koncel-kedziorski-etal-2016-mawps,patel2021nlp-SVAMP,lan2021mwptoolkit,cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2, jie2022learning, yuan2023large,fu2023chainofthought-hub}, which requires the ability to understand mathematical concepts, computation and multi-step reasoning~\cite{zheng2023php,zhao2023modelselection,wang2023planandsolve-ps,imani2023mathprompter,yuan2023RFT,lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step,uesato2022deepmind-orms}. Addtitionly,  models are evaluated across different levels of MWP benchmarks on some mathematical reasoning datasets such as AddSub~\cite{hosseini-etal-2014-learning-addsub}, MultiArith~\cite{roy-roth-2015-solving-multiarith}, SingleEQ~\cite{koncel2015parsing-singleEQ}, SVAMP~\cite{patel2021nlp-SVAMP}, GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2}, AQuA~\cite{ling2017program-aqua} and MATH~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring}. 

To enhance the reasoning ability of LLMs, ~\cite{wei2022chain} proposed Chain-of-Thought Prompting, which attaches multiple reasoning steps before obtaining the answer for a question. By employing the simple few-shot reasoning strategy, LLMs are able to perform better in complex reasoning problems. Least-to-Most~\cite{Zhou2022LeasttoMostPE} prompting decomposes the problem into sub-problems that are then solved incrementally. Additionally each step has a more detailed reasoning process. Similarly, the Complex CoT~\cite{fu2023complexity-cot-based} underscores the pivotal role of prompt complexity by strategically choosing the most intricate problems and their corresponding solutions to function as prompts. To alleviate the burden of manual efforts, ~\cite{zhang2022automatic-cot} introduced Auto-CoT, an approach that automates the process of acquiring k samples through the application of clustering techniques on a provided dataset. With the objective of mitigating manual intervention, ~\cite{kojima2022large-zeroshot-cot} proposed Zero-shot-CoT, which entails the straightforward practice of appending the phrase "Let's think step by step" to each answer, eliciting the inference steps without examples. Moreover, ~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency} expanded upon this notion by suggesting the exploration of diverse inference paths throughout the reasoning process. Consequently, the ultimate outcome is determined through either the aggregation of answers using majority voting or by leveraging a validation mechanism, as posited by~\cite{li-etal-2023-making}. ~\cite{yuan2023RFT} employs a straightforward approach for  generating augmented samples, focusing on probing the correlation between LLMs and math reasoning ability.

\paragraph{Large Language Models For Reinforcement Learning.}  Nevertheless, even state-of-the-art models frequently manifest logical errors and a range of illusions~\cite{bubeck2023sparks, maynez2020faithfulness}. These anomalies become especially challenging within domains necessitating multi-step reasoning, where a singular logical misstep maybe precipitate the unraveling of an entire solution. An effective strategy involves the training of reward models aimed at discriminating between favorable and unfavorable outputs~\cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step}. Early outcome-based approaches were mainly performed on algorithmic tasks~\cite{graves2014neural,reed2016neural, Li2016NeuralPL, cai2017making}. ~\cite{cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2} demonstrated the significant benefits of reward models or validators, and ~\cite{li2022advance} proposed a heuristic-based step-size-aware RM.~\cite{ziegler2020finetuning,stiennon2022learning,nakano2022webgpt,ouyang2022training}  proposed the use of reward models for a reinforcement learning pipeline. ~\cite{nichols2020collaborative, shen2021generate,cobbe2021training_gsm8k_2,bai2022constitutional-claude,yuan2023rrhf, dong2023raft,song2023preference, touvron2023llama2} employed rejection sampling for searching to achieve alignment of LLMs with human preferences.

The differences between outcome-based and process-based reward modelling are further discussed by~\cite{uesato2022deepmind-orms}. Outcome-supervised reward models (ORMs) undergo training exclusively utilizing the ultimate outcomes derived from the model's chain-of-thought process. Conversely, process-supervised reward models (PRMs) are designed to solicit feedback for each individual step within the chain-of-thought progression. In the domain of logical reasoning, ORMs frequently employ incorrect reasoning pathways yet yield the correct final answer~\cite{zelikman2022star, creswell2022selectioninference}. Notably, PRMs has been demonstrated to effectively alleviate this phenomenon of inconsistent behavior~\cite{uesato2022deepmind-orms}. ~\cite{lightman2023openai-verify-step-by-step,Zhu_2023, ni2023learning} amassed an expansive corpus of process-based supervised signals through meticulous manual annotation, which verified that PRMs and supervision with manual annotation yielded more pronounced advantages for LLMs as compared to ORMs. 

\paragraph{Large Language Models For Instruction Fine-Tuning.} The initial endeavors in instruction-following training work primarily focused on enhancing the language model's capacity for generalization across diverse tasks. This often involves the process of fine-tuning across substantially available Natural Language Processing datasets, and evaluates on the different NLP tasks. T5~\cite{t5} undertake the earliest attempts to train a range of NLP tasks, including Question and Answer, Document Summarization, and Sentiment Classification, by employing a consistent prompt format across all the data. Subsequently, instruction fine-tuning work such as FLAN~\cite{longpre2023flan}, ExT5~\cite{ExT5}, T0~\cite{T0}, UnifiedQA~\cite{UnifiedQA}, ZeroPrompt~\cite{ZeroPrompt}, and FLAN-T5~\cite{flan-t5} emerged to adapt for a large number of downstream tasks. To address the challenge of misalignment between model outputs and human requirements, OpenAI manually annotates the instruction library to construct a diverse range of tasks. Simultaneously, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback technology is employed, which facilitate the rapid development of LLMs such as InstructGPT~\cite{ouyang2022training}, ChatGPT\footref{fn:chatgpt}, GPT-4~\cite{openai2023gpt4}. To reduce manual involvement, self-instruct~\cite{wang2022self} improves instruction-following through self-generated instructions. Alpaca~\cite{alpaca} used a dataset of 50k instructions generated from a limited (e.g., 175
samples) seed set of manually-written instructions. Vicuna~\cite{vicuna2023} used 70k user-shared conversations with ChatGPT collected from ShareGPT.com. Meanwhile, WizardLM~\cite{xu2023wizardlm} introduces the evol-instruct approach, which seeks to refine the existing instruction data by enhancing both its complexity and diversity.


\section{Conclusion and Future Work}


This paper introduces \modelname{}, a mathematics model fine-tuned with \textbf{\REInameS{}}. The experimental results demonstrate that \modelname{} achieves SOTA performance surpassing all existing open-source LLMs on two widely recognized mathematical reasoning benchmarks: GSM8k and MATH. Furthermore, \modelname{} exhibits superior performance compared to some of the largest close-source LLMs, including ChatGPT, GPT-3.5, Claude Instant, PaLM-2, PaLM-1 and Minerva on the GSM8k benchmark.

\paragraph{Future Work.} Although our \modelname{} achieves impressive mathematics performance, as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:gsm8k_baselines}, our model still falls significantly behind the SOTA LLM, GPT-4 and Claude-2. Therefore, future work will prioritize the enhancement of the \textbf{\REInameS{}} or better method to further augment the performance of our model.

\paragraph{Broader Impact.} Similar to the other LLMs, our \modelname{} could also generate unethical, harmful, or misleading information sometimes. Therefore, future research to address the ethical and societal implications is needed.
