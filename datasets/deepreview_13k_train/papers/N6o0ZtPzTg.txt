\title{Query-Dependent Prompt Evaluation and Optimization with Offline Inverse RL}

\begin{abstract}
In this study, we aim to enhance the arithmetic reasoning ability of Large Language Models (LLMs) through zero-shot prompt optimization. We identify a previously overlooked objective of query dependency in such optimization and elucidate two ensuing challenges that impede the successful and economical design of prompt optimization techniques. One primary issue is the absence of an effective method to evaluate prompts during inference when the golden answer is unavailable. Concurrently, learning via interactions with the LLMs to navigate the expansive natural language prompting space proves to be resource-intensive.
To address this, we introduce Prompt-OIRL, which harnesses offline inverse reinforcement learning to draw insights from offline prompting demonstration data. Such data exists as by-products when diverse prompts are benchmarked on open-accessible datasets. With Prompt-OIRL, the query-dependent prompt optimization objective is achieved by first learning an offline reward model. This model can evaluate any query-prompt pairs without accessing LLMs. Subsequently, a best-of-N strategy is deployed to recommend the optimal prompt. Our experimental evaluations across various LLM scales and arithmetic reasoning datasets underscore both the efficacy and economic viability of the proposed approach.


\end{abstract}

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT~\citep{ouyang2022training}, underscore the value of aligning with human preferences~\citep{ouyang2022training,openai2023gpt}. Using an offline alignment dataset generated by annotators, the harmlessness, helpfulness, and ability to follow instructions of LLMs can be largely enhanced~\citep{bai2022training} through the technique of RLHF~\citep{NIPS2017_deeprlfromhumanpreference}. 
That said, even for state-of-the-art general-purpose LLMs such as GPT-4, solving intricate problems such as \textit{arithmetic reasoning} can still be challenging. 
Further unlocking LLMs' full potential for those complex tasks remains an open challenge.

\textbf{Query-Dependent Zero-Shot Prompting for Arithmetic Reasoning: Opportunities and Challenges.} Out of the many attempts, \textit{prompting} --- a natural language prefix or instruction that explains how to complete the task --- stands out as a lightweight promising solution for eliciting the capabilities of LLMs without model parameter tuning~\citep{wei2022chain}. 
While the advances in zero-shot prompting strategies~\citep{kojima2022large} highlight the potential in finding effective query-independent solutions, its reliance on manual crafting efforts and the vast search space over natural language intensifies the difficulty in discovering effective prompts~\citep{deng2022rlprompt}. 
This study aims to augment the arithmetic reasoning capacities of LLMs, a capability considered crucial~\citep{2023opencompass,liu2023mmbench}. While there is a wealth of both expert-devised and machine-generated prompts in this domain~\citep{kojima2022large,zhou2022large,yang2023large}, our endeavor is to advance further via prompt optimization. Our approach begins with the articulation of an adjusted objective, subsequently highlighting two inherent challenges.
\textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Adjusted Objective: Query-Dependent Prompt Optimization.}} In the literature, research on zero-shot prompting mainly focused on finding prompts that work better on a distributional level rather than an instance level. For instance, while multi-agent debate \citet{liang2023encouraging,du2023improving} can improve reasoning in general, there are cases where not using a prompt can be even better than using generally effective prompts. Figure~\ref{fig:motiv_example} showcases a motivating example.
Motivated by the fact that no prompt is perfect for all queries, in this work, we set an adjusted objective of query-dependent prompt optimization, rather than a distributional-level prompt optimization.

\textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Challenge 1: Inference Time Evaluation is Hard.}} A primary challenge arises in evaluating the effectiveness of prompts during inference, especially when the true answer to a query is not known a priori.
For arithmetic reasoning, enumerating many \textit{potentially effective} prompts and enquire language models with those prompted queries will only result in a batch of answers, yet determining which response is correct in the absence of a ground-truth label requires extra non-trivial effort. Additionally, the computational cost associated with this prompt enumeration process is substantial.

\textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Challenge 2: Online Prompt Evaluation and Optimization is Expensive.}}
On the other hand, searching for \textit{potentially effective} prompts heavily relies on expensive online evaluation. In the literature, evaluating the effectiveness of a proposed prompt requires assessing its performance on multiple datasets and LLMs, to show its \textit{distributional} superiority over the others. In the arithmetic reasoning task, using the GPT-3.5-turbo API~\citep{openai2023gpt} to evaluate a single prompt on a medium-sized dataset with 10k query-answer pairs will lead to an $\approx$\$1 cost, yet learning from trials and errors often requires millions of interactions even for tasks with a few actions to choose from~\citep{schulman2017proximal}. The vast action space of natural language further increases the prohibitive cost for such interactive search~\citep{zhang2022tempera,deng2022rlprompt}.



\textbf{Solution: Query-Dependent Prompt Evaluation and Optimization with Offline Inverse RL.}
Having witnessed the significant advancements achieved in expert-crafted and algorithm-discovered prompting in arithmetic reasoning~\citep{zhou2022large,pryzant2023automatic,kojima2022large}, our work seeks a solution of combining existing human knowledge in prompting systematically, effectively, and cost-efficiently. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Formally, we identify the overlooked query-dependent prompt optimization objective and its challenges, and introduce Offline Inverse Reinforcement Learning to integrate rich human expertise as a systematic approach.
    \item Methodologically, we introduce Prompt-OIRL to first perform a query-dependent offline prompt evaluation with a learned reward model and then perform offline prompt optimization to improve prompting performance.
    \item Practically, we highlight the existence of offline datasets generated --- as by-products when existing prompting strategies are benchmarked on open-access tasks --- can be directly adopted for Prompt-OIRL learning. 
    \item Empirically, we validate the \textit{efficacy} and \textit{efficiency} of Prompt-OIRL in offline prompt evaluation and optimization through experiments with 3 distinct LLMs, namely GPT-3.5-turbo, LLaMA-2-7B-Chat, and TigerBot-13B-Chat, across 3 arithmetic datasets: GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021training}, MAWPS~\citep{roy2016solving}, and SVAMP\citep{patel2021nlp}
\end{itemize}
    





\section{The Query-Dependent Prompting Problem}

We first introduce preliminaries to provide a formal definition of the query-dependent prompting problem. We then introduce our solution in the next section.

\textbf{Queries \& Answers}~~
We consider the task of answering queries $x\in\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{V}^{\infty}$ expressed in a natural language with vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. We assume that each query $x$ has an expected answer $y^*\in\mathcal{Y}$. Such an answer can be a real-valued number or composed by natural language. For example, $x$ can be an arithmetic question and $y^*$ the mathematically correct answer to the question, or $x$ can be the review of a movie and $y^*$ a sentiment label for a movie. We denote a dataset containing queries and answers with $\mathcal{D}$.

\textbf{Language Model}~~
Such tasks can be performed using a language model $\ell:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}$ by feeding queries $x$ to the language model and getting answers $\hat{y} = \ell(x)$.
We assume that the quality of these answers can be evaluated using a metric $r:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$. For example, in tasks where golden labels are available, this metric can be $r(y^*,\hat{y})=\mathbbm{1}\{\hat{y}=y^*\}$. Generally, a higher value of $r(y^*,\hat{y})$ indicates $\hat{y}$ matches the expected answer $y^*$ better.

\textbf{Prompting}~~
It is well known that the performance of a language model in answering queries can be boosted through appropriate prompting---a natural language prefix or instruction that explains how to complete the task. We consider prompts $\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}$ that maps original queries $x$ to modified \textit{prompted query} $\pi(x)$. (For clarity, we use \textbf{prompt} to denote the prefix and instructions, and \textbf{prompted query} to denote the final input to language models after prompting.) These prompted queries are then fed into the language model to get better answers $\hat{y} = \ell(\pi(x))$.
\textbf{Objective}~~
Given a dataset $\mathcal{D}=\{x^{(i)},y^{*(i)})\}_{i\in[N]}$ of queries and their expected answers, the objective of \textit{query-agnostic} zero-shot prompt optimization usually studied in the literature is to find the \textit{distributional} optimal prompt $\bar{\pi}^*$ that maximize the \textit{expected} quality of answers w.r.t. metric $r$:
\begin{align}
\label{eqn:obj_dist}
    \bar{\pi}^* = \arg\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{(x^{(i)},y^{*(i)})\sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ r(y^{*(i)},\ell(\pi(x^{(i)}))) \right],
\end{align}
Instead, in this work we meet the \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Adjusted Objective}} by using a query-dependent approach:
\begin{align}
\label{eqn:obj}
    \pi^* = \arg\max_{\pi} r(y^{*(i)},\ell(\pi(x^{(i)}))),
\end{align}
In a nutshell, Equation(\ref{eqn:obj_dist}) seeks a single prompt that achieves good performance on the dataset, yet Equation(\ref{eqn:obj}) seeks different prompts for different queries in the dataset. Clearly, $\pi^*$ should be better than $\bar{\pi}^*$ in the sense 
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{(x^{(i)},y^{*(i)})\sim \mathcal{D}}\left[ r(y^{*(i)},\ell(\pi^*(x^{(i)}))) \right] \ge \mathbb{E}_{(x^{(i)},y^{*(i)})\sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ r(y^{*(i)},\ell(\bar{\pi}^*(x^{(i)}))) \right].    
\end{equation}

Having established the learning objective in Equation~(\ref{eqn:obj}), applying RL to the search for an optimal prompt $\pi$ may seem a natural approach. Yet, as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:main_pic_prompt_rl}, such a method confronts two distinct challenges: \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Challenge 1}} pertains to the inability to compute rewards during inference, while \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Challenge 2}} concerns the necessity for extensive interactions with LLMs. 
Different from conventional RL tasks on which algorithms are developed and optimized, the vast action space of vocabulary, sparse trajectory level feedback that is only available when the final answer is generated, and the significant overheads associated with LLM interactions, all complicate the pursuit of an ideal prompt. Furthermore, the absence of definitive labels during the inference phase renders the prompt evaluation phase infeasible. With this context, we now present Prompt-OIRL as our proposed solution.


\paragraph{Prompt-OIRL: Prompting as Offline Inverse RL}
In pursuance of the new objective and addressing the challenges, we draw inspiration from the successes that have been achieved by RLHF. We highlight the prompting problem is essentially aligning prompts based on LLM preferences, a departure from RLHF's goal of aligning LLMs with human preferences.

Aiming for an efficient and economical prompting strategy, we sidestep the complexities of extensive LLM interactions by leveraging readily available datasets and propose offline IRL as a viable learning solution. We outline its three fundamental steps: \textbf{Step 1.} construction of offline dataset, \textbf{Step 2.} offline query-dependent prompt evaluation through reward modeling, and \textbf{Step 3.} offline query-dependent prompt optimization using the learned reward model.


\paragraph{Step 1: Existence of Offline Prompt Demonstrations}
We start by emphasizing the presence and significance of prompt demonstrations used in benchmarking existing prompts. In the realm of research dedicated to improving the arithmetic reasoning capacities of LLMs, several zero-shot prompts have been proposed. Notable examples include CoT~\citep{kojima2022large}, APE~\citep{zhou2022large}, and a concurrent work OPRO~\citep{yang2023large}, among others.

It's important to highlight that none of these prompts uniformly enhance the LLMs' arithmetic reasoning capabilities. Their efficacy is assessed at a \textit{distributional level}, as per Equation(\ref{eqn:obj_dist}). Different language models might exhibit varied preferences for prompts~\citep{yang2023large}.

To benchmark the effectiveness of the proposed prompts, prior researchers have utilized standardized, openly accessible arithmetic reasoning datasets and reported the overall success rate of obtaining correct answers. Denoting different existing prompts with superscripts, e.g.,
$\pi^{(1)}(x)=(x)$ the no prompting, $\pi^{(2)}=\text{CoT prompting}$, $\pi^{(3)}=\text{APE prompting}$, and so on --- 
when there are $K$ prompts have been evaluated, the following demonstrations will be constructed as by-products:
\begin{align}
\label{eqn:dataset}
    \mathcal{D}^\ell_{\text{dem}} = \{x^{(i)},\pi^{(k)},r^{(i,k)}=r(y^{*(i)},\ell(\pi^{(k)}(x^{(i)}))\}_{i\in[N],k\in[K]}
\end{align}
This process is visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:main_pic_prompt_offlinerl}, depicting the generation of prompt-alignment demonstration datasets from evaluating existing prompts. Notably, the rewards in $\mathcal{D}^\ell_{\text{dem}}$ are influenced by $\ell$.
\paragraph{Step 2: Offline Reward Modeling: Inverse RL without an Environment}
Upon initial inspection, it might seem that reward modeling is unnecessary given the reward metric. i.e., $r(y^*,\hat{y})=\mathbbm{1}\{\hat{y}=y^*\}$. However, a more thorough examination of this metric reveals two concerns that limit its broader utilization: first, the reward metric is a function over language model $\ell$, making every single call of such a metric costly; second, the computation of the reward necessitates access to the ground truth answer $y^*$, which is always not available in inference (deployment) time. Those are essentially the challenges associated with the input dependency of this reward metric demonstrated in Figure~\ref{fig:main_pic_prompt_rl}.
To address those issues, we introduce a parameterized \textit{proxy reward model} that is a function of query $x$ and prompt $\pi$. We denote the proxy reward as $\Upsilon_\theta(x,\pi(x))$ with parameters $\theta$. Notably, this proxy reward omits the language models $\ell$ and the ground-truth labels from its calculations. By design, its output should align with the true reward $r$. Therefore, we establish a supervised learning objective to minimize the discrepancies between the proxy reward model and the true reward.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{figs/long_fig.png}
    \caption{\small \textit{Prompt-OIRL addresses the specified Objective and challenges}. It first learns a proxy reward model from the offline demonstration dataset we created in the last section. Such a learned reward model can be applied in inference time to evaluate prompts in a query-dependent manner without access to the language model, hence optimizing prompt w.r.t. such a proxy reward model solves all issues identified.}
    \label{fig:main_pic_prompt_oirl}
\end{figure}
In the case of arithmetic reasoning tasks, the reward signal is binary, hence the proxy reward model can be trained as a classification task, predicting whether the prompt can result in a correct answer when fed to the language model. Specifically, we consider the Cross-Entropy loss given the demonstration data collected in the previous section:
\begin{equation}
     \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\theta;\mathcal{D}^\ell_{\text{dem}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{i\in[N],k\sim[K]} \left[
    r^{(i,k)} \log \sigma \left( \Upsilon^{(i,k)}_{\theta} \right)
  + (1-r^{(i,k)}) \log \left(1-\sigma\left( \Upsilon^{(i,k)}_{\theta}\right) \right)  \right]
\end{equation}
where we use $\Upsilon^{(i,k)}_{\theta}$ to denote $\Upsilon_\theta(x^{(i)},\pi^{(k)}(x^{(i)}))$ for conciseness, and $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function. Different from the \textit{online} reward signal provided by $r(y^*,\ell(x'))$ that requires expensive interactions with black-box LLMs, the proxy reward model $\Upsilon_{\theta}(x,\pi(x))$ can provide \textit{offline} white-box feedback to prompts. Therefore, it can be more accessible in guiding the search for a better prompt.
Figure~\ref{fig:main_pic_prompt_oirl} illustrates how such a reward model can be learned and leveraged in providing feedback to prompt optimization. 
To achieve computational efficiency and good performance, we use the embeddings of $x$ and $\pi$ generated by existing LLMs, and use those embeddings as inputs for the proxy reward model. Empirically, we find the gradient boosting methods~\citep{chen2015xgboost,ke2017lightgbm} are significantly better than using neural networks in reward modeling. In our experiment section, we demonstrate the effectiveness of Prompt-OIRL using a minimalist approach by directly applying the XGBoost models and leaving further investigation on model selection to future work.}



\textbf{Step 3: Offline Prompt Optimization with the Learned Reward Model.}~~

Given the learned reward model is an effective proxy of the performance evaluator for query-prompt pairs, we would then be able to solve the prompt optimization problem of Euqation~(\ref{eqn:obj}) with an alternative \textbf{\textit{offline}} objective that is feasible to execute in inference-time --- without the requirement of a language model $\ell$ and the non-accessible inference-time golden label $y^*$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:obj_proxy}
\pi^*  =   \arg\max_\pi \Upsilon_\theta(x,\pi(x)) \approx  \arg\max_{\pi} r(y^*,\ell(\pi(x)))
\end{equation}
In general, any policy optimization technique has the potential to be applied in solving Equation~(\ref{eqn:obj_proxy}). To name a few examples, such a technique can be any of the previous approaches used in \textit{\textbf{online}} prompt optimization like RL~\citep{deng2022rlprompt}, beam search~\citep{pryzant2023automatic}, evolution strategies~\citep{zhou2022large}. 

In this work, we would like to highlight the \textbf{effectiveness} and \textbf{cost-efficiency} of optimizing prompts with regard to such a learned reward model as an accessible offline proxy to the true reward. We choose to use a minimalist approach and isolate the source of gains by using a \textbf{best-of-n} approach: we generate a batch of candidate prompts with a general-purpose language model and select the best one according to the learned reward model.
We leave the investigation of other approaches, which could be more tailored and better-performing, yet computationally more expensive as a promising direction for future exploration.

\textbf{Learning-Based Prompt Optimization}
The Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO)~\citep{pryzant2023automatic} leverages training data and initial prompts to perform a "gradient descent" process operated in the natural language space guided by large language models. A generate-and-select process is then applied to update the prompting mechanism. 
Automatic Prompt Engineer (APE)~\citep{zhou2022large} asks LLMs to generate a set of instruction candidates
based on demonstrations and then evaluate the performance of those instructions by computing corresponding scores, finally, APE improves the best candidates by proposing semantically similar instruction variants to specific tasks.
There are also works that explore the usage of auxiliary models or differentiable representations of the prompt~\citep{shin2020autoprompt,li2021prefix, qin2021learning, vu2021spot,gu2021ppt}. Yet those methods require access to the embeddings of the LMs for soft prompt optimization, hence falling short in reusability across LMs.
In the work of \cite{hao2022optimizing}, reinforcement learning is used to optimize a prompt policy in text-to-image generation tasks in pursuance of more aesthetically pleasing images preserving the original user intentions.
\cite{zhang2022tempera} proposes TEMPERA to perform test time prompt editing using reinforcement learning. TEMPERA designs the action space to be editing operations, such as swapping, deleting, adding, or changing verbalizers.
The RLPrompt~\cite{deng2022rlprompt} uses reinforcement learning to optimize LLMs to specialize as prompting agents. Yet their prompts generated are task-agnostic, and limited to combinations of words that can not be easily transferred to insights for human prompt engineers. 

\textbf{Reinforcement Learning with Human Knowledge and Demonstrations}
Human knowledge and demonstration have pushed forward the progress of machine learning in many domains including natural language processing~\citep{rajpurkar2018know,ouyang2022training} and many others~\citep{deng2009imagenet,redmon2016you,atkeson1997robot,zhang2018deep,mandlekar2020learning}. 
By injecting prior knowledge of the task from human, reinforcement learning algorithms are able to achieve human-level performance or even outperform human~\citep{silver2016mastering,vinyals2019grandmaster}. Our \textit{solver} of the prompting problem is related to inverse RL~\citep{ng2000algorithms,abbeel2004apprenticeship,ho2016generative} that aims at inferring reward and learning policies with a batch of behavioral demonstrations, which is unique compared with existing literature in prompt optimization.
The main distinctions between this study and those related works are summarized and highlighted in Table~\ref{tab:alihan_dan}.

\subsection{Improving Arithmetic Reasoning by Fulfilling the \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Adjusted Objective}}}
\label{sec:exp_5.1}

\paragraph{Experiment Setup}
In this section, we demonstrate the superiority of query-dependency in prompting. We showcase that Prompt-OIRL effectively fulfills the \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Adjusted Objective}}.
In the experiment, we use \textbf{BoTr Eqn.(1)} to denote 
the setting of selecting the \underline{b}est-\underline{o}f-\underline{tr}aining time prompts according to objective Eqn.(\ref{eqn:obj_dist}). In this case, the prompt to be chosen in test or inference time should be the one that achieves the overall best training performance. We then consider the setting of \textbf{BoTr Eqn.(2)}, when Eqn.(\ref{eqn:obj}) becomes the objective. In such a setting, the proxy reward model will be used to select in the query-dependent best-of-N selection. Alternatively, we compare the LLM-confidence-based baseline in approaching Eqn.(\ref{eqn:obj}), which uses LLMs to reflect how confident they are for different prompted answers. \textbf{LLM Confidence} thence selects the most confident answers that correspond to training prompts according to confidence scores provided by LLMs. Finally, our method \textbf{Prompt-OIRL} selects the best prompt from both training time prompts and held-out prompts with regard to the learned reward model.

\subsection{Addressing \textcolor{brown}{\textbf{Challenge 2}}: Cost-Efficient Prompt Optimization}
\label{sec:exp_5.3}

\paragraph{Experiment Setup} 

As previously argued, utilizing the proxy reward model for prompt optimization, as opposed to directly using language models, would result in significantly reduced costs. Indeed, the expenses associated with inference time can be even more expensive~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt}. In this section, we emphasize the cost-efficiency advantage of Prompt-OIRL during the inference time prompt optimization.

Prompt-OIRL evaluates prompts through its offline reward model and obviates the need for extra LLM interactions during inference. Picture a scenario where, for each query at inference, there exist $K$ potential prompts to assess and select. With Prompt-OIRL, the optimal prompt is chosen without LLM interaction, ensuring that only the chosen prompt undergoes inference with the LLM to produce a singular response. Conversely, evaluating prompts via LLMs --- by gauging their confidence or dissecting the nuances of various answers --- demands extensive interaction prior to settling on a choice. We provide a detailed cost analysis of these methods, outlining the monetary implications of employing the GPT-3.5-turbo and TigerBot-13B-Chat APIs. We disclose the inference time, quantified in GPU hours for LLaMA2-7B-chat, which operated locally on an NVIDIA A4000 GPU.

\section{Conclusion}

We propose Prompt-OIRL, a novel approach grounded in offline inverse reinforcement learning, designed to reconcile effective and cost-efficient query-dependent prompt evaluation and optimization. This method leverages offline datasets from existing evaluations, utilizing Inverse-RL to craft a reward model tailored for offline, query-specific prompt evaluations. Prompt-OIRL offers several benefits: it forecasts prompt efficacy, minimizes costs, and explores the prompt space more effectively --- all at a query-dependent level. We validate our approach across various LLMs and arithmetic reasoning datasets, underscoring its viability as a formidable solution for query-dependent offline prompt evaluation and optimization.
