\title{Sparse Covariance Neural Networks}

\begin{abstract}
Covariance Neural Networks (VNNs) perform graph convolutions on the covariance matrix of tabular data and achieve success in a variety of applications. However, the empirical covariance matrix on which the VNNs operate may contain many spurious correlations, making VNNs' performance inconsistent due to these noisy estimates 
and decreasing their computational efficiency.
To tackle this issue, we put forth Sparse coVariance Neural Networks (S-VNNs), a framework %pipeline 
that applies sparsification techniques on the sample covariance matrix before convolution. When the true covariance matrix is sparse, we propose hard and soft thresholding to improve covariance estimation and reduce computational cost. Instead, when the true covariance is %also 
dense, we propose stochastic sparsification where data correlations are dropped in probability according to principled strategies. We show that S-VNNs are more stable than nominal VNNs %their nominal dense counterparts 
as well as sparse principal component analysis. By analyzing %, and we analyze %model 
the impact of sparsification on their behavior, we provide %providing 
novel connections between S-VNN stability and data distribution.
We support our theoretical findings with experimental results on various application scenarios, ranging from brain data to human action recognition, and 
show an improved task performance, stability, and computational efficiency of S-VNNs %our approach 
compared with nominal VNNs. 

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


Covariance-based data processing is key to machine learning pipelines due to its %the 
ability to whiten data distributions, identify principal directions, and estimate the interdependencies among features. Such advantages have been shown %This has shown advantages 
in several applications including brain connectivity estimation~\citep{bessadok2022graph,QIAO2016399}, financial data~\citep{cardoso2020algorithms,wang2022network} and human action recognition through motion sensors measurements~\citep{liao2022har,wang2023mhagnn}. 
One prominent approach %example 
is Principal Component Analysis (PCA), which maximizes the variance of data points by projecting them onto the eigenvectors of their covariance matrix~\citep{Jolliffe2016PrincipalCA}.
However, PCA-based %these 
approaches are unstable to errors in the covariance estimation, i.e., a poor estimate of the covariance matrix and its eigenvectors may lead to unpredictably bad results, especially in high-dimensional and low-data regimes or when the covariance matrix eigenvalues are close to each other~\citep{Jolliffe2002pca,paul2007asymptotics,baik2005phase}. 
To overcome this issue, coVariance Neural Networks (VNNs) have been proposed~\citep{sihag2022covariance}. VNNs apply graph convolutions to the covariance matrix, an operation that, similarly to PCA, learns the importance of the principal directions of the data. Due to their graph convolution operation, they inherit the stability property of GNNs~\citep{gama2020stability} and extend it to errors in covariance estimation, which guarantees more reliable performance even in the presence of poor covariance estimates since their output difference when operating on the sample and true covariance is bounded~\citep{sihag2022covariance,sihag2024explainable,sihag2023transferablility}.
Nevertheless, VNNs operate on the sample covariance matrix, which results in %leads to 
two %relevant 
limitations. First, VNNs are sensitive to settings in which covariance estimation is problematic, especially in high-dimensional and low-data regimes%where the estimates of the covariance matrix can be poor
~\citep{paul2007asymptotics,baik2005phase,F_ral_2007}. Second, VNNs are computationally expensive and memory inefficient as the sample covariance matrix is typically dense no matter whether the true covariance matrix is dense or sparse due to sample estimation errors, 
which is %and this becomes 
especially problematic on high-dimensional datasets. 

To overcome these limitations, we propose %develop %propose to study the effect of 
sparsification-based covariance regularizers for VNNs to improve the covariance matrix estimation.
We perform stability analysis for sparse VNNs (S-VNNs) to investigate the effects of covariance sparsification techniques on the performance of VNNs. 
\textit{When true covariance matrices are sparse}, we propose hard and soft thresholding strategies and show that S-VNNs are more robust to both the finite-data estimation error and the covariance sparsification thresholding. 
\textit{For generic scenarios where true covariance matrices are not necessarily sparse}, we propose a stochastic %covariance 
sparsification approach and prove the stability of S-VNNs in this setting. Our findings quantify a trade-off between stability and sparsification (i.e., efficiency). That is, dropping covariances according to their absolute values leads to higher stability but, in some cases, low sparsification, whereas dropping a fixed percentage of covariance values improves sparsification at the expense of stability. We corroborate our findings with experiments on synthetic and real datasets highlighting the benefits of sparsity on covariance-based neural processing both in terms of performance and computation. %time. 
Our specific contributions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{C\arabic*)}, start = 1]
\item \textbf{Sparse covariance neural networks.} We propose covariance sparsification techniques for VNNs to improve performance and reduce computation. When the true covariance is sparse, we perform hard and soft covariance thresholding. When the true covariance is dense, we propose a stochastic sparsification framework that allows controlling the sparsification level, and design specific strategies in this framework based on application requirements. 
\item \textbf{Stability to covariance sparsification.} We characterize the effects of covariance sparsification techniques on the VNN performance through stability analysis. We show that S-VNNs can not only reduce the computation but also improve the stability under appropriate settings, compared to the nominal VNN and the sparse PCA.  
\item \textbf{Empirical validation.} We validate our theoretical findings with experiments on real and synthetic datasets, demonstrating the importance of sparsification in low-data settings and the improved efficiency of S-VNNs on brain data and human action recognition use cases.
\end{enumerate}



\section{Problem Formulation}

\label{sec:problem_formulation}
Consider $t$ samples $\{\vcx_i\}_{i=1}^t$ of a random vector $\vcx \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with mean $\vcmu = \mathbb{E}[\vcx]$, covariance $\mtC = \mathbb{E}[(\vcx-\vcmu)(\vcx-\vcmu)^\Tr]$ and respective estimates %. As we usually do not have access to the true moments $\vcmu$ and $\mtC$, we estimate them from the available samples as 
$\vchmu = 1/t\sum_{i=1}^t\vcx_i$, $\mthC = 1/t\sum_{i=1}^t(\vcx_i-\vchmu)(\vcx_i-\vchmu)^\Tr$. The estimated covariance $\mthC$ captures the data interdependencies and can be used
for principal component analysis (PCA) via its eigendecomposition $\mthC = \mthV\mathbf{\hat{\Lambda}}\mthV^\Tr$ with eigenvectors $\mthV = [\vchv_0, \dots, \vchv_{N-1}]\in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ %contains the eigenvectors 
and eigenvalues $\mathbf{\hat{\Lambda}} = \text{diag}(\schlambda_0, \dots, \schlambda_{N-1})$, to %is a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues, is used in PCA, which 
maximize the variance of the data points by projecting them on its eigenvectors, i.e., $\vctx = \mthV^\Tr\vcx$. 
However, this finite-sample PCA is unstable %(i.e., small changes in the input data may lead to large unpredictable changes in the output) 
as the estimated eigenvectors $\mthV$ may differ significantly from the true directions $\mtV$ especially when the true %, where $\mtC = \mtV\mtLambda\mtV$ is the eigendecomposition of the true covariance, when 
eigenvalues in $\mtLambda$ are close to each other~\citep{Jolliffe2002pca}. 

To overcome this issue, \citet{sihag2022covariance} proposed graph convolutional covariance filters as an extension of PCA that is stable to finite-sample estimation uncertainties even with close eigenvalues. 
Each data point is seen as a node of a graph and the data sample $\mathbf{x}$ as a graph signal, where the covariance $\mathbf{C}$ represents the graph structure. Covariance filters build on the standard graph convolutional filters, which shift a signal $\mathbf{x}$ over a graph represented by the shift operator $\mathbf{S}$ (e.g., adjacency matrix or Laplacian) according to the equation $\sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\mathbf{S}^k\mathbf{x}$, where $K$ is the order of the filter and $h_k$ are learnable parameters~\citep{Gama_2020,ortega2018gsp,isufi2024graphfilters}. Several popular GNNs, such as GCN~\citep{kipf2016semi}, are special instances of the generic graph convolutional filter.
Building on this, the covariance filter of order $K$ and the VNN layer, which assembles a filter bank of $F_\text{in}\times F_\text{out}$ covariance filters and a non-linear activation function $\sigma(\cdot)$, are
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cov_filter}
    \vcu = \sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\mthC^k\vcx = \mtH(\mthC)\vcx~~\textnormal{and}~~\vcu^l_f = \sigma\left(\sum_{g = 1}^{F_{\text{in}}}\mtH^l(\mthC)\vcu^{l-1}_g\right)~f = 1, \ldots, F_{\text{out}},~l = 1, \ldots, L.
\end{align}
This operation is related to PCA as it processes the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix (see \Cref{app:pca_background} for details).
We denote the VNN architecture as $\fnPhi(\vcx, \mthC, \mathcal{H})$, where $\mathcal{H} = \{h_{klgf}\}$ contains all parameters. 
The output of the last layer $\mathbf{u}^L = \Phi(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{C}}, \mathcal{H})$ contains the final representations generated by VNN and can be directly used for a downstream task (e.g., classification or regression) or further processed by a readout layer.
 The model parameters $\mathcal{H}$ are optimized to minimize a task-specific loss (e.g., cross-entropy for classification or mean squared error for regression tasks) over a training set. 
Inheriting the stability to perturbations of neural network structures~\citep{Gama_2020_stability,bruna2013invariant}, VNNs have been shown %to be 
stable to errors in covariance estimation, as stated in the following theorem. 
\begin{theorem}[Stability of VNNs \citep{sihag2022covariance}]
\label{th:vnn_stability}
    Consider a VNN $\fnPhi(\vcx, \mtC, \mathcal{H})$ of $L$ layers with $F$ covariance filters $\mtH(\mtC)$ per layer %of covariance filters 
    and nonlinearities $\sigma(\cdot)$ such that $|\sigma(a)-\sigma(b)|\leq |a-b|$. Let each filter's frequency response be Lipschitz with constant $P$\footnote{For a definition of Lipschitz filter see \Cref{as:lipschitz}.}. Consider a generic data sample $\vcx \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mtC)$ such that $\|\vcx\| \leq 1$. %(throughout the paper, $\|\cdot\|$ denotes 2-norm for vectors and operator norm for matrices) and $\mtC \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ is the true covariance matrix and consider the sample covariance matrix $\mthC$ estimated from $t$ data samples. 
    Then, %the following holds 
    with probability $1-\mathcal{O}(1/t)$ it holds that 
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{gathered}
    \label{eq:vnn_stability}
    \|\mtH(\mthC) \!-\! \mtH(\mtC)\| \!\leq\! \frac{P}{\sqrt{t}}\mathcal{O}\left( \!\!\sqrt{N} \!+\! \frac{\|\mtC\|\sqrt{\log (Nt)}}{\nu t} \!\right)\!\!=\!\beta\!~\textnormal{and}~
 \|\fnPhi(\vcx, \mtC, \mathcal{H}) \!-\! \fnPhi(\vcx, \mthC, \mathcal{H})\| \!\leq\! LF^{L\!-\!1}\!\beta
    \end{gathered}
    \end{equation*}
    for some $\nu > 0$ capturing the data distribution, and $\|\cdot\|$ denoting the $2-$ or the spectral norm.
\end{theorem}

This notion of stability follows an extensive line of research on stability of GNNs to generic graph perturbations~\citep{gama2020stability,cervino2022training,arghal2022robust,Parada_Mayorga_2021,keriven2020convergence,Ruiz_2020}, but it specifically considers covariance estimation errors, which is fundamental for VNNs. Indeed, since in practical applications the true covariance is not available and VNNs operate on an estimate of it, this notion of stability acts as a certificate of performance guarantee w.r.t. the ideal scenario when working with the true covariance matrix and characterizes the impact of data and model characteristics on the performance. 
More in detail, 
the output difference of VNN caused by the sample estimation error is bounded proportionally by the square root of the number of samples $t$. When $t$ increases, the bound approaches zero, ultimately, showing convergence to the true data distribution. 
This result is specific to covariance estimation errors, whereas generic GNN stability bounds do not decrease with the number of samples.
This improves the stability w.r.t. PCA, which %whose stability 
we provide in the following lemma. 
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma_pca}
Consider a true covariance matrix $\mtC$ and a sample covariance estimate $\mthC$ with respective eigendecompositions $\mtC = \mtV\mtLambda\mtV^\Tr$ and $\mthC = \mthV\mathbf{\hat{\Lambda}}\mthV^\Tr$.
Then, for any signal $\vcx$ with $\|\vcx\|\leq 1$, it holds with high probability that
$\|\mtV^\Tr\vcx-\mthV^\Tr\vcx\| \leq \mathcal{O}(t^{-1/2}(\min_{i,j\neq i}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|)^{-1})$.
\end{lemma}
\Cref{lemma_pca} shows that PCA stability is inversely proportional to the smallest gap between covariance eigenvalues, leading to unstable behaviors %instabilities 
when the eigenvalues are close. VNNs do not suffer from this as the covariance filter can exhibit a stable response to close eigenvalues at the expense of lower discriminability~\citep{sihag2022covariance}.
Because of this advantage in finite-data settings, VNNs have been shown %proved 
effective in covariance-based learning tasks, both on static and temporal data~\citep{sihag2024explainable,sihag2023transferablility,cavallo2024stvnn,cavallo2024fairvnn}. However, they have two major limitations. %are several limitations in our concern. 
First, when the true covariance matrix is sparse, the finite-sample estimate contains spurious correlations~\citep{baik2005phase,paul2007asymptotics}, %ultimately 
affecting the performance %stability 
of the VNN significantly. Second, when the true covariance is dense, a VNN on the finite-sample estimate is limited by its high quadratic computational complexity in the data dimension, restricting its applicability to low-dimensional settings only.


\noindent\textbf{Paper objective.} Our goal is to overcome the above limitations by %study the effect of 
sparsifying the sample covariance matrix for the VNN. %to overcome both the above limitations. 
While %for 
PCA-based processing sparsification has been studied~\citep{bickel2008covariance,deshp2016sparse}, its extension to the VNN is challenging because it is unclear: (i) how to perform sparsification depending on whether the true covariance matrix is sparse or dense; (ii) what are the effects of sparsification on VNN stability. When the true covariance matrix is sparse, we apply 
thresholding-based sparsification strategies for %regularizers of 
the sample covariance matrix, %estimates 
and prove that they result in stability improvements 
(Thms.~\ref{cor:sparse_stab_hard}-\ref{cor:vnn_stability_sparsecov}). When the true covariance matrix is dense, we put forth a stochastic sparsification framework in a form akin to dropout (Def.~\ref{def:stochastic_sparsification}), characterize its impact on the %\zhan{improvement of} 
VNN stability (Thm.~\ref{th:stability_random}), and propose principled sparsification strategies based on these findings (Sec.~\ref{sec:designsparse}).




\section{Sparse True Covariance}

\label{sec:sparse_true_covariance}

In applications involving brain and spectroscopic imaging, weather forecasting, and finance, the sample covariance matrix is notoriously prone to spurious correlations due to limited sample size~\citep{Bickel_2008,jobson1980estimation,ledoit2003honey}.
Sparsity-based regularizers for the covariance estimation have been proposed as a backup \citep{bickel2008covariance}. In this section, %Here, 
we consider the scenario where true convariance matrices are sparse, propose %study the effect of 
two sparsification strategies, hard and soft thresholding, and analyze their effects on %for the VNN. %on 
the VNN embeddings.
The choice of hard and soft thresholding instead of other regularized covariance estimations~\citep{ledoit2003honey,Bickel_2008,bien2011sparse,friedman2007lasso} lies in the fact that thresholding yields a fast approach and allows theoretical tractability.

Instrumental tool for our analysis will be the frequency response of a covariance filter. By computing the graph/covariance Fourier transform of input and output signal of the covariance filter (cf. \eqref{eq:cov_filter}) we get
$\vctu = \mthV^\Tr\vcu = \mthV^\Tr\sum_{k=0}^Kh_k[\mthV\mathbf{\hat{\Lambda}}\mthV^\Tr]^k\vcx = \sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\mathbf{\hat{\Lambda}}^k\mthV^\Tr\vcx$, which, for the $i$-th entry, leads to
$\vctu_{i} =  \sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\hat{\lambda}_i^k\vctx_{i} = h(\hat{\lambda}_i)\vctx_{i}$.
This indicates that the frequency response of the covariance filter is a polynomial $h(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\lambda^k$ with frequency variable $\lambda$ specified on the eigenvalues $\hat{\lambda}_i$. 

\begin{definition}[Lipschitz covariance filter]
\label{as:lipschitz}
    The covariance filter is Lipschitz with constant $P$ if, for every pair of eigenvalues $\lambda_i,\lambda_j$, $i \neq j$, its frequency response satisfies: $|h(\lambda_i)-h(\lambda_j)| \leq  P|\lambda_i - \lambda_j|$. %it holds that %the following holds:
\end{definition}
Lipschitz covariance filters control the variability of the frequency response through the constant $P$. Higher $P$ allows the frequency response to generate different outputs at close eigenvalues and improves %provides %allows for 
the filter's discriminability, but may degrade its stability as shown in the following. %as the frequency response can provide more different outputs for close input eigenvalues.


\subsection{Hard thresholding}

Hard thresholding removes the covariance entries below a given value and it has been shown to improve covariance estimation in high-dimensional low-data settings~\citep{bickel2008covariance}.
\begin{definition}[Hard thresholding]
\label{def:hard_thr}
    Given the sample covariance matrix $\mthC$ and a coefficient $\tau>0$, the hard thresholding function is
    $\eta(\mthC)_{ij} = \schc_{ij}$ if $|\schc_{ij}| \geq \tau/\sqrt{t}$, 0 otherwise.
\end{definition}

The hard threshold is inversely dependent on the number of samples as $1/\sqrt{t}$. %following~\citep{bickel2008covariance}. 
This follows the intuition that %given the sparse true covariance, 
the non-thresholded estimator approaches the true covariance as $t$ increases, %when enough samples are available, the non-thresholded covariance estimator gets closer to the true covariance 
hence, the sparsification is less needed and disappears in the limit of $t \rightarrow \infty$.
Hard thresholding provides a more reliable covariance estimate because it removes small spurious finite-sample errors and, thus, improves the performance of VNNs when the true covariance is sparse. We analyze the impact of hard thresholding on the VNN stability in the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}
\label{cor:sparse_stab_hard}
    Let the true covariance $\mtC$ belong to the sparse class $\mathcal{C} = \{\mtC:c_{ii}\leq M,\sum_{j=1}^N \mathbbm{1}[c_{ij} \neq 0]\leq c_0, \forall i\}$ where $M>0$ is a constant, $\mathbbm{1}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function and $c_0$ is the maximum number of non-zero elements in each row of $\mtC$. Consider a hard-thresholded sample covariance matrix $\mtbC$ following Def.~\ref{def:hard_thr} with %the function in \Cref{def:hard_thr} where 
    $\tau = M'\sqrt{\log N}$ and $M'$ large enough. With a high probability, it holds that
    \begin{align*}\label{eq:sparse_stab_hard}
        \|\mtH(\mtbC)\vcx-\mtH(\mtC)\vcx\| \leq t^{-1/2}Pc_0\sqrt{N\log{N}}(1+\sqrt{2N}) + \mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\right).
    \end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\Cref{cor:sparse_stab_hard} shows that hard-thresholded covariance filters --and consequently VNN from the results in Thm.~\ref{th:vnn_stability}-- are stable to covariance estimation errors and that they 
converge to the respective filter and VNN operating with the true covariance as $1/\sqrt{t}$ in the number of data samples. %in an order of %as 
Importantly, %Furthermore, 
hard thresholding improves the stability with a tighter bound than dense VNNs~\citep{sihag2022covariance} and sparse PCA~\citep{bickel2008covariance}.

\underline{\textit{Comparison with dense VNN.}} Contrasting the results of Thm.~\ref{cor:sparse_stab_hard} and Thm.~\ref{th:vnn_stability}, we see that both bounds decrease with the same order $\mathcal{O}(t^{-1/2})$. However, the hard-thresholded VNN bound depends linearly on the number of non-zero elements in a row $c_0$, whereas that of the dense VNN depends on the spectral norm $\|\mtC\|$. For sparse covariance, $c_0 \ll \|\mtC\|$ and the hard thresholding \textit{improves} stability.


\underline{\textit{Comparison with sparse PCA.}} 
To provide further insights on the stability of sparse 
covariance filters, we contrast it also with the stability of sparse PCA. %We provide the stability result for sparse PCA with covariance thresholding for comparison.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:pca_stab}
    Consider a true covariance matrix $\mtC$ and the thresholded sample covariance estimate $\mtbC$ with respective eigendecompositions $\mtC = \mtV\mtLambda\mtV^\Tr$ and $\mtbC = \mtbV\mathbf{\bar{\Lambda}}\mtbV^\Tr$. Then, for any signal $\vcx$ with $\|\vcx\|\leq 1$, it holds with high probability that
    \begin{align}
        \|\mtV^\Tr\vcx -\mtbV^\Tr\vcx\| \leq t^{-1/2} (\min_i|\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}|)^{-1} c_0N\sqrt{2\log{N}} 
    \end{align}
\end{proposition}

That is, the stability of sparse PCA is %also 
inversely proportional to the minimum eigenvalue gap. This term is not present in the stability of S-VNN, i.e., Thm.~\ref{cor:sparse_stab_hard}, as it is absorbed by the filter Lipschitz constant $P$: indeed, the covariance filter can exhibit a stable frequency behavior for close eigenvalues at the expense of lower discriminability, but the latter is compensated in the subsequent VNN layers that use cascades of filterbanks and non-linearities. Thus, %analogously to the VNN result in a dense covariance setting~\citep{sihag2022covariance}, 
hard-thresholded VNNs attain improved stability w.r.t. sparse PCA in sparse covariance setting.


\subsection{Soft thresholding}
\label{sec:soft_thresholding}

When the data follows the spiked covariance model, such as in electrocardiogram or brain image data~\citep{johnstone2009sparse}, soft thresholding has been studied as a better alternative to achieve more reliable covariance estimates~\citep{deshp2016sparse}.
In this model, data points follow $\vcx_i = \sum_{q=1}^r\sqrt{\beta_q}u_{q,i}\vcv_q + \vcz_i$,
where $\vcv_1,\dots,\vcv_r\in\mathbb{R}^N$ are orthonormal vectors with exactly $c_0$ non-zero entries of magnitudes lower-bounded by $\theta/\sqrt{c_0}$ for some constant $\theta > 0$, $u_{q,i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and $\vcz_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mtI)$ are i.i.d., and $\beta_q\in\mathbb{R}_+$ is a measure of the signal-to-noise ratio. 
In this case, we sparsify the covariance estimate with soft thresholding.
\begin{definition}[Soft thresholding]
\label{def:soft_thr}
    Given the sample covariance matrix $\mthC$ and a coefficient $\tau>0$, we define the soft thresholding function as
    $\eta(\mthC)_{ij} = \schc_{ij} - \textnormal{sign}(\schc_{ij}) \tau/\sqrt{t}$ if $|\schc_{ij}| > \tau/\sqrt{t}$, 0 otherwise.
\end{definition}
Unlike hard thresholding which only removes small noisy values, soft thresholding subtracts a value from all entries to remove noise also from non-zeroed coefficients.
Again, we set the threshold to decrease with the number of samples analogously to~\citep{deshp2016sparse} as more accurate covariance estimates reduce the need for sparsification. 
The following theorem links soft thresholding to the VNN stability. %provides the stability result for VNN with soft thresholding.
\begin{theorem}
    \label{cor:vnn_stability_sparsecov}
     Consider a soft-thresholded estimate of the covariance matrix $\mtbC$ as per \Cref{def:soft_thr} with $\tau = M'\sqrt{\log(N/c_0^2)}$ and $M',C$ two large enough constants.
     Let the eigenvalues of the true covariance $\{\lambda_i\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ be all distinct. Then, the following holds with high probability:
    \begin{align*}\label{eq:sparse_stab_soft}
    \|\mtH(\mtbC)\vcx\!-\!\mtH(\mtC)\vcx\| \!\leq\! t^{-1/2}P\sqrt{N}Cc_0\max(1,\!\lambda_\textnormal{max})\sqrt{\max\left(\log (N/c_0^2),1\right)}(1+\sqrt{2N}) \!+\! \mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\!\right)\!.
    \end{align*}    
\end{theorem}
\Cref{cor:vnn_stability_sparsecov} shows that VNNs are stable also when the covariance estimate is soft thresholded and the stability bound decreases with the number of available samples as $t^{-1/2}$. 
The main takeaways for hard thresholding w.r.t. dense VNNs and sparse PCA hold also for soft-thresholded VNNs.
Furthermore, soft thresholding provides better stability than hard thresholding in low-data settings under spiked covariance, as the bound in Thm.~\ref{cor:vnn_stability_sparsecov} contains $\sqrt{\log(N/c_0^2)}$ which is smaller than $\sqrt{\log N}$ in Thm.~\ref{cor:sparse_stab_hard}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Computational complexity.}
A sparse VNN layer has a computational complexity of order $\mathcal{O}(\|\mtbC\|_0KF_\text{in}F_\text{out})$, where $\|\mtbC\|_0$ is the number of non-zero values of the thresholded sample covariance. This is significantly better than a VNN with dense covariance matrix, which is of order $\mathcal{O}(N^2KF_\text{in}F_\text{out})$, since $\|\mtbC\|_0$ is generally much smaller than $N^2$. %thanks to thresholding.


\section{Generic True Covariance}


Often, the true covariance matrix and its estimate are both dense which makes the VNN computationally heavy. While we can use hard or soft thresholding or any graph sparsification approach, the resulting sparse VNN may be significantly different from the VNN operating on the true covariance, ultimately, leading to divergent outputs and degraded performance. 
To reduce the computational cost in a tractable manner, we propose a stochastic sparsification framework in a form akin to dropout. Such an approach is general and does not have any structure assumption %assume any structure 
on the covariance matrix.% nor relies %it relies 

\begin{definition}[Stochastic sparsification]
\label{def:stochastic_sparsification}
Let $\mtDelta$ be a matrix of the same support as the sample covariance $\mthC$ with entries $\delta_{ij} = \delta_{ji} = 1$ with probability $p_{ij}$ and 0 otherwise (i.e., $\textnormal{Bernoulli}(p_{ij})$) and $\delta_{ii} = 1$. 
A sparsified covariance matrix is $\mttC = \mtDelta \odot \mthC$, where $\odot$ is the elementwise product.
\end{definition}

Stochastic sparsification generates random sparsified matrices $\mttC$ %is a random submatrix 
of the sample covariance matrix via element-wise independent sampling in a way that $\mttC$ preserves: (i) the symmetric property in consistency with the covariance principle; and (ii) the variance of the data points on the main diagonal. The sparsification can be controlled by the probabilities $p_{ij}$ depending on application requirements.

\subsection{VNNs with stochastic covariance sparsification}
\label{subsec:generic_true_cov_theory}

We now %To 
investigate the %sparsification 
effects of stochastic sparsification on VNNs' stability by %and %we 
framing the stochastic sparsification as a stochastic perturbation problem following~\citep{gao2021stability} and defining stochastic sparsified covariance filters. %We begin by defining the operation of a covariance filter on randomly sparsified covariance.
\begin{definition}[Stochastic covariance filter]
\label{def:cov_filter_random_cov}
    Given a sequence of i.i.d. realizations $\mttC_k,\dots,\mttC_1$ of the stochastic sparsified covariance $\mttC$ independent on data distribution, a covariance filter $\mtH(\mttC)$ performs convolution of a generic signal $\vcx$ as $\vctu = \mtH(\mttC)\vcx = \sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\mttC_k\dots\mttC_1\mttC_0\vcx$ with $\mttC_0 = \mtI$. 
\end{definition}
Stochastic filters %implies that 
shift the signal $\vcx$ %is shifted via 
over $K$ different random realizations $\{\mttC_k\}_{k=1}^K$ of $\mttC$ rather than shifting over a fixed $\mthC$. 
This %this stochastic covariance filter 
leads to a generalized frequency interpretation.

\begin{definition}[Generalized covariance filter frequency response]
\label{def:generalized_freq_resp}
The generalized %covariance 
frequency response of the stochastic covariance filter is a multivariate function of the form
        $h(\vclambda) = \sum_{k=0}^Kh_k\prod_{\kappa=0}^k\lambda_\kappa$
    where $\vclambda = [\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_K]^\Tr$ with $\lambda_0=1$ is a generic vector variable and each frequency variable $\lambda_k$ corresponds to the covariance realization $\mttC_k$ for $k=1,\ldots,K$.
\end{definition}

The derivations of such a frequency response are reported in \Cref{app:generalized_freq_derivation}. \Cref{fig:2d_freq_resp} illustrates an example of $K=2$. This extends the deterministic case, in which all realizations and corresponding eigenvalues are equal with %and therefore 
$\prod_{\kappa=0}^k\lambda_\kappa = \lambda^k$.

\begin{definition}[Lipschitz gradient]
\label{def:lipschitz_gradient}
    Consider the analytic generalized frequency response $h(\vclambda)$ and two instantiations $\vclambda_1 = [\lambda_{11},\dots,\lambda_{1K}]^\Tr$ and $\vclambda_2 = [\lambda_{21},\dots,\lambda_{2K}]^\Tr$ of vector variable $\vclambda$. Consider also the auxiliary vector that concatenates the first $k$ entries of $\vclambda_2$ and the last $K-k$ entries of $\vclambda_1$, i.e., $\vclambda^{(k)} \!=\! [\lambda_{21},\dots,\lambda_{2k},\lambda_{1(k+1)},\dots,\lambda_{1K}]^\Tr$. The Lipschitz gradient of $h(\vclambda)$ between $\vclambda_1$ and $\vclambda_2$ is
    \begin{align*}
        \nabla_Lh(\vclambda_1,\vclambda_2) = \left[ \partial h(\vclambda^{(1)})/\partial\lambda_1, \dots, \partial h(\vclambda^{(K)})/\partial\lambda_K \right]^\Tr
    \end{align*}
    where $\partial h(\vclambda^{(k)})/\partial\lambda_k$ is the partial derivative w.r.t. $\lambda_k$ at $\vclambda^{(k)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Generalized integral Lipschitz filter]
\label{def:filter_lipschitz}
    A covariance filter is generalized integral Lipschitz if there exists a constant $P$ s.t. %given its generalized frequency response $h(\vclambda)$ and two frequency vectors $\vclambda_1, \vclambda_2$,
        $\|\nabla_Lh(\vclambda_1,\vclambda_2)\|_2 \leq P$ and $\|\vclambda_1 \odot \nabla_Lh(\vclambda_1,\vclambda_2)\|_2 \leq P. $
\end{definition}

The Lipschitz gradient characterizes the variability of %the function 
$h(\vclambda)$ since, for two %generic 
multivariate frequency vectors $\vclambda_1,\vclambda_2$, we have $h(\vclambda_2)-h(\vclambda_1)=\nabla_L^\Tr h(\vclambda_1,\vclambda_2)(\vclambda_2-\vclambda_1)$.
The generalized integral Lipschitz filter limits this variability to be at most linear in the multidimensional space and to decrease as the frequency $\vclambda$ is specified at large values, which extends the %generalizes the 
standard integral Lipschitz property %behavior 
to the multivariate frequency domain. %and limits the variability of $h(\vclambda)$ to be at most linear in the multidimensional space and to decrease as the frequency $\vclambda$ is specified at large values.

\begin{theorem}
\label{th:stability_random}
    Consider a randomly sparsified covariance filter $\mtH(\mttC)$ [cf. Def.~\ref{def:cov_filter_random_cov}] that is generalized integral Lipschitz with constant P [cf. Def.~\ref{def:filter_lipschitz}]. Let also $\mtH(\mtC)$ denote the same filter (fixed parameters) operating on the true covariance matrix. Then, for a generic signal $\|\vcx\|\leq 1$, the expected squared difference between the two filters can be upper-bounded as
    \begin{equation*}
        \label{eq:stability_random}
        \mathbb{E}[\|\mtH(\mtC)\vcx-\mtH(\mttC)\vcx\|^2] \leq \underbrace{NP^2Q + \mathcal{O}((1-p_1)(1-p_2))}_\textnormal{sparsification error} + \underbrace{\frac{P^2}{t}\mathcal{O}\left(N + \frac{\|\mtC\|^2\log(Nt)}{\nu^2t^2} \right)}_\textnormal{covariance uncertainty}
    \end{equation*}
    where $Q=\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{n=1}^N \schc_{in}^2(1-p_{in})$, $p_1,p_2$ are generic probabilities such that $\mathcal{O}((1-p_1)(1-p_2))$ is dominated by the linear terms in the probability value in $Q$ and $\nu$ is defined in \Cref{th:vnn_stability}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Stochastic sparsification strategies}\label{sec:designsparse}
Following our theoretical analysis, we propose two strategies to assign the sparsification probabilities to covariance values and assess their impact on the stability of VNN.

\smallskip \noindent 
\textbf{Absolute covariance values (ACV).} Despite being dense, the covariance matrix may contain some values of lower magnitude that may be reminiscent of spurious correlations. We want therefore to drop these small covariance values with a high probability to save computational cost while keeping large covariance values to maintain the %making it 
useful dependencies. Thus, we define the probability as $p_{ij} = |\schc_{ij}| / \schc_\text{max}$, where $\schc_\text{max} = \max_{i,j}|\schc_{ij}|$. \Cref{fig:stab_insights} (left) shows that the stability term $q_{in}$ within $Q$ [cf. \Cref{th:stability_random}] is small when the covariance value $\schc_{in}$ approaches zero or the maximal value $\hat{c}_{\max}$. This is because small covariance values have little impact on the VNN stability even if they are more likely to be dropped, and large covariance values (close to $\schc_\text{max}$) are less likely to be dropped though their removal would affect stability more. 
It peaks around $\schc_{in} = 2/3\schc_\text{max}$, which identifies an interval in which ACV may have a notable impact on the stability.

\smallskip \noindent 
\textbf{Ranked covariance values (RCV).} While ACV improves efficiency by preserving stronger correlations, it does not allow any control over the amount of sparsification (e.g., if all covariance values are high, very few are dropped). To overcome this, we define a set of probability values with a desired mean $p$ and assign them to covariance values based on their positions in this absolute ranking. Formally, consider an ordered set of probabilities $\mathcal{P}=\{p'_1, \dots, p'_{N'}\}$, where $p'_i \leq p'_{i+1}$, and each $p'_i$ is sampled from $\mathcal{N}(p,\sigma)$ with $\sigma = \min((1-p)/3, p/3))$ (such that the number of values not in $[0,1]$, which we clip to the interval, is negligible) and $N'$ is the number of probability values for assignment. We set $p_{ij} = p'_{k}$ where $k = |\{\scc_{lm}:|\scc_{lm}|<|\scc_{ij}|;l,m=1,\dots,N\}|$ is the position of $c_{ij}$ in the ordered ranking of absolute covariances.
Consequently, the expected percentage of dropped covariances is $1-p$, which allows controlling the sparsification level at the risk of removing useful covariances.
In practice, the value of $p$ can be treated as a hyperparameter and tuned through cross-validation or on a validation set based on the desired accuracy and stability.

\Cref{fig:stab_insights} (right) shows how the term $Q$ changes for ACV and RCV with different means of covariance value distribution. %means. 
RCV with a smaller $p$ corresponds to lower stability especially when the covariance values are high, because they are dropped regardless of their value. ACV, instead, maintains a consistent level of stability since it balances the covariance magnitudes and the dropping probabilities. 
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Computational complexity.}
The expected computational complexity of a VNN layer with stochastic sparsification is of the order $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{E}[\|\mttC\|_0]KF_\text{in}F_\text{out})$, where $\mathbb{E}[\|\mttC\|_0]$ is the expected number of non-zero elements of the sparsified covariance. For ACV, we have that $\mathbb{E}[\|\mttC\|_0] = \schc_\text{mean}(\|\mthC\|_0-N)+N$, where $\schc_\text{mean} = 1/N^2\sum_{i,j=1,\dots,N,i\neq j}|\schc_{ij}|$, so the complexity depends on the data. Instead, for the RCV we have $\mathbb{E}[\|\mttC\|_0] = p(\|\mthC\|_0-N)+N$, so the complexity can be controlled through $p$. %\textcolor{red}{EI: @Zhan, please check these.}

\begin{remark} 
The stochastic sparsification with RCV generalizes the hard thresholding approach %seen 
in Sec.~\ref{sec:sparse_true_covariance}, which can be recovered by setting probabilities as $p_{ij} = 1$ if $|c_{ij}|>\tau/\sqrt{t}$, $0$ otherwise.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
Both thresholding and stochastic sparsification provide symmetric covariance estimates but do not necessarily preserve positive semidefiniteness (PSD), which is common in related works~\citep{bickel2008covariance,deshp2016sparse,li2021braingnn,liao2022har} and places our techniques within the scope of the literature. \citet{bickel2008covariance} also provide a sufficient condition for PSD under hard-thresholding, which we report in \Cref{app:suff_cond_psd}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sparse VNN on real data}
\label{sec:real_exp}

\noindent 
\textbf{Experimental setup -- (\textbf{O3}).}
We consider four real datasets of brain measurements and human action recognition data.
\textit{Epilepsy}~\citep{kramer2008seizure} consists of an electrocardiography time series equally split before and after an epileptic seizure event. We perform binary classification to predict whether a time sample was recorded before or after the seizure.
\textit{CNI}~\citep{schirmer2021cni} contains resting-state fMRI time series of patients with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and neurotypical controls (NC), which we use as labels for binary classification.
\textit{MHEALTH}~\citep{banos2014mhealth} and \textit{Realdisp}~\citep{misc_realdisp_activity_recognition_dataset_305} contain measurements of wearable devices placed on subjects performing different actions. The goal is to classify the action performed by each subject. Additional details are reported in \Cref{app:datasets}. 
We compare various sparsification approaches (thresholding, RCV, ACV) against the dense VNN. For completeness, we report additional results for various PCA-based classifiers and GraphSAGE in \Cref{app:additional_results}, although our objective is not to achieve state-of-the-art performance on these datasets but to reduce the impact of spurious correlations while improving computational efficiency of VNNs.
For Hthr and Sthr, we set the threshold to achieve a level of sparsification comparable to RCV with $p=0.75,0.5,0.25$ to ease the comparison.
\noindent 
\textbf{Results -- (\textbf{O3}).} From \Cref{fig:realworld}, we see that the S-VNNs improve substantially on the computation time and increase the accuracy w.r.t. the nominal VNN. 
This is particularly emphasized for Epilepsy, CNI, and MHEALTH, where most S-VNNs perform better than dense VNN, likely because the sample covariance matrices in these datasets contain spurious correlations. 
On Realdisp, the accuracy improvement is smaller, indicating that %and this may indicate that 
in this dataset most correlations carry useful information to solve the task. 
Both for thresholding and RCV, changing the value of the threshold or $p$ does not lead to large changes in accuracy, meaning that the majority of covariance values are not relevant for performance, while it affects the time efficiency of the model by increasing sparsity.
Finally, on 3 out of 4 datasets, stochastic sparsification achieves a slightly superior performance than deterministic thresholding, likely due to the regularization effect of different realizations of a sparsified covariance over different runs in a form akin to dropout-like techniques~\citep{papp2021dropgnn,Rong2020DropEdge}.




\section{Related Works}


\noindent
\underline{\textit{Sparse PCA.}} The instability of the finite-sample covariance matrix is widely studied for PCA~\citep{Jolliffe2002pca,paul2007asymptotics,baik2005phase} and various regularized estimators have been proposed including covariance shrinkage~\citep{ledoit2003honey,Ledoit_2012}, lasso penalties \citep{bien2011sparse,lui2014sparseeigenvalue}, and thresholding \citep{bickel2008covariance,deshp2016sparse}. We leverage here hard and soft thresholding given their benefits in PCA in low-data sparse-covariance settings and study their impact on VNN stability.
\noindent
\underline{\textit{Stability of GNNs-VNNs.}} VNNs can be seen as graph convolutional neural networks operating on the covariance graph~\citep{sihag2022covariance}, which draws analogies with PCA, but have been shown to be more stable extending the small perturbation analysis of GNNs \citep{gama2020stability, kenlay2021interpretable, kenlay2021stability, levie2021transferability, maskey2023transferability, gao2023trade} to covariances.
Due to their robustness, they have proved successful in brain data processing~\citep{sihag2024explainable}, transferability to large-dimension data~\citep{sihag2023transferablility}, temporal settings~\citep{cavallo2024stvnn} and biased datasets~\citep{cavallo2024fairvnn}.
All these results, however, hold for the dense covariance matrix which is suboptimal in low-data regimes and computationally heavy. Here, we propose S-VNNs and study their stability when the true covariance matrix is sparse and dense. This merits a different treatment than the perturbation assumption studied in \citep{sihag2022covariance} and overcomes the limitations of dense VNNs. Our stochastically sparsified VNN draws analogies with the stability of GNNs to random link drops~\citep{gao2021stability,gao2021stochastic} but we generalize those findings to the more challenging case where all sparsified probabilities differ rather than being identical. We also propose design strategies for the sparsified probabilities based on our theoretical analysis. %\textcolor{red}{EI: @Zhan: add here in "papers here" the works on stabiluty of GNNs - Fernando, Xiaowen, Ron Levie etc. Better include all here.}

\noindent
\underline{\textit{Graph sparsification in GNNs.}} Graph sparsification techniques have been used for multiple purposes in GNNs but predominantly to improve: (i) scalability in large and dense graphs~\citep{hamilton2018inductive,zhang2019heterogeneous,zeng2021decoupling,peng2022towards,srinivasa2020fast,ye2021sparse}; (ii) GNN expressiveness in graph classification tasks and alleviate oversmoothing~\citep{papp2021dropgnn,Rong2020DropEdge,Fang_2023,zheng2020robust,morris2021power}; and (iii) enhance interpretability~\citep{rathee2021learnt,Li_2023,naber2024mapselect}. 
Our S-VNNs aim on the one hand to improve scalability and on the other to preserve the tractability and theoretical links with covariance-based data processing. 
While our sparsification approaches can be applied also to weighted graphs (see \Cref{app:graph_extension}), in this work we focus on covariance sparsification to characterize theoretically how this affects stability w.r.t. covariance estimation errors and spurious correlations.



\section{Conclusion}



In this work, we develop %propose 
sparse covariance neural networks (S-VNNs) and study the effects of sparsification techniques %, including hard thresholding, soft thresholding and stochastic sparsification. %-based estimators 
on covariance neural networks. 
We show that S-VNNs %operating on sparsified covariance estimates 
are more stable to the finite-data sample effect and more computationally efficient. %the sparsified operator. 
In particular, when the true covariance is sparse, we propose S-VNNs with hard/soft thresholding and show that they
transfer to the nominal VNN with a rate inversely proportional to the square root of the number of data points. When the true covariance is dense, we put forth a stochastic sparsified approach to reduce the computational complexity while maintaining mathematical tractability and stability. In the framework of stochastic sparsification, %Following these insights, 
we propose principled %sparsification 
strategies, i.e., ACV and RCV, that align with the theoretical observations to tune sparsification impact and computation efficiency. Experimental results on four real datasets show that the proposed S-VNNs improve substantially on the computation time and achieve competitive or better performance compared with nominal VNNs, thus validating %indicating 
that spurious correlations are present and damaging in real datasets.
Although VNNs can be applied to several other regularized covariance estimators~\citep{ledoit2003honey,Bickel_2008,bien2011sparse,friedman2007lasso} to reduce the impact of spurious correlations, we here focus on thresholding since, unlike other estimators, it allows to control the sparsity of the covariance matrix (fundamental for time and memory efficiency), and allows for theoretical tractability.
Future work will investigate other covariance estimators and characterize the regularization effect of stochastic sparsification, which allows the model to observe multiple instances of the same covariance with potential benefits for robustness, expressiveness and reduced oversmoothing.
