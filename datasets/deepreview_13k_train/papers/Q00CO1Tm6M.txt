\title{Theoretical Hardness and Tractability of POMDPs in RL with Partial Online State Information}

\begin{abstract}

Partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) have been widely applied in various real-world applications. However, existing theoretical results have shown that learning in POMDPs is intractable in the worst case, where the main challenge lies in the lack of latent state information. A key fundamental question here is: how much online state information (OSI) is sufficient to achieve tractability? In this paper, we establish a lower bound that reveals a surprising hardness result: unless we have full OSI, we need an exponentially scaling sample complexity to obtain an $\epsilon$-optimal policy solution for POMDPs. Nonetheless, inspired by the insights in our lower-bound design, we identify important tractable subclasses of POMDPs, even with only partial OSI. In particular, for two subclasses of POMDPs with partial OSI, we provide new algorithms that are proved to be near-optimal by establishing new regret upper and lower bounds. Both our algorithm design and regret analysis involve non-trivial developments for joint OSI query and action control.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}





We investigate partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) in reinforcement learning (RL) systems, where an agent interacts with the environment sequentially without observing the latent state. In these systems, the agent only has access to a noisy observation generated by the latent state via an emission model. The goal of the agent is to achieve a large expected cumulative reward. POMDPs generalize the classic (fully observable) MDPs, and have been widely applied to capture real-world applications. For example, an AI-trained robot often receives only noisy observations of the environment from its sensors due to sensory noise~\citep{akkaya2019solving}; autonomous cars typically do not have a global view of traffic conditions due to their limited reception~\citep{levinson2011towards}. Similar scenarios can occur in games~\citep{berner2019dota}, healthcare~\citep{hauskrecht2000planning}, recommendation systems~\citep{li2010contextual}, economic systems~\citep{zheng2020ai}, and so forth.

Existing information-theoretical results have shown that learning in POMDPs is intractable in the worst case~\citep{krishnamurthy2016pac,liu2022partially} and PSPACE-complete~\citep{papadimitriou1987complexity,mundhenk2000complexity,vlassis2012computational}. This is in contrast to fully observable MDPs, where many efficient algorithms have been developed, e.g.,~\citet{azar2017minimax,jin2018q,agarwal2019reinforcement,bai2019provably,ayoub2020model,xie2020learning,foster2021statistical,wang2021online,jin2022v,zhong2023can,zhu2023provably}, among others. The challenge of POMDPs lies in the lack of latent state information, such that the Markov property that simplifies fully observable MDPs does not hold any more.

Despite the intractability issue of standard POMDPs, recent works have studied variants of POMDPs, for which efficient algorithms with polynomial dependency (on the number of actions $\ac$ and states $\st$, and episode length $\hh$) can be developed, e.g., $m$-step decodable POMDPs~\citep{efroni2022provable}, POMDPs with block MDPs~\citep{zhang2022efficient} or latent MDPs~\citep{kwon2021rl}, and POMDPs with reachability~\cite{xiong2022sublinear}. For smoothness, we relegate more discussions about related work in~\cref{app:relatedwork}. 

An important line of works studied tractable subclasses under weakly revealing conditions~\citep{liu2022partially,liu2023optimistic}, predictive state representations~\citep{zhong2022posterior}, $B$-stability~\citep{chen2022partially}, etc. However, these conditions may not hold in practical cases, e.g., robotics~\citep{pinto2018asymmetric,lee2023learning} and networking~\citep{sinclair2023hindsight,lee2023learning}. Moreover, the regret obtained in these works can be arbitrarily large if the emission measure differences between disjoint state supports are small. Another important line of works revolves around interactive decision making with the decision-estimation coefficient~\citep{foster2021statistical,chen2022unified}, which differs from the focus of our work. Notably, our focus in this paper is to show the value of partial online state information in POMDPs.

To circumvent the dependency or strong assumptions on the emission model, recent work has exploited hindsight state information~\citep{sinclair2023hindsight,lee2023learning,guo2023sample}, where full state information is revealed \emph{at the end of each episode}. This significant line of work is motivated by the fact that, although the precise information about the true latent state is not available before the agent takes an action, some information may become available in hindsight. However, these studies assume \emph{full} hindsight state information. Thus, a natural and practical question one may ask is: \textbf{will the obtained tractability still hold if the state information would \emph{not fully} be revealed at the end of the episode?} It can be trivially shown (based on the existing lower bounds in~\citet{krishnamurthy2016pac,liu2022partially}) that such a situation becomes \emph{intractable}.

Therefore, this motivates us to investigate the important and practical setting on the value of partial (i.e., not full) state information inside (i.e., not at the end of) the episode. We call this \emph{partial ``Online State Information'' (OSI)}. For example, in autonomous driving~\cite{levinson2011towards,pinto2018asymmetric,jennings2019study}, only the conditions of the located or probed paths will be known to the cars/robots; in classic wireless channel scheduling formulated by POMDPs~\citep{zhao2007decentralized,chen2008joint,ouyang2015exploiting}, only the feedback about the scheduled or sensed channels will be available to the users.

In order to model such partial OSI more concretely, we first provide a problem formulation. Specifically, we consider vector-structured states~\cite{jin2020provably,agarwal2019reinforcement,ayoub2020model}, which are motivated by the aforementioned practical examples. In other words, the state is given by a $d$-dimensional vector with each element representing an abstract feature, such as the condition of one path in autonomous driving~\cite{jennings2019study} and the feedback about one wireless channel~\cite{zhao2007decentralized}. Partial OSI means that at each step of an episode, a subset of $\td$ ($1\leq \td <d$) elements in the state-vector will be revealed to the agent \emph{after} her query. Note that such a model allows the agent to \emph{actively query} partial OSI for different elements at different times. This prevents the trivial case, where one state-element cannot be known throughout the process (so that the problem becomes equivalent to a POMDP problem with that specific unknown state-element being the hidden state). Therefore, a key fundamental open question is:

\begin{center}
\textbf{With such partial OSI, can POMDPs be tractable?}
\end{center}

Our Contributions: In this paper, we study the value of partial OSI and provide in-depth answers to this open question.

(1) We establish a lower bound in~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2} that reveals \textbf{a surprising hardness result}: unless we have \emph{full} OSI, we need an exponentially scaling sample complexity of  $\tilde{\Omega}(\frac{\ac^\hh}{\epsilon^2})$ to find an $\epsilon$-optimal policy for POMDPs, where $\ac$ and $\hh$ are the number of actions and the episode length, respectively. This result indicates a sharp gap between POMDPs with \emph{partial} OSI and POMDPs with \emph{full} OSI (or full hindsight information~\citep{lee2023learning}). This may seem somewhat \textbf{counter-intuitive}, because by combining partial OSI from multiple steps (e.g., querying each state-element one-by-one), one may construct full information of a state, and thus enjoy a similar tractability as that with full OSI. However, in~\cref{sec:problem2}, we design a worst-case instance with special state representations and transitions, such that with only polynomial complexity, partial OSI at each step and even a combination of it from multiple steps are \emph{not} sufficient to achieve an $\epsilon$-optimal solution. Nonetheless, one may ask:

\begin{center}
\textbf{Is there any subclass that is tractable with partial OSI?}
\end{center}

Indeed, to push the boundary further along this axis, we identify two intriguing \textbf{tractable subclasses} of POMDPs with only partial OSI, which are rich in practice.

(2) Inspired by our state-transition design for the lower bound, in~\cref{sec:problem4} we identify a tractable subclass of POMDPs with partial OSI, where the transitions of the state-elements in the state-vector are independent of each other. This subclass is motivated by practical examples ranging from autonomous driving~\citep{pinto2018asymmetric,jennings2019study} and Martian rock-sampling~\citep{levinson2011towards} to wireless channel scheduling~\citep{zhao2007decentralized,chen2008joint,ouyang2015exploiting}. For this subclass, we provide \emph{two novel near-optimal algorithms}. The main novel ideas of the algorithm design involve \textbf{adversarial importance weights} (for addressing in-episode biases), \textbf{heterogeneous decay parameters} (for addressing across-episode biases), and \textbf{query-based $Q$-value functions} (for addressing parameter-related inconsistent learning rates). We note that without any one of these ideas, the final performance would not hold. The regrets of both algorithms achieve a polynomial dependency on all parameters (see~\cref{theorem:regretproblem4general} and~\cref{theorem:regretproblem4simplee}). In addition, our theoretical analysis for the case with $\td>1$ shows that the regret can be further reduced as the \emph{query capability} $\td$ increases. Moreover, our regret analysis includes new technical developments to handle the correlations (i) between the query and action policies, and (ii) between the in-episode and across-episode biases.

(3) Inspired by our state-representation design for the lower bound, in~\cref{sec:problem3} we identify another tractable subclass with partial OSI, where additional \emph{partial} noisy observations for the state-elements in the state-vector that are not queried are available. For this subclass, we provide \emph{a novel near-optimal algorithm}. Our algorithm design and regret analysis involve \textbf{a non-trivial generalization of the observable operator method}~\citep{liu2022partially,jaeger2000observable} to handle \emph{the tricky complexity structure of the partial noisy observations under the adaptively queried partial OSI}. Moreover, we provide \emph{a new regret lower-bound} in~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem3} that demonstrates the near-optimality of the regret that we achieve.

\section{Problem Formulation}


For clarity, first we introduce the standard episodic partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), which is known to be intractable in the worst case. Then, we introduce partial online state information (OSI). In this paper, we focus on investigating the value of partial OSI in POMDPs.




\subsection{The Standard Episodic POMDP}\label{subsec:genericpomdp}

Episodic POMDPs are usually modelled by a tuple $\mcm = (\mcs,\mca,\mco,\hh,\Delta_1,\mbp,\mbo,r)$~\citep{liu2022partially,liu2023optimistic,chen2022partially,chen2022unified,cai2022reinforcement}, where $\mcs$, $\mca$ and $\mco$ denote the state space with $\st$ states, the action space with $\ac$ actions and the observation space with $\oo$ observations, respectively; $\hh$ denotes the number of steps in an episode; $\Delta_1: \mcs \rightarrow [0,1]$ denotes a probability measure supported on the state space $\mcs$ and determines the randomness of the initial state at the beginning of an episode; $\mbp = \{ \mbp_h: \mcs \times \mcs \times \mca \rightarrow [0,1] \}_{h=1}^{\hh-1}$ and $\mbo = \{ \mbo_h: \mco \times \mcs \rightarrow [0,1] \}_{h=1}^{\hh}$ denote the \emph{unknown} transition and emission probability measures, respectively; and $r = \{ r_h: \mco \times \mca \rightarrow [0,1] \}_{h=1}^{\hh}$ denotes the known reward function. Specifically, an online agent interacts with the environment in $\kk$ episodes. At each step $h=1,...,\hh$ of an episode $k=1,...,\kk$, the agent receives a global noisy observation $o_h^k$ that is generated according to the emission probability $\mbo_h(\cdot \vert s_h^k)$, where $s_h^k$ is the \emph{unknown} true latent state. Next, the agent takes an action $a_h^k$ and receives the reward $r_h(o_h^k,a_h^k)$. Then, the environment transits to the next state $s_{h+1}^k$, which is drawn according to the transition probability $\mbp_h(\cdot \vert s_h^k,a_h^k)$. Please see~\cref{fig:sketchgeneralpomdp} for a sketch of one step. The goal of the agent is to find a near-optimal policy that achieves an expected cumulative reward close to that of the optimal policy. Due to the lack of latent state information in POMDPs, the observation is non-Markovian and the policy needs to maintain memory.




\subsection{The Episodic POMDP with Partial OSI}\label{subsec:formulatestructuredstate}

As discussed in the introduction, we make the first effort to investigate the key open problem on the value of partial OSI in POMDPs. Based on the general POMDP introduced above, we provide a concrete formulation for partial OSI. Specifically, we consider the vector-structured states~\citep{jin2020provably,ayoub2020model,agarwal2019reinforcement}. Each state $s$ is represented by a $d$-dimensional feature vector $\vph(s) = [\phi_1(s),...,\phi_d(s)]^{\text{T}} \in \mbs^d$, where $\mbs$ is the universal set of the values for each sub-state (i.e., element) in $\vph(s)$, and $[\cdot]^{\text{T}}$ denotes the transpose of a vector. We use $\calsss$ to denote the cardinality of the set $\mbs$. Then, at each step $h$ of an episode $k$, the agent interacts with the environment as follows (please see~\cref{fig:sketchpomdplimitedho} for a sketch of one step):

(Step-i) According to a \textbf{query policy} $\pi_{q,h}^k$, the agent actively queries $\td$ sub-states of the unknown latent state $s_h^k$, where $1 \leq \td <d$. (Step-ii) The queried sub-states $\{\phi_i(s_h^k)\}_{\{i\in \hi_h^k\}}$, i.e., the \textbf{partial OSI}, are revealed to the agent\footnote{Note that we do not require global noisy observations $o_h^k$. In~\cref{sec:problem3}, we study the value of partial noisy observations.}, where $\hi_h^k$ denotes the indices\footnote{With slight abuse of notation, we also directly call $\hi_h^k$ the ``sub-states'' when it is clear from the context.} of the queried sub-states. (Step-iii) \emph{Depending on the queried partial OSI} and according to an \textbf{action policy} $\pi_{a,h}^k$, the agent chooses one sub-state $i_h^k \in \hi_h^k$ and takes an action $a_h^k \in \mca$. (Step-iv) The agent receives a \textbf{reward} $r_h(\phi_{i_h^k}(s_h^k),a_h^k)$, where $r_h: \mbs \times \mca \rightarrow [0,1]$. %is a function of the sub-state $i_h^k$ among queries $\hi_h^k$, and thus \textbf{depends on the query policy} of the agent. %$\tilde{\mcs} \triangleq \{\phi_{\hi}(s): |\hi|=\td, s\in \mcs\}$ is the sub-state space for any union of $\td$ sub-states. 
(Step-v) The environment transits to the next state $s_{h+1}^k$. 

This model is motivated by various practical scenarios, e.g., autonomous driving~\citep{levinson2011towards,pinto2018asymmetric,jennings2019study}, robotics~\citep{akkaya2019solving,lee2023learning,silver2010monte}, wireless channel scheduling~\citep{chen2008joint,ouyang2015exploiting}, and healthcare~\citep{qu2022scalable,hauskrecht2000planning}. Below, we elaborate on two of these motivating examples.

\textbf{Motivating example 1:} In an autonomous delivery system~\citep{jennings2019study}, in order to deliver products to destinations, a robot/agent explores multiple roads and chooses one at each intersection. Here, each \emph{sub-state} $\phi_i(s)$ of $s$ represents the condition, e.g., traffic intensity, of one road. At each step, the robot first actively queries, and then observes the conditions of the queried roads $\hi_h^k$, i.e., the \emph{partial OSI}. However, due to delay requirements, unknown dynamics in the environment, and occlusion, the precise conditions of other roads may not be available. Finally, she chooses one queried road $i_h^k \in \hi_h^k$ to follow, and receives a \emph{reward} associated with this chosen road $i_h^k$ and \emph{action} $a_h^k$.

\textbf{Motivating example 2:} Consider a cognitive MAC (medium access control) system~\citep{ouyang2015exploiting}, where a secondary user/agent wishes to search for spectrum-access opportunities. Here, each \emph{sub-state} $\phi_i(s)$ represents the condition, e.g., busy or idle, of one wireless channel. At each step, the user first actively probes the conditions of a number of channels $\hi_h^k$, and then observes the conditions of the sensed channels, i.e., the \emph{partial OSI}. However, due to energy constraints and latency requirements, the agent cannot sense all the channels. Finally, she transfers packets using one sensed channel $i_h^k \in \hi_h^k$, and receives a \emph{reward} associated with this chosen channel $i_h^k$ and \emph{action} $a_h^k$. 




\subsection{Performance Metric}\label{subsec:formulateperformancemetric}

\textbf{Feedback:} (i) \emph{Before} the partial OSI for step $h$ is revealed in episode $k$, the feedback revealed to the agent is $\Phi_h^k = (\phi_{\hi_1^k}(s_1^k),a_1^k,...,\phi_{\hi_{h-1}^k}(s_{h-1}^k),a_{h-1}^k) \in \hph_h$, where $\hph_h$ denotes the feedback space. $\Phi_h^k$ includes the partial OSI and actions taken before step $h$. (ii) \emph{After} the partial OSI for step $h$ has been revealed, the feedback revealed to the agent is $\Phi_h^{k,'}=$ $\{ \Phi_h^k \cup \phi_{\hi_{h}^k}(s_{h}^k) \} \in \hph_h^{'}$, where $\hph_h^{'}$ denotes the feedback space. $\Phi_h^{k,'}$ includes the new partial OSI $\phi_{\hi_{h}^k}(s_{h}^k)$. In the case with additional partial noisy observation $\tto_{1:h-1}^k$ (please see~\cref{sec:problem3}), both $\Phi_h^k$ and $\Phi_h^{k,'}$ will include $\tto_{1:h-1}^k$.

\textbf{Joint policies:} (i) At each step $h$ of episode $k$, the agent queries sub-states $\hi_h^k$ according to a \textbf{query policy} $\pi_{q,h}^k \in \{\pi_{q,h}: \hph_h \rightarrow \hat{\Delta}_h(\{\hi\}|\td)\}$, which maps $\hph_h$ to a probability measure $\hat{\Delta}_h(\{\hi\}|\td)$ supported on the query space $\{\hi:\vert\hi\vert=\td\}$. (ii) \emph{After} receiving the partial OSI $\phi_{\hi_{h}^k}(s_{h}^k)$, the agent takes an action $a_h^k$ according to an \textbf{action policy} $\pi_{a,h}^k \in \{\pi_{a,h}: \hph_h^{'} \rightarrow \tilde{\Delta}_h([1,d],\mca)\}$, which maps $\hph_h^{'}$ to a probability measure $\tilde{\Delta}_h([1,d],\mca)$ supported on the sub-state index space $[1,d]$ and action space $\mca$. Note that the partial OSI $\phi_{\hi_{h}^k}(s_{h}^k)$ is included in the feedback $\Phi_h^{k,'} \in \hph_h^{'}$. Thus, \textbf{the action policy $\pi_{a,h}^k$ relies on the output of the query policy $\pi_{q,h}^k$}. We use the $V$-value $V^{\pi^k} \triangleq \expect_{\{\pi_{q}^k,\pi_{a}^k,\mbp,\Delta_1\}} [ \sum_{h=1}^{\hh} r_h(\phi_{i_h^k}(s_h^k),a_h^k) ]$ to denote the expected total reward in episode $k$ by following $\pi_{q}^k = \{\pi_{q,h}^k\}_{h=1}^{\hh}$ and $\pi_{a}^k = \{\pi_{a,h}^k\}_{h=1}^{\hh}$, where $\pi^k = (\pi_q^k,\pi_a^k)$. 

\textbf{Regret:} We take the regret as the performance metric, which is the difference between the expected cumulative reward of the online joint policies and that of the optimal policy, i.e.,
\begin{align}\label{eq:defregret}
\reg^{\pi^{1:\kk}}(\kk) \triangleq \sum_{k=1}^{\kk} \big[ V^{\pi^*} - V^{\pi^k} \big],
\end{align}
where $\pi^* \triangleq \argsup_{\{\pi_q,\pi_a\}} V^{\pi}$ denotes the optimal policy. Notably, the main challenge and new difficulty here are: how to design the query policy $\pi_q$, such that an action policy $\pi_a$ (which \textbf{depends on} the outputs of $\pi_q$) can also be intelligently developed to achieve a near-optimal regret. 






\section{Perils of not Having Full OSI: A New Lower Bound}


In this section, we answer the long-standing open question: \textbf{whether POMDPs with online state information are tractable without \emph{full} OSI (i.e., $\td=d$)?} In~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2} below, we establish a lower bound that reveals a \emph{surprising hardness result}: unless we have full OSI, we need an exponential sample complexity to find an $\epsilon$-optimal policy for POMDPs, where a policy $\pi$ is $\epsilon$-optimal if $V^{\pi} \geq V^{\pi^*} - \epsilon$.

Ber$(1/2)$ represents the Bernoulli distribution with mean $1/2$}

\begin{theorem}\label{theorem:lowerboundproblem2}

\textbf{(Intractability for not having full OSI)} For POMDPs with only partial online state information introduced in~\cref{subsec:formulatestructuredstate}, there exist hard instances, such that with a probability $p\geq 1/3$, any algorithm needs at least $\Omega(\ac^\hh / \epsilon^2)$ samples to find an $\epsilon$-optimal policy.

\end{theorem}

\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2} demonstrates the hardness of POMDPs without full OSI: a polynomially scaling sample complexity Poly$(\ac,\hh,\st,\kk)$ is impossible for finding an $\epsilon$-optimal policy. The result in~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2} may seem \textbf{counter-intuitive}, because by combining partial OSI from multiple steps (e.g., querying each sub-state one-by-one), one may construct full information of a state, and thus enjoy a similar tractability as that with full OSI. To illustrate the surprising inaccuracy of this intuition, we craft a tricky yet elegant worst-case instance, and provide our key proof ideas for~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2}.

\begin{remark}
    
The intractability result in~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2} still holds even if in addition to partial OSI, there exist noisy observations (please see~\cref{sec:problem3}). This is because we can construct a worst-case instance trivially based on the one that we construct in this section, while letting the emission probabilities of the additional noisy observations to be exactly the same for all latent sub-states, such that the noisy observations do not provide any additional information to the agent.

\end{remark}




\subsection{Our Key Proof Ideas for~\cref{theorem:lowerboundproblem2}}\label{subsec:problem2construction}

For simplicity, we focus on the simpler case with $d=2$ and $\td=1$, which makes it easier to understand our key proof ideas. Please see~\cref{app:pftheoremlowerboundproblem2} for the complete proof. The important parts in our proof are to design special state representations and transitions, such that partial OSI cannot help the agent to improve her statistical knowledge about the true latent state. Towards this end, we construct a worst-case instance with four states, i.e., $s(1)$, $s(2)$, $s(3)$ and $s(4)$.

\textbf{Idea I (Semi-correlated state representations):} Our first key idea is to construct semi-correlated states, such that by only observing $\td=1$ sub-state, it is impossible to infer the true latent state. Specifically, we let $\vph(s(1)) = [x_1,x_2]^{\text{T}}$, $\vph(s(2)) = [x_3,x_4]^{\text{T}}$, $\vph(s(3)) = [x_1,x_4]^{\text{T}}$ and $\vph(s(4)) = [x_3,x_2]^{\text{T}}$, where $x_1,...,x_4$ are sub-states (please see~\cref{fig:lowbndproblem2}).

We now introduce the high-level idea for constructing such state representations. Let us consider states $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ as a group of states, and we call it group $a$. Similarly, we call states $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ group $b$. Thus, under our construction of the state representations, each state in group $a$ (i.e., $s(1)$ and $s(2)$) must contain one and only one common sub-state as that in each state of group $b$ (i.e., $s(3)$ and $s(4)$). This is why we call it ``\emph{semi-correlated}''. For example, the first sub-states of both state $s(1)$ and state $s(3)$ are $x_1$. This means that, by only querying the first sub-state $i=1$, the agent cannot know whether she is in a state from group $a$ or group $b$. As another example, the second sub-states of both state $s(1)$ and state $s(4)$ are $x_2$. This means that, by only querying the second sub-state $i=2$, the agent cannot know whether she is in a state from group $a$ or group $b$. As a result, if (i) there is only one specific action sequence that guarantees the agent to be in group $a$, and (ii) group $a$ generates a larger reward, then the agent has to keep trying all exponential number $\Omega(\ac^{\hh})$ of possible action sequences to figure out these two properties with high probability.

However, as we mentioned before, another question still remains: \textbf{whether a combination of partial OSI from multiple steps would be enough?} For example, one can simply query the first and second sub-states one-by-one, and thus form a complete picture of the state. To answer this question, we construct special state transitions using our idea II below. Together with the state representation that we construct above, this state transition causes difficulty for the agent, even when multiple partial OSI are combined.

\textbf{Idea II (Closed-loop state transitions):} Our second key idea is to construct closed-loop state transitions, such that even by combining the partial OSI from multiple steps, it is still impossible for the agent to infer the true latent state. Specifically, in each episode, the agent starts from state $s_1 = s(1)$ (see~\cref{fig:lowbndproblem2}). At step $h=1$, (i) if action $a(1)$ is chosen, the state will transition to $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ with the same probability (wsp); (ii) if action $a(2)$ is chosen, the state will transition to $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ wsp. At step $h=2$, (i) if action $a(1)$ is chosen, both states $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ will transition to $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ wsp; (ii) if action $a(2)$ is chosen, they will transition to $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ wsp. At step $h=3$, (i) if action $a(1)$ is chosen, states $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ will transition to $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ wsp; (ii) if action $a(2)$ is chosen, they will transition to $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ wsp. For states $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ at step $h=2$ and $h=3$, no matter which action is chosen, the states will transition to $s(3)$ and $s(4)$ wsp.

Then, together with the semi-correlated state representations, even when the partial OSI about the first and second sub-states from multiple steps are combined, such a \emph{closed-loop} state transition still prevents the agent from knowing which group of states she is in. For example, at step $h=1$ of two episodes, the agent can keep taking action $a(1)$ and query the first and second sub-states one-by-one. Then, the partial OSI at step $h=2$ could be $\phi_1(s_2^k) = x_1$ (i.e., the first sub-state of $s(1)$) and $\phi_2(s_2^{k+1}) = x_4$ (i.e., the second sub-state of $s(2)$). However, note that the first and second sub-states of $s(3)$ are also $x_1$ and $x_4$. Thus, \textbf{such a combination of partial OSI} (i.e., $\phi_1(s_2^k) = x_1$ and $\phi_2(s_2^{k+1}) = x_4$) \textbf{is not enough for the agent to distinguish} whether she is visiting $s(1)$ and $s(2)$ or she is simply visiting $s(3)$.

\textbf{Idea III (Group-based reward functions):} Up to here, we can see that only with partial OSI, the agent cannot improve her statistical knowledge about the true latent states. Thus, she can only rely on the statistical relation between the sequence of actions that is taken and the reward that is received. Hence, to create difficulties, (i) we let the rewards $r_h$ at steps $h=1,2,3$ be $0$; (ii) if the final state is in \emph{group} $b$, i.e., $s(3)$ or $s(4)$, the reward at step $h=4$ follows Bernoulli distribution with mean $\frac{1}{2}$; (iii) if the final state is in \emph{group} $a$, i.e., $s(1)$ or $s(2)$, the reward at step $h=4$ follows Bernoulli distribution with a slightly higher mean equal to $\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon$. In this way, the optimal policy will take action sequence $(a(1),a(2),a(1))$ for all episodes, so that she can remain in group $a$ and enjoy a expected total reward equal to $\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon$ in every episode. In contrast, the online agent has to try every possible sequence of actions to figure out which sequence provides larger reward with high probability. Since there are $\ac^{\hh}$ number of possible action sequences, according to the Hoeffding's inequality, we can show that the sample complexity for achieving an $\epsilon$-optimal policy is $\Omega(\ac^{\hh} / \epsilon^2)$.






\section{Tractability and Optimality under Partial OSI and Independent Sub-States}


To study the value of partial OSI and push the boundary further along this axis, we answer a key open question: \emph{Is there any subclass that is tractable with partial OSI?} To this end, we identify two intriguing tractable subclasses under partial OSI and provide new near-optimal algorithms. The tractable subclass that we study in this section is as follows.

\begin{subclass}\label{subclass1}

\textbf{(POMDPs with partial OSI and independent sub-states)} At each step $h$ of an episode $k$: (Step-i) the agent actively queries sub-states $\hi_h^k$. (Step-ii) The partial OSI $\phi_{\hi_h^k}(s_h^k)$ is revealed. (Step-iii) The agent chooses a sub-state $i_h^k \in \hi_h^k$, takes an action $a_h^k$, and then receives a reward $r_h(\phi_{i_h^k}(s_h^k),a_h^k)$. (Step-iv) The next state $s_{h+1}^k$ is drawn according to the transition probability $\mbp_h(\cdot \vert s_h^k,a_h^k) = \prod_{i=1}^{d} \mbp_{h,i}(\phi_i(\cdot) \vert \phi_i(s_h^k),a_h^k)$, where the product form indicates that the sub-states have independent transition kernels.

\end{subclass}

This subclass is motivated by many important practical applications. For example, in autonomous driving~\cite{pinto2018asymmetric,jennings2019study} and Martian RockSampling~\citep{silver2010monte}, the sub-states of each potential rock and path could change independently; in classic wireless channel scheduling~\cite{zhao2007decentralized,chen2008joint,ouyang2015exploiting} and smart healthcare~\citep{qu2022scalable,hauskrecht2000planning}, the conditions of each channel and patient could evolve independently. 

We make two claims below. (i) \emph{Without partial OSI in Step-ii of~\cref{subclass1}}, learning under independent sub-states could still be intractable. Please see~\cref{app:pfpropindependentintractable} for the proof. 

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:independentintractable}

\textbf{(Intractability for not having partial OSI)} There exist POMDPs with independent sub-states, such that learning an $\epsilon$-optimal policy requires $\tilde{\Omega}( \ac^{\hh} / \epsilon^2 )$ samples.

\end{proposition}

(ii) By \emph{replacing partial OSI with well-structured noisy observations}, POMDPs with independent sub-states could be decoupled into paralleled sub-POMDPs, which can be readily solved. In contrast, the query in Step-i of~\cref{subclass1} \emph{couples} potential sub-POMDPs together, such that existing solutions do not apply or result in poor performance.




\subsection{Optimistic-In-Pessimistic-Out Learning (\algoo)}

We develop two new near-optimal algorithms for~\cref{subclass1}. The new challenge here is: \textbf{how to \emph{query partial OSI} to avoid intractability in~\cref{prop:independentintractable} and achieve optimality?} To overcome this difficulty, our algorithms involve three interesting and important ideas. Due to page limits, we focus on introducing our new algorithm \algoo~for the case with $\td>1$ (see~\cref{alg:algoointro}). Please see~\cref{app:pftheoremregretproblem4simple} for another algorithm. We use $mod(k,x)$ to denote the remainder when $k$ is divided by $x$, and let $\del = \lceil (d-1)/(\td-1) \rceil$. 

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Optimistic-In-Pessimistic-Out Learning}
\begin{algorithmic}
\FOR{$k=1:\kk$}
\IF{$mod(k,\del)=1$}
\STATE \textit{Step-1:} Update the global weights $w^k(i)$ and probabilities $p^k(i)$ according to~\cref{eq:prob4algooupdateweightprob}.
\STATE \textit{Step-2:} Choose a leading sub-state $\ti^{\left\lceil k/\del \right\rceil}$, i.e., the leader, according to the global probability $p^k(i)$.
\STATE \textit{Step-3:} Initialize the local weight $\tw^k(i)$ according to the global weight $w^k(i)$, i.e., $\tw^k(i) = w^k(i)$.
\ENDIF
\STATE \textit{Step-4:} Choose $\td-1$ supporting sub-states, i.e., the follower, uniformly randomly from the sub-states that have not yet been chosen in most-recent $\del$ episodes, i.e., from $\left\lfloor \frac{k-1}{\del} \right\rfloor \cdot \del + 1$ to $(\left\lfloor \frac{k-1}{\del} \right\rfloor + 1) \cdot \del$.
\STATE \textit{Step 5:} According to~\cref{eq:prob4algooupdatelocalweightprob}, update the local weights $\tw^k(i)$ and probabilities $\tp^k(i)$ for sub-state $i$ queried.
\STATE \textit{Step-6:} Choose the rewarding sub-state $i_h^k$ according to the updated local probability $\tp^k(i)$.
\FOR{$h=\hh:1$}
\STATE \textit{Step-7:} Update $Q$-values according to~\cref{eq:prob4algooqvalue}.
\ENDFOR
\FOR{$h=1:\hh$}
\STATE \textit{Step-8:} Take an action $a_h^k$ that maximizes the updated $Q$-value function, and collect the partial OSI.
\ENDFOR
\ENDFOR 
\end{algorithmic}
\label{alg:algoointro}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Idea-I (Adversarial importance weights for addressing in-episode biases):} Note that the query $\hi$ could cause \emph{errors} in $V$-values. Additionally, these errors could result in \emph{non-stationary in-episode biases} for future decisions (although the state-transition and reward are stationary). Hence, in contrast to existing POMDP solutions that maintain a confidence set, a more conservative solution is required.

Step-1 and Step-2 of~\cref{alg:algoointro}: Specifically, at the beginning of every $\del$ episodes,~\algoo~updates the global weights and probabilities for each sub-state $i=1,...,d$ according to our \emph{new exponential weighting method} as follows,
\begin{align}\label{eq:prob4algooupdateweightprob}
& w^k(i) = w^{k-\del}(i) \cdot e^{\frac{(d-1)\ta}{d(\td-1)} \sum_{\tau=k-\del}^{k-1}\sum_{h=1}^{\hh}\hat{r}_h^{\tau}(\phi_{i}(s_h^{\tau}),a_h^{\tau})}, \nonumber \\
& \text{ and } p^k(i) = (1-\ta) w^k(i) / \sum\nolimits_{i'=1}^d w^k(i') + \ta / d,
\end{align}
and then chooses a leading sub-state according to $p^k(i)$. We note that (i) $\ta$ is the first key decay parameter (see the second one $\tta$ in~\cref{eq:prob4algooupdatelocalweightprob}). $\ta$ determines how pessimistic the algorithm is, e.g., with a smaller $\ta$, the global weight increases more slowly, and thus the algorithm behaves more pessimistically. (ii) The estimated reward $\hat{r}_h^{\tau}(\phi_{i}(s_h^{\tau}),a_h^{\tau})$ is $r_h^{\tau}(\phi_{i}(s_h^{\tau}),a_h^{\tau}) - r_h^{\tau}(\phi_{\ti^{\left\lfloor k/\del \right\rfloor}}(s_h^{\tau}),a_h^{\tau})$ if $i \in \hi^{\tau}$, and is $0$ otherwise. Removing the \emph{common} leading sub-state reward $r_h^{\tau}(\phi_{\ti^{\left\lfloor k/\del \right\rfloor}}(s_h^{\tau}),a_h^{\tau})$ is the critical idea for eliminating the in-episode bias. (iii) The first term in $p^k(i)$ captures how important the sub-state $\phi_i(s)$ is, and the second term is a uniform distribution for exploiting different sub-states.

\textbf{Idea-II (Heterogeneous decay parameters for addressing across-episode biases):} Note that sub-optimal queries $\hi$ at the beginning of episodes result in \emph{unavoidable across-episode biases} for choosing rewarding actions at each step. Indeed, inside each episode, online mirror descent with a same parameter $\ta$ would result in a linear-in-$\kk$ regret. Hence, in contrast to gradient descents that use homogeneous learning rates, a heterogeneous solution is required.

Step-5 and Step-6 of~\cref{alg:algoointro}: At the beginning of episode $k$, \algoo~updates the local weights $\tw^k(i)$ and probabilities $\tp^k(i)$ for sub-states $\phi_i(s)$ in query set $\hat{i}^k$ (formed by the leading sub-state $\ti^k$ and $\td-1$ supporting sub-states):
\begin{align}\label{eq:prob4algooupdatelocalweightprob}
\textstyle & \tw^k(i) = \tw^{k-1}(i) \cdot e^{\frac{\tta}{\td} \sum_{h=1}^{\hh}r_h^{k-1}(\phi_{i}(s_h^{k-1}),a_h^{k-1})}, \text{ and } \nonumber \\
\textstyle & \tp^k(i) = (1-\tta) \tw^k(i) / \sum\nolimits_{i'\in \hat{i}^k} \tw^k(i') + \tta / \td,
\end{align}
and chooses the rewarding sub-state $i_h^k$ according to $\tp^k(i)$. We note that (i) to make the algorithm optimistic enough \emph{in} the episode, the value of the \emph{decay parameter} $\tta$ should be larger than the value of $\ta$. Indeed,~\cref{theorem:regretproblem4general} below provides a sufficient condition on how much $\tta$ should be larger than $\ta$. (ii) The \emph{local} weight $\tw^k(i)$ is updated based on the weight $w^{k-1}(i)$ that is one episode earlier, while the \emph{global} weight $w^k(i)$ is updated based on the weight $w^{k-\del}(i)$ that is $\del$ episodes earlier. (iii) The factor $(d-1) / (\td-1)$ in~\cref{eq:prob4algooupdateweightprob} does not appear in~\cref{eq:prob4algooupdatelocalweightprob}, because the local weight is updated for the sub-states in query set $\hat{i}^k$. (iv) The denominator in the first term of $\tp^k(i)$ only includes $i\in\hat{i}^k$.

\textbf{Idea-III (Query-based $Q$-value functions for addressing parameter-related inconsistent learning rates):} One remaining question in Idea-I above is: how to get the correct factor (i.e., $(d-1)\ta / d(\td-1)$) for $w^k(i)$? The answer is surprisingly simple: choose $\td-1$ supporting sub-states uniformly randomly from the sub-states that have not yet been queried in most-recent episodes (i.e., Step-3 and Step-4 in~\cref{alg:algoointro}). Then, conditioned on the leading sub-state, each sub-state is chosen with probability $\frac{\td-1}{d-1}$, which results in the factor $\ta / (d \frac{\td-1}{d-1}) = \frac{(d-1)\ta}{d(\td-1)}$. In contrast, due to biases from partial OSI, the standard importance sampling method could result in a linear-in-$\kk$ regret in our case. 

Step-7 and Step-8 of~\cref{alg:algoointro}: In order to address the inconsistent-learning-rate issue due to heterogeneous decay parameters, we construct query-based $Q$-value functions that follow an \textbf{optimism-in-face-of-partial-OSI} principle,
\begin{align}\label{eq:prob4algooqvalue}
Q_h^k & (\phi_{i}(s),a) = \min\{r_h(\phi_{i}(s),a) + [\mbp_h^k V_{h+1}^k](\phi_{i}(s),a) \nonumber \\
& + O(\sqrt{\hh^2 / \mathcal{N}_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a)}),\hh\}, \text{ for all } i \in \hi^k,
\end{align}
where $\mbp_h^k(\phi_{i}(s') | \phi_{i}(s),a) = \frac{\mathcal{N}_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a,\phi_{i}(s'))}{\mathcal{N}_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a)}$ is the estimated transition kernel, $\mathcal{N}_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a)$ and $\mathcal{N}_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a,\phi_{i}(s'))$ are the number of times $(\phi_{i}(s),a)$ and $(\phi_{i}(s),a,\phi_{\hat{i}}(s'))$ have been visited at step $h$ up to episode $k$, respectively, and $V_h^k(\phi_{i}(s)) = \max_{a} Q_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a)$. Finally,~\algoo~takes an action to maximize $Q_h^k(\phi_{i}(s),a)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{theorem:regretproblem4general}

\textbf{(Regret)} For POMDPs with partial online state information and independent sub-states, by choosing $\ta=\tilde{O}(1/\sqrt{\kk})$ and $\tta=\frac{16(d-1)}{\td-1} \ta$, with probability $1-\delta$, the regret $\reg^{\text{\algoo}}(\kk)$ can be upper-bounded by
\begin{align}\label{eq:regretproblem4general}
\tilde{O}\left( \hh^{\frac{5}{2}} \calsss^2 \ac \sqrt{\frac{d  \kk \ln d}{\td-1}} \left( \ln \frac{\hh^2\calsss\ac\kk}{\delta} \right)^2 \right).
\end{align}

\end{theorem}

\cref{theorem:regretproblem4general} shows that (i) the regret of~\algoo~depends \emph{polynomially} on all problem parameters $\ac$, $\hh$, $\calsss$ and $d$. (ii) The regret of~\algoo~\emph{decreases further as the query capability} $\td$ increases. (iii) The dependency on $\kk$ is $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\kk})$, which \emph{cannot} be further improved. \emph{To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such near-optimal result for POMDPs with partial OSI.} Further, our regret analysis includes new technical developments to handle the correlations (i) between the action and query policies, and (ii) between the in-episode and across-episode biases. See more details in~\cref{app:pftheoremregretproblem4general}.


 



\section{Tractability and Optimality under Partial OSI and Partial Noisy Observations}



In this section, we identify another tractable subclass (see~\cref{fig:sketchpomdplimitedhopwrc} for a sketch), and provide a new algorithm.

\begin{subclass}\label{subclass2}

\textbf{(POMDPs with partial OSI and partial noisy observations)} At each step $h$ of an episode $k$: (Step-i) the agent actively queries sub-states $\hi_h^k$. (Step-ii) The partial OSI $\phi_{\hi_h^k}(s_h^k)$ is revealed. (Step-iii) The agent receives the \emph{\textbf{partial} noisy observation} $\tto_h^k$ for the other $d-\td$ sub-states that are not queried, where $\tto_h^k$ is generated according to the \emph{partial} emission probability $\tilde{\mbo}_h^{\hi_h^k}\left(\cdot \big\vert \{\phi_i(s_h^k)\}_{\{i\notin \hi_h^k\}}\right)$. The partial emission matrix $\tio_h^{\hi} \in \mbr^{\oo \times \calsss^{d-\td}}$ satisfies the \emph{partially} revealing condition: there exists a constant $\alpha > 0$, such that $\sigma_{\ts}(\tio_h^{\hi}) \geq \alpha$ for any sub-states $\hi$ and step $h$, where $\ts = \calsss^{d-\td}$ and $\sigma_{\ts}(\cdot)$ denotes the $\ts$-th largest singular value of a matrix. Namely, $\min_{\{h,\hi\}} \sigma_{\ts}(\tio_h^{\hi}) \geq \alpha$ holds. 
(Step-iv) The agent chooses a sub-state $i_h^k \in \hi_h^k$, takes an action $a_h^k$, and receives a reward $r_h(\phi_{i_h^k}(s_h^k),a_h^k)$. (Step-v) The next state $s_{h+1}^k$ is drawn according to the joint (possibly correlated) transition probability $\mbp_h(\cdot \vert s_h^k,a_h^k)$.

\end{subclass}

We make three claims below. (i) In contrast to classic POMDPs\footnote{A vein of work revolves around decision making with the decision-estimation coefficient~\citep{foster2021statistical,chen2022unified}, which differs from our focus on the value of partial OSI.}~\citep{chen2022partially,liu2022partially,liu2023optimistic}, the partial noisy observation $\tto_{h}^k$ in~\cref{subclass2} \emph{depends on the query policy, whose outputs further affect the action policy}. These two new dependencies require non-trivial developments in the algorithm design and regret analysis. (ii) The \emph{partially revealing condition in Step-iii of~\cref{subclass2} is milder than the weakly revealing condition} in~\cite{liu2022partially} that requires $\min_{h} \sigma_{\st}(\mbo_h) \geq \alpha$, where $\st=\calsss^d$ is the total number of states and $\mbo_h \in \mbr^{\oo \times \calsss^{d}}$ is the emission matrix that we introduced in~\cref{subsec:genericpomdp}. This is because for an $m\times n$ matrix $\mathbb{A}$ and an $m\times (n-l)$ sub-matrix $\mathbb{B}$ of $\mathbb{A}$, we have that $\sigma_{i+l}(\mathbb{A}) \leq \sigma_i(\mathbb{B})$~\cite{horn1994topics}. (iii) \emph{Without the partially revealing condition} in Step-ii of~\cref{subclass2}, the POMDP with partial OSI is intractable in the worst case. This can be shown by letting the partial emission probability $\tio_h^{\hi}$ of each $\hi$ be the same for all possible queries.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Optimistic MLE with Partial OSI}
\begin{algorithmic}
\STATE \textbf{Initialization:} $\Theta^0 = \{\theta\in \Theta: \min_{\{h,\hi\}} \sigma_{\ts}(\tio_h^{\hi}) \geq \alpha\}$.
\FOR{$k=1:\kk$}
\STATE \textit{Step-1:} Estimate the problem model $\hth \triangleq (\hbp,\hbo,\hdl)$: 
\begin{align}\label{eq:mlealgomleposi}
& \Theta^k = \Theta^0 \cap \Big\{ \hth \in \Theta^0: \sum\nolimits_{\tau=1}^{k-1} \log P_{\hth}^{\pi^{\tau}}(\Gamma^{\tau}) \geq \nonumber \\
& \max\limits_{(\mbp',\tio',\Delta_1')\in \Theta^0} \sum\nolimits_{\tau=1}^{k-1} \log P_{\mbp',\tio',\Delta_1'}^{\pi^{\tau}}(\Gamma^{\tau}) - \beta \Big\}.
\end{align}
\STATE \textit{Step-2:} Update the joint policy $\pi^{k} \triangleq \arg\max_{\pi:\hth \in \Theta^k} \expect_{\{\pi_q,\pi_a,\Delta_1,\hth\}} [ \sum_{h=1}^{\hh} r_h(\phi_{i_h^k}(s_h^k),a_h^k)]$.
\FOR{$h=1:\hh$}
\STATE \textit{Step-3:} Query the partial OSI $\phi_{\hat{i}_h^k}(s_h^k)$ according to the query policy $\pi_{q,h}^{k}$. Collect partial noisy observation $\tto_h^k$. Specify one sub-state $i_h^k$ and take an action $a_h^k$ according to the action policy $\pi_{a,h}^k$.
\ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\label{alg:pomle}
\end{algorithm}



\subsection{Optimistic MLE with Partial OSI (\pomle)}

We develop a near-optimal algorithm for~\cref{subclass2}, called Optimistic Maximum Likelihood Estimation with Partial OSI (\pomle). See~\cref{alg:pomle}. The new challenge here is: \textbf{how to design the query policy, such that the combination of \emph{partial OSI} and \emph{partial noisy observations} guarantee the existence of a near-optimal OMLE solution?} More specifically, the difficulty lies in the non-trivial \emph{complexity structure of partial noisy observations under adaptively queried partial OSI}. To overcome this difficulty, our algorithm involves a non-trivial generalization of the observable operator method~\citep{liu2022partially,jaeger2000observable}.

\textbf{Idea-I (Partial-information-based bonus term):} In contrast to the noisy observation or full hindsight state information in standard POMDPs, only partial noisy observation is available in our case. To make it worse, it is affected by the adaptive query of the agent. Hence, when applying OMLE, we design \emph{a new bonus term} $\beta=O\left( (\calsss^{2d}\ac+\calsss^{d-\td}\oo) \ln(\calsss^{d}\ac\oo\hh\kk)\right)$, which depends on the size of the non-queried sub-state space $\calsss^{d-\td}$. This new bonus term is crucial due to the partial-information complexity. See Step-1 of~\cref{alg:pomle}, where $\ggam^{\tau} \triangleq \{\phi_{\hi_1^{\tau}}(s_1^{\tau}),\tto_1^{\tau},a_1^{\tau},...,\phi_{\hi_{\hh}^{\tau}}(s_{\hh}^{\tau}),\tto_{\hh}^{\tau},a_{\hh}^{\tau}\}$ denotes the feedback including partial noisy observations $\tto_{1:\hh}^{\tau}$. Moreover, \pomle~only estimates partial emission model $\tio$. 

\textbf{Idea-II (Bilevel query-and-action-policy optimization):} In contrast to standard POMDPs, the action policy $\pi_a$ here relies on the output of a query policy $\pi_q$. Thus, the query $\hi_h^k$ and action $a_h^k$ cannot be mapped to a single simple decision space. As a result, the reward maximization becomes a bilevel optimization problem (see Step-2 of~\cref{alg:pomle}).

\begin{theorem}\label{theorem:regretproblem3}

\textbf{(Regret)} For POMDPs with the partial OSI and partially revealing condition, with probability $1-\delta$, when $\calsss > (d / \td)^2$, the regret $\reg^{\text{\pomle}}(\kk)$ of~\pomle~can be upper-bounded by,
\begin{align}\nonumber
\tilde{O}\left( \calsss^{2d-\td}\oo\ac\hh^4 \sqrt{\kk ( \calsss^{2d}\ac+\calsss^{(d-\td)/2}\oo)} / \alpha^2 \right).
\end{align}

\end{theorem}

\cref{theorem:regretproblem3} shows that (i) the regret depends \emph{polynomially} on $\ac$, $\hh$ and $\calsss$. (ii) The regret \emph{further decreases} exponentially as $\td$ increases. (iii) The regret of \pomle~depends on $\sqrt{\kk}$, which is tight. \emph{To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such near-optimal result for POMDPs with partial OSI.} Recall that the \emph{non-trivial complexity structure of partial noisy observations under adaptively queried partial OSI} is the main difficulty in the proof of~\cref{theorem:regretproblem3}. Indeed, directly applying observable operator method (OOM)~\cite{jaeger2000observable,liu2022partially} will result in a regret that does not decrease with $\td$. Please see~\cref{app:pftheoremregretproblem3} for details.

Note that the only parameter that the above regret does not have a polynomial dependency on is $d$. Below, we provide a lower bound (see the proof in~\cref{app:pftheoremlowerboundproblem3} ), which shows that such a polynomial dependency on $d$ is \emph{impossible}.
\begin{theorem}\label{theorem:lowerboundproblem3}

\textbf{(Lower bound)} For POMDPs with partial OSI and partially revealing condition, the regret of any algorithm $\pi$ can be lower-bounded as follows,
\begin{align}\label{eq:lowerboundproblem3}
\reg^{\pi}(\kk) \geq \tilde{\Omega}\left( \sqrt{\ac\hh} \cdot \calsss^{d/2} \cdot \sqrt{\kk} \right).
\end{align}

\end{theorem}

Our key proof idea is to construct a special state transition, such that even with partial OSI, all combinations of sub-states must be explored to achieve a sub-linear regret. We conjecture a stronger lower bound depending on the \emph{query capability} $\tilde{\Omega}\big( \calsss^{(d-\td)/2} \big)$, and leave this as a future question.






\section{Discussion and Conclusion}


In this paper, we establish a lower bound that reveals a \emph{surprising hardness} result: unless we have \emph{full} OSI, we need an exponentially scaling sample complexity to obtain an $\epsilon$-optimal policy for POMDPs. Nonetheless, we identify two intriguing tractable subclasses of POMDPs with only \emph{partial} OSI, which are important in practice. For these two subclasses, we provide three new RL algorithms, which are shown to be \emph{near-optimal} by establishing new regret upper and lower bounds. There are several interesting future work. First, it would be interesting to study the value of partial OSI in more general POMDPs, e.g., with continuous state spaces~\cite{cai2022reinforcement,liu2023optimistic}. Second, the regret upper and lower bounds that we achieved could be further tightened, e.g., improve the dependency on $d$ and $\oo$ using ideas from~\cite{chen2023lower}.
