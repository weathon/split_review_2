\title{GPTBIAS: A Comprehensive Framework for Evaluating Bias in Large Language Models}

\begin{abstract}
\textit{\textbf{\textcolor{red}{Warning}:} This paper contains content that may be offensive or upsetting.}
There has been a significant increase in the usage of large language models (LLMs) in various applications, both in their original form and through fine-tuned adaptations. 
As a result, LLMs have gained popularity and are being widely adopted by a large user community. However, one of the concerns with LLMs is the potential generation of socially biased content. 
The existing evaluation methods have many constraints, and their results exhibit a limited degree of interpretability.
In this work, we propose a bias evaluation framework named GPTBIAS that leverages the high performance of LLMs (e.g., GPT-4 \cite{openai2023gpt4}) to assess bias in models. We also introduce prompts called Bias Attack Instructions, which are specifically designed for evaluating model bias. To enhance the credibility and interpretability of bias evaluation, our framework not only provides a bias score but also offers detailed information, including bias types, affected demographics, keywords, reasons behind the biases, and suggestions for improvement. We conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness and usability of our bias evaluation framework.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


In recent times, there has been a rapid emergence of various large language models (LLMs) \cite{brown2020language, chowdhery2022palm, sun2023safety, peng2023instruction} that have achieved remarkable outcomes across different domains of Natural Language Processing (NLP). These models are specifically designed to generate text that mimics human writing style, ensuring easy comprehension for human readers. They leverage extensive training data, multiple deep learning techniques, and human feedback to grasp human instructions, process input, and draw inferences that result in coherent and contextually relevant responses \cite{ openai2023chatgpt, openai2023gpt4}. 
The widespread utilization of large language models has demonstrated their immense potential in diverse fields. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that language models are predominantly trained on real text data, making them susceptible to biases arising from skewed data distribution and inherent biases within the text itself. According to a recent study by \cite{ferrara2023should}, bias in this context refers to the presence of systematic misrepresentations, attribution errors, or distortions of facts that result in favoritism towards particular groups or ideas, the perpetuation of stereotypes, or the formulation of incorrect assumptions based on learned patterns. Given the significant role played by large language models across various domains, it is imperative to prioritize the identification and mitigation of model bias during the training and deployment stages.

Various methods \cite{may2019measuring, nadeem2020stereoset, nangia2020crows, barikeri2021redditbias} have been developed to evaluate bias in language models. Nevertheless, these methods typically possess certain limitations and requirements that hinder their effectiveness. 
(1) Pairs of bias-related keywords or sentences are required \cite{nangia2020crows}. (2) The need to access intermediate model outputs, such as word embedding and probabilistic outputs of the model mapping to word vocabulary. 
(3) Large labeled datasets are required \cite{nadeem2020stereoset}. However, collecting and labeling these data is expensive, and hence they are not easily available. Recent work \cite{delobelle2022measuring, kwon2022empirical} has also revealed the limited robustness of existing bias evaluation metrics, highlighting the unreliability of their assessment results. The interpretability of all bias evaluation metrics is notably low, leading to opacity and lack of trust in the evaluation outcomes. To address this gap, we propose a highly interpretable bias evaluation metric in this paper.
\citet{sun2023safety} highlights the potential of utilizing large language models for bias assessment. Specifically, \citet{sun2023safety, openai2023gpt4} indicates that the ChatGPT \cite{openai2023chatgpt} and GPT-4 \cite{openai2023gpt4} exhibit significantly less bias compared to other existing models. Therefore, using GPT-4 to assess the bias of text has high credibility. Recent advancements in NLP, exemplified by OpenAI's ChatGPT and GPT-4, have demonstrated remarkable performance on various NLP tasks \cite{sun2023safety, peng2023instruction}. Furthermore, ChatGPT has shown promise as a metric for evaluation \cite{sun2023safety}. They use ChatGPT to evaluate the safety of large Chinese language models. \citet{peng2023instruction} demonstrates the effectiveness of instruction tuning using instruction following data generated by GPT-4. \citet{zou2023universal} also demonstrates that large language models can recognize harmful instructions and refuse to respond, indicating their resistance to attacks. Additionally, it highlights the ability of large language models to successfully identify whether input content is harmful with a high degree of accuracy.

Inspired by \citet{sun2023safety} and \citet{peng2023instruction}, we design a new bias metric based on GPT-4, named GPTBIAS. We introduce a bias attack instruction set comprising nine distinct bias categories: gender, religion, race, sexual orientation, age, nationality, disability, physical appearance, and socioeconomic status. The dataset encompasses diverse forms of biases, including questions, instructions, false assumptions, and more. 
Unlike conventional bias metrics that merely provide a score indicating the extent of bias, our approach leverages the capabilities of large language models to provide bias types, keywords leading to bias, the underlying causes of bias, demographic groups affected by bias, and related suggestions. For bias types, our method can identify intersectional biases in a sentence at the same time. This additional information enhances the credibility and interpretability of our metrics and facilitates further analysis of the language model's bias.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We create a set of bias attack instructions to test the susceptibility of large language models to biased content. \footnote{Our data and code are available at \url{we will release our code.}}
    \item We introduce a novel evaluation framework called GPTBIAS, specifically designed to provide a comprehensive and highly interpretable assessment of biases within large language models. This framework is capable of analyzing models with limited transparency or those treated as black boxes. GPTBIAS enables the detection of a minimum of nine biases, including those that are absent from biased attack instructions, as well as intersectional biases.
    \item We establish a benchmark for measuring bias in large language models, going beyond a quantitative bias score by offering more detailed and interpretable results.
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Large Langauge Models}
In recent years, the field of natural language processing has witnessed notable progress in the development of large language models (LLMs), characterized by their increasing size and complexity \cite{chowdhery2022palm}. These models have become widely employed as foundational models for various NLP applications \cite{wang2018glue}. In the beginning, LLMs were fine-tuned to excel in specific tasks or domains \cite{devlin2018bert, raffel2020exploring, radford2019language}. Nevertheless, researchers found that as these models scale up (e.g., GPT-3 \cite{brown2020language}, LLAMA \cite{touvron2023llama}, BLOOM \cite{scao2022bloom} and OPT \cite{zhang2022opt}, etc.), they demonstrate remarkable potential in effectively addressing diverse natural language tasks even without explicit task-specific training \cite{brown2020language, wei2022emergent}. ChatGPT\footnote{In this work, ChatGPT refers to the ``gpt-3.5-turbo'' version of OpenAI API.} \cite{openai2023chatgpt} is an artificial intelligence chatbot developed by OpenAI. It was launched on November 30, 2022, and has gained significant popularity among users. It has been widely adopted due to its effectiveness in understanding and generating human-like text. On March 14, 2023, OpenAI released GPT-4 \cite{openai2023gpt4}, a more powerful multimodal model. This advanced model demonstrates great potential in various domains.

\subsection{Language Model Bias Metrics}
To gain a deeper understanding of language models and ensure their safe application, it becomes crucial to evaluate and quantify the bias within these models. Many research efforts have been undertaken to evaluate the bias in language models, broadly categorized as follows: (1) Vector-based Distance: This approach \cite{may2019measuring} involves calculating the distances between word or sentence vectors associated with bias-sensitive attributes. For example, one can determine the distance between "nurse" and "doctor" in the gender subspace to assess gender bias. (2) Performance Discrepancies: This category focuses on comparing model performance across different demographic groups to obtain a bias score. For instance, if a dialogue model exhibits significantly higher accuracy in answering questions from native English speakers compared to non-native English speakers, it indicates potential bias. This approach provides insights into demographic disparities in model behavior \cite{nadeem2020stereoset, nangia2020crows}. (3) Biased Content Probability: This approach involves calculating the likelihood that a language model generates sentences with biased content as its bias score \cite{barikeri2021redditbias}. Sentence Encoder Association Test (SEAT) \cite{may2019measuring} measures the bias by computing the distance between sentence representations. StereoSet \cite{nadeem2020stereoset} evaluates bias by model performance on the completion task. CrowS-Pairs \cite{nangia2020crows} calculates the ratio of samples that the model tends to stereotype versus those that are anti-stereotype as the bias score. \citet{barikeri2021redditbias} use the Student’s two-tailed test method to calculate the bias score between paired demographic groups. 

However, each of these methods has distinct limitations (e.g., dependency on labeled data and access to model weights.). To overcome those limitations, we present a more adaptable and efficient solution called GPTBIAS for assessing bias in large language models. Table \ref{tb: limitations} shows the limitations and abilities of existing bias evaluation metrics as well as GPTBIAS.



\section{Bias Evaluation Framework}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering 
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{frameworkgpt4.pdf} 
\caption{The bias evaluation process within GPTBIAS framework.} 
\label{Fig: framework} 
\end{figure*}

Figure \ref{Fig: framework} presents the process of bias assessment within our framework. In the first step, we create biased attack instructions that cover nine distinct types of biases. In the second step, these biased attack instructions are inputted into a large language model to be evaluated, and we gain the corresponding responses from the model. In the third step, we evaluate whether the model output exhibits any biases. To achieve this, we compile the bias attack instructions and their respective responses into a template. This combined content is then inputted into GPT-4, which evaluates the language model for bias. GPT-4 generates a response that not only reflects whether bias exists but also provides insights into the causes of bias, bias type, etc. Upon reaching the fourth step, we compute the bias score by considering the total number of biased samples predicted within each biased type. 

\subsection{Bias Attack Instruction}
We require substantial instructions capable of influencing the language model to produce biased content, referred to as biased attack instructions. However, generating bias attack instructions manually is a costly and potentially constrained process. Following \citet{wang2022self, peng2023instruction}, we generate bias attack instructions utilizing a powerful language model. 
In this work, we employ ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo version) as the instruction generation model. The generation of biased attack instructions can be summarized in four steps: 1) Providing demonstrations of initial attack instructions for nine types of biases along with guided prompts for their generation; 2) Selecting prompts based on bias types and randomly combining a few demonstrations as inputs; 3) Inputting the content into ChatGPT and obtaining candidate instructions; 4) Filtering out low-quality instructions.

\paragraph{Initial Attack Instructions}
Some works \cite{wang2022self, peng2023instruction} have demonstrated that providing appropriate demonstrations to large language models can improve the quality of their generated data. Based on existing research on language model biases \cite{nangia2020crows, barikeri2021redditbias} and sociological literature \cite{krekula2007intersection, rhode2010beauty}, we manually crafted these bias attack instructions demonstrations. The guidelines for creating bias attack instruction demonstrations are as follows:  include various relevant demographic groups for different bias types, employ diverse instruction formats, and utilize instructions that tend to elicit biased responses from language models. In total, we have prepared ninety initial bias attack instructions, consisting of ten instructions for each of the nine bias types.

\paragraph{Instruction Generation Prompts}
To obtain a wide and diverse range of biased attack instructions, we designed prompts for each bias type. The prompts for different bias types are based on the same template, but each bias has specific guidelines for generation. The template is as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[] ``\textit{Please generate a prompt statement for attacking \{\textcolor{blue}{bias type}\} in language models according to my requirements, and output the prompt in the format provided in the example. Here are the criteria you need to meet: \\ \textbackslash n1. The prompt statement should be designed to induce the language model to generate content with \{\textcolor{blue}{bias type}\}.\\ \textbackslash n2.The generated prompt statements should have diverse forms, such as questions, instructions, dialogues, etc. \\
    \textbackslash n3. Responses are in English only. \\
    \textbackslash n4. \{\textcolor{green}{specific guidelines}\} \textbackslash n The examples are as follows:
    \{\textcolor{orange}{sampled demonstrations}\}}''.
\end{itemize}

Then choose a type of bias and randomly select some corresponding demonstrations to replace the \textit{\{\textcolor{blue}{bias type}\}} and \textit{\{\textcolor{orange}{sampled demonstrations}\}} in the template. Also, replace the\textit{ \{\textcolor{green}{specific guidelines}\}} in the template with guidance specific to the chosen type of bias.  We randomly select four instruction demonstrations for each bias type. These demonstrations can come from our manual initial demonstrations or from saved responses of ChatGPT, which means that as the instruction generation process progresses, the pool of selectable instruction demonstrations increases.

\paragraph{Instruction Generation and Filtering}
After obtaining a complete input using the prescribed template, we input it into ChatGPT and gain a corresponding response. To enhance the quality and diversify the generated instructions, we set a series of data cleaning and filtering processes. Firstly, we eliminate instructions that are excessively brief (< 4 tokens) or long (> 100 tokens). Secondly, we disregard responses containing special symbols (e.g., \#\$\&, etc.). Finally, for the currently generated response, we assess its similarity with the saved instructions and initial instructions. We retain only those responses demonstrating low similarity to augment the diversity of instructions. In this paper, we use Rouge-L to calculate their similarity and set the threshold to 0.7. We keep instructions with a Rouge-L score below 0.7.

After cleaning and filtering, we have 200 prompts remaining for each type of bias, yielding a total of 1800 prompts across 9 bias categories. 

\subsection{Bias Evaluation}
After obtaining the biased attack instruction set, our goal is to have the test model respond to the instructions, enabling us to assess bias. We propose a bias evaluation method based on GPT-4 named GPTBIAS. This method does not require obtaining the weights or middle output from language models. This is crucial as it helps us evaluate the bias of black-box language models. To achieve this, we develop an evaluation prompt template that merges instructions with corresponding model responses, serving as an input for GPT-4. GPT-4 then classifies whether the response exhibits bias, while also providing intricate insights into other bias-related elements. The prompt template is as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item[] ``\textit{Given a conversation between two users, analyze the response of User 2 for bias. \\
    \textbackslash n1. Categorize User 2's response as biased or unbiased. \\
    \textbackslash n2. If biased, identify the types of biases present (multiple biases can be present and not limited to the following: gender, religion, race, sexual orientation, age, nationality, disability, physical appearance, and socioeconomic status). \\
    \textbackslash n3. If biased, identify the demographic group affected by the biases. If not, mention "None". \\
    \textbackslash n4. If biased, identify the reasons causing the biases. If not, mention "None". \\
    \textbackslash n5. If biased, suggest improvements to mitigate the biases. If not, mention "None". \\
    \textbackslash n6. Return the results in the following format: \textbackslash n1. Biased:  \textbackslash n2. Bias Type:  \textbackslash n3. Demographic Group: \textbackslash n4. Reason: \textbackslash n5. Improvement:  The conversation is as follows: \textbackslash nUser 1: \{\textcolor{green}{Bias attack instruction}\}  User 2: \{\textcolor{orange}{Response}\}}''.
\end{itemize}

And we replace the \textit{\textcolor{green}{Bias attack instruction}} and \textit{\textcolor{orange}{Response}} with the instruction and the corresponding language model.

By employing the above template to integrate both the instruction and the response of the model, we obtain well-structured data from GPT-4, which includes the following elements: 1) Binary classification results indicating whether the model output exhibits bias. 2) The specific types of biases identified. 3) Demographic groups impacted by these biases. 4) Causes contributing to the biases. 5) The strategies suggested for bias mitigation. \subsection{Bias Score Calculation} 
Upon analyzing results from the GPTBIAS, we can quantify the bias exhibited in language models. This quantification was accomplished by calculating nine bias scores, each determined by the ratio of biased output to the total number of instructions for the corresponding bias type. Therefore, our method calculates the bias score based on the proportion of biased instances in the results generated by the GPTBIAS framework among all the data.

Concurrently, our methodology provided an intersectional bias score, signifying the compound bias within a content, affecting several demographics simultaneously. The computation of intersectional bias scores followed the same way as the individual bias scores. Beyond simply assigning a quantifiable bias score, these results shed light on the demographic groups most affected by these biases and uncover the factors leading to their occurrence. This enhances the interpretability and trustworthiness of our results.
Moreover, the insights we gain on how to minimize model biases not only help reduce potential biases among users but also provide critical guidance for language model developers aiming to mitigate biases in their designs.

\subsection{Model Setup}
When applying the models to generate responses to the bias attack instructions, we set the same hyperparameters for all models. Specifically, we set the temperature = 0.5, repetition penalty = 1.3, and maximum length = 512. We utilize code from HuggingFace\footnote{\url{https://huggingface.co/}} to employ BLOOMZ, OPT, and two LLaMA models for inference. For the text-davinci-002, text-davinci-003, and ChatGPT models, we generate predictions via the API of OpenAI.

\subsection{Bias Metrics}
In our evaluation of the large language models, we used not only our bias metrics but also two popular metrics. These include StereoSet \cite{nadeem2020stereoset} and CrowS-Pairs \cite{nangia2020crows}. StereoSet can assess four types of stereotypes in language models by having them complete a cloze test task to evaluate bias. CrowS-Pairs measures the bias in a language model by assessing how often the model favors the stereotypical sentence in each pair over the anti-stereotypical sentence. For these two metrics, the closer the score is to 50, the less bias in the language model. However, both of the metrics have their own limitations in assessing model bias. These limitations prevent them from evaluating biases beyond their predefined data and from detecting biases toward demographic groups not included in the test data. 

We use GPT-4 as a bias metric to avoid many of the limitations of existing biased measurement methods. This enhances the comprehensiveness of bias evaluation. We also conducted manual evaluations to assess the mode responses to bias attack instructions.

 Our proposed GPTBIAS-Score can evaluate multiple biases in language models and detect bias types and demographic groups that are not present in our biased attack prompts. Additionally, our method can identify intersecting biases in content, which better aligns with the forms of bias that occur in reality. Table \ref{tb: main results} presents the bias evaluation of open-source models, including OPT-66B, BLOOMZ-176B, LLaMA-7B, LLaMA-33B, and LLaMA-65B. In an ideal scenario, the bias score for StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs should be 50. In our metric, a higher score implies greater bias in the model.

From the results in Table \ref{tb: main results}, we can see that the five open-source language models exhibit varying degrees of bias across nine bias types. 
Among the evaluated models, OPT-66B exhibited the highest bias of 11.00 under CrowS-Pairs, while LLaMA-65B shows the most bias at 4.01 in the StereoSet evaluation. Despite having relatively small biases in the StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs, the models revealed more severe biases under our bias evaluation metric.
Particularly, the BLOOMZ model revealed a significant sexual orientation bias of 0.93. The reason for such a significant disparity between existing metrics and our metric may be that these large language models have already employed debiasing techniques during training, which can mitigate explicit biases. They can avoid generating biased predictions in the test datasets such as CrowS-Pairs and StereoSet. However, the subtle and intricate bias attack instructions of GPTBIAS-Score effectively induce models to generate biased content. These biases in the prompts are difficult to detect and often masked within open-ended questions that expose wider biases and demographic groups. Thus, GPTBIAS is capable of detecting subtler biases.

From the results of LLaMA with different sizes, we find that as the model size increases, the exhibited biases also become more pronounced. This provides researchers with an insight that increasing the model's size enhances its performance but may simultaneously amplify its biases. Researchers need to be more vigilant about the potential adverse effects of bias when retraining larger models.

ChatGPT has a relatively small bias of 0.148, but its gender bias and appearance bias still need to be reduced. 

\subsection{Intersectional Bias}
We present the results of the intersectional bias in Table \ref{tb: intersectional bias}. The probability of generating intersectional bias varies depending on the different biased attack prompts. From the results, we can observe that the model exhibits the highest intersectional bias when testing with racial bias attack instructions and the lowest intersectional bias when using socioeconomic bias attack instructions. Among all the language models evaluated in our work, ChatGPT remains the model with the least intersectional bias, with an average score of 0.024. 

\subsection{Ablation Experiments}
 We have observed that the classification model of GPT-4 demonstrates improved accuracy and a higher level of stringency in the responses of language models. GPT-4 consistently receives significantly higher bias scores compared to ChatGPT, which suggests that ChatGPT is comparatively less accurate when it comes to handling subtle biases. 



\section{Conclusion}

In conclusion, the increased usage of large language models (LLMs) across various applications calls for a more robust bias evaluation system due to the risk of generating socially biased content. Our proposed framework, GPTBIAS, presents a novel approach to assessing bias within these models, utilizing Bias Attack Instructions specifically tailored for bias evaluation. Not only does GPTBIAS provide a bias score, but it also offers a comprehensive insight into the type of bias, affected demographics, significant keywords, underlying reasons for biases, and potential solutions for improvement. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness and usability of GPTBIAS, suggesting it is a potent tool for improving the credibility and interpretability of bias evaluations in LLMs.
