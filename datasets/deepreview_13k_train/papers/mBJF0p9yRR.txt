\title{Almost Sure Convergence of Average Reward Temporal Difference Learning}

\begin{abstract}
Tabular average reward Temporal Difference (TD) learning
is perhaps the simplest and the most fundamental policy evaluation algorithm in average reward reinforcement learning.
After at least 25 years since its discovery,
we are finally able to provide a long-awaited almost sure convergence analysis.
Namely,
we are the first to prove that, under very mild conditions, 
tabular average reward TD converges almost surely to a sample-path dependent fixed point.
Key to this success is a new general stochastic approximation result concerning nonexpansive mappings with Markovian and additive noise,
built on recent advances in stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


Temporal Difference learning (TD, \citet{sutton1988learning}) is the most fundamental algorithm in Reinforcement Learning (RL, \citet{sutton2018reinforcement}).
In this paper,
we investigate the almost sure convergence of TD in its simplest form with a tabular representation,
in average reward Markov Decision Processes (MDPs, \citet{bellman1957markovian,puterman2014markov}).
Namely,
we investigate the following iterative updates
\begin{align}
    \label{eq: avg td update}
    J_{t+1} &= J_t + \beta_{t+1} \paren{R_{t+1} - J_t}, \tag{Average Reward TD}\\ 
    v_{t+1}\paren{S_t} &= v_t\paren{S_{t}} + \alpha_{t+1} \paren{R_{t+1}  - J_t + v_t\paren{S_{t+1}} - v_t\paren{S_t} },  
\end{align}
where $\qty{S_0, R_1, S_1, \dots}$ is a sequence of states and rewards from an MDP with a fixed policy and a finite state space $\fS$,
$J_t \in \R$ is the scalar estimate of the average reward,
$v_t \in \R^{\ns}$ is the tabular value estimate,
and $\qty{\alpha_t, \beta_t}$ are learning rates.
This iterative update algorithm, known as average reward TD,
dates back to at least \citet{tsitsiklis1999average}. Surprisingly, despite its simplicity and fundamental importance, its almost sure convergence had not been established in the 25 years since its inception until this work.
Even more surprisingly, the theoretical analysis of average reward TD with linear function approximation has seen more progress than that of the tabular version we consider here. 
In this paper, after presenting the necessary background in Section~\ref{sec:background}, we will elaborate on the difficulty in analyzing tabular average reward TD with existing techniques in Section \ref{sec:hardness}, offering insight into why progress on this topic has been unexpectedly slow.
Then we proceed to our central contribution, where we prove that under mild conditions,
the iterates $\qty{v_t}$ in~\eqref{eq: avg td update} converge almost surely to a sample-path-dependent fixed point. 

This almost sure convergence is achieved by extending recent advances in the convergence analysis of Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann (SKM) iterations \citep{bravo2019rates,bravo2024stochastic} to settings with Markovian and additive noise.
This line of research originates from the seminal work \citet{cominetti2014rate},
which introduces a novel fox-and-hare race model to analyze Krasnoselskii-Mann (KM) iterations \citep{krasnosel1955two}. By extending this line of work to Markovian settings, we not only establish the almost sure convergence of average reward TD, but also pave the way for further analysis of other RL algorithms through the lens of SKM iterations. %\sz{Cite the origin of KM.}


\section{Background}

In this paper, all vectors are column.
We use $\norm{\cdot}$ to denote a generic operator norm and use $e$ to denote an all-one vector. 
We use $\norm{\cdot}_2$ and $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$ to denote $\ell_2$ norm and infinity norm respectively.
We use $\fO(\cdot)$ to hide deterministic constants for simplifying presentation,
while the letter $\zeta$ is reserved for sample-path dependent constants.

In reinforcement learning, we consider an MDP with a finite state space $\fS$,
a finite action space $\fA$,
a reward function $r: \fS \times \fA \to \R$,
a transition function $p: \fS \times \fS \times \fA \to [0, 1]$,
an initial distribution $p_0: \fS \to [0, 1]$.
At time step $0$,
an initial state $S_0$ is sampled from $p_0$.
At time $t$,
given the state $S_t$,
the agent samples an action $A_t \sim \pi(\cdot | S_t)$, 
where $\pi: \fA \times \fS \to [0, 1]$ is the policy being followed by the agent.
A reward $R_{t+1} \doteq r(S_t, A_t)$ is then emitted and the agent proceeds to a successor state $S_{t+1} \sim p(\cdot | S_t, A_t)$. 
In the rest of the paper, we will assume the Markov chain $\qty{S_t}$ induced by the policy $\pi$ is irreducible and thus adopts a unique stationary distribution $d_\mu$.
The average reward (a.k.a. gain, \citet{puterman2014markov}) is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \textstyle \bar{J}_{\pi} \doteq \lim_{T\rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \E\left[R_t\right].
\end{equation}
Correspondingly,
the differential value function (a.k.a. bias, \citet{puterman2014markov}) is defined as 
\begin{align}
    \textstyle v_\pi(s) \doteq \lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{T} \sum_{\tau=1}^T \E\left[ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau} (R_{t+i} - \bar{J}_{\pi}) \mid S_t = s\right].
\end{align}
The corresponding Bellman equation (a.k.a. Poisson's equation) is then
\begin{align}
    \label{eq bellman equation}
    v = r_\pi -\bar{J}_{\pi}e + P_\pi v ,
\end{align} 
where $v \in \R^\ns$ is the free variable,
$r_\pi \in \R^\ns$ is the reward vector induced by the policy $\pi$, i.e., $r_\pi(s) \doteq \sum_a \pi(a|s) r(s, a)$, and $P_\pi \in \R^{\ns \times \ns}$ is the transition matrix induced by the policy $\pi$, i.e., $P_\pi(s, s') \doteq \pi(a|s)p(s'|s, a)$. 
It is known \citep{puterman2014markov} that all solutions to~\eqref{eq bellman equation} form a set 
\begin{align}
    \label{eq fixed points}
    \fV_* \doteq \qty{v_\pi + ce \mid c \in \R}.
\end{align}

The policy evaluation problem in average reward MDPs is to estimate $v_\pi$,
perhaps up to a constant offset $ce$.
In view of~\eqref{eq bellman equation} and inspired by the success of TD in the discounted setting \citep{sutton1988learning},
\citet{tsitsiklis1999average} use~\eqref{eq: avg td update} to estimate $v_\pi$ (up to a constant offset).
In~\eqref{eq: avg td update},
$J_t$ estimates the average reward $\bar J_\pi$.
Its learning rate, $\beta_t$, does not need to be the same as $\alpha_t$,
the learning rate for updating the differential value function estimation.  



\section{Hardness of Average Reward TD}


To elaborate on the hardness in analyzing~\eqref{eq: avg td update},
we first rewrite it in a compact form.
Define the augmented Markov chain $Y_{t+1} \doteq (S_t, A_t, S_{t+1})$.
It is easy to see that $\qty{Y_t}$ evolves in the finite space
$\fY \doteq \qty{(s, a, s') \mid \pi(a|s) > 0, p(s'|s, a) > 0}$.
We then define a function $H: \R^\ns \times \fY \to \R^\ns$ by defining the $s$-th element of $H(v, (s_0, a_0, s_1))$ as
\begin{align}
    H(v, (s_0, a_0, s_1))[s] \doteq \mathbb{I}\qty{s = s_0} \left(r(s_0, a_0) - \bar J_\pi + v(s_1) - v(s_0)\right) + v(s). \label{eq: H avg rew td update}
\end{align}
Then, the update to $\qty{v_t}$ in~\eqref{eq: avg td update} can then be expressed as
\begin{align}
    \label{eq avg td update compact}
    v_{t+1} = v_t + \alpha_{t+1} \left(H(v_t, Y_{t+1}) - v_t + \epsilon_{t+1}\right).
\end{align}
Here, $\epsilon_{t+1} \in \R^\ns$ is the random noise vector defined as
$\epsilon_{t+1}(s) \doteq \mathbb{I}\qty{s = S_t} (J_t - \bar J_\pi)$.
This $\epsilon_{t+1}$ is the current estimate error of the average reward estimator $J_t$.
Intuitively, the indicator $\mathbb{I}\qty{s = S_t}$ reflects the asynchronous nature of~\eqref{eq: avg td update}.
For each $t$, 
only the $S_t$-indexed element in $v_t$ is updated.
To better analyze~\eqref{eq avg td update compact},
we investigate the expectation of $H$.
We define
\begin{align}
    \label{eq: expected h}
    h(v) \doteq& \E_{s_0 \sim d_\mu, a_0 \sim \pi(\cdot | s_0), s_1 \sim p(\cdot |s_0, s_1)}\left[H(v, (s_0, a_0, s_1))\right] \\
    =& D(r_\pi - \bar J_\pi e + P_\pi v - v) + v,
\end{align}
where $D \in \R^{\ns \times \ns}$ is a diagonal matrix with the diagonal being the stationary distribution $d_\mu$.
Then~\eqref{eq avg td update compact} can be viewed as Euler's discretization of the ODE
\begin{align}
    \label{eq expected ode}
    \dv{v(t)}{t} = h(v(t)) - v(t).
\end{align}
\paragraph*{Hardness in Stability.}
Stability (i.e., $\sup_t \norm{v_t} < \infty$ almost surely) is a necessary condition for almost sure convergence.
In ODE based stochastic approximation methods to establish almost sure convergence,
the first step is usually to establish the stability \citep{DBLP:books/sp/BenvenisteMP90,kushner2003stochastic,borkar2009stochastic}.
The ODE@$\infty$ technique \citep{borkar2000ode,borkar2021ode,liu2024ode} is perhaps one of the most powerful stability techniques in RL,
which considers the function 
\begin{align}
    h_\infty(v) \doteq \lim_{c\to\infty} \frac{h(cv)}{c} = D(P_\pi - I)v +v.
\end{align}
Correspondingly,
the ODE@$\infty$ is defined as  
\begin{align}
    \label{eq ode infity}
    \dv{v(t)}{t} = h_\infty(v(t))-v(t) = D(P_\pi - I)v(t).
\end{align}
If the ODE \eqref{eq ode infity} is globally asymptotically stable,
existing results such as \citet{borkar2021ode,liu2024ode} can be used to establish the desired stability of $\qty{v_t}$.
Unfortunately,
the vector $ce$ with any $c \in \R$ is an equilibrium of~\eqref{eq ode infity}, so it cannot be globally asymptotically stable.
This problem comes from the lack of a discounting factor in average reward MDPs.
In the discounted setting, with a discount factor $\gamma \in [0, 1)$,
the corresponding ODE@$\infty$ is $\dv{v(t)}{t} = D(\gamma P_\pi - I)v(t)$.
It is well known that $D(\gamma P_\pi - I)$ is negative definite \citep{tsitsiklis1997analysis} and therefore Hurwitz.
As a result, it is globally asymptotically stable.
Alternatively,
if a discount factor is present,
an inductive argument can also be used to establish stability following the method in \cite{gosavi2006boundedness}.
However, in the average reward setting,
there is no discounting, so neither the Hurwitz argument nor the inductive argument applies.

\paragraph*{Hardness in Convergence.}
Suppose we were somehow able to establish the desired stability,
then standard stochastic approximation results can be used to show that $\qty{v_t}$ converge almost surely to a bounded invariant set of the ODE~\eqref{eq expected ode},
or more precisely speaking,
a possibly sample-path dependent compact connected internally chain transitive invariant set\footnote{We refer the reader to Chapter 2 of \citet{borkar2009stochastic} for the definition of a connected internally chain transitive invariant set.} \citep{kushner2003stochastic,borkar2009stochastic}.
Unfortunately, we are not aware of any finer characterization of this set. 
Even if it was proved that this set must be a subset of $\fV_*$ in~\eqref{eq fixed points} (we are not aware of any such proof yet),
the best we could say is still that $\qty{v_t}$ converges to this set.
It is still possible that $\qty{v_t}$ oscillates within this set or around the neighborhood of this set and never settles down on any particular fixed point.
This gives rise to the central open question that this paper aims to answer:
\begin{center}
    can we prove that $\qty{v_t}$ converge almost surely to a single fixed point in $\fV_*$?
\end{center}
We shall give an affirmative answer shortly. 
We note that this affirmative answer is quite intuitive.
Notice that
\begin{align}
    h(v) = (I + D(P_\pi - I)) v + D(r_\pi - \bar J_\pi e). \label{eq: h linear in v}
\end{align}
It is easy to verify that $I + D(P_\pi - I)$ is a stochastic matrix.
It then follows that $\norm{I + D(P_\pi - I)}_\infty = 1$.
As a result,
the operator $h$
is a nonexpansive mapping w.r.t. $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$ (Lemma \ref{lem: h nonexpansive}).
Since the ODE~\eqref{eq expected ode} can be expressed as
\begin{align}
    \dv{v(t)}{t} = h(v(t)) - v(t),
\end{align}
the nonexpansivity of $h$ confirms that any solution $v(t)$ to the ODE~\eqref{eq expected ode} will converge to an initial value dependent fixed point in $\fV_*$ (Theorem 3.1 of \citet{borkar1997analog}).
So intuitively,
if $\qty{v_t}$ approximates a solution $v(t)$ well,
it should also converge to a single fixed point.
However,
existing ODE-based convergence analysis is limited, as it only establishes convergence to a bounded invariant set.
This difficulty stems from two sources.
The first is still the lack of the discount factor $\gamma$ in average reward MDPs.
Otherwise, $h$ can easily be a contraction, and the ODE~\eqref{eq expected ode} would then be globally asymptotically stable.
As a result, the invariant set would be a singleton.
The second is the lack of a reference value \citep{abounadi2001learning}.
In a recent differential TD algorithm \citep{wan2020learning},
the corresponding ODE is
\begin{align}
    \label{eq diff td ode}
    \dv{v(t)}{t} = r_\pi - ee^\top v(t) + P_\pi v(t) - v(t),
\end{align}
where $ee^\top v(t)$ serves as a reference value to stabilize the trajectories.
\cite{wan2020learning} prove that the ODE~\eqref{eq diff td ode} is globally asymptotically stable.
As a result, 
its invariant set is a singleton.
We note that to use this reference value technique in learning algorithms such as \citet{abounadi2001learning,wan2020learning},
we have to replace the learning rate $\alpha_t$ with a count-based learning rate $\alpha_{n(Y_t, t)}$.
Here $n(y, t) \doteq \sum_{\tau=0}^t \mathbb{I}\qty{Y_\tau = y}$ counts the number of visits to the a state $y$ until time $t$.
The detailed benefits (in terms of convergence) of this count-based learning rate are beyond the scope of this work, and we refer the reader to Chapter 7 of \citet{borkar2009stochastic} for more discussion.
Here, we only argue that
this count based learning rate is unnatural compared with the straightforward $\alpha_t$,
and it cannot be used once function approximation is introduced.
Alternatively,
to make use of this reference value technique without a count-based learning rate,
one has to resort to synchronous updates,
where at each time step $t$, all elements of $v_t$, not just $v(S_t)$, are updated \citep{zhang2021finite,bravo2024stochastic}.
Such synchronous updates are impossible when we have access to only one Markovian data stream $\qty{S_0, A_0, R_0, S_1, \dots}$.

\paragraph*{Hardness with Linear Function Approximation.}
The~\eqref{eq: avg td update} has also been extended to linear function approximation \citep{tsitsiklis1999average,konda2000actor,wu2020finite,zhang2021finite}.
Unfortunately,
the results in linear function approximation do not contribute much to the understanding of the tabular version.
In the following paragraphs,
we elaborate on this surprising fact.

Instead of using a look-up table $v \in \R^\ns$ to store the value estimate,
the idea of linear function approximation is to approximate $v(s)$ with $\phi(s)^\top w$,
where $\phi: \fS \to \R^K$ is the feature function mapping a state $s$ to a $K$-dimensional feature $\phi(s) \in \R^K$ and $w$ is the learnable weights.
Let $\Phi \in \R^{\ns \times K}$ be the feature matrix,
whose $s$-th row is the $\phi(s)^\top$.
Then, linear function approximation essentially uses $\Phi w$ to approximate $v$.
It is obvious that if $\Phi = I$ (i.e., a one-hot feature encoding is used),
linear function approximation degenerates to the tabular method.
Thus, one would expect the results in linear function approximation to subsume tabular results.
This is true in most settings, but for~\eqref{eq: avg td update} there is some subtlety.
The linear average reward TD \citep{tsitsiklis1999average} updates $\qty{w_t}$ iteratively as
\begin{align}
    \label{eq linear avg td update}
    w_{t+1} = w_t + \alpha_{t+1} \left(R_{t+1} - J_t + \phi(S_{t+1})^\top w_t - \phi(S_t)^\top w_t\right)\phi(S_t),
\end{align}
where the update of $\qty{J_t}$ is identical to~\eqref{eq: avg td update}.
This update can be viewed as the Euler's discretization of the ODE
\begin{align}
    \label{eq linear avg td ode}
    \dv{w(t)}{t} = \Phi^\top D(P_\pi - I)\Phi v(t) + \Phi^\top D(r_\pi - \bar J_\pi e).
\end{align}
Unfortunately, the matrix $\Phi^\top D(P_\pi - I)\Phi$ is not necessarily Hurwitz. 
Consequently,
the ODE~\eqref{eq linear avg td ode} is not necessarily globally asymptotically stable.
The problem still arises from the lack of a discount factor -- it is well known that $\Phi^\top D(\gamma P_\pi - I)\Phi$ is negative definite and thus Hurwitz.

Nevertheless,
to proceed with the theoretical analysis,
besides the standard assumption that $\Phi$ has linearly independent columns,
\citet{tsitsiklis1999average,konda2000actor} further assume that for any $c \in \R, w \in \R^d$, it holds that $\Phi w \neq ce$.
Under this assumption,
\citet{tsitsiklis1999average} prove that $\Phi^\top D(P_\pi - I)\Phi$ is negative definite (\citet{wu2020finite} assume this negative definiteness directly) and the iterates $\qty{w_t}$ converges almost surely.
Unfortunately,
this additional assumption does not hold in the tabular setting where $\Phi = I$ (apparently, $Ie=e$).
As a result,
the almost sure convergence in \citet{tsitsiklis1999average} does not shed light on the behavior of tabular average reward TD.
A more recent work \citet{zhang2021finite} proves that
\begin{align}
    \label{eq linear avg td nd}
    \min_{\norm{w}_2 = 1, w \in E} w^\top \Phi^\top D(P_\pi - I)\Phi w > 0
\end{align}
without requiring $\Phi w \neq ce$,
where $E$ is a subspace of $\R^K$.
Based on this,
\citet{zhang2021finite} prove that
\begin{align}
    \E\left[\abs{J_t - \bar J_\pi}^2 + \norm{\Pi_E(w_t - w_*)}_2^2\right]
\end{align}
converges to 0, 
where $w_*$ is one desired fixed point and $\Pi_E$ denotes the orthogonal projection onto the subspace $E$.
\cite{zhang2021finite} further provide a convergence rate.
This is a significant improvement over \citet{tsitsiklis1999average}, but still not satisfactory in two aspects.
First,
this result is convergence in $L^2$,
not almost sure convergence.
It is well known that almost sure convergence and $L^2$ convergence usually do not imply each other.
It is also not clear whether~\eqref{eq linear avg td nd} can be used to establish an almost sure convergence under the presence of the projection.
Second,
if we consider the tabular case where $\Phi = I$,
1 of \citet{zhang2021finite},
$E$ becomes the orthogonal complement of $\qty{ce \mid c \in \R}$.
Even if we were able to similarly prove that almost surely $\lim_{t\to\infty} \norm{\Pi_E (v_t - v_*)}_2 = 0$ for some $v_*$ (again, it is not clear how this can be done),
we could still only conclude that $\qty{v_t}$ converges to a set, 
not a point.

To summarize,
recent advances with linear average reward TD present insightful results, but those results do not say much (if anything) about the almost sure convergence of tabular average reward TD.
\paragraph*{Hardness in Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann Iterations.}
Having elaborated on the hardness in analyzing~\eqref{eq: avg td update} with ODE-based approaches,
we now resort to an alternative approach, 
the Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann (SKM) iterations.
In its simplest and deterministic form,
Krasnoselskii-Mann (KM) iterations study the convergence of iterates
\begin{align}
    \label{eq km update}
    x_{t+1} = x_t + \alpha_{t+1} (T x_t - x_t),
    \tag{KM}
\end{align}
where $T$ is some nonexpansive mapping.
Since we have already demonstrated that $h$ is nonexpansive in $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$,
SKM appears promising in analyzing~\eqref{eq: avg td update}.
It, however, turns out that
the current state of results for SKM iterations is insufficient for proving the almost sure convergence of \eqref{eq: avg td update}. 
We elaborate on this fact here.

Earlier works on the convergence of~\eqref{eq km update} typically require that the operator $T: C \to C$
has a compact image,
i.e., $T(C)$ is a compact subset of $C$.
Under some other restrictive conditions, 
\cite{krasnosel1955two} first proves the convergence of~\eqref{eq km update} to a fixed point of $T$.
This result is further generalized by \citet{Edelstein1966ARO,Schaefer1957,ishikawa1976fixed,reich1979weak}.
More recently, \citet{cominetti2014rate} use a novel fox-and-hare model to connect KM iterations with Bernoulli random variables, providing a sharper convergence rate for $\norm{x_k - Tx_k} \to 0$.

However, in many scenarios such as RL,
requiring an algorithm to satisfy the exact form of~\eqref{eq km update} is usually not plausible.
Instead,
some noise may appear.
This gives rise to the study of the inexact KM iterations (IKM).
\begin{align}
    \label{eq inexact km update}
    x_{t+1} = x_t + \alpha_{t+1} (T x_t - x_t + e_{t+1}),
    \tag{IKM}
\end{align}
where $\qty{e_t}$ is a sequence of deterministic noise.
\citet{bravo2019rates} extend \citet{cominetti2014rate}
and establish the convergence of~\eqref{eq inexact km update},
under some mild conditions on $\qty{e_t}$.

However,
a deterministic noise is still not desirable in many problems.
To this end,
a stochastic version of~\eqref{eq inexact km update} is studied,
which considers the iterates
\begin{align}
    \label{eq skm update}
    x_{t+1} = x_t + \alpha_{t+1} (T x_t - x_t + M_{t+1}),
    \tag{SKM}
\end{align}
where $\qty{M_t}$ is a Martingale difference sequence.
Under mild conditions,
\citet{bravo2024stochastic} prove the almost sure convergence of~\eqref{eq skm update} to a fixed point of $T$.

Using \eqref{eq avg td update compact} and \eqref{eq: expected h}, 
we can write \eqref{eq: avg td update} as,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq avg td one more}
    v_{t+1} = v_t + \alpha_{t+1}\qty(h(v_t) - v_t +H(v_t,Y_{t+1})-h(v_t) + \epsilon_{t+1}),
\end{align}
where we recall that $h$ is nonexpansive in $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$.
This,
however,
does not fit into~\eqref{eq skm update}.
First,
there is an additive stochastic noise $\qty{\epsilon_t}$.
Second,
the sequence $\qty{H(v_t, Y_{t+1}) - h(v_t)}$ is not a Martingale difference sequence.
If the sequence of noise $\qty{Y_t}$ was i.i.d.,
then $\qty{H(v_t, Y_{t+1}) - h(v_t)}$ would have been a Martingale difference sequence.
But unfortunately,
in~\eqref{eq: avg td update},
the sequence $\qty{Y_t}$ is a Markov chain,
far from being i.i.d.
Moreover, 
the noise $\qty{\epsilon_t}$ is now stochastic.
So~\eqref{eq inexact km update} concerning a deterministic noise would not apply either.
These demonstrate the hardness in analyzing~\eqref{eq: avg td update} with existing~\eqref{eq skm update} results.

Nevertheless,
this motivates us to extend the results from \citet{bravo2024stochastic} to study~\eqref{eq skm update} with Markovian and additive noise,
in the form of~\eqref{eq avg td one more}.


\section{Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann Iterations with Markovian and Additive Noise}

As promised,
we are now ready to extend the analysis of~\eqref{eq skm update} in \citet{bravo2019rates,bravo2024stochastic} to SKM with Markovian and additive noise.
Namely,
we consider the following iterates
\begin{equation} 
\label{eq:skm_markov}
    x_{n+1} = x_n + \alpha_{n+1}\left(H(x_n, Y_{n+1}) - x_n + \e{1}_{n+1}\right)  \tag{SKM with Markovian and Additive Noise}.
\end{equation}
Here $\qty{x_n}$ are stochastic vectors evolving in $\R^d$,
$\qty{Y_n}$ is a Markov chain evolving in a finite state space $\fY$,
$H: \R^d \times \fY \to \R^d$ defines the update,
$\qty{\e{1}_{n+1}}$ is a sequence of stochastic noise evolving in $\R^d$,
and $\qty{\alpha_n}$ is a sequence of deterministic learning rates.  
We make the following assumptions.

\begin{xassumption}[Ergodicity]\label{as:steadystate}
The Markov chain $\qty{Y_n}$ is irreducible and aperiodic. 
\end{xassumption}
The Markov chain $\qty{Y_n}$ thus adopts a unique invariant distribution,
denoted as $d_\mu$.
We use $P$ to denote the transition matrix of $\qty{Y_n}$.
\begin{xassumption}[1-Lipschitz] \label{as:H 1 Lipschitz}
    The function $H$ is 1-Lipschitz continuous in its first argument w.r.t. some operator norm $\norm{\cdot}$ and uniformly in its second argument, i.e., for any $x, x', y$, it holds that
    \begin{align}
        \norm{H(x, y) - H(x', y)} \leq \norm{x - x'}.
    \end{align}
\end{xassumption}
This assumption has two important implication.
First, it implies that $H(x, y)$ can grow at most linearly.
Indeed, let $x' = 0$, we get
    $\norm{H(x, y)} \leq \norm{H(0, y)} + \norm{x}$.
Define $C_H \doteq \max_y \norm{H(0, y)}$,
we get
\begin{align}
    \norm{H(x, y)} \leq C_H + \norm{x}. \label{eq:H linear growth}
\end{align}
Second,
define
the function $h: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ as the expectation of $H$ over the stationary distribution $d_\mu$: \begin{align} h(x) \doteq \E_{y\sim d_\mu}[H(x, y)]. \label{def: h} \end{align}
We then have that $h$ is nonexpansive. 
Namely,
\begin{align}
    \textstyle \norm{h(x) - h(x')} \leq \sum_y d_\mu(y) \norm{H(x, y) - H(x', y)} \leq \norm{x - x'}. \label{eq:h nonexpansive}
\end{align}
This $h$ is exactly the nonexpansive operator in the SKM literature.
We of course need to assume that the problem is solvable.
\begin{xassumption}[Fixed Points]\label{as: fixed points nonempty}
    The nonexpansive operator $h$ adopts at least one fixed point.
\end{xassumption}
We use $\fX_* \neq \emptyset$ to denote the set of the fixed points of $h$.
\begin{xassumption}[Learning Rate]\label{as:lr}
    The learning rate $\qty{\alpha_n}$ has the form
    \begin{align}
        \textstyle \alpha_n = \frac{1}{(n+1)^b}, \alpha_0 = 0,
    \end{align}
    where $b \in (\frac{4}{5}, 1]$.
\end{xassumption}
The primary motivation for requiring $b \in (\frac{4}{5}, 1]$ is that our learning rates $\alpha_n$ need to decrease quickly enough for certain key terms in the proof to be finite. The specific need for $b > \frac{4}{5}$ can be seen in the proof of \eqref{eq: ai aj^2 tj^2} in Lemma \ref{lem:lr}.
We now impose assumptions on the additive noise.
\begin{xassumption}[Additive Noise]\label{as:e1}
\begin{align}
    \textstyle \sum_{k=1}^\infty \alpha_k \norm{\e{1}_k} <& \infty \qq{a.s.,} \label{as: total noise} \\
    \textstyle \E\left[\norm{\e{1}_n}^2 \right] =& \textstyle \fO\qty(\frac{1}{n}). \label{as: second moment}
\end{align}
\end{xassumption}
The first part of Assumption \ref{as:e1} can be interpreted as a requirement that the total amount of additive noise remains finite, 
akin to the assumption on $e_t$ in~\eqref{eq inexact km update} in \citet{bravo2019rates}. Additionally, we impose a condition on the second moment of this noise, requiring it to converge at the rate $\fO \qty(\frac{1}{n})$. 
While these assumptions on ${\e{1}_n}$ may seem restrictive, we introduce $\e{1}_n$ because it is essential for proving the convergence of~\eqref{eq: avg td update}. It is worth noting that even if $\e{1}_n$ were absent, our work would still extend the results of \citep{bravo2024stochastic} to cases involving Markovian noise, as the Markovian noise component is already incorporated within $Y_n$, which represents a significant result.
We are now ready to present the main result. 

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:main_conv_result}
Let Assumptions \ref{as:steadystate} - \ref{as:e1} hold.
Then the iterates $\qty{x_n}$ generated by~\\\eqref{eq:skm_markov}
satisfy 
\begin{align}
    \lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = x_* \qq{a.s.,}
\end{align}
where $x_* \in \fX_*$ is a possibly sample-path dependent fixed point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    We start with a decomposition of the error $H(x, Y_{n+1}) - h(x)$ using Poisson's equation akin to \citet{metivier1987theoremes,DBLP:books/sp/BenvenisteMP90}.
    Namely,
    thanks to the finiteness of $\fY$,
    it is well known (see, e.g., Theorem 17.4.2 of \citet{meyn2012markov}
  or Theorem 8.2.6 of \citet{puterman2014markov}) that there exists a function $\nu(x, y): \R^d \times \fY \to \R^d$ such that
    \begin{align}
        H(x, y) - h(x) = \nu(x, y) - (P\nu)(x, y). \label{eq: Poisson decomp}
    \end{align}
    Here, we use
    $P\nu$ to denote the function $(x, y) \mapsto \sum_{y'} P(y, y')\nu(x, y')$.
    The error can then be decomposed as
    \begin{align}
        \label{eq poisson noise representation}
        &H(x, Y_{n+1}) - h(x) = M_{n+1} + \e{2}_{n+1} + \e{3}_{n+1},
    \end{align}
    where
    \begin{align}
 M_{n+1} &\doteq \nu(x_n,Y_{n+2}) - (P\nu)(x_n,Y_{n+1}), \label{eq:M_define} \\
 \e{2}_{n+1} &\doteq \nu\paren{x_n,Y_{n+1}} - \nu\paren{x_{n+1},Y_{n+2}}, \label{eq:e2_define} \\
 \e{3}_{n+1} &\doteq  \nu\paren{x_{n+1},Y_{n+2}} - \nu\paren{x_n,Y_{n+2}}. \label{eq:e3_define}
    \end{align}
Here $\qty{M_{n+1}}$ is a Martingale difference sequence.
We then use
\begin{align}
    \xi_{n+1} &\doteq \e{1}_{n+1}+ \e{2}_{n+1} + \e{3}_{n+1}, \label{eq:xi_define}
\end{align}
to denote all the non-Martingale noise, yielding
\begin{align}
        x_{n+1}&=\paren{1-\alpha_{n+1}}x_n + \alpha_{n+1}\paren{h\paren{x_n}+M_{n+1}+\xi_{n+1}}.
\end{align}
We now define an auxiliary sequence $\qty{U_n}$ to capture how the noise evolves
\begin{align}
    U_0 \doteq& \, 0, \\
    U_{n+1} \doteq& \, \paren{1-\alpha_{n+1}}U_n + \alpha_{n+1}\paren{M_{n+1}+\xi_{n+1}}. \label{eq:U_n_define}
\end{align} 
If we are able to prove that the total noise is well controlled in the following sense
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^\infty \alpha_k \norm{U_{k-1}} &< \infty \qq{a.s.,} \label{eq:sum_Uk_converges}\\
    \lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}\norm{U_n}&=0 \qq{a.s.,} \label{eq:limit_to_0} 
\end{align}
then a result from \citet{bravo2024stochastic} concerning the convergence of~\eqref{eq inexact km update} can be applied on each sample path to complete the almost sure convergence proof.
The rest of the proof is dedicated to the verification of those two conditions.
To this end,
we first define shorthand
\begin{align}
\alpha_{k,n} &\doteq \alpha_k \prod_{j=k+1}^n \paren{1-\alpha_j}, \, \alpha_{n,n} \doteq \alpha_n \label{eq:alpha_in_define}.
\end{align}
Telescoping~\eqref{eq:U_n_define} then yields
\begin{align}
    U_n =& \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_{k,n} M_k}_{\Mb_n} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_{k,n} \e{1}_k}_{\eb{1}_n}+ \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_{k,n} \e{2}_k}_{\eb{2}_n} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_{k,n} \e{3}_k}_{\eb{3}_n}. \label{eq:Un_norm_bar_bounds}
\end{align}
Then, we can upper-bound \eqref{eq:sum_Uk_converges} as
\begin{align}
    \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \norm{U_{k-1}} &\leq  \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \norm{\Mb_{k-1}}}_{\Mbb_n} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_i \norm{\eb{1}_{k-1}}}_{\ebb{1}_n} + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_i \norm{\eb{2}_{k-1}}}_{\ebb{2}_n}  + \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \norm{\eb{3}_{k-1}}}_{\ebb{3}_n}. \label{eq:sumUn_derivation}
\end{align}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:sup_M},~\ref{lem:sup_e1},~\ref{lem:sup_e2}, and~\ref{lem:sup_e3}  respectively prove that all terms in~\eqref{eq:sumUn_derivation} are bounded almost surely,
which verifies~\eqref{eq:sum_Uk_converges}.

We now verify~\eqref{eq:limit_to_0}.
This time, rewrite $U_n$ as
\begin{align}
    U_n &= -\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k U_{k-1}+\alpha_k\paren{M_k + \e{1}_k + \e{2}_k + \e{3}_k}.
\end{align}
Lemma~\ref{lem:martingale_series_bound}, Assumption \ref{as:e1}, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:ak_e2},~\ref{lem:ak_e3} prove that
  $\sup_n \norm{\sum_{k=1}^n\alpha_k M_k} < \infty$ and $\sup_n \norm{\sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \e{j}_k} < \infty$ for $j \in \qty{1,2,3}$ respectively.
Together with~\eqref{eq:Un_norm_bar_bounds},
this means that $\sup_n \norm{U_n} < \infty$.
In other words,
we have established the stability of~\eqref{eq:U_n_define}.
Then, it can be shown (Lemma~\ref{lem:xi_noise_convergence}), using an extension of Theorem 2.1 of \citet{borkar2009stochastic} (Lemma~\ref{thm:borkar_2_thm1}),
that $\qty{U_n}$ converges to the globally asymptotically stable equilibrium of the ODE
    $\dv{U(t)}{t} = -U(t)$,
which is 0.
This verifies~\eqref{eq:limit_to_0}.
Lemma~\ref{lem: applying bravo 2.1} then invokes a result from \citet{bravo2024stochastic} and completes the proof.
\end{proof}


\section{Average Reward Temporal Difference Learning}

We are now ready to prove the convergence of~\eqref{eq: avg td update}.
Throughout the rest of the section, we utilize the following assumption.
\begin{xassumption}[Ergodicity]
    \label{as:rl_chain}
    Both $\fS$ and $\fA$ are finite.
    The Markov chain $\qty{S_t}$ induced by the policy $\pi$ is aperiodic and irreducible.
\end{xassumption}
\begin{theorem} \label{thm:avg_rew_td}
Let Assumption \ref{as:rl_chain} hold. 
Consider the learning rates in the form of $\alpha_t = \frac{1}{(t+1)^b}, \beta_t = \frac{1}{t}$ with $b \in (\frac{4}{5}, 1]$.
Then the iterates $\qty{v_t}$ generated by~\eqref{eq: avg td update} satisfy
\begin{align}
    \lim_{t\to\infty}{v_t} = v_* \qq{a.s.,}
\end{align}
where $v_* \in \fV_*$ is a possibly sample-path dependent fixed point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    We proceed via verifying assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main_conv_result}. 
    In particular,
    we consider the compact form~\eqref{eq avg td update compact}.
Under Assumption~\ref{as:rl_chain}, it is obvious that $\qty{Y_t}$ is irreducible and aperiodic and adopts a unique stationary distribution.
    
    To verify Assumption \ref{as:H 1 Lipschitz}, we demonstrate that $H$ is $1-$Lipschitz in $v$ w.r.t $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$. For notation simplicity, let $y=(s_0, a_0, s_1)$. We have,
    \begin{align}
        H(v, y)[s] - H(v',y)[s] = \mathbb{I}\qty{s = s_0}\qty(v(s_1)-v'(s_1)-v(s_0)+v'(s_0))+v(s)-v'(s).
    \end{align}
    Separating cases based on $s$, if $s\neq s_0$, we have
    \begin{equation}
      \abs{H(v,y)[s] - H(v',y)[s]} = \abs{v(s)-v'(s)}\leq \norm{v-v'}_\infty.  
    \end{equation}
    For the case when $s=s_0$, we have
    \begin{equation}
        \abs{H(v,y)[s] - H(v',y)[s]} = \abs{v(s_1)-v'(s_1)} \leq \norm{v-v'}_\infty.
    \end{equation}
    Therefore
    \begin{equation}
        \norm{H(v,y)-H(v,y)}_\infty = \max_{s \in \fS} \abs{H(v,y)[s] - H(v',y)[s]}\leq \norm{v-v'}_\infty.
    \end{equation}
    It is well known that the set of solutions to Poisson's equation $\fV_*$ defined in \eqref{eq fixed points} is non-empty \citep{puterman2014markov}, verifying Assumption \ref{as: fixed points nonempty}.
    Assumption \ref{as:lr} is directly met by the definition of $\alpha_t$.
    
    To verify Assumption \ref{as:e1}, we first notice that for~\eqref{eq: avg td update}, we have
        $\norm{\e{1}_t}_\infty = \abs{\bar{J}_{\pi} - J_t}$.
    It is well-known from the ergodic theorem that $J_t$ converges to $\bar J_\pi$ almost surely.
    To verify Assumption~\ref{as:e1}, however, requires both an almost sure convergence rate and an $L^2$ convergence rate.
    To this end, we rewrite the update of $\qty{J_t}$ as
    \begin{align}
        J_{t+1} = J_t + \beta_{t+1} \left(R_{t+1} + \gamma J_t \phi(S_{t+1}) - J_t \phi(S_t) \right) \phi(S_t),
    \end{align}
    where we define $\gamma \doteq 0$ and $\phi(s) \doteq 1 \, \forall s$.
    It is now clear that the update of $\qty{J_t}$ is a special case of linear TD in the discounted setting \citep{sutton1988learning}.
    Given our choice of $\beta_t = \frac{1}{t}$,
    the general result about the almost sure convergence rate of linear TD (Theorem~1 of \citet{tadic2002almost}) ensures that
    \begin{align}
        \abs{J_t - \bar J_\pi} \leq \frac{\zeta_{\ref{thm:avg_rew_td}} \sqrt{\ln\ln t}}{\sqrt{t}} \qq{a.s.,}
    \end{align}
    where $\zeta_{\ref{thm:avg_rew_td}}$ is a sample-path dependent constant.
    This immediately verifies~\eqref{as: total noise}.
    We do note that this almost sure convergence rate can also be obtained via a law of the iterated logarithm for Markov chains (Theorem 17.0.1 of \citet{meyn2012markov}).
    The general result about the $L^2$ convergence rate of linear TD (Theorem~11 of \citet{srikant2019finite}) ensures that
    \begin{align}
        \E\qty[\abs{J_t - \bar J_\pi}^2] = \fO\qty(\frac{1}{t}).
    \end{align}
    This immediately verifies~\eqref{as: second moment} and completes the proof.
    \end{proof}



\section{Related Work}


It is now clear that our success fundamentally originates from the novel fox-and-hare racing model introduced by \cite{cominetti2014rate}.
This fox-and-hare model is too complicated to be detailed here, but it is for sure an entirely different paradigm from the ODE and Lyapunov based methods in RL \citep{bertsekas1996neuro,konda2000actor,borkar2000ode,srikant2019finite,borkar2021ode,chen2021lyapunov,DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2111-02997,meyn2022control,zhang2023convergence,liu2024ode,meyn2024projected}.
\citet{bravo2024stochastic} is the first to introduce this SKM based method in RL,
which analyzes a synchronous version of RVI $Q$-learning \citep{abounadi2001learning}.
The method in \cite{bravo2024stochastic} is only applicable to synchronous RL algorithms because it requires Martingale difference noise.
By extending \cite{bravo2024stochastic} to Markovian noise,
we are the first to use the SKM method to analyze asynchronous RL algorithms.

Poisson's equation has been very powerful in dealing with Markovian noise.
In particular, the noise representation~\eqref{eq poisson noise representation} is not new.
However,
our work bounds the error terms in~\eqref{eq poisson noise representation} differently from previous works concerning the almost sure convergence.
Namely,
\citet{DBLP:books/sp/BenvenisteMP90,konda2000actor} use stopping times to bound the error terms while \citet{borkar2021ode} use scaled iterates.
Instead, we rely on the 1-Lipschitz continuity (Assumption~\ref{as:H 1 Lipschitz}) to bound the growth of the error terms directly.
Moreover,
previous works with such error decomposition (e.g., \citet{DBLP:books/sp/BenvenisteMP90,konda2000actor,borkar2021ode}) usually only need to bound terms like ${\sum_k \alpha_k \e{1}_k}$.
For our setup, besides ${\sum_k \alpha_k \e{1}_k}$,
we also need to bound terms like $\eb{1}_n = \sum_k \alpha_{k, n} \e{1}_k$
and $\ebb{1}_n = \sum_i \alpha_i \norm{\eb{1}_{k-1}}$,
which appear novel and more challenging.

The~\eqref{eq: avg td update} algorithm has inspired the design of many other temporal difference algorithms for average reward MDPs,
for both policy evaluation and control,
including \citet{konda2000actor, yang2016efficient, wan2021average, zhang2021policy, wan2020learning, he2022emphatic, saxena2023off}.
We envision that our work will shed light on the almost sure convergence of those follow-up algorithms.


\section{Conclusion}

After more than 25 years since the discovery of \eqref{eq: avg td update}, we have finally established its almost sure convergence to a potentially sample-path dependent fixed point. This result highlights the underappreciated strength of SKM iterations, a tool whose potential is often overlooked in the RL community. Addressing several follow-up questions could open the door to proving the convergence of many other RL algorithms. Do SKM iterations converge in $L^p$? Do they follow a central limit theorem or a law of the iterated logarithm? Can they be extended to two-timescale settings? And can we develop a finite sample analysis for them? Resolving these questions could pave the way for significant advancements across reinforcement learning theory. We leave them for future investigation.
