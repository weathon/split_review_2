\title{FOSP: Fine-tuning Offline Safe Policy through World Models}

\begin{abstract}
    Model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown its high training efficiency and capability of handling high-dimensional tasks. Regarding safety issues, safe model-based RL can achieve nearly zero-cost performance and effectively manage the trade-off between performance and safety. Nevertheless, prior works still pose safety challenges due to the online exploration in real-world deployment. To address this, some offline RL methods have emerged as solutions, which learn from a static dataset in a safe way by avoiding interactions with the environment. 
    In this paper, we aim to further enhance safety during the deployment stage for vision-based robotic tasks by fine-tuning an offline-trained policy. We incorporate in-sample optimization, model-based policy expansion, and reachability guidance to construct a safe offline-to-online framework. Moreover, our method proves to improve the generalization of offline policy in unseen safety-constrained scenarios. Finally, the efficiency of our method is validated on simulation benchmarks with five vision-only tasks and a real robot by solving some deployment problems using limited data.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}



\keywords{Safe Model-based Reinforcement Learning, Offline-to-Online} 
\label{sec:intro}
	
Model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) has proven to be a powerful tool to enhance sample efficiency by constructing a world model of the environment\cite{hafner2019dreamerv1, cang2021mbrl, hafner2019mbrl, hafner2020mastering,hafner2023dreamerv3}. The world model reduces random explorations by predicting the outcomes of different actions, 
leading to a fast convergence speed during online fine-tuning after minimal interactions  \cite{feng2023finetuning}. Moreover, it significantly improves action safety in high-dimensional tasks such as vision-based operations due to its prediction ability\cite{as2022LAMBDA,huang2023safedreamer}. Consequently, We consider utilizing model-based methods to tackle safety-critical problems in real-world deployment.

Offline RL has been widely studied within the academic community, using pre-collected datasets to learn policies without exploration\cite{levine2020offline}. At the offline training stage, safety constraints will be incorporated into policy learning, which means learning to be safe other than learning safely. In this way, offline trained policy is promising to avoid constraint violations during the online deployment. However, offline dataset cannot fully simulate complicated real-world environments and will cause out-of-distribution (OOD) issues in the case of unseen data. Such generalization tasks are significant challenges for offline learning.

In this work, we propose developing an offline-to-online approach to address the generalization issue with safety guarantees. We consider the problem of pretraining an offline policy and a world model, and then fine-tuning them safely during online interactions. However, such a strategy usually causes degradation of policy performance and a lack of safety after fine-tuning\cite{nair2020awac}. The main reason lies in too many mixed constraints (both soft and hard), such as safety constraints and behavior regularization, making it difficult to ensure both optimality and safety. Meanwhile, the world model will introduce prediction errors due to distribution shift during the fine-tuning phase, leading to increasing safety risks. Therefore, we seek to unify and handle mixed constraints, and use the offline safe policy as a guidance for online correction. Based on this key insight, we proposed \textbf{FOSP} (\textbf{F}ine-tuning \textbf{O}ffline \textbf{S}afe \textbf{P}olicy through World Models), a method that enhances offline safe training and bridges model-based offline training with online fine-tuning.

To evaluate our method, we design different experiments in the simulation including offline training and online fine-tuning. It shows robust performance in both offline and online fine-tuning tasks in Safety-Gymnasium\cite{ji2023safety} and achieves nearly zero-cost. We also deploy FOSP in a real-world Franka manipulator for trajectory planning tasks. It turns out that our method performs well across tasks with different safety regions and can be transferred to unseen scenarios through few-shot fine-tuning. 

We summarize our contributions as follows: (1) To the best of our knowledge, FOSP is the \textbf{first approach} that extends model-based RL to safe offline-to-online areas. (2) It handles the trade-off between the performance and constraints satisfaction across offline and online training phases on various vision-only tasks. (3) In real-world deployment, it does \textbf{not need sim-to-real transfer} and can be safely fine-tuned in \textbf{unseen safety-constrained scenarios}. (4) To inspire further research in this area, we will publicly release our code and dataset.

\section{Related Works}

\label{sec:rel}

\paragraph{Offline-to-online RL} Offline RL, which trains a policy by leveraging a large amount of existing data, is valuable to real-world scenarios\cite{wu2019behavior, fujimoto2019off}. Prior offline RL works focus on addressing the distribution shift and value overestimation problem\cite{yu2020mopo,yu2021combo,rafailov2021offline,kumar2020conservative,kidambi2020morel,fujimoto2021minimalist}. Recently, in-sample methods have demonstrated their robust performance by avoiding out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs\cite{kostrikov2021iql,xu2023offline,garg2023extreme,peng2019advantage,nair2020awac,he2024aligniql}. Unlike training solely on offline data, offline-to-online RL involves further fine-tuning the offline policy online. And many offline RL algorithms have extended to online fine-tuning\cite{kostrikov2021iql,nair2020awac}. But these methods have shown that the more effective the offline RL method is, the worse online fine-tuning performance is\cite{xiao2023sample}. Some previous works have explored how to effectively fine-tune from offline pretrained policy, including balanced sampling in replay buffer\cite{lee2022offline}, policy calibrating\cite{nakamoto2024cal,rudner2021pathologies}, and parameter transferring\cite{xie2021policy,rajeswaran2017learning}. However, existing solutions are restricted by policy transitions or the need to estimate the density of the online policy distribution\cite{zhao2022adaptive}. PEX\cite{zhang2023pex} is an approach that bridges offline and online algorithms and is adaptive for fine-tuning different online methods with offline policy guidance. As it is shown as an effective approach in offline-to-online training, we extend it to model-based algorithms that not only require fine-tuning the policy but also the model.

\paragraph{Safe RL} Safe RL focuses on optimizing objectives with different constraints\cite{altman2021cmdp}. Many methods are based on policy search algorithms that ensure adherence to near-constraints iteratively\cite{achiam2017cpo} and Lagrangian methods that convert it to an unconstrained problem to solve\cite{chow2018lag1, ding2020lag3, tessler2018lag2}. Model-based RL methods utilize world models for action selection so that they ensure safety by background planning. Some recent works are trying to address challenges remaining in vision-input tasks\cite{sikchi2022learning,hansen2022tdmpc,hafner2019mbrl}. LAMDBA\cite{as2022LAMBDA} is a method extending DreamerV1\cite{hafner2019dreamerv1} to safe RL by using augmented Lagrangian. Safe-SLAC\cite{hogewind2022safeslac} incorporating the Lagrangian method into SLAC\cite{lee2020slac} improves the computation complexity of LAMBDA. SafeDreamer\cite{huang2023safedreamer} is the latest work in safe model-based reinforcement learning. It enhances safety in online or background planning with DreamerV3\cite{hafner2023dreamerv3}, achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance. However, prior works only aim to address issues in the online setting, which cannot avoid costs incurring during exploration in the real world. By contrast, some safe offline RL can learn policies from offline datasets by using OOD action detection\cite{cao2024offline,xu2022cpq,li2022ood}, DICE-based theory\cite{lee2022coptidice}, and sequence modeling\cite{lin2023seq2,liu2023seq1}. Nonetheless, they only utilize soft constraints which sometimes can not effectively decrease constraint violations. Some works consider the hard constraints by using control theory methods such as control barrier function (CBF)\cite{choi2020cbf} and HJ reachability\cite{zheng2023feasibility,yu2023hj,ganai2024iterative,fisac2019hj}. Our work draws from these works but unifies all the constraints with model-based background planning. 



\section{Preliminaries}

\label{sec:pre}

\paragraph{World Model} Our work extends DreamerV3\cite{hafner2023dreamerv3}, a powerful and robust baseline model-based RL using world model and actor-critic. It has the ability to model high-dimensional observations of the environment and increase the data efficiency of RL. The world model learns compressed image input representations and predicts future states and rewards sequences for decision-making. It is implemented as a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM)\cite{hafner2019mbrl}. First, the model maps the high-dimensional observations $x_t$ to stochastic states $z_t$. And deterministic latent states $h_t$ are predicted by $z_t$ and actions $a_t$. By concatenating both of them, we get the model state $s_t = \{ h_t, z_t\}$. Finally, we facilitate $s_t$ to predict the next observations and rewards. Due to the safety considered in the environment, we add a cost decoder to the original model. All components in the world model are as follows:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \text{latent encoder:} & \latent_t \sim q_\theta(\latent_t \mid \history_t, \obs_t)\\
    \text{deterministic state:} & \history_t = f_\theta(\latent_{t-1}, \history_{t-1}, \action_{t-1}) \\
    \text{stochastic state:}  & \hat{\latent}_{t} \sim \dynamics(\latent_{t} \mid \history_t)\\
    \end{array}
\end{split}
\;
\begin{split}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \text{observation decoder:} &\hat{\obs}_t \sim p_\theta(\obs_t \mid \latent_t, \history_t)\\
    \text{reward decoder:} & \hat{\rew}_t \sim p_\theta(\rew_t \mid \latent_t, \history_t)\\
    \text{cost decoder:}  & \hat{\cost}_t \sim p_\theta(\cost_t \mid \latent_t, \history_t)\\
    \end{array}
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The model is trained end-to-end by the ELBO or variational free energy
of a hidden Markov model loss function with KL balancing:
\begin{align}\label{eq:model_eq}
& \mathcal{L}^{\text{model}}(\theta) =  \E_{\tau \sim \mathcal{D}} \Big[ 
\sum_{t=1}^T -\ln p_\theta(\obs_t \mid \state_t) - \ln p_\theta(\rew_t \mid \state_t)  - \ln p_\theta(\cost_t \mid \state_t) + \nonumber\\
&\mathbb{D}_{KL}[sg(q_{\theta}(\latent_{t}|\history_{t}, \obs_{t}))||\dynamics(\latent_{t}|\history_{t})] + \beta\mathbb{D}_{KL}[q_{\theta}(\latent_{t}|\history_{t}, \obs_{t})||sg(\dynamics(\latent_{t}|\history_{t}))]\Big].
\end{align}

The stop-gradient operator makes the representations more predictable in the imagination training.

\paragraph{Safe Model-based RL} Safe RL is frequently formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathbb{P}, \mathcal{R}, \mathcal{C}, \mu, \gamma)$\cite{altman2021cmdp}. $\mathcal{S}$ and $\mathcal{A}$ denote the state and action space. $\mathbb{P}(s'|s,a)$ represents the transition probability from $s'$ to $s$ under action $a$. $\mathcal{R}$ is reward space that maps $\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}$ to $\mathbb{R}$. And $\mathcal{C}$ is a cost function set containing cost functions $c:\mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A}\rightarrow [0,C_{max}]$. $\mu(\cdot): \mathcal{S}\rightarrow[0,1]$ is the initial state distribution and $\gamma\in (0,1)$ is the discount factor. Given a policy distribution $\pi_{\psi}$, we define the performance and constraints as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rewardfunction}
    J^\mathcal{R}(\pi_{\psi}) = \E_{\action_t \sim \pi, \state_{t + 1} \sim p_\theta, \state_0 \sim \rho} \left[\sum_{t = 0}^{T} r_t \big\rvert \state_0\right],
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:costfunction}
    J^\mathcal{C}_i(\pi_{\psi}) = \E_{\action_t \sim \pi, \state_{t + 1} \sim p_\theta, \state_0 \sim \rho} \left[\sum_{t = 0}^{T} c_t^i \big\rvert \state_0\right] \le d^i, \; \forall i \in \{1, \dots, C\},
\end{equation}
where $i$ denotes different safety constraints we want to avoid and $d^i$ are cost thresholds. Therefore, our safe model-based RL problem can be formulated as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{safepro}
    \max_\psi J^\mathcal{R}(\pi_{\psi}) \;\;\; \text{s.t. } \; J^\mathcal{C}_i(\pi_{\psi}) \le d^i, \; \forall i \in \{1, \dots, C\}.
\end{equation}


\section{Methods}

\label{sec:method}

In this section, we introduce our framework (Figure \ref{fig:FOSP}) for fine-tuning safe actions with offline pretrained world models. It alleviates the distribution shift error during offline training and balances the trade-off between performance and violations via the reachability estimation function. In the online fine-tuning phase, using offline-trained policy as guidance helps mitigate the performance drop and further improves the performance of the algorithm. 
In section \ref{subsec:iql}, to address the distribution shift in offline training, we use the in-sample optimization approach to train critics.
Next, we introduce an offline-to-online approach called model-based policy expansion to further improve online performance in section \ref{subsec:pex}.
The reachability guidance for safety consideration is provided in section \ref{subsec:ref}.
\subsection{In-sample Optimization for Offline Training}
\label{subsec:iql}

Value overestimation is an extensive challenge in offline training due to the distribution shift between the state-action pair from the dataset and generated by the learned policy. Moreover, model-based offline RL algorithms not only learn a policy but also a dynamic model $p_{\theta}$ by the offline dataset. It makes this challenge more severe that the latent dynamic estimation and return prediction are also affected. Therefore, we introduce the in-sample optimization to address this issue. 

First, we consider a sequence of data $\mathcal{B} = (\obs_{1:T}, \action_{1:T}, \rew_{1:T}, \cost_{1:T})$ sampled from the offline dataset. Then, the model generates latent states $\state_{1:T} \sim q_\theta(\state_{1:T}|\history_{1:T}, \obs_{1:T})$ and uses them as initial states to generate rollouts: 
\begin{equation}\label{rollout}
\hat{\action}_{1:T}^h\sim \pi_{\psi}(\action|\hat{\state}_{1:T}^{h-1}), \ \hat{\state}_{1:T}^{h+1} \sim p_{\theta}(\state|\hat{\action}_{1:T}^h, \hat{\state}_{1:T}^h),\  \hat{\rew}_{1:T}^h\sim p_{\theta}(\rew|\hat{\state}_{1:T}^h),\  \hat{\cost}_{1:T}^h\sim p_{\theta}(\cost|\hat{\state}_{1:T}^h). 
\end{equation}
We use $Q_\phi(\state, \action)$ as the critic network and $\hat{Q}_\phi(s, a)$ as the target network. Similar to Implicit Q-learning (IQL)\cite{he2024aligniql}, we only use in-sample actions as calculated components of $\lambda$-return. Specifically, a large target value estimated by $Q_\phi$ might not represent an existing action leading to a good state due to the out-of-sample actions $\pi(\state)$. Thus, a separate value network $V_\varphi(\state)= \E_{\action\sim \pi(\cdot|\state)} [Q_\phi(\state, \action)]$ only conditioned on states is proposed to mitigate the out-of-distribution issue. It approaches the action distribution expectation of Q-value and can be optimized by expectile regression:
\begin{equation}\label{valueloss}
\mathcal{L}_{V}(\varphi) = |\tau-\mathds{1}_{\{Q_{\phi}(\state_t^j, \action_t^j)-V_{\varphi}(\state_t^j)<0\}}|(Q_{\phi}(\state_t^j, \action_t^j)-V_{\varphi}(\state_t^j))^2,
\end{equation}

where $\tau \in (0,1)$ is a constant. Note that the action $\action$ will be sampled from the dataset according to the loss.\ref{apx:imple}). The objective can replace the TD-target that was originally used to optimize the Q-value. However, only updating the critic depending on model rollout is not enough, while the real state-action pairs from the offline dataset can supervise the critic to additionally learn in-distribution information. So the critic distribution is optimized on both model data and offline data:
\begin{equation}\label{criticloss1}
\mathcal{L}_{Q}^{1}(\phi) = - \frac{1}{HT} \sum_{t = 1}^T \sum_{h = 1}^H \log{\text{Pr}_{Q_\phi}(R_t^h|\state_t^h)},\;\;\; \mathcal{L}_{Q}^{2}(\phi) = - \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t = 1}^T \log{\text{Pr}_{Q_\phi}(\rew_t + \gamma V_{\varphi}(\state_{t+1})|\state_t)}
\end{equation}
where $\rew_t$ is the reward from the offline dataset. Finally, the critic loss is the combination of the above.
\begin{equation}\label{criticloss}
\mathcal{L}_{Q}(\phi) = \mathcal{L}_{Q}^{1}(\phi) + \mathcal{L}_{Q}^{2}(\phi)
\end{equation}
\subsection{Model-based Policy Expansion for World Models Fine-tuning}
\label{subsec:pex}

After offline pretrain, we fine-tune the world model with online exploration to enhance performance and improve its generalization to similar tasks. However, directly using online algorithms suffers from non-recoverable performance drops potentially caused by the distribution shift and the change of the dynamic model. On the other hand, standard offline RL algorithms typically exhibit worse online fine-tuning performance due to their conservative design\cite{kostrikov2021iql,kumar2020conservative}. Therefore, we try to bridge offline-online RL by fully utilizing offline pretraining policies\cite{zhang2023pex}. 
Suppose that $\pi_\beta$ is the offline policy. Instead of directly fine-tuning it in the online stage, we optimize a new policy $\pi_\psi$ by gradually learning from prior policy $\pi_\beta$ and online exploring simultaneously. Concretely, given a current state $\state$, an action set is composed of actions generated by two policies: $\mathcal{A} = \{ \action_\beta \sim \pi_\beta(\state), \action_\psi \sim \pi_\psi(\state)\}.$ The actions are selected probabilistically based on their potential utilization value: 
\begin{equation}\label{valuechoose}
P(\omega)[k] = \frac{\exp(Q_\phi(\state, \action_k)/\alpha)}{\exp(Q_\phi(\state, \action_\beta)/\alpha) + \exp(Q_\phi(\state, \action_\psi)/\alpha)}, \; \forall k \in \{\beta, \psi\}, 
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is temperature. According to this probability function, the two policies can be trained in a joint adaptive manner that has the advantage of adaptiveness in the sense of allowing generalizing of new abilities. In this way, the offline replay-buffer can be directly transferred to the online fine-tuning stage simplifying the complexity of balanced sampling\cite{lee2022offline}.

In the model-based setting, the dynamic model also needs to be fine-tuned since the model primarily generates the policy update data. The world model benefits from its robust design in capturing temporal dependencies and modeling latent dynamics, which contributes to its stability during fine-tuning. However, the dynamic model might be influenced by the incorrect estimates learned from conservative offline training, making online fine-tuning more challenging. Thus, we develop an additional dynamic model for fine-tuning, expanding the policy to adapt to new tasks.
\subsection{Reachability Estimation Function as Safety Guarantee}
\label{subsec:ref}

To ensure safety throughout the entire training process, prior works consider adopting the relaxation of the Lagrangian multiplier method on \eqref{safepro}. The optimization problem is transformed into an unconstrained safe model-based problem with the Lagrangian multiplier $\lambda_i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagproblem}
\max_{\pi_\psi}\min_{\lambda_i\geq 0}J^\mathcal{R}(\pi_{\psi}) - \lambda_i(J^\mathcal{C}_i(\pi_{\psi}) - d^i), \; \forall i \in \{1, \dots, C\}.
\end{equation}
However, the soft constraints will still lead to a certain chance of violation. It becomes more severe in the offline setting because of the difficulty in estimating the cost values. Hence, we replace soft constraints with hard constraints by the feasibility guidance approach. We first divide our optimization problem into two parts: the feasible part and the infeasible part. 
For the feasible part, we want the agent to maintain safety within the feasible set and maximize the reward return. For the infeasible part, we want the policy to avoid the violations as little as possible and return to the feasible set. As the safe offline RL problem has another objective to optimize: $D(\pi_\psi||\pi_b)$ ($\pi_b$ is the behavior policy), we can reformulate our offline safe model-based problem as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible}
\textbf{Feasible\ part:} \;\; \max_{\pi_\psi} \E_{\state} \Big[ V_\varphi^r (\state) \cdot \mathds{1}_{s\in \mathcal{S}_f} \Big],\;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; V_\varphi^c (\state) = 0,\;\; D(\pi_\psi||\pi_b) \leq \epsilon,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infeasible}
\textbf{Infeasible\ part:} \;\; \max_{\pi_\psi} \E_{\state} \Big[- V_\varphi^c (\state) \cdot \mathds{1}_{s\notin \mathcal{S}_f} \Big],\;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; D(\pi_\psi||\pi_b) \leq \epsilon,
\end{equation}

where $V_\varphi^r (\state) = \E_{\action\sim\pi_\psi}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^tr(\state_t, \action_t)]$ and $V_\varphi^c (\state) = \E_{\action\sim\pi_\psi}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^tc(\state_t, \action_t)]$ represent the expected cumulative rewards and costs, $\mathcal{S}_f$ is the feasible set containing all feasible states. Note that the policy constraint is a soft constraint so the feasible part becomes a problem incorporating coupled hard and soft constraints. Thus, we use the reachability estimation function $u^\pi(\state) = \E_{\tau\sim\pi}[\max_{\state_t\in \tau}\mathds{1}_{(\state_t|\state_0, \pi)\in\mathcal{S}_f}]$ to handle the mixed constraints while prioritizing the hard constraints from RESPO\cite{ganai2024iterative}. It also assists the agent in reentering the feasible region even when it is in the infeasible region.

Therefore, we couple the out-of-distribution actions avoidance into the problem by using advantage functions. Also, we take advantage of the Augmented Lagrangian with a proximal relaxation method that is quite effective in safe model-based RL algorithms. The final problem is as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:advproblem}
\max_{\pi_\psi} \; \E_{\state} \Big[ (A^r (\state, \action) - \Phi( V_\varphi^c(\state), \lambda_p^k, \mu^k)) \cdot u^\pi_\psi - A^c (\state, \action)\cdot (1-u^\pi_\psi) \Big],\;\; \text{s.t.}\;\; D(\pi_\psi||\pi_b) \leq \epsilon.
\end{equation}
where $A^r (\state, \action) = Q_\phi^r(\state, \action)-V_\varphi^r (\state)$ and $A^c (\state, \action) = Q_\phi^c(\state, \action)-V_\varphi^c (\state)$. We can finally obtain a closed-form solution of $\pi_\psi$, which we can use to extract the optimal policy.\ref{apx:der})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy}
    \pi^*(\action|\state)=\frac{1}{Z}w\cdot \pi_b(\action|\state),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policyloss}
\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(\psi) = - \E_{\tau\sim \mathcal{D}}\Big[\sum_{t = 1}^T (w\cdot\log\pi_\psi(\action_t|\state_t) + \eta H[\pi_\psi(\action_t|\state_t)]) - \Phi( V_\varphi^c(\state), \lambda_p^k, \mu^k) \cdot u^\pi_\psi \Big],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:advweight}
\text{where}\;\;\;w = u^\pi_\psi \cdot \exp(\beta_1 A^r(\state, \action) )+ (1-u^\pi_\psi) \cdot \exp(-\beta_2 A^c(\state, \action)).
\end{equation}

In the online fine-tuning stage, we apply policy expansion as follows: use $\pi_b$ as the frozen offline policy trained by \eqref{eq:policyloss} and learn a new policy $\pi_\psi$ without policy behavior constraint in \eqref{eq:advproblem}. Thus, the online policy loss can change the advantage weight as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onlineadvweight}
w = u^\pi_\psi \beta_1 A^r(\state, \action) -  (1-u^\pi_\psi) \beta_2 A^c(\state, \action).
\end{equation}
For cost critic $Q^c(s, a)$ and value $V^c(s)$ training, we use the same way as Section \ref{subsec:iql}.\ref{apx:imple}.

\section{Experimental Results}

\label{sec:result}
In this section, we evaluate our method on different agents and tasks from the Safety-Gymnasium in simulation. We deploy it in motion planning control tasks on a real Franka robot as well. We aim to address the following issues: (1) How does FOSP perform in offline and online fine-tuning? (2) How does each component in FOSP contribute to its overall functionality? (3) Does FOSP successfully handle unseen safety regions in the real world?

\subsection{Experimental Setup} 

\paragraph{Simulation Tasks} We consider five tasks on Safety-Gymnasium benchmark\cite{ji2023safety} environments. The agents need to navigate to predetermined goals without collision with hazards and vases. We measure the performance with three metrics: average undiscounted episode reward return (Reward), average undiscounted episode cost return (Cost) and average cost value throughout the training phase (Cost Regret). To evaluate the advantage of world models in handling high-dimensional features, we use 64$\times$64 pixels RGB image as inputs obtained from the first-person perspective of the agent, which is more representative of real-world environments. The offline datasets are sampled by three different behaviors: unsafe policy, safe policy and random policy to evaluate the performance of FOSP on diverse behavior samples. They are mixed in a 1:1:1 ratio, with each part containing 200 trajectories.\ref{apx:exp}.
\paragraph{Baselines} Because our work is the first approach in the safe model-based offline-to-online area to the best of our knowledge, there are not many baselines for us to compare. Prior works in offline safe RL use the model-free method which typically performs poorly in vision-only tasks. Therefore, we compare FOSP with the following two model-based baselines: \textbf{SafeDreamer}\cite{huang2023safedreamer}, an online powerful algorithm in safe model-based reinforcement learning without online planning and \textbf{DreamerV3}\cite{hafner2023dreamerv3}, a strong baseline in visual tasks but overlooks constraint violations.

\paragraph{Real Robot} The real robot environment includes a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda robot as the controlled agent and a static third-person Intel RealSense camera to obtain images as inputs. Also, the agent has additional inputs: the robot end-effector pose information. We design the task \textit{SafeReach} that controls the robot to reach a desired goal while avoiding collisions with predefined obstacles in its field of view. The rewards and costs are sparse and detected manually determining whether the robot reaches the goal or violates the constraints. The dataset consists of demonstration data, safety violation data, and failure data. Furthermore, we design some unseen safe regions and different targets that are not contained in the offline dataset. These unseen tasks are related to some undefined obstacles that need to be identified through observation which can not be solved by motion planning methods. The task further illustrates the practicality of our approach. 

\subsection{Main Results}

\paragraph{Offline Pretrain} We show our offline training result in Figure \ref{fig:offline}. FOSP outperformed the similar safe algorithm SafeDreamer, excelling in decreasing costs and better balancing task performance with constraint avoidance. Furthermore, It has comparable or slightly lower performance than DreamerV3 while achieving nearly zero violations. Note that all methods struggle to get higher rewards in SafetyPointPush because the line-of-sight obstructions necessitate online exploration.

\paragraph{Online Fine-tuning} The results of online fine-tuning are shown on Table \ref{tab:online}. It proves that our algorithm not only performs well offline but can also be fine-tuned to achieve better performance. Meanwhile, FOSP further reduces the costs during the online fine-tuning. We also compare our method with the OSRP version of SafeDreamer which can only be trained online. We notice that the SafeDreamer training exclusively online outperforms the offline-to-online fine-tuning approach. This may be because direct fine-tuning is affected by distribution shifts, making it difficult to improve performance in limited steps. So FOSP is necessary for us to bridge the safety of offline and online while maintaining its performance.\ref{apx:addexp})

\section{Conclusion}

\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduce FOSP, a novel method that integrates model-based reinforcement learning 
with offline-to-online fine-tuning to enhance safety in robotic tasks. FOSP addresses the challenges of balancing performance and safety during both offline and online phases using the reachability estimation function to unify different constraints and offline safe policies to guide online exploration. Our experimental results demonstrate that FOSP achieves robust performance in various safe planning tasks, especially those with high-dimensional vision inputs. Furthermore, we evaluate FOSP on unseen safety-critical tasks in dynamic real-world environments highlighting its practical value in safe few-shot fine-tuning. FOSP is the first approach to extend MBRL into safe offline-to-online training, showing promising results in the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark. Overall, FOSP shows a significant step forward in ensuring safe and efficient decision-making in embodied intelligence.
