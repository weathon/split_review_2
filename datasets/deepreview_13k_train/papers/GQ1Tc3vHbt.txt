\title{Optimizing $(L_0, L_1)$-Smooth Functions by Gradient Methods}

\begin{abstract}

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}




In this paper, we focus on the deterministic unconstrained optimization problem
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq-problem}
    f^* \defeq \min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^d} f(x),
\end{equation}
where $f \colon \R^d \to \R$ is a smooth function.
With the rise of deep learning, ensuring efficient convergence has become increasingly critical. Traditional optimization methods, such as the gradient descent method and its variants, often rely on assumptions like Lipschitz-smoothness to guarantee convergence rates. However, in modern machine learning problems, these assumptions might be too restrictive, especially when optimizing deep neural network models. 

In experiments provided in~\parencite{zhang2019gradient}, it was shown that the norm of the Hessian correlates with a norm of the gradient of a loss when training neural networks. This observation motivated the authors to introduce a new, more realistic assumption on a function class named $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth. The class of $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions includes the class of Lipschitz-smooth functions. Also, they provided the convergence rate of the gradient method with fixed, normalized, and clipped stepsizes for non-convex optimization and showed that normalized and clipped methods are favorable in the new setting. In recent years, many works have studied methods for solving $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth optimization problems. Despite this interest from the community, the existing convergence results are suboptimal in some important cases, and the analysis of such methods is not satisfactory. 

Motivated by this gap, the present work investigates properties of $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions and gradient methods for optimizing these functions. The following subsection discusses existing results for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth optimization.

\paragraph{Contributions.}

Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
\item In Section~\ref{section-properties}, we provide novel results and insights into the $(L_0, L_1)$-class by (i) providing new examples and operations that preserve the $(L_0, L_1)$-smoothness of functions and (ii) deriving new properties for this function class, leading to tighter bounds on descent inequalities. In Section~\ref{section-gradient}, we propose new, intuitive step sizes that follow by directly minimizing our tighter upper bounds on the function growth. We also discuss the relation between these stepsizes and those used in the normalized and clipped gradient method.
\item For nonconvex functions, we achieve the best-known $\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{ L_0 F_0}{\epsilon^2} + \frac{ L_1 F_0}{\epsilon}\right)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-nonconvex}) complexity bound for finding an $\epsilon$-stationary point, where $F_0 = f(x_0) - f^*$ is a function residual at the initial point. For convex problems, we significantly improve existing results by achieving $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2) $ (Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex})  complexity bound, where $R = \|x_0 -x^*\|$ is an initial distance to a solution.
\item We also study two new methods: normalized gradient method and gradient method with Polyak stepsizes, which do not require the knowledge of the parameters $L_0, L_1$. For both methods, we show that they enjoy the same complexity bound as the gradient method knowing $L_0$ and $L_1$ (see Theorem~\ref{thm-normalized} and Theorem~\ref{thm-polyak} ).
\item Finally, in Section~\ref{section-accelerated}, we present a simple procedure achieving the accelerated complexity of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{L_0 R^2 }{\epsilon}} + L_1^2 R^2)$. This procedure prescribes running the monotone version of the accelerated gradient method~\cite{nesterov2021primal} from the initial point constructed after a certain number of iterations of the (nonaccelerated) gradient method. 
\end{itemize}

In contrast to other results in the literature, all our complexity bounds neither depend on the initial gradient norm, nor have an exponential dependency on $L_1$.

\paragraph{Related work.}

Following the introduction of the $(L_0, L_1)$-class by \cite{zhang2019gradient}, subsequent works have explored other smoothness generalizations and analyzed gradient methods under these new assumptions.
\cite{DBLP:conf/icml/00020LL23} introduced the $\alpha$-asymmetric class, relaxing the assumption on twice differentiability and allowing a sublinear growth on the norm of a gradient. In \parencite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23}, authors went further and proposed the weakest $(r, l)$-smooth class, which allows even quadratic growth of the norm of the Hessian with respect to the norm of the gradient. Despite the generality of this assumption, there are still some issues and open questions regarding the existing results even for the basic $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth class.

In \parencite{zhang2020improved}, the authors analyzed the clipped gradient method with momentum and improved complexity bound with respect to problem parameters $L_0, L_1$. Using the right choice of clipping parameters, \cite{koloskova2023revisiting} proved, for nonconvex and convex problems, respectively, the $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 F_0}{\epsilon^2} + \frac{L_1 F_0}{\epsilon})$ and $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R}{\epsilon} + \sqrt{\frac{L}{\epsilon}} L_1 R^2)$ oracle complexity bounds for obtaining an $\epsilon$-approximate solution, where $L$ is a Lipschitz constant. For convex problems, \cite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23} proposed an (asymptotically) faster accelerated gradient method achieving the complexity of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{(L_0 + L_1\|\nabla f(x_0)\|) R^2}{\epsilon}})$.
Several works have studied adaptive optimization methods that do not require the $(L_0, L_1)$ parameters to be known. \cite{faw2023beyond, wang2023convergence} studied convergence rates for AdaGrad for stochastic nonconvex problems. \cite{hubler2024parameter} proposed a gradient method with the backtracking line search and showed the $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 F_0}{\epsilon^2} + \frac{L_1^2 F_0^2}{\epsilon^2})$ complexity bound for nonconvex problems, where $L$ is a Lipschitz constant. For convex problems, \cite{takezawa2024polyak} obtained the complexity of  $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R}{\epsilon} + \sqrt{\frac{L}{\epsilon_g}} L_1 R^2)$ for the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes, which requires knowing the optimal function value.

One interesting paper which is highly related to our work and independently appeared online during the finalization of our manuscript is \cite{gorbunov2024methods}. 
In this paper, the authors propose a new formula (called ``smooth clipping'') for choosing stepsizes in the gradient method for convex $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions. Up to absolute constants, this formula coincides with one of our suggestions (see \cref{section-gradient}), and the corresponding efficiency bound is the same as ours. The authors also show that the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes achieves the same result as in our work.
Additionally, they provide an accelerated method with complexity $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} L_1 R \exp(L_1 R)}$ and study also the strongly convex and stochastic cases as well as some adaptive methods.
In contrast, our work has a slightly different scope and offers deeper insights by providing the intuition on arriving at the ``right'' stepsize formulas, an analysis for nonconvex functions, the study of normalized gradient methods, and 
a superior version of the accelerated scheme with significantly better complexity.
Importantly, our proof techniques are somewhat different from those in~\cite{gorbunov2024methods} and are arguably more elegant, at least in the specific cases we consider in our paper.



\section{Definition and Properties of $(L_0, L_1)$-Smooth Functions}

\label{section-properties}
In this section, we state our assumptions and discuss important properties of generalized smooth functions. We start with defining our main assumption on $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions.

Throughout this paper, unless specified otherwise, we use the standard inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ and the standard Euclidean norm $\| \cdot \|$ for vectors, and the standard spectral norm $\| \cdot \|$ for matrices.

\begin{definition}
\label{asum:smooth}
A twice continuously differentiable function $f \colon \R^d \to \R$ is called \emph{$(L_0, L_1)$-smooth} (for some $L_0, L_1 \geq 0$) if it holds that
\begin{equation}
\label{def-generalized-smooth}
\| \nabla^2 f(x) \| \leq L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| \qquad\hbox{for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$}. %X$}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
The class of $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function is a wide class of functions, which includes the class of Lipschitz-smooth functions. Definition~\ref{asum:smooth} of generalized smooth function was introduced in~\parencite{zhang2019gradient}. For twice differentiable function, this definition is equivalent to definition of $\alpha$-symmetric functions with $\alpha=1$ provided in~\parencite{DBLP:conf/icml/00020LL23}. All our further results also hold for 
$\alpha$-symmetric functions, however, we use the stricter assumption for clarity in presentation. 
Any $\alpha$-symmetric twice differentiable function is also $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, but with a different choice of parameters; thus, our results also hold for $\alpha$-symmetric functions.
For the purpose of analysis of the methods, we provide an alternative and more useful first-order characterization of the class.
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma:smooth-2}
Let $f$ be a twice continuously differentiable function,
Then, $f$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth if and only if any of the following inequalities holds for any $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$:\footnote{Hereinafter, for $L_1 = 0$ and any $t \geq 0$, we assume that $\frac{e^{L_1 t} - 1}{L_1} \equiv t$, $\frac{\phi(L_1 t)}{L_1^2} \equiv \frac{1}{2} t^2$, etc., which are the limits of these expressions when $L_1 \to 0; L_1 > 0$.}
\begin{gather}
    \label{eq-lemma-smooth-2}
    \|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\| \leq (L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|) \frac{e^{L_1 \| y - x \|} - 1}{L_1},
    \\
    \label{eq-smooth-ineq-1}
    |f(y) - f(x) - \langle \nabla f(x), y - x \rangle| \leq (L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|) \frac{\phi(L_1 \|y - x\|)}{L_1^2}, %\frac{e^{L_1 \|y - x\|} - L_1 \|y - x\| - 1}{L_1^2}.
\end{gather}
where $\phi(t) = e^t - t - 1$ ($t \geq 0$).
\end{lemma}
 
It is worth noting that the bound in inequality~(\ref{eq-lemma-smooth-2}) of Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-2} is stronger than the one provided in~\parencite[Corollary~A.4]{zhang2020improved}.
The bound in inequality~(\ref{eq-smooth-ineq-1}) of Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-2} is tighter than those presented in previous works (see, for example, Lemma~A.8 in~\parencite{zhang2020improved}, Lemma~8 in~\parencite{hubler2024parameter}). These tighter estimates allow us to construct gradient methods in the sequel. In our analysis we use the properties of a function $\phi(t)$ and its conjugate $\phi^*(t)$, which we summarize in the following lemma.
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma-phi-properties}
The following statements for the function $\phi(t) = e^t - t - 1$ hold true,
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\phi(t) \leq \frac{t^2}{2 - \frac{2}{3} t }$ for all $t \in [0, 3) $.
    \item $\phi^*(\gamma) = (1 + \gamma) \ln (1 + \gamma) - \gamma$ ($\gamma \geq 0$) is the conjugate function to $\phi$.
    \item $\frac{\gamma^2}{2 + \gamma} \leq \phi^*(\gamma) \leq \frac{\gamma^2}{2}$ for all $\gamma \geq 0$
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
In the following lemma, we summarize some important properties of  $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions.  
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma:smooth-5}
    Let $f$ be a convex $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth nonlinear\footnote{According to \cref{lemma:smooth-2}, this means that $L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| > 0$ for any $x \in \R^d$.} function. Then, for any $x, y\in\mathbb{R}^d$, 
\begin{gather}
    \label{eq-smooth-ineq-4}
    f(y)
    \geq
    f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), y - x \rangle
    +
    \frac{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) \|}{L_1^2} \phi_*\Bigl( \frac{L_1 \| \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x) \|}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) \|} \Bigr),
    \\
    \label{eq-smooth-ineq-5}
    \begin{multlined}
        \langle \nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y), x - y\rangle
        \geq
        \frac{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) \|}{L_1^2} \phi_*\Bigl( \frac{L_1 \| \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x) \|}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) \|} \Bigr)
        \\
        \quad +
        \frac{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|}{L_1^2} \phi_*\Bigl( \frac{L_1 \| \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x) \|}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|} \Bigr),
    \end{multlined}
\end{gather}
where $\phi_*(\gamma) = (1 + \gamma) \ln(1 + \gamma) - \gamma$ ($\gamma \geq 0$)
is the conjugate function to $\phi$ from \cref{lemma:smooth-2}. Moreover, since $\phi_*(\gamma) \geq \frac{\gamma^2}{2 + \gamma}$ by Lemma~\ref{lemma-phi-properties} (3), lower bound in \cref{eq-smooth-ineq-4} can be simplified as follows
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SimpleLowerBoundOnBregmanDistance}
    f(y)
    \geq
    f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), y - x \rangle
    +
    \frac{\| \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x) \|^2}{2 (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) \|) + L_1 \| \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x) \|}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Notice that Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-5} is a generalization of \parencite[Theorem 2.1.5]{nesterov2018lectures} for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, and matches it when $L_1 = 0$ (since $\frac{1}{L_1^2} \phi_{*}(L_1 \alpha) \to \frac{1}{2} \alpha^2$ as $L_1 \to 0$).
 Next, we present some examples to support the choice of $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth assumption. 
\begin{example}
\label{prop-example-1}
The function $f(x) = \frac{1}{p}\|x\|^p$, where $p > 2$, is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth with arbitrary $L_1 >0$ and $L_0 =(\frac{p - 2}{L_1})^{p - 2}$.
\end{example} 
\begin{example}
    \label{example-logistic}
    The function $f(x) = \ln(1 + e^x)$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth with arbitrary $L_1 \in [0,1]$ and $L_0 = \frac{1}{4} (1 - L_1)^2$.
\end{example}
The preceding examples also show that the choice of $L_0, L_1$ parameters is generally not unique. While we cannot guarantee that the class is closed under all standard operations, such as the summation, affine substitution of the argument, we can still show that some operations do preserve $(L_0, L_1)$-smoothness under certain additional assumptions.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop-big}
    Let $f \colon \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a twice continuously differentiable $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function.
    Then, the following statements hold:
    \begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt, itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=20pt]
        \item Let $g \colon \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be an $L$-smooth and $M$-Lipschitz twice continuously differentiable function. Then, the sum $f + g$ is $(L_0', L_1')$-smooth with $L_0' = L_0 + M L_1 + L$ and $L_1' = L_1$.
        \item Let $f_i \colon \mathbb{R}^{d_i} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be an $(L_{0, i}, L_{1,i})$-smooth function for each $i=1,\ldots,n$. Then, the function $h \colon \mathbb{R}^{d_1} \times \ldots \times \R^{d_n} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ given by  $h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x_i)$, where $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_n)$, is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth with $L_0 = \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} L_{0,i}$ and $L_1=\max_{1 \leq i \leq n} L_{1,i}$.
        \item If $f$ is univariate ($d = 1$) and $h(x) = f(\langle a, x\rangle + b)$, $x \in \R^d$, where $a \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $b\in\mathbb{R}$, then $h$ is $(L_0', L_1')$-smooth with parameters $L_0' = \|a\|^2 L_0$ and $L_1' = \|a\| L_1 $.
        \item 
        Let additionally $\nabla^2 f(x) \succ 0$ for all $x \in \R^d$ and $f$ be $1$-coercive\footnote{This means that $\frac{f(x)}{\| x \|} \to +\infty$ as $\| x \| \to \infty$.}. Then, $f$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth iff its conjugate $f_*$ (which is, under our assumptions, defined on the entire space and also twice continuously differentiable) satisfies $\nabla^2 f_*(s) \succeq \frac{1}{L_0 + L_1 \|s\|} I$ for all $s \in \R^d$, where $I$ is the identity matrix.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
One simple example of the additive term~$g$ satisfying the assumptions in the first item of Proposition~\ref{prop-big} is an affine function (for which $L = 0$); another interesting example is the soft-max function $g(x) = \mu \ln(\sum_{i = 1}^m e^{[\langle a_i, x \rangle + b_i] / \mu})$, where $a_i \in \R^d$, $b_i \in \R$, $\mu > 0$. Based on the second statement of \cref{prop-big} and \cref{prop-example-1}, 
the function $f(x) = \frac{1}{p} \|x\|_p^p \equiv \frac{1}{p} \sum_{i=1}^d |x_i|^p$ with $p > 2$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth with arbitrary $L_1 > 0$ and $L_0 = (\frac{p - 2}{L_1})^{p - 2}$. Using the third statement, we can generalize Example~\ref{example-logistic} and conclude that $f(x) = \ln(1 + e^{\langle a, x \rangle})$ is also $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth with arbitrary $L_1 \in [0, \|a\|]$ and $L_0 = \frac{1}{4} (\| a \| - L_1)^2$.
Also, we can use the last statement of the proposition to show that $f(x) = \frac{L_0}{L_1^2} \phi(L_1 \| x \|) \equiv \frac{L_0}{L_1^2 }(e^{L_1 \|x\|} - L_1 \|x\| - 1)$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth since the Hessian of its conjugate $f_*(s) = \frac{L_0}{L_1^2} \phi_*(\frac{L_1 \| s \|}{L_0}) \equiv \frac{L_0}{L_1^2} [( (1 + \frac{L_1 \|s\|}{L_0}) \ln (1 + \frac{L_1 \|s\|}{L_0}) - \frac{L_1 \|s\|}{L_0}]$ has the form $\nabla^2 f_* (s) = \frac{1}{L_0 + L_1 \|s\|} I$. In particular, we can construct an $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function by taking any convex function $h_*$, adding to it $\phi_*$ and taking the conjugate (this corresponds to the infimal convolution of $h$ with $\phi$).



\section{Gradient Method}

\label{section-gradient}
Having established a few important properties of an $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function~$f$, we now turn our attention to the \emph{gradient method} for minimizing such a function:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq-gradient}
x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k \nabla f(x_k),
\qquad
k \geq 0,
\end{equation}
where $x_0\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is a starting point and $\eta_k \geq 0$ are certain stepsizes.

We start with showing that the gradient update rule~\eqref{eq-gradient} and the ``right'' formula for the stepsize~$\eta_k$ both naturally arise from the classical idea in optimization theory---choosing the next iterate~$x_{k + 1}$ by minimizing the global upper bound on the objective function value constructed around the current iterate~$x_k$ (see~\parencite{nesterov2018lectures}).
Indeed, let $x \in \R^d$ be the current point and let $a := L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| > 0$.
According to \cref{eq-smooth-ineq-1}, for any $y \in \R^d$,
\begin{equation*}
    f(y) \leq f(x) + \langle  \nabla f(x), y - x \rangle +  \frac{a}{L_1^2} \phi(L_1 \| y - x \|).
\end{equation*}
Our goal is to minimize the right-hand of the above inequality in~$y$.
Since the last term in this bound depends only on the norm of $y - x$, the optimal point $y^* = T(x)$ is the result of the gradient step $T(x) = x - r^* \frac{\nabla f(x)}{\| \nabla f(x) \|}$ for some $r^* \geq 0$ ensuring the following progress in decreasing the function value:
\begin{equation*}
    f(x) - f(T(x)) \geq \max_{r \geq 0} \Bigl\{ \|\nabla f(x)\| r -  \frac{a}{L_1^2} \phi(L_1 r) \Bigr\} = \frac{a}{L_1^2}\phi_*\Bigl( \frac{L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|}{a} \Bigr),
\end{equation*}
where $\phi_*$ is the conjugate function to~$\phi$ (see \cref{lemma-phi-properties}).
furthermore, $r^*$ is exactly the solution of the above optimization problem, satisfying $L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| = a \phi'(L_1 r^*)$.
Solving this equation, using the fact that $(\phi')^{-1}(\gamma) = \phi_*'(\gamma) = \ln(1 + \gamma)$, we obtain $r^* = \frac{1}{L_1} \phi_*'(\frac{L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|}{a}) = \frac{1}{L_1} \ln(1 + \frac{L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|}{a})$.

The above considerations lead us to the following \emph{optimal} choice of stepsizes in~\eqref{eq-gradient}:
\begin{align}
\label{eq-grad-step-1}
    \boxed{
        \eta^*_k = \dfrac{1}{L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|} \ln\Bigl( 1 + \frac{L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|}{ L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x_k) \| } \Bigr),
    }
    \qquad k \geq 0,
\end{align}
resulting in the following progress in decreasing the objective:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:exact-expression-for-function-progress}
    f(x_k) - f(x_{k + 1}) \geq \frac{L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|}{L_1^2}\phi_*\Bigl( \frac{L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x_k) \|} \Bigr)
    \defeq \Delta_k.
\end{equation}
The above expression for~$\Delta_k$ is quite cumbersome but, in fact, it behaves as the simple fraction $\frac{\| \nabla f(x_k) \|^2}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x_k) \|}$.
More precisely, from \cref{lemma-phi-properties}(3), we see that
\begin{align*}
\frac{ \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|} \leq \Delta_k \leq \frac{ \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2}{ 2 (L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|)}.
\end{align*}
Thus, there is not much point in keeping the complicated expression~\eqref{eq:exact-expression-for-function-progress} and we can safely simplify it as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:simplified-expression-for-function-progress}
    f(x_k) - f(x_{k + 1}) \geq \frac{ \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|}.
\end{equation}

Interestingly, we can also arrive at exactly the same bound~\eqref{eq:simplified-expression-for-function-progress} by using a simpler choice of stepsizes.
Specifically, replacing $\ln(1 + \gamma)$ with its lower bound $\frac{2 \gamma}{2 + \gamma}$ (which is responsible for the inequality in \cref{lemma-phi-properties}(3) that we used to simplify \cref{eq:exact-expression-for-function-progress} into \cref{eq:simplified-expression-for-function-progress}), we obtain
the following \emph{simplified stepsizes}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq-grad-step-2}
    \boxed{
        \eta_k = \frac{1}{L_0 + \frac{3}{2} L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|},
    }
    \qquad k \geq 0.
\end{equation}
 

Further, note that, up to absolute constants, stepsize~\eqref{eq-grad-step-2} acts as $\frac{1}{\max\{ L_0, L_1 \| \nabla f(x_k) \| \}} = \min\{ \frac{1}{L_0}, \frac{1}{L_1 \| \nabla f(x_k) \|} \}$, which is the so-called clipping stepsize used in many previous works~\cite{zhang2019gradient,zhang2020improved,koloskova2023revisiting}.
Thus, with the right choice of absolute constants, we can expect the corresponding clipping stepsizes, to satisfy a similar inequality to~\eqref{eq:simplified-expression-for-function-progress}.
This is indeed the case and we can show, in particular, that the \emph{clipping stepsizes}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq-clipping}
    \boxed{
        \eta^{\ClippingIndex}_k = \min \Bigl\{ \frac{1}{2 L_0}, \frac{1}{3 L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|} \Bigr\},
    }
    \qquad
    k \geq 0,
\end{equation}

We have thus demonstrated in this section that clipping stepsizes~\eqref{eq-clipping} are simply a convenient approximation of the optimal stepsizes~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} ensuring a similar bound on the objective progress.
This observation seems to be a new insight into clipping stepsizes which has not been previously explored in the literature.
\subsection{Nonconvex functions}
Based on preceding developments, we present a convergence rate result for nonconvex functions.
\begin{theorem} \label{thm-grad-nonconvex} Let $f(\cdot)$ be an $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, and let $\{x_k\}$ be iterate sequence of gradient decent method~\eqref{eq-gradient} with one of the stepsize choices given by~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} or~\eqref{eq-grad-step-2}.
Then, after $K \geq 0$ iterations, the following bound holds with $F_0 := f(x_0) - f^*$:
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{0 \leq i \leq K} \|\nabla f(x_i)\| \leq \sqrt{ \dfrac{2 L_0 F_0}{K+1} } + \dfrac{3 L_1 F_0}{2 (K+1)}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}
 By Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-nonconvex}, the smallest number $K$ of iterations required to achieve an $\epsilon_g$-stationary point is $K \geq \max \{\frac{4 L_0 F_{0}}{\epsilon_g^2},  \frac{6 L_1 F_0}{\epsilon_g} \}$.
This rate matches, up to absolute constants, the rate in \parencite{koloskova2023revisiting} for clipped gradient descent with $\eta = \frac{1}{9}(L_0 + cL_1)$ for $c=\frac{L_0}{L_1}$, or equivalently the gradient method with stepsize $\eta_k = \frac{1}{18 L_0} \min \{1, \frac{L_0}{L_1 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|}\}$. Furthermore, our rate is significantly better than  rate $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 F_0}{\epsilon_g^2} + \frac{L_1^2 F_0 }{L_0})$ obtained in~\parencite{zhang2019gradient} for the clipped gradient method since $\frac{L_1 F_0}{\epsilon_g} \leq \frac{L_0^2 F_0}{2 \epsilon_g} + \frac{ L_1^2 F_0}{2 L_0}$, and the latter expression can be arbitrarily far away from the former whenever $L_0$ is sufficiently small and $L_1$ is distinct from zero.
In addition to that, our convergence rate result does not depend on the gradient norm at the initial point, in contrast to~\cite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23} who consider a wider class of generalized smooth functions but whose rate depends on $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|$. Also, our rate from Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-nonconvex}  is better than $\mathcal{O}\bigl(\frac{L_0 F_0}{\epsilon_g^2} + \frac{L_1^2 F_0^2}{\epsilon_g^2}\bigr)$ provided in \parencite{hubler2024parameter} for the gradient method with the backtracking line search.

\subsection{Convex functions}
Let us now provide the convergence rate for convex functions.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm-grad-convex} Let $f(\cdot)$ be convex and $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, and let $\{x_k\}$ be the iterate sequence of gradient descent method~\eqref{eq-gradient} with one of the stepsize choices given in~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} or~\eqref{eq-grad-step-2}.
Then, for a given $\epsilon > 0$, we have $f(x_K) - f^* \leq \epsilon$ when the number $K$ of iterations satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    K \geq  \max \Bigl\{ \frac{4 L_0 R_0^2}{\epsilon}, 36 L_1^2 R_0^2 \Bigr\},
\end{equation*}
where $R = \|x_0 - x^*\|$ and  $x^*$ is an arbitrary solution to the problem in~\eqref{eq-problem}.
\end{theorem}

To prove this estimate, we define a monotonically increasing function $\psi(g) = \frac{g^2}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 g}$.
Next, we use Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-2} with substituted  $x = x^*$ and $y=x_k$ in \cref{eq:SimpleLowerBoundOnBregmanDistance}, and provide a lower bound 
\begin{equation*}
    f(x_k) - f^* \geq \psi(\|\nabla f(x_k)\|).
\end{equation*}
Hence, by denoting $R_{k}= \|x_k - x^*\|$, $g_k = \|\nabla f(x_k)\|$, $f_k = f(x_k) - f^*$ we have  $g_k \leq \psi^{-1}(f_k)$. Then, by using the preceding estimate and the updated rule of the method, we obtain that the following inequality holds for all $k \geq 0$
\begin{equation*}
    R_{k}^2 - R_{k+1}^2 \geq \frac{f_k \psi(g_k)}{g_k^2} \geq \frac{f_k^2}{[\psi^{-1}(f_k)]^2}.
\end{equation*}
Finally, we obtain the desired result by summing these inequalities over $k = 0, \ldots, K$ and simplifying the equation. 

It is worth noting that the convergence rate in Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex} includes parameter $L_1$ without dependency on $\epsilon$, while for parameter $L_0$, the dependency is as in the standard rate for $L_0$-smooth functions $\mathcal{O
}(\frac{L_0}{\epsilon})$. It can be seen as, for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions, the gradient method with the proposed step sizes requires a constant number of iterations $\mathcal{O}(L_1^2 R^2)$ to reach a region where the function $f(\cdot)$ behaves like $L_0$-smooth function. After reaching this number of iterations, the rate of the method depends only on the parameter $L_0$ and does not depend on $L_1$.

In Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex}, we do not make an assumption on $L$-smoothness of a function, while this assumption is used in~\parencite{koloskova2023revisiting}. Moreover, the rate in the theorem is better than $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + \sqrt{\frac{L}{\epsilon}} L_1 R^2)$ rate provided in \parencite{koloskova2023revisiting} for clipped gradient method, since it does not have $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}$ dependency on $L_1$ and $L$. Also, our result does not include the norm $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|$  of the gradient at an initialization point in the estimate, while the rate provided in~\parencite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23} does depend on $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|$ which can be large and be an order of $L$. Consider for example $f(x) = \frac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}$ (see Proposition~\ref{prop-example-1})  for $p >2$ and starting point $x_0$ sufficiently far from the origin, in this case, the gradient $\|\nabla f(x_0)\| = \|x_0\|^{p-1}$ can be arbitrary large.


\section{Normalized Gradient Method}

\label{section-normalized}
To run the gradient method from \cref{section-gradient}, it is necessary to know the parameters $(L_0, L_1)$ in advance. In many real-life examples, those parameters are unknown, and it might be computationally expensive to estimate them. 
Furthermore, for any given function~$f$, the pair $(L_0, L_1)$ is generally not unique (see \cref{prop-example-1,example-logistic}), and it is not clear in advance which pair would result in the best possible convergence rate of our optimization method.
To address this issue, in this section, we present another version of the gradient method that does not require knowing $(L_0, L_1)$.
This is the \emph{normalized gradient method}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq-normalized}
    x_{k+1} = x_k - \frac{\beta_k}{\|\nabla f(x_k)\|}\nabla f(x_k),
    \qquad
    k \geq 0,
\end{equation}
where $x_0 \in \R^d$ is a certain starting point, and $\beta_k$ are positive coefficients.
The following result describes the efficiency of the normalized gradient method.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm-normalized}
Let $f$ be a convex $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, and let $x_k$ be the iterates of the normalized gradient method~\eqref{eq-normalized} with constant coefficients $\beta_k = \frac{\hat{R}}{\sqrt{K+1}}$, $0 \leq k \leq K - 1$, where $\hat{R} > 0$ is a parameter and $K \geq 1$ is the total number of iterations of the method (fixed in advance). Let $x_K^* =\argmin_{x \in \{x_0,\ldots, x_K\}} f(x)$ be the iterate with the smallest function value, and let $\bar{R} \defeq \frac{R^2}{\hat{R}} + \hat{R}$, where $R \defeq \| x_0 - x^* \|$. Then, $f(x_K^*) - f^* \leq \epsilon$ whenever
\begin{equation*}
    K \geq  \max\Bigl\{ \frac{L_0 \bar{R}^2 }{\epsilon}, \frac{4}{9} L_1^2 \bar{R}^2 \Bigr\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}
The parameter $\hat{R}$ in the formula for coefficients~$\beta_k$ is an estimation of the initial distance~$R$ to a solution, and the best complexity bound of $K^* \defeq \BigO(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2)$ is achieved whenever $\hat{R} = R$. Note that, even if $\hat{R} \neq R$, the method still converges but with a slightly worse total complexity of $K^* \rho^2$, where $\rho = \max \{\frac{R}{\hat{R}}, \frac{\hat{R}}{R}\}$.

The proof of \cref{thm-normalized} is based on the following two important facts~\cite[Section~3]{nesterov2018lectures}.
First, under the proper choice of coefficients~$\beta_k$, the normalized gradient method ensures that the minimal value~$v_K^*$ among $v_k \defeq \frac{\langle \nabla f(x_k), x_k - x^* \rangle}{\| \nabla f(x_k) \|}$, $0 \leq k \leq K$, converges to zero at the rate of $\frac{\bar{R}}{\sqrt{K}}$.
These quantities $v_k$ have a geometrical meaning---each of them is exactly the distance from the point~$x^*$ to the supporting hyperplane to the sublevel set of~$f$ at the point~$x_k$.
Second, whenever $v_K^*$ converges to zero, so does $\min_{0 \leq k \leq K} f_k - f^*$.
Moreover, we can express the latter convergence rate via the former whenever we can bound, for any given $v \geq 0$, the function residual $f(x) - f^*$ over the ball $\| x - x^* \| \leq v$:
\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Lemma 3.2.1]{nesterov2018lectures}}]
\label{lemma-nesterov}
Let $f \colon \R^d \to \R$ be a differentiable convex function. Then, for any $x, y \in \R^d$ and\footnote{Here $[t]_+ \defeq \max\{ t, 0 \}$ is the nonnegative part of $t \in \R$.} $v_f(x; y) \defeq \frac{ [\langle \nabla f(x), x - y\rangle]_+}{\| \nabla f(x)\| }$, it holds that
\begin{align}
f(x) - f(y) \leq \max_{z \in \R^d} \{ f(z) - f(y) : \|z -y\| \leq v_f(x; y) \}.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
In our case---when the function~$f$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth---the corresponding bound can be obtained from~\cref{lemma:smooth-2}.
In \cref{thm-normalized}, we fix the number of iterations $K$ before running the method, which is a standard approach for the (normalized)-(sub)gradient methods (Section~3.2 in~\cite{nesterov2018lectures}).
However, doing so may be undesirable in practice since it becomes difficult to continue running the method if the time budget was suddenly increased and also prevents the method from using larger stepsizes at the initial iterations.
To overcome these drawbacks, one can use time-varying coefficients by setting $\beta_k = \frac{\hat{R}}{\sqrt{k+1}}$, $0 \leq k \leq K - 1$.

Moreover, one can completely eliminate this extra logarithmic factor by switching to an appropriate modification of the normalized gradient method such as Dual Averaging~\cite{Nesterov2005PrimaldualSM}.

When $\hat{R} = R$, the complexity of the normalized gradient method is $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2)$ which matches that of the previously considered gradient method (see \cref{thm-grad-convex}). However, recall that the previous algorithm requires knowing $(L_0, L_1)$, and its rate depends on the particular choice of these constants. In contrast, the normalized gradient method does not require the knowledge of these parameters, and its ``real'' complexity is 
\[
\BigO(1) \min_{L_0, L_1} 
\Bigl\{ \frac{L_0^2 \bar{R}^2 }{\epsilon} + L_1^2 \bar{R}^2 \colon \text{$f$ is $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth} \Bigr\},
\]
where $\BigO(1)$ is an absolute constant.


\section{Gradient Method with Polyak Stepsizes}

\label{section-polyak}
In the previous sections, the parameters required to run the methods were $L_0, L_1$ for the gradient method and the initial distance to a solution $R$ for the normalized gradient method. Sometimes, both of these parameters are unknown while the optimal value of the function is available. One of the examples of such problems is over-parametrized models in machine learning, where $f^*$ is usually 0.
In this section, we focus on the case when $f^*$ is known and analyze the gradient method~\eqref{eq-gradient} with \emph{Polyak stepsizes}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq-polyak-step}
    \eta_k = \frac{f(x_k) - f^*}{\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2},
    \qquad
    k \geq 0.
\end{equation}
We show that in the convex case, the convergence rate of gradient method with Polyak stepsizes does not have an exponential dependency on $L_0, L_1$ constants.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm-polyak} Let $f(\cdot)$ be convex and $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function, let $x_k$ be iterate from gradient method~\eqref{eq-gradient} with stepsizes~\eqref{eq-polyak-step}.  Let $x_K^* =\argmin_{x \in \{x_0,\ldots, x_K\}} f(x)$, then to get $f(x_K^*) - f^* \leq \epsilon$ the method requires at most the following number of iterations:
\begin{equation*}
    K \leq \max \Bigl\{ \frac{4 L_0 R^2}{\epsilon},  36 L_1^2 R^2 \Bigr\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}
We prove the theorem by using a standard inequality for the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes,
\begin{equation*}
    R_k^2 - R_{k+1}^2 \geq \frac{f_k^2}{g_k^2},
\end{equation*}
where $R_{k}= \|x_k - x^*\|$, $g_k = \|\nabla f(x_k)\|$ and $f_k = f(x_k) -f^*$ for all $k \geq 1$. We then leverage the simplified lower bound~\eqref{eq:SimpleLowerBoundOnBregmanDistance} from Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-5} and bound gradient norm $g_k$ by $\psi^{-1}(f_k)$, where $\psi(g) =\frac{g^2}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 g} $ for $g\geq 0$, and obtain,
\begin{equation*}
    R_k^2 - R_{k+1}^2 \geq \frac{f_k^2}{[\psi^{-1}(f_k)]^2}.
\end{equation*}
Further, by summing these equations, taking a minimum over all $f_k$, and simplifying the resulting inequalities, we obtained the desired bound on $f_K^*$
Notice that the rate $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm-polyak} is the same as the rate for gradient method in Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex} and normalized gradient method in Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-nonconvex}. Our rate is better than $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + \sqrt{\frac{L}{\epsilon}} L_1 R^2)$ provided in~\cite{takezawa2024polyak}, since it does not have a dependency on $\epsilon$ with respect to $L_1$ and $L$ parameters. Furthermore, it is more general since we do not assume $L$-smoothness of the function. Finally, it is worth mentioning that the rate for the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes holds for any choice of  $(L_0, L_1)$. Thus, the rate holds for the best choice of $(L_0, L_1)$ pair and is the same as for the normalized gradient method.



\section{Accelerated Gradient Method}

\label{section-accelerated}


This section focuses on developing an accelerated method for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth function.
We highlight the following important observations. Consider a point $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, such that $\|\nabla f(x)\| \leq \frac{L_0}{L_1}$, then by the definition of $(L_0, L_1)$-smoothness, the following relation holds,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq-accelerated-property}
    \|\nabla^2 f(x) \| \leq L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\| \leq 2 L_0.
\end{equation}
Hence, at all points $x$  whose gradients are bounded by a constant $\frac{L_0}{L_1}$, the function $f$ behaves like $2L_0$-smooth function.
Furthermore, leveraging the results of Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-5},  it is possible to bound a gradient norm by a function progress $f(x)- f^*$. In case when the function progress along the trajectory of the method is non-increasing, one can guarantee that the iterates satisfy the bound~\cref{eq-accelerated-property}.
However, the monotonic decrease cannot be guaranteed for all standard versions of the Fast Gradient Method (FGM). Nevertheless, one monotone version of the FGM exists, which turns out to preserve precisely the same property \parencite{nesterov2021primal}.
In contrast to the existing works and motivated by the above reasoning, we propose to use a two-stage procedure (see Algorithm~\ref{algo:Two-Stage}): use gradient method to find a point $\bar{x}$ with small gradient norm and run Accelerated Gradient Method with Small-Dimensional Relaxation (AGMsDR) with initial point $x_0 = \bar{x}$ , which ensures that the gradient norms of the iterates stays small. For convenience of the reader we re-stated AGMsDR in Algorithm~\ref{algo:AGMsDR}. 

By running the gradient method, we ensure that the starting point of AGMsDR satisfies the following condition, $F_0 = f(x_0) - f^* \leq \frac{L_0}{5 L_1^2}$. This consequently helps us to upper bound a norm of the gradient $\|\nabla f(x)\|$ on a sublevel set $x \in \{z \in \mathbb{R}^d : f(z) \leq f(x_0)\}$, using the simplified lower bound in Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-5}, as follows:
\[
   \psi(\| \nabla f(x)\|) \leq f(x) - f^* \leq  F_0 \leq \frac{L_0}{5 L_1^2}, \]
where $\psi(\gamma) = \frac{\gamma^2}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \gamma}$, from this inequality we obtain that $\|\nabla f(x)\| \leq \psi^{-1}(F_0) = \frac{L_0}{L_1}$, and consequently \cref{eq-accelerated-property} holds.

Our second ingredient for the further analysis, is an operation $T(\cdot): \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$, which has the following property. For any point $x \in \mathcal{F}_0 = \{ x: f(x) \leq f(x_0)\}$ in the sublevel set, the point $T(x)$ belongs to a sublevel set $\mathcal{F}_0$, $T(x) \in \mathcal{F}_0$, and
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq-lemma-accelerated-sublevel}
        f(T(x)) \leq f(x) - \frac{1}{2L} \|\nabla f(x)\|^2.
    \end{equation}
 

\begin{algorithm}[tbh!]
    \caption{Two-Stage Procedure } 
    \label{algo:Two-Stage}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \State \textbf{Input:} Initial point $x_s \in \R^d$, constants $L_0, L_1 > 0$, update rule $T(\cdot)$.
        \State Run gradient method from~$x_s$ with stepsizes~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} or~\eqref{eq-grad-step-2} to get $x_0 \in \R^d \colon f(x_0) -f^* \leq \frac{L_0}{5 L_1^2}$. 
        \State Run $K$ iterations of $\text{AGMsDR}(x_0, 3L_0, T(\cdot))$ to get $x_K \in \R^d$.
	\end{algorithmic} 
\end{algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[tb]
    \caption{Accelerated Gradient Method with Small-Dimensional Relaxation (AGMsDR)~\cite{nesterov2021primal}} 
    \label{algo:AGMsDR}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \State \textbf{Input:} Initial point $x_0 \in \R^d$, constant $L>0$, update rule $T(\cdot)$, number of iterations~$K \geq 1$.
        \State $v_0 = x_0$, $A_0 = 0$, $\zeta_0(x) = \frac{1}{2} \|x - x_0\|^2$.
		\For {$k = 0, 1, \ldots, K - 1$}
            \State $y_k = \argmin_y \{ f(y) \colon y = v_k + \beta(x_k - v_k), \beta \in [0, 1] \}$.
            \State $x_{k+1} = T(y_{k})$.
            \State Find $a_{k+1} > 0$ from the equation $L a_{k+1}^2 = A_k + a_{k+1}$.
                   Set $A_{k+1} = A_k + a_{k+1}$.
            \State $v_{k+1} = \argmin_{x \in \R^d} \{ \zeta_{k+1}(x) := \zeta_k(x) + a_{k+1} [f(y_k) + \langle \nabla f(y_k), x - y_k \rangle] \}$.
		\EndFor
        \Return $x_K$.
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Our procedure guarantees two important properties of the iterates: (1) the gradient norms of iterates $x_k$ and $y_k$ of AGMsDR stay bounded by constant $L_0/L_1$, which is independent of the gradient norm of the initial point, and guarantees the small local Lipschitz constant along the trajectory (2)  the monotonically decreasing function values along the iterates $x_k, y_k$, meaning that $f(x_{k+1}) \leq f(y_k) \leq f(x_k)$ for all $k\geq 0$. 

The rest of the analysis of the method is similar to the proof of Theorem~1 in~\cite{nesterov2021primal}, which leads to the following theorem.
The full proof of 

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}
    Let $f(\cdot)$ be a convex function, and let  $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$,  $L > 0$ and an operator $T_L : \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ be such that for any $x\in \{z \in \mathbb{R}^d : f(z) \leq f(x_0)\}$, 
    it holds that
    \[f(x) - f(T_L(x)) \geq \frac{1}{2L} \|\nabla f(x)\|^2.\]
    Then the following statements are true for  AGMsDR (\cref{algo:AGMsDR}) with
    initial point $x_0$ and update rule $T_L(\cdot)$ for all $k \geq 0$:
    \begin{align*}
    & (1)\quad f(x_{k+1}) \leq f(y_k) \leq f(x_k),
    &&  (2) \quad f(x_k) - f^* \leq \frac{2 L R^2}{k^2}.
    \end{align*}
    where $R = \|x_0 - x^*\|$.
\end{theorem}

Based on Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}, the smallest number of iterations required to achieve
$f(x_k) - f^* \leq \epsilon$ with a right choice of starting point $x_0$ and parameter $L$ is $\sqrt{\frac{16 L_0 }{\epsilon}} R_0$, where $R_0 = \|x_0 - x^*\|$.
Denote $R = \|x_s - x^*\|$, where $x_s$ is a starting point of two-stage procedure and observe that $R_0 \leq R$. Also, by Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex}, the gradient method requires $34 L_1^2 R^2$ number of iterations to obtain a point $\bar{x}$, such that $f(\bar{x}) - f^* \leq \frac{L_0}{5 L_1^2}$.
Combining these two results, we obtain the total complexity number, which is presented in the next theorem.
\begin{theorem}
\label{thm-accelerated}
    Let $f(\cdot)$ be an $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth and convex function and $\{x_k\}$ be iterates from the two step-procedure (Algorithm~\ref{algo:Two-Stage})
    with $T_L(\cdot)$ being a gradient update with stepsizes~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} or~\eqref{eq-grad-step-1} ,
    and $L=3 L_0$. Then for a given $\epsilon > 0$, we have $f(x_K) - f^* \leq \epsilon$ when the number $K$ of oracle calls satisfies
    \[K \geq  m \sqrt{\frac{12 L_0 R^2}{\epsilon}} + 36 L_1^2 R^2, \]
    where $m$ is a number of oracle calls to compute $y_k$.
\end{theorem}
Existing results on convex $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth problems include convergence of Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) method provided in~\parencite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23} and Similar Triangles Method with Max (STM-max) from~\parencite{gorbunov2024methods}. 
However, the preceding rates depend the gradient norm $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|$ at the initial point or the exponential factor $\exp(L_1 R)$, both of which could be quite large if the initial point is far away from the minimizer.

Observe that the convergence rate from Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated} includes $L_1$ without dependency on $\epsilon$, and it does not have an exponential dependency on the parameters $L_0$ and $L_1$ and initial distance $R$. Moreover, unlike the convergence rate for NAG obtained in~\cite{DBLP:conf/nips/LiQTRJ23} and Similar Triangles Method (STM) in~\cite{gorbunov2024methods}, our rate does not depend on the gradient norm at the starting point. Because after the first stage of our procedure the gradient norm becomes smaller than $\frac{L_0}{L_1}$ and all the iterates of the second stage maintain this property and stay in the sublevel set $\mathcal{L}$ by construction.
However, AGMsDR at every step requires solving the one-dimensional problem to find the iterate $y_k$, which leads to an additional multiplicative factor $m$ in $m \sqrt{\frac{12 L_0 R^2}{\epsilon}}$. The corresponding one-dimensional problem can usually be solved efficiently with a small number $m$ of oracle calls for many practical problems. For simplicity, we are not estimating this number precisely and leave it for future work to understand how to estimate this complexity more precisely and whether it is possible to remove this one-dimensional search from the accelerated procedure.

\section{Conclusion}

This work investigates gradient methods for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth optimization problems. We have provided new insights into this function class and presented  examples along with the operations preserving the $(L_0, L_1)$-smoothness. Moreover, we have provided new properties of the function class that have rendered tighter bounds on the descent inequalities. Based on these tighter bounds, we derived new stepsizes for the gradient method and connected them with normalized and clipped stepsizes. For such stepsizes,  we have shown the best-known $\mathcal{O}(\frac{ L_0 F_{0}}{\epsilon_g^2} + \frac{ L_1 F_0}{\epsilon_g})$ complexity for finding an $\epsilon_g$-stationary point in non-convex problems.
For convex problems, we have significantly improved the existing results and obtained the $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2)$ complexity for the gradient method with our stepsizes. We have also analyzed the gradient method with Polyak stepsizes and a normalized gradient method that achieve a similar $\mathcal{O}(\frac{L_0 R^2}{\epsilon} + L_1^2 R^2)$ complexity bound, which is significantly better than previously known complexity bounds.
Both of these methods are useful because they automatically adjust to the best possible pair of possible parameters $(L_0, L_1)$.
Finally, we have proposed a new procedure by combining our results on the gradient method with the AGMsDR method, and showed fast $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{L_0 R^2 }{\epsilon}} + L_1^2 R^2)$ complexity bound which does not have the dependency on the initial gradient norm and does not have an exponential dependency on $L_1$, in contrast to previous works.
Whether it is possible to eliminate the line search for determining $y_k$ in AGMsDR method is an interesting open question for further research.

Let $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be arbitrary and let $h := y - x \neq 0$ (otherwise the claim is trivial).
Then, for any $t \in [0, 1]$, using \cref{def-generalized-smooth}, we can estimate
\[
    \| \nabla f(x + t h) - \nabla f(x) \|
    \leq
    \| h \| \int_0^t \| \nabla^2 f(x + \tau h) \| d \tau
    \leq
    \| h \| \int_0^t (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x + \tau h) \|) d \tau
    =:
    \nu(t).
\]
Our goal is to upper bound $\nu(1)$.
We may assume that $L_1 > 0$ since otherwise $\nu(1) = L_0 \| h \|$ and the proof is finished.
Differentiating, we obtain, for any $t \in [0, 1]$,
\[
    \nu'(t)
    =
    L_0 \| h \| + L_1 \| h \| \| \nabla f(x + t h) \|
    \leq
    (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|) \| h \| + L_1 \| h \| \nu(t),
\]
where the final bound is due to the triangle inequality and the previous display.
Hence, for any $t \in [0, 1]$, we have
\[
    \frac{d}{d t} \ln\bigl[ (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| + \epsilon) \| h \| + L_1 \| h \| \nu(t) \bigr] \leq L_1 \| h \|,
\]
where $\epsilon > 0$ is arbitrary\footnote{This additional term is needed to handle the possibility of $L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|$ being zero.}.
Integrating this inequality in $t \in [0, 1]$ and noting that $\nu(0) = 0$, we get
\[
    \ln \frac{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| + \epsilon + L_1 \nu(1)}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| + \epsilon} \leq L_1 \| h \|,
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
    \nu(1)
    \leq
    (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \| + \epsilon) \frac{e^{L_1 \| h \|} - 1}{L_1}.
\]
Passing now to the limit as $\epsilon \to 0$, we obtain \cref{eq-lemma-smooth-2}.

[\cref{eq-lemma-smooth-2} $\implies$ \cref{eq-smooth-ineq-1}]
Let $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be arbitrary points and let $h := y - x$.
Then, using \cref{eq-lemma-smooth-2}, we can estimate
\begin{align*}

    \leq
    \int_0^1 |\langle \nabla f(x + t h) - \nabla f(x), h \rangle| d t

    (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|) \| h \| \int_0^1 \frac{e^{L_1 \| h \| t} - 1}{L_1} d t
    =
    (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|) \frac{e^{L_1 \| h \|} - L_1 \| h \| - 1}{L_1^2},
\end{align*}

\[
    | f(x + t h) - f(x) - t \langle \nabla f(x), h \rangle | \leq (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|) \frac{e^{L_1 t} - L_1 t - 1}{L_1^2}.
\]
Dividing both sides by $t^2$ and passing to the limit as $t \to 0$, we get
\[
    | \langle \nabla^2 f(x) h, h \rangle | \leq L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|.
\]
This proves \cref{def-generalized-smooth} since the unit vector $h$ was allowed to be arbitrary.\qedhere


\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop-big}}%~\ref{prop-sum-of-L0-L-1-and-bounded}}
\begin{proof}

[Claim 1]
Since, $g$ and $\nabla g$ are $M$ and $L$ Lipschitz continuous, $\|\nabla g(x)\| \leq M$ and $\|\nabla^2 g(x)\| \leq L$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Let $F = f + g$, then, for any $x \in \R^d$, we can estimate
\begin{align*}
    \|\nabla^2 F(x) \|
    &\leq \|\nabla^2 f(x)\| + \|\nabla^2 g(x)\|
    \leq L_0 + L + L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|
    \\
    &\leq L_0 + L + L_1 \|\nabla g(x)\| + L_1 \|\nabla F(x) \|
    \\
    &\leq (L_0 + L_1 M + L ) + L_1 \|\nabla F(x) \| .
\end{align*}
[Claim 2]
Notice, that the gradient of $f$ is $\nabla f(x) = (\nabla f_1(x_1)^\top, \ldots, \nabla f_n(x_n)^\top)^\top$ and the Hessian of $f$ is $\nabla^2 f(x)$ is a block-diagonal matrix, with $\nabla^2 f_i(x_i)$ blocks. Thus,
\begin{align*}
    \|\nabla^2 f(x)\| &= \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} \|\nabla^2 f_i(x_i)\|
    \leq \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} \{L_{0,i} + L_{1,i}\|\nabla f_i(x_i)\|\}
    \\
    &\leq \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} \{L_{0,i} + L_{1,i}\|\nabla f(x)\|\}
    \leq \max_{1 \leq i \leq n} L_{0,i} + (\max_{1 \leq i \leq n} L_{1, i}) \|\nabla f(x)\|.
\end{align*}

[Claim 3]
Observe that the gradient of a function is $\nabla f(x) = g'(\langle a, x \rangle + b) a$, and the Hessian is $\nabla^2 f(x) = g''(\langle a, x \rangle + b) a a^{\top}$. Hence,
\begin{align*}
\|\nabla^2 f(x) \| = |g''(\langle a, x \rangle + b)| \| a \|^2
&\leq (L_0 + L_1 |g'(\langle a, x \rangle + b)|) \|a\|^2
\\
&= L_0 \|a\|^2 + \|a\| L_1 \|\nabla f(x)\|.
\end{align*}

[Claim 4]
Under our assumptions, $s = \nabla f(x)$ is a one-to-one transformation from $\R^d$ to $\R^d$ (whose inverse transformation is $x = \nabla f_* (s)$); moreover, the Hessians at such a pair of points are inverse to each other: $\nabla^2 f_*(s) = [\nabla^2 f(x)]^{-1}$ (see, e.g., Corollaries~4.1.4 and~4.2.10 in~\cite{hiriart1993convex}, as well as Example~11.9 from~\cite{rockafellar2009variational}).
Thus, for any pair of points $x, s \in \R^d$ such that $s = \nabla f(x)$, our assumption $\| \nabla^2 f(x) \| \leq L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|$ which, due to the convexity of~$f$, can be equivalently rewritten as $\nabla^2 f(x) \preceq (L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|) I$, is equivalent to
\[
    \nabla^2 f_*(s)
    \equiv
    [\nabla^2 f(x)]^{-1}
    \succeq
    \frac{1}{L_0 + L_1 \| \nabla f(x) \|} I
    \equiv
    \frac{1}{L_0 + L_1 \| s \|} I.
\]
This proves the claim since the transformation $s = \nabla f(x)$ is one-to-one.
\end{proof}

\]
Since $\frac{\psi(g)}{g^2}$ is decreasing in~$g$ and $g_k \leq \psi^{-1}(f_k)$, we can estimate $\frac{\psi(g_k)}{g_k^2} \geq \frac{f_k}{[\psi^{-1}(f_k)]^2}$.
Thus,
\[
    R_k^2 - R_{k + 1}^2 \geq \Bigl( \frac{f_k}{\psi^{-1}(f_k)} \Bigr)^2,
\]
In both cases similar inequality holds
By summing these inequalities over $k \in [0, \ldots, K]$ and rearranging terms, we get
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{k=0}^{K} \Bigl( \frac{f_k}{\psi^{-1}(f_k)}\Bigr)^2 \leq R_0^2 - R_{k+1}^2.
\end{align*}
Note that $\frac{\psi^{-1}(t)}{t}$ is increasing in $t$ (as the composition of increasing in $\gamma$ function $\frac{\psi(\gamma)}{\gamma} \equiv \frac{\gamma}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \gamma}$ with increasing in $t$ function $\gamma = \psi^{-1}(t)$). Thus, by taking a minimum over the terms on the left-hand side of the above display and denoting $x_K^* = \argmin_{x \in \{x_0, \ldots, x_K\}} f(x)$ and $f_K^* = f(x_K^*) - f^* = \min_{0 \leq k \leq K} f_k$, we get
\begin{align*}
     K \Bigl( \frac{f_K^*}{\psi^{-1}(f_K^*)}\Bigr)^2 \leq  R_0^2.
\end{align*}
Rearranging, we obtain
\begin{align*}
     \psi^{-1}(f_K^*) \geq \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0},
\end{align*}
or, equivalently,
\begin{align*}
     f_K^*
     \geq
     \psi\Bigl( \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0} \Bigr)
     \equiv
     \frac{K (f_K^*)^2}{R_0^2\bigl( 2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0} \bigr)}
     =
     \frac{(f_K^*)^2}{\frac{2 L_0 R_0^2}{K} + \frac{3 L_1 R_0}{\sqrt{K}} f_K^*}.
\end{align*}
Hence,
\begin{align*}
     f_K^* \leq  \frac{2 L_0 R_0^2}{K (1 - 3 L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K})},
\end{align*}
whenever $3 L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K} < 1$.
Thus, to achieve desired accuracy $\epsilon > 0$, the number $K$ of iterations should satisfy  the following conditions:
\begin{equation*}
    3L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K} \leq \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{4 L_0 R_0^2}{K} \leq \epsilon.
\end{equation*}
Thus, the final iteration complexity is $K \geq \max \{ \frac{4 L_0 R_0^2}{\epsilon}, 36 L_1^2 R_0^2 \}$.
\qedhere

\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm-normalized}}
\begin{proof}
Let $x^*$ be a minimizer of~$f$.
Then, according to the normalized gradient method update~\eqref{eq-normalized} and the definition of $v_k \defeq \frac{\langle \nabla f(x_k), x_k - x^* \rangle}{\|\nabla f(x_k)\|}$ ($\geq 0$), we have
\begin{align*}
    \|x_{k+1} - x^*\|^2 &= \|x_k - x^*\|^2 - 2 \eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k), x_k - x^* \rangle + \eta_k^2 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \cr
    &=\|x_k - x^*\|^2 - 2 \beta_k v_k + \beta_k^2.
\end{align*}
Summing up these relations over $k = 0, \ldots, K$ and rearranging the terms, we obtain
\begin{align*}
   2 \sum_{k = 0}^{K} \beta_k v_k  \leq  R^2 + \sum_{k=0}^K \beta_k^2,
\end{align*}
where $R \defeq \| x_0 - x^* \|$.
Denoting $v_K^* = \min_{0 \leq k \leq K} v_k$ and substituting $\beta_k = \frac{\hat{R}}{\sqrt{K+1}}$, we get
\begin{align*}
    v_K^*
    \leq
    \frac{ R^2 + \sum_{k=0}^K \beta_k^2}{2\sum_{k = 0}^{K} \beta_k }
    =
    \frac{ R^2 + \hat{R}^2}{2 \hat{R} \sqrt{K + 1}}
    =
    \frac{\bar{R}}{2 \sqrt{K+1}},
\end{align*}
where $\bar{R} \defeq \frac{R^2}{\hat{R}} + \hat{R}$. 
Let $f_K^* \defeq \min_{0 \leq k \leq K} f(x_k)$.
Then, by Lemma~\ref{lemma-nesterov},
\begin{align*}
    f_K^* - f^* \leq \max_{z} \{ f(z) - f^* : \|z -x^*\| \leq  v_K^* \}.
\end{align*}
Applying \cref{lemma:smooth-2} and the fact that $\phi(t) = e^t - t -1 \leq \frac{3 t^2}{6 - 2 t}$ for $t \in [0, 3)$, we obtain
\[
    f_K^* - f^*
    \leq \frac{L_0}{L_1^2} \phi(L_1 \|x_K - x^*\|)
    \leq \frac{L_0}{L_1^2} \phi(L_1 v_K^*)
    \leq \frac{3 L_0 (v_K^*)^2}{6 - 2 L_1 v_K^*}
    \leq \frac{3 L_0 \bar{R}^2}{\left(K+1\right) (6 - \frac{2 L_1 \bar{R}}{\sqrt{K+1}})},
\]
whenever the corresponding denominators are strictly positive.
Thus, to achieve the desired accuracy $\epsilon > 0$, it suffices to set $K$ satisfying the following two inequalities:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{2 L_1 \bar{R}}{\sqrt{K+1}} \leq 3, \qquad \dfrac{L_0 \bar{R}^2}{K+1} \leq \epsilon.
\end{equation*}
This is $K \geq  \max\{ \frac{L_0 \bar{R}^2 }{\epsilon}, \frac{4}{9} L_1^2 \bar{R}^2\}$. 

In case when the stepsizes $\beta_k = \frac{\hat{R}}{\sqrt{k+1}}$ are decreasing, the bound on $v_K^*$ has a form, 
\begin{align*}
    v_K^* \leq  \frac{ \|x_{0} - x^*\|^2 + \sum_{k=0}^K \beta_k^2}{2\sum_{k = 0}^{K} \beta_k } \leq \frac{R^2 + \hat{R}^2 \log(K +1)}{4 \hat{R} (\sqrt{K+1} - \sqrt{2})},
\end{align*}
And by using the same reasoning be arrive at 
\begin{align*}
    f(x_K^*) - f^* &\leq \frac{3 L_0 \bar{R}^2 (\log(K+1))^2}{4  (\sqrt{K+1} - \sqrt{2})^2 } \frac{1}{\left(6 - \frac{L_1 \bar{R} \log(K+1)}{ \hat{R} (\sqrt{K+1} - \sqrt{2})}\right)}.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm-polyak}}


\begin{proof}
Let $x^*$ be an arbitrary solution.
By the method's update rule and convexity of $f(\cdot)$, we get, for all $k \geq 0$,
\begin{align*}
    \|x_{k+1} - x^*\|^2 &= \|x_k - x^*\|^2 - 2 \eta_k \langle \nabla f(x_k), x_k - x^* \rangle + \eta_k^2 \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \cr
    &\leq \|x_k - x^*\|^2 - \frac{(f(x_k) - f^*)^2}{\|\nabla f (x_k)\|^2}.
\end{align*}
Denote $R_k = \|x_k - x^*\|$, $g_k = \|\nabla f(x_k)\|$ and $f_k = f(x_k) - f^*$.
According to Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-5}, for each $k \geq 0$, it holds that
\begin{align*}
     f_k \geq \psi(g_k),
     \qquad
     \text{where} \quad
     \psi(g) := \frac{g^2}{2L_0 + 3L_1 g}, \quad g \geq 0.
\end{align*}
Observe that the function $\psi$ is increasing, so its inverse $\psi^{-1}$ is well-defined and is increasing as well.
In terms of this function, $g_k \leq \psi^{-1}(f_k)$ and hence
\begin{align*}
    R_k^2 - R_{k+1}^2 \geq \frac{f_k^2}{g_k^2} \geq \Bigl( \frac{f_k}{\psi^{-1}(f_k)}\Bigr)^2.
\end{align*}
By summing these inequalities over $k \in [0, \ldots, K]$ and rearranging terms, we get
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{k=0}^{K} \Bigl( \frac{f_k}{\psi^{-1}(f_k)}\Bigr)^2 \leq R_0^2 - R_{k+1}^2.
\end{align*}
Note that $\frac{\psi^{-1}(t)}{t}$ is increasing in $t$ (as the composition of increasing in $\gamma$ function $\frac{\psi(\gamma)}{\gamma} \equiv \frac{\gamma}{2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \gamma}$ with increasing in $t$ function $\gamma = \psi^{-1}(t)$). Thus, by taking a minimum over the terms on the left-hand side of the above display and denoting $x_K^* = \argmin_{x \in \{x_0, \ldots, x_K\}} f(x)$ and $f_K^* = f(x_K^*) - f^* = \min_{0 \leq k \leq K} f_k$, we get
\begin{align*}
     K \Bigl( \frac{f_K^*}{\psi^{-1}(f_K^*)}\Bigr)^2 \leq  R_0^2.
\end{align*}
Rearranging, we obtain
\begin{align*}
     \psi^{-1}(f_K^*) \geq \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0},
\end{align*}
or, equivalently,
\begin{align*}
     f_K^*
     \geq
     \psi\Bigl( \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0} \Bigr)
     \equiv
     \frac{K (f_K^*)^2}{R_0^2\bigl( 2 L_0 + 3 L_1 \frac{\sqrt{K} f_K^*}{R_0} \bigr)}
     =
     \frac{(f_K^*)^2}{\frac{2 L_0 R_0^2}{K} + \frac{3 L_1 R_0}{\sqrt{K}} f_K^*}.
\end{align*}
Hence,
\begin{align*}
     f_K^* \leq  \frac{2 L_0 R_0^2}{K (1 - 3 L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K})},
\end{align*}
whenever $3 L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K} < 1$.
Thus, to achieve desired accuracy $\epsilon > 0$, the number $K$ of iterations should satisfy  the following conditions:
\begin{equation*}
    3L_1 R_0 \sqrt{K} \leq \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \frac{4 L_0 R_0^2}{K} \leq \epsilon.
\end{equation*}
Thus, the final iteration complexity is $K \geq \max\{ \frac{4 L_0 R_0^2}{\epsilon}, 36 L_1^2 R_0^2 \}$.
\end{proof}



\section{Missing Proofs in Section~\ref{section-accelerated}




The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR} is similar to the original proof Theorem 1 in~\cite{nesterov2021primal}, but instead of smoothness of $f$ we use more general property provided in the statement of Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}. 

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}}
\begin{proof}
Let us prove by induction that, for any $k \geq 0$, we have
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lower-bound-for-minimum-of-estimating-function}
    A_k f(x_k) \leq \zeta_k^* \defeq \zeta_k(v_k).
\end{equation}
This trivially holds for $k=0$ since $A_0 = 0$ and $\zeta_0^* = 0$. Now assume that \cref{eq:lower-bound-for-minimum-of-estimating-function} is satisfied for some $k \geq 0$ and let us prove that it is also satisfied for the next index $k' = k + 1$. We start by noting that
\begin{align}
    \label{eq-thm-accelerated-1}
    \zeta_{k + 1}^* &= \zeta_{k+1}(v_{k+1}) =  \zeta_{k}(v_{k + 1}) + a_{k+1} [f(y_k) + \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_{k + 1} - y_k \rangle] \cr
    &\geq   \zeta_{k}^* + \frac{1}{2}\|v_{k + 1} - v_k\|^2 + a_{k+1} [f(y_k) + \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_{k + 1} - y_k \rangle] \cr
    &\geq A_k f(x_k) + \frac{1}{2}\|v_{k + 1} - v_k\|^2 + a_{k+1} [f(y_k) + \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_{k + 1} - y_k \rangle], 
\end{align}
where the first inequality holds due to the strong convexity of $\zeta_k$, and the second one is due to the induction hypothesis.
Further, note that, by construction, $y_k \in [v_k, x_k]$.
Considering separately any of the three possible situations, $y_k = v_k$, $y_k = x_k$ and $y_k \in (v_k, x_k)$, we see that, in all cases,
\[
    \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_k - y_k \rangle \geq 0.
\]
Then, by adding and subtracting $\langle \nabla f(y_k), v_k \rangle$ in \cref{eq-thm-accelerated-1} and using the estimate in the preceding inequality, as well as $f(y_k) \leq f(x_k)$ (which holds by construction), we obtain
\begin{align*}
    \zeta_{k+1}^* &\geq  A_k f(x_k) + a_{k+1} f(y_k) + \frac{1}{2}\|v_{k + 1} - v_k\|^2 + a_{k+1} \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_{k + 1} - v_k \rangle \cr
&\geq  A_{k + 1} f(y_k) + \frac{1}{2}\|v_{k + 1} - v_k\|^2 + a_{k+1} \langle \nabla f(y_k), v_{k + 1} - v_k \rangle
\\
&\geq
A_{k+1} f(y_k)  -\frac{a_{k+1}^2}{2} \|\nabla f(y_k)\|^2
=
A_{k + 1} \Bigl[ f(y_k) - \frac{1}{2 L} \| \nabla f(y_k) \|^2 \Bigr],
\end{align*}
where the final identity is due to fact that, by the definition of $a_{k + 1}$,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:equation-for-scaling-coefficient}
    L a_{k + 1}^2 = A_{k + 1}.
\end{equation}
By the induction step and by construction, $y_k$ stays in the sublevel set $\mathcal{F}_0$, since $f(y_k) \leq f(x_k) \leq f(x_0)$. Then, by the statement of the theorem, 
we have $f(x_{k+1}) \leq f(y_k) -\frac{1}{2L}\| \nabla f(y_k)\|^2$.
This proves that $\zeta_{k+1}^* \geq A_{k + 1} f(x_{k + 1})$ completing the induction.

Let $k \geq 1$ be arbitrary.
By the convexity of~$f$ and the definition of $A_k$, we have
\[
\zeta_k^*
\leq \zeta_k(x^*)
= \frac{1}{2} R^2 + \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} a_{i+1} [f(y_{i}) + \langle \nabla f(y_i), x^* - y_i\rangle]
\leq
\frac{1}{2} R^2 + A_{k} f^*.
\]
where $R = \|x_0 - x^*\|$.
Combining this with \cref{eq:lower-bound-for-minimum-of-estimating-function}, we conclude that
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bound-of-function-residual}
    f(x_k) - f^* \leq \frac{L R^2}{2 A_k}.
\end{equation}

It remains to estimate the rate of growth of the coefficients~$A_k$.
From \cref{eq:equation-for-scaling-coefficient} and the definition of~$A_{k + 1}$, it follows, for any $k \geq 0$, that
\begin{align*}
    \sqrt{\frac{A_{k + 1}}{L}}
    =
    a_{k + 1}
    =
    A_{k + 1} - A_k
    &=
    (\sqrt{A_{k + 1}} + \sqrt{A_k}) (\sqrt{A_{k + 1}} - \sqrt{A_k})
    \\
    &\leq
    2 \sqrt{A_{k + 1}} (\sqrt{A_{k + 1}} - \sqrt{A_k}).
\end{align*}
Dividing both sides of this inequality by $\sqrt{A_{k+1}}$ and summing up the result, we see that,
for any $k \geq 1$, it holds that
\begin{align*}
    A_{k}  \ge \frac{k^2}{4 L }.%  \frac{(k+2)^2}{4L}.
\end{align*}
Substituting this estimate into \cref{eq:bound-of-function-residual}, we obtain the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated}}
\begin{proof}
Based on Theorem~\ref{thm-grad-convex} the number of iterations required to find a point $\bar{x}$, such that  $f(\bar{x})- f^* \leq \frac{L_0}{5 L_1^2}$ is $36 L_1^2 R^2$. Using the Lemma~\ref{lemma:smooth-2} and reasoning from Section~\ref{section-accelerated}, we have $\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\| \leq \frac{L_0}{L_1}$ and consequently $L_0 + L_1 \|\nabla f(\bar{x})\| \leq 2L_0$.
By equation~\eqref{eq:simplified-expression-for-function-progress}, 
\[f(T_L(x)) \leq f(x) - \frac{1}{5 L_0} \|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \leq  f(x) - \frac{1}{2L} \|\nabla f(x)\|^2.\]
Hence, a point $x_0 = \bar{x}$ and parameter $L = 3 L_0$ satisfy the condition of Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}. Then, by Theorem~\ref{thm-accelerated-AGMsDR}, AGMsDR with this choice of $x_0$ and $L$, requires $\sqrt{\frac{12 L_0}{\epsilon}}R$ number of iterations to achieve $f(x_k) - f^* \leq \epsilon$. Combining complexities of both methods we obtained the desired $36 L_1^2 R^2 + \sqrt{\frac{12 L_0}{\epsilon}}R$ complexity. 

\end{proof}
