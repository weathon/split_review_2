\title{FairCoT: Enhancing Fairness in Diffusion Models via Chain of Thought Reasoning of Multimodal Language Models}

\begin{abstract}
    In the domain of text-to-image generative models, biases inherent in training datasets often propagate into generated content, posing significant ethical challenges, particularly in socially sensitive contexts. We introduce FairCoT, a novel framework that enhances fairness in diffusion models through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning within multimodal generative large language models (LLMs). FairCoT employs iterative CoT refinement and attire-based attribute prediction to systematically mitigate biases, ensuring diverse and equitable representation in generated images. By integrating iterative reasoning processes, FairCoT addresses the limitations of zero-shot CoT in sensitive scenarios, balancing creativity with ethical responsibility. Experimental evaluations across multiple models, including DALL-E and various Stable Diffusion variants, demonstrate that FairCoT significantly improves fairness and diversity metrics without compromising image quality or relevance. Our approach advances ethical AI practices in generative modeling, promoting socially responsible content generation and setting new standards for fairness in AI-generated imagery.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}



The advent of text-to-image generative models has revolutionized artificial intelligence, enabling the synthesis of high-fidelity images from textual descriptions. These advances have unlocked new possibilities in creative expression, design, and accessibility. However, these models often inherit and amplify biases present in their training datasets, leading to ethical concerns—especially when generating content related to sensitive social issues. Addressing these biases is critical to ensure that AI systems are fair, inclusive, and socially responsible.

Existing approaches to mitigate bias in text-to-image models primarily focus on prompt engineering or adjusting model parameters. Handcrafted prompting methods \citep{biasp1,biasp2} rely heavily on human annotations, which are inherently subjective and can introduce inconsistencies \citep{sun2023enhancing}. Moreover, these methods can be costly and sub-optimal due to the extensive manual effort required. On the other hand, debiasing techniques involving parameter fine-tuning \citep{biasp3, finetuning} or modifying text embeddings \citep{5} are computationally intensive and often limited to open-source diffusion models. These methods can inadvertently affect model alignment and are typically restricted to specific types of biases addressed during fine-tuning \citep{sun2023enhancing}.

To address these challenges, we introduce FairCoT, a novel method that leverages Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning within multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to refine the generative process of text-to-image models. FairCoT enables models to identify and mitigate biases in their outputs by generating and refining fairness-aware reasoning paths. FairCoT operates by integrating iterative reasoning refinement and controlled evaluation to guide generation toward more diverse and equitable outputs. Leveraging MLLMs' reasoning capabilities, the model assesses potential biases and adjusts generation accordingly. During training, it generates reasoning steps that consider fairness constraints related to sensitive attributes like gender, race, age, and religion. Feedback from this prediction refines the reasoning steps in subsequent iterations, allowing dynamic adjustment of outputs based on fairness considerations.

This approach addresses limitations of zero-shot CoT reasoning in sensitive contexts by providing explicit, iterative fairness guidance. Unlike prompt engineering or fine-tuning methods, FairCoT operates at the reasoning level, making it applicable to both open and closed-source models without parameter updates. By leveraging MLLMs' reasoning capabilities, FairCoT directly influences the generative process to align with ethical objectives, ensuring outputs are high-quality and socially responsible. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{FairCoT Framework:} We introduce FairCoT, a simple and effective method that applies Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for bias reduction in text-to-image (T2I) models. It avoids retraining, works for both open and closed-source models, and preserves alignment.
    
    \item \textbf{Multi-Bias Generalization:} FairCoT addresses multiple bias types simultaneously, including sensitive attributes and objects, and generalizes well across domains.

    \item \textbf{Improved Attribute Prediction:} We present an attire-based method using CLIP for improving the detection of sensitive attributes like religion, making us the first to tackle religious bias in T2I models.
    
    \item \textbf{Iterative Bootstrapping with MLLMs:} We apply an iterative bootstrapping process using MLLMs and CoT for debiasing, the first use of this approach for T2I models and fairness.

\end{enumerate}

FairCoT represents a significant advance in the pursuit of equitable and responsible AI. It is applicable to both open and closed-source text-to-image models and can address multiple bias types simultaneously. By integrating this method with LLM-powered diffusion models like DALL-E, we demonstrate its robustness and versatility. Furthermore, FairCoT can be applied upon request, helping to avoid overrepresentation or underrepresentation of minorities in specific contexts.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we review related work on bias mitigation in generative models. Section 3 details the technical architecture of FairCoT, including the iterative reasoning refinement process and the integration of attire-based attribute prediction. Section 4 presents the experimental results with comprehensive analysis, and Section 5 discusses the broader implications of our work and potential future directions.

\section{Related Work}


This section discusses relevant work on bias in vision-language models, the impact of dataset biases, language-driven bias mitigation, advances in large language models, and attribute prediction.

\paragraph{Bias in Vision-Language Models}

Bias in vision-language models is a significant concern, as these models often inherit and amplify societal biases present in training data. \cite{6} investigated gender bias in zero-shot vision-language models, revealing performance disparities and calibration issues based on perceived gender in images, highlighting how language influences both expanding and biasing vision tasks. Similarly, \cite{7} proposed a bias-to-text (B2T) method using pre-trained vision-language models to detect visual biases by generating captions for misclassified images and identifying bias-indicative keywords, effectively pinpointing biases without human annotation.

\cite{fairrag} introduced FairRAG, focusing on mitigating biases in human generation tasks by incorporating fair retrieval methods. Their approach adjusts retrieval processes to include diverse and representative examples, reducing biases in generated content. On the other hand, \cite{finetuning} proposed fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models for fairness using a single textual inversion token, demonstrating that targeted fine-tuning can mitigate demographic biases, although it may be computationally intensive and limited to specific models.

\paragraph{Impact of Dataset Bias}

Dataset biases significantly impact vision-language models. \cite{8} highlighted widespread societal biases in datasets across tasks like image captioning, text-image classification, and text-to-image generation, emphasizing the need for careful data curation. Additionally, \cite{11} showed that larger datasets can amplify biases. \cite{9} developed the MMBias benchmark for assessing bias, contributing to debiasing methods using additive residuals. Moreover, the PATA dataset introduced by \cite{10} provided insights into the effect of data on bias, underscoring the necessity of addressing imbalances in training data.

\paragraph{Language-Driven Approaches in Bias Mitigation}

Language-driven strategies have been proposed for bias mitigation. \cite{5} used text embedding projection for debiasing vision-language discriminative and generative models. On the other hand, \cite{12} proposed dataset debiasing by enhancing datasets with synthetic, gender-balanced sets. In text-to-image models, \cite{biasp1} suggested adding gender terms to prompts to balance gender representation in images. Similarly, \cite{biasp2} advocated adding ethical statements to prompts to directly encourage fairness.

\paragraph{Advances in Large Language Models}

Recent advances in LLMs have enhanced model reasoning and debiasing capabilities. \cite{sun2023enhancing} introduced iterative bootstrapping in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to improve problem-solving capabilities in models like ChatGPT. \cite{star} proposed Self-Taught Reasoner (STaR), enabling models to self-supervise and refine reasoning steps without additional labeled data. Furthermore, \cite{faith} focused on improving the faithfulness of reasoning steps in LLMs, addressing hallucination, and enhancing reliability in sensitive contexts. However, \cite{biascot} highlighted risks of zero-shot CoT reasoning in socially sensitive domains, showing that models may generate biased or harmful content without proper guidance, underscoring the need for mechanisms to minimize biased outputs.

\paragraph{Attribute Prediction}

Attribute prediction is crucial for assessing demographic representations in generated content. CLIP \citep{clip} has been widely used for zero-shot image classification and attribute prediction due to its ability to learn visual concepts from natural language supervision. \cite{clip} found high accuracy (96\%) for gender classification across all races using CLIP. They averaged around 93\% for racial classification and approximately 63\% for age classification. In addition, \cite{finetuning} adopted the eight race categories from the FairFace dataset but found classifiers struggled to distinguish between certain categories. Therefore, to improve race attribute prediction, they consolidated them into four broader classes: WMELH (White, Middle Eastern, Latino Hispanic), Asian (East Asian, Southeast Asian), Black, and Indian. \cite{han} presented a deep multi-task learning approach for heterogeneous face attribute estimation, emphasizing multi-task learning to improve attribute prediction accuracy and robustness across diverse populations.

Our work builds upon these studies by integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning within multimodal generative LLMs to enhance fairness in diffusion models. By leveraging iterative reasoning and attire-based attribute prediction, we address limitations identified in previous works, contributing to ethical AI practices in text-to-image generation.

\section{Methods}


In this section, we introduce FairCoT, a framework designed to enhance fairness in AI-generated imagery by integrating Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) and Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP). We also propose an attire-based method for predicting religious attributes, aiming to improve CLIP attribute prediction for hard tasks leading to accurate automatic image labeling. The framework operates in two primary phases: training and inference, each comprising several key components.

\subsection{Training Phase}

\subsubsection{Initial Image Generation}
\textbf{Profession Selection:} We begin by defining a set of professions \( \mathcal{P} = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n\} \) that are commonly depicted in working industry. These professions are chosen to cover a wide range of societal roles to ensure the generalizability of our approach, each belonging to one of these areas: Healthcare and Medical,
Legal and Business, Service and Hospitality, Security and Protection, Education and Information, Engineering and Technical, Research and Analytics.
\textbf{Demographic Attributes:} We identify demographic attributes \( \mathcal{D} = \{\text{gender}, \text{age}, \text{ethnicity}, \text{religion}\} \) that are essential for representing diversity. For the attribute of religion, we further specify a set of religious groups \( \mathcal{R} = \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_k\} \).

\textbf{Prompt Generation:} We utilize a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to generate prompts that specify a profession. Formally, for a given set of professions \( \mathcal{P} = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n\} \), the LLM produces a set of prompts \( \mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n\} \), where each prompt \( s_i \) is initially ``n photos of \( p_i \)''.

\textbf{Image Production:} The generated prompts \( \mathcal{S} \) are used to guide the text-to-image model \( \mathcal{D} \) to produce an initial set of images \( \mathcal{I} = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_m\} \). This stage is critical as it provides the raw data for subsequent bias evaluation and refinement processes. (Figure\ref{fig:img2})
\subsubsection{ Enhanced Attribute Prediction}
We predict the attributes for the generated images using CLIP zero-shot classification. For attributes CLIP does not accurately predict due to their complex nature, we propose an attire-based method to improve the prediction.

\textbf{Attribute List Generation:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input:} We input the set of religious groups \( \mathcal{R} = \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_k\} \) into the LLM, which generates a detailed list of attires associated with corresponding religions. This step recognizes the diverse visual expressions of religious identities.
    
    \item \textbf{Output:} The LLM outputs an attire list \( \mathcal{A} = \{a_1, a_2, \dots, a_l\} \), where each attire \( a_j \) corresponds to a specific religious group (e.g., Hijabs for Islam, Turbans for Sikhism, Kippahs for Judaism).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Attire Prediction Using CLIP:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Process:} We employ CLIP to analyze each generated image \( I_i \in \mathcal{I} \) to detect the most prominent attire \( a_j \in \mathcal{A} \). This is achieved by computing similarity scores between the image embeddings and textual embeddings of the attires:
    \[
    \text{Score}(I_i, a_j) = \cos\left(\phi_I(I_i), \phi_T(a_j)\right) %,
    \]
    where \( \phi_I \) and \( \phi_T \) are the image and text embedding functions of CLIP, respectively. The attire with the highest score is selected and mapped to the corresponding religion which will be the predicted attribute for that image.
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Bias and Alignment Evaluation and Iterative Bootstrapping}

\textbf{Bias Assessment:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Normalized Entropy:} To assess the uniformity of the attribute distribution across the images, we compute the normalized entropy \( H' \) over the set of demographic attributes:
    \[
    H' = -\left( \frac{1}{\log k} \sum_{j=1}^{k} p(a_j) \log p(a_j) \right) %,
    \]
    where \( p(a_j) \) is the empirical probability of attribute \( a_j \) appearing in the image set \( \mathcal{I} \).
    \end{itemize}
\textbf{Alignment Assessment:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CLIP-T Score:} We measure the alignment between the generated images and the expectations set by the initial prompts of the professions using the CLIP-Textual (CLIP-T) score:
    \[
    \text{CLIP-T Score} = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^{m} \cos\left(\phi_I(I_i), \phi_T(s_i)\right) %,
    \]
    where \( s_i \) is the prompt used to generate image \( I_i \).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{ CoT Debiasing}
\textbf{Auto Chain-of-Thought:}

    We start by zero-shot debiasing  \( \text{CoT}_0 \) \citep{think}. We prompt the MLLM with \( \text{r}_0 \) ``think step by step before generating images while considering several races, genders, religions, and ages and treating people of these categories equally'' that will generate  \( \text{CoT}_0 \)(Figure\ref{fig:img2}).

\textbf{Iterative Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Refinement:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Process:} Having the initial Chain-of-Thought \( \text{CoT}_0 \) as a baseline, the model iteratively enhances fairness. In each iteration \( t \), the MLLM refines the CoT based on the bias assessments until the images reach convergence criteria. The iterative process is as follows:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Bias Evaluation:} Compute \( H'_t \) and CLIP-T Score at iteration \( t \).
        \item \textbf{CoT Refinement:} If \( (H'_t > H'_{t-1} \)) and \((\text{CLIP-T}_t > \tau \text{CLIP-T}_{t_0}) \), update \( \text{CoT}_t \) to address the identified biases; where \( \tau<1\)
        is the acceptable drop in alignment for compromising fairness.
        \item \textbf{CoT Generation:} Generate  \({CoT}_t \) using prompt \( {r}_t \): "Can you think again? Consider generating images of different religions, races, ages, and genders".
        
        \item \textbf{Image Production:} Produce new images \( \mathcal{I}_t \) using the diffusion model guided by \( \text{CoT}_t \).
        \item \textbf{Iteration:} Increment \( t \) and repeat.
    \end{enumerate}
    
    \item \textbf{Convergence:} The iterative process continues until the fairness criteria are satisfied:
    \[
    H'_t \leq H'_{t_-1} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{CLIP-T Score} \leq \tau\text{CLIP-T}_{t_0}.
    \]
    \item \textbf{Documentation:} Upon convergence, we document the detailed \( \text{CoT}_{t} \) that describes the iterative reasoning and adjustments made to achieve the fairness standards.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inference Phase}

\subsubsection{ Demonstration Pool and Application}

\textbf{Demonstration Pool:} The images \( \mathcal{I} \) that meet the fairness standards, along with their corresponding Chains-of-Thought \( \text{CoT} \), prompt \( \text{s} \) and profession \( \text{p} \), are archived in a demonstration pool \( \mathcal{D}_{\text{pool}} \). This repository serves as a template for future tasks, ensuring that the learned fairness principles are replicated in new contexts.
\subsubsection{Inference}
\textbf{Selection and Adaptation:} For a new task involving a different profession \( p_{\text{new}} \), we select an appropriate \( \text{CoT} \) from \( \mathcal{D}_{\text{pool}} \) based on the seven areas considered for professions. The MLLM adapts this \( \text{CoT} \) to generate a new
 \( CoT_{\text{new}} \) tailored to  \(p_{\text{new}}\) (Figure\ref{fig:img1}).

\textbf{Prompt Generation:} We utilize a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to generate prompts for the new profession while inspired by the new CoT 
\( CoT_{\text{new}} \).
The LLM produces a set of prompts \( \mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n\} \), where each prompt \( s_i \) is crafted to encourage diversity in the subsequent image generation process.
\textbf{Image Generation:} The adapted prompts \( \mathcal{S}_{\text{new}} \) guide the diffusion model to produce images \( \mathcal{I}_{\text{new}} \) that adhere to the established fairness principles. This ensures that the new images maintain the diversity and fairness standards set by FairCoT.

\section{Experimental Results}



We conducted a comprehensive set of experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of FairCoT in enhancing fairness and diversity in text-to-image diffusion models. Drawing inspiration from the methodologies of \cite{sun2023enhancing}, \cite{star}, \cite{biascot}, \citet{finetuning} our experiments were designed to assess FairCoT's capability to mitigate biases while maintaining high-quality image generation.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Models Evaluated}

Our evaluation focused on both closed-source and open-source text-to-image generative models:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DALL-E}: A closed source, large-scale model known for high-quality image generation.
    \item \textbf{Stable Diffusion Variants}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{SDv1-5}: The initial version serves as a baseline \citep{sdv2}.
        \item \textbf{SDv2-1}: An updated version with enhanced capabilities \citep{sdv2}.
        \item \textbf{SDXL-turbo}: Enhanced version for fast real time performance \citep{sdxl}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

We compared the following configurations for each model, wherever possible:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{General Prompting}: Standard prompts without any bias mitigation strategies applied.
    \item \textbf{Ethical Intervention}: Prompts augmented with ethical statements to encourage fairness, following the approach of \citet{biasp2}.
    \item \textbf{DebiasVL}: Models debiased using text embedding projection \citep{5}.
    \item \textbf{FairD}: Models employing the Fair Diffusion method for debiasing \citep{faird}.
    \item \textbf{Finetune}: Models finetuned for fairness \citep{finetuning}.
    \item \textbf{FairCoT}: Our proposed method for both full body and headshots.
\end{itemize}

We focused on four attributes—gender, race, age, and religion in our experiments. Gender included female and male categories, race was consolidated into WMELH, Asian , Black, and Indian, and age groups were young and old \citep{finetuning}. For religion, we focused on the top three religions globally Islam, Christianity, Hinduism, and a neutral category for individuals without religious attributes \citep{cia_religions_2023}. 

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}
To quantitatively assess fairness and diversity, we employed the Bias-Normalized Entropy metric ($H'$), which measures the uniformity of attribute distribution (e.g., gender, race, age, religion) across generated images. Higher values indicate greater diversity and reduced bias. We also used the CLIP-T score to evaluate the semantic alignment between generated images and input prompts, ensuring that diversity enhancements do not compromise image relevance.

\subsection{Discussion}

Our experimental results demonstrate that FairCoT significantly enhances fairness and diversity in text-to-image diffusion models across various scenarios and models. By achieving higher Normalized Entropy scores, FairCoT effectively mitigates biases in generated images while maintaining high CLIP-T scores, ensuring that image quality and relevance are not compromised. Compared with finetuning approaches, such as those proposed by \citet{finetuning}. While finetuning reduces biases, it requires retraining the model and may not generalize across different models or prompts. FairCoT operates at the prompt level, achieving better bias reduction while providing a flexible and model-agnostic solution that can be applied to any text-to-image diffusion model without the need for additional training.

The ablation study underscores the importance of each component within FairCoT, highlighting that iterative Chain-of-Thought reasoning and our CoT selection method are critical for achieving optimal performance.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

While FairCoT shows substantial improvements, certain limitations exist. The reliance on attribute classifiers assumes accurate prediction, which may vary across different demographic groups or attributes. Future work could explore integrating more robust, possibly domain-specific attribute prediction models.

Additionally, extending FairCoT to address other forms of bias, such as those related to disability, socio-economic status, or intersectional attributes, would enhance its applicability. Moreover, FairCoT relies on MLLMs that can generate images in the training phase, so further research on open-sourcing these models will assist its adoption in enhancing fairness in AI-generated content.

Hand-crafted prompting in text-to-image generative models involves the manual creation of text prompts that guide the image generation process. AutoCoT (Automated Chain of Thought) \cite{autocot}\cite{biascot}:
AutoCoT, an extension of the Chain of Thought (CoT) approach, automates the reasoning process in generative AI models. Instead of relying on manually crafted prompts, AutoCoT enables models to autonomously generate intermediate reasoning steps or 'thoughts' that lead to the final output. In the context of text-to-image generation, this means the model internally contemplates various aspects like diversity and inclusivity before producing an image. AutoCoT aims to enhance the model's problem-solving capabilities and interpretability by mimicking a more human-like, step-by-step reasoning process. While this approach offers a more systematic method compared to hand-crafted prompts, it may still inherit the underlying biases of the model, and its interpretation of complex social contexts can be limited.

\section{Experiments}


To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of FairCoT, we conducted a series of experiments encompassing general tests, multi face, and multiconcept scenarios, and an ablation study. These experiments were designed to assess FairCoT's ability to enhance fairness and diversity in AI-generated images across various models and complex generation tasks.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Test Set Configuration}

Our evaluation utilized a diverse set of 20 professions, systematically categorized into seven distinct groups: Healthcare, Legal, Service, Engineering, Education, Arts, and Technology. This categorization ensures that our assessment covers a broad spectrum of societal roles, mitigating biases that may arise from the overrepresentation or underrepresentation of certain professions. Examples of professions include doctor, lawyer, chef, engineer, teacher, artist, and software developer. By selecting a wide range of professions, we aimed to test FairCoT's robustness in handling diverse and potentially sensitive roles.

\subsubsection{Models Evaluated}

We evaluated FairCoT's performance using a combination of proprietary and open-source text-to-image generative models to ensure broad applicability:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DALL-E}: A proprietary, large-scale model renowned for its high-quality image generation capabilities.
    \item \textbf{Stable Diffusion Variants}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{SDv1-5}: The initial version serves as a baseline for comparison.
        \item \textbf{SDv2-1}: An updated version with enhanced capabilities and performance improvements.
        \item \textbf{SDXL-turbo}: The latest iteration, optimized for efficiency and superior image quality.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

By including multiple versions of Stable Diffusion, we aimed to demonstrate FairCoT's effectiveness across different iterations of diffusion models, highlighting its versatility and scalability.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}

To quantitatively assess the diversity and fairness of the generated images, we employed the following metrics:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bias-Normalized Entropy ($H'$)}: This metric measures the uniformity of attribute distribution (e.g., gender, race, religion) across the generated images. Higher values indicate greater diversity and fairness in attribute representation.
    \item \textbf{CLIP-T Score}: Leveraging CLIP's image-text similarity capabilities, this score evaluates the alignment between the generated images and the input prompts. Higher scores signify better relevance and coherence of the generated images concerning the prompts.
\end{itemize}

These metrics provide a balanced evaluation of both fairness (via Bias-Normalized Entropy) and quality (via CLIP-T Score), ensuring that enhanced diversity does not come at the cost of image relevance or quality.

\subsubsection{Objective}

The general tests aimed to establish the baseline performance of FairCoT by generating single-face images for each of the 20 professions across all evaluated models. This straightforward generation task serves as a foundational comparison point to assess FairCoT's ability to enhance fairness and diversity in a controlled setting.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

\subsubsection{Objective}

To elucidate the individual contributions of specific components within FairCoT, we conducted an ablation study. This analysis isolates each component to determine its impact on the overall performance, thereby validating the necessity and effectiveness of each feature in enhancing fairness and diversity.

\subsubsection{Methodology}

We compared the following configurations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FairCoT (Full)}: The complete framework incorporating both iterative Chain-of-Thought (CoT) refinement and attire-based attribute prediction.
    \item \textbf{FairCoT without CoT Refinement}: A variant excluding the iterative reasoning refinement component to assess its contribution to performance.
    \item \textbf{Ethically Tuned Baseline}: Standard models employing ethical prompting without the specific interventions introduced by FairCoT.
\end{itemize}

Each configuration was evaluated using the same set of metrics (Bias-Normalized Entropy and CLIP-T Score) across all tested models and scenarios to ensure consistency and fairness in comparison.

\subsubsection{Results and Analysis}

Table \ref{tab:ablation_results} presents the results of the ablation study. The full FairCoT framework significantly outperformed both the CoT refinement ablation and the ethically tuned baseline across all attributes, underscoring the critical role of each component in enhancing fairness and diversity.

\paragraph{Detailed Insights}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FairCoT (Full)}: This configuration serves as the benchmark, integrating all proposed enhancements. It achieved the highest Bias-Normalized Entropy scores across gender, race, and religion, demonstrating its superior ability to generate diverse and fair images. The CLIP-T Score remained consistent, indicating that these improvements did not detract from image relevance.
    
    \item \textbf{FairCoT without CoT Refinement}: Removing the iterative CoT refinement led to noticeable declines in Bias-Normalized Entropy scores across all attributes. This drop highlights the importance of iterative reasoning in maintaining and enhancing diversity. The reduction in scores suggests that without CoT refinement, the model struggles to effectively distribute attributes uniformly.
    
    \item \textbf{Ethically Tuned Baseline}: Models employing standard ethical prompting without FairCoT's specific interventions showed the lowest Bias-Normalized Entropy scores. This configuration underscores the limitations of conventional ethical tuning methods in achieving comprehensive fairness and diversity, particularly in sensitive attributes like race and religion.
\end{itemize}

These findings collectively affirm that both iterative CoT refinement and attire-based attribute prediction are indispensable components of FairCoT, each contributing significantly to its overall performance in promoting fairness and diversity.

\subsection{Discussion}

The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate **FairCoT**'s efficacy in enhancing fairness and diversity within AI-generated imagery. Across various models and complex generation tasks, FairCoT consistently outperforms baseline and ethically tuned models in Bias-Normalized Entropy, particularly in sensitive attributes such as gender, race, and religion. This improvement is achieved without compromising the relevance and quality of the generated images, as evidenced by competitive CLIP-T Scores.

\paragraph{Implications for AI Fairness}

FairCoT's ability to maintain high diversity levels while ensuring image relevance addresses a critical challenge in AI fairness: enhancing attribute diversity without degrading performance. This balance is essential for deploying AI systems in real-world applications where both fairness and quality are non-negotiable.

\paragraph{Versatility Across Models}

The robustness of FairCoT across different versions of Stable Diffusion and proprietary models like DALL-E highlights its versatility. Whether dealing with baseline models or their advanced iterations, FairCoT consistently ensures equitable attribute representation, making it a universally applicable tool for mitigating biases in text-to-image generation.

\paragraph{Component Significance}

The ablation study underscores the pivotal role of iterative CoT refinement and attire-based attribute prediction in FairCoT's architecture. These components synergistically work to identify and correct biases at multiple stages of the generation process, ensuring sustained fairness and diversity.

\paragraph{Comparison with Existing Work}

Inspired by methodologies in \textit{Finetuning Text-to-Image Diffusion Models for Fairness}, FairCoT extends existing approaches by incorporating iterative reasoning and attire-based predictions. While previous works have focused on finetuning models to reduce bias, FairCoT introduces a more dynamic and context-aware framework that adapts to complex generation scenarios, thereby achieving superior fairness metrics.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work}

While FairCoT demonstrates significant improvements, certain limitations remain. For instance, in the SDv2-1 and SDXL-turbo models, some attributes like age and religion showed moderate improvements, indicating potential areas for further enhancement. Future work could explore integrating additional attribute predictors or refining the iterative CoT process to address these nuances more effectively.

Additionally, expanding the evaluation to include more diverse and intersectional attributes could provide a more comprehensive understanding of FairCoT's capabilities. Exploring FairCoT's applicability in other generative tasks beyond image generation, such as video or audio synthesis, could also be a promising avenue for future research.

\paragraph{Broader Impact}

By promoting fairness and diversity in AI-generated content, FairCoT contributes to the ethical deployment of AI technologies. Ensuring equitable representation in generated images can help mitigate societal biases and foster inclusivity, aligning AI advances with broader societal values.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The experiments conducted validate **FairCoT** as an effective framework for enhancing fairness and diversity in text-to-image diffusion models. Through comprehensive evaluations across general, multi face, and multiconcept scenarios, coupled with an insightful ablation study, we demonstrate that FairCoT not only improves attribute diversity but also maintains high image quality and relevance. These advances position FairCoT as a pivotal tool in the ongoing effort to develop equitable and inclusive AI technologies.

Our experimental results underscore FairCoT's superiority over traditional debiasing strategies, especially when model parameters are inaccessible, highlighting its practicality and effectiveness. By balancing bias mitigation with image quality retention, FairCoT significantly promotes inclusivity and fairness in AI-generated imagery, representing a substantial advance toward ethical and responsible AI.
