## Summary

This paper proposes a zero-shot image classification method that fuses three types of features derived from a test image: (1) the standard CLIP visual embedding, (2) a free-form image description generated by a multimodal LLM (Gemini Pro), and (3) an initial class prediction also generated by the same LLM. All three feature vectors are encoded into CLIP's joint embedding space, summed element-wise, L2-normalized, and classified by a standard zero-shot linear classifier using precomputed class label features. The method uses a single fixed prompt set across all ten evaluated datasets, eliminating per-dataset prompt engineering.

Empirically, the method achieves a +4.1 percentage point average top-1 accuracy gain over CLIP (ViT-L/14) across ten benchmarks, with a +6.8 pp gain on ImageNet, outperforming prior zero-shot methods including CuPL, CALIP, and other CLIP variants. Ablation studies confirm that all three feature types contribute to performance, with the full fusion giving the best results on most datasets.

**Core strengths:** The paper presents a simple, intuitive idea with consistent empirical gains across diverse benchmarks. The method is easy to understand and implement. Including failure cases and initial prediction mismatches shows good scientific transparency.

**Core weaknesses:** The comparison is not fully controlled — the proposed method uses Gemini Pro at inference time while baselines use it only offline, conflating methodological innovation with additional LLM capacity. No statistical significance or variance is reported for any result. The equal-weighted fusion lacks theoretical justification and has no mechanism to handle cases where the LLM's confident but wrong prediction dominates. The substantial inference cost (~1.4s/image for two LLM queries) is understated. Novelty claims cannot be fully evaluated in this run due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode (external search unavailable).

## Strengths
1. **Simple and intuitive method.** The core idea — using a multimodal LLM to generate instance-level textual descriptions and fusing them with visual features — is straightforward to understand and implement. The paper provides clear pseudocode (Algorithm 1) and a working Python example (Code 1), which significantly aids reproducibility.

2. **Consistent empirical gains.** Across ten diverse datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, Food-101, SUN397, Cars, DTD, Caltech-101, Pets, Places365), the method consistently outperforms the CLIP ViT-L/14 baseline. The ablation studies (Table 2) convincingly show that all three features (image, description, prediction) contribute to the final performance.

3. **Universal prompt set.** The paper uses fixed prompts ("What do you see?" for description; a classification prompt with the class list for prediction) across all datasets, without per-dataset engineering. This is a meaningful practical simplification compared to prior work that required dataset-specific templates.

4. **Transparency about failure cases.** The inclusion of Figure 6 (failure examples) and the discussion of cases where the LLM's initial prediction is wrong but semantically plausible shows honest scientific reporting. The paper acknowledges that the LLM sometimes produces outputs that do not match the target class list, explaining why direct LLM-as-classifier is insufficient.

5. **Controlled CuPL re-implementation.** The authors re-implement CuPL using Gemini Pro (the same LLM used by the proposed method) rather than the original GPT-3, which partially controls for LLM capability differences in the class description step. This is a good-faith effort toward fair comparison, even though the inference-time asymmetry remains.

## Weaknesses
1. **Uncontrolled comparison (Major).** The paper claims state-of-the-art results but the comparison conflates two variables: the proposed fusion method and the use of a stronger LLM (Gemini Pro) at inference time. CuPL is re-implemented with Gemini Pro for class descriptions, but the proposed method additionally uses Gemini Pro for two per-instance queries (description + prediction). This asymmetry means the observed gains cannot be cleanly attributed to the methodological contribution.

2. **No statistical significance or variance reporting (Major).** All results across all tables are reported as single-point estimates. No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests are provided. Many between-variant differences are under 2 percentage points (e.g., CIFAR-100: 65.3 vs 65.4 vs 65.8), making it impossible to determine if these are meaningful or noise.

3. **Feature fusion lacks justification (Major).** The equal-weighted additive fusion of visual, description, and prediction features (Eq. 8-9) is presented without theoretical or empirical justification for why equal weighting is appropriate. The ablation (Table 3) only tests three alternatives on a 5,000-image subset. When the LLM produces a confidently wrong prediction (Figure 6), the fusion has no mechanism to downweight the misleading signal.

4. **Inference cost substantially understated (Minor→Major).** The paper states "Each LLM query takes roughly 700 ms" without clarifying that two queries are needed per image (1.4s/image). On ImageNet (50K images), this is ~19.4 hours of sequential API calls — three orders of magnitude slower than the CLIP baseline (~15ms/image). The option for local deployment (16 GPUs/TPUs, 256GB memory) is impractical for most researchers.

5. **Title and narrative framing (Minor).** The title "WHAT DO YOU SEE?" is catchy but not informative. The introduction spends excessive text on generic praise of multimodal LLMs rather than precise problem formulation and gap identification. Contribution claims are somewhat redundant ("improves accuracy" and "outperforms existing methods" convey the same information).

6. **Novelty assessment deferred.** Due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode in this run, external literature verification was not performed. The paper's novelty relative to the strongest concurrent methods (e.g., DCLIP, PLOT, prompt ensembling approaches) cannot be assessed here and requires manual verification.

### Issue 1: Uncontrolled Comparison (Must Fix)

**Problem:** The paper compares against CuPL re-implemented with Gemini Pro for class descriptions, but the proposed method uses Gemini Pro additionally at inference time for per-instance queries. This creates an asymmetric comparison where the proposed method gets more LLM budget.

**Evidence:** Page 5 — "For CuPL, we utilized Gemini Pro for computing class descriptions instead of GPT-3, ensuring a fair comparison with our method." This only equalizes the class description step, not the overall LLM usage.

**Required action:** Add one ablation experiment where the proposed method's per-instance LLM queries are replaced by a fixed (non-instance-specific) textual input, isolating the effect of instance-level descriptions from the effect of additional LLM computation.

### Issue 2: Missing Statistical Significance (Must Fix)

**Problem:** All 10 datasets × multiple method variants are reported as single-point estimates without variance.

**Evidence:** Tables 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 — no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance indicators anywhere.

**Required action:** Report mean ± std over at least 3 independent runs. Add significance test (McNemar's or paired t-test) between the proposed method and CLIP ViT-L/14 baseline for the main results.

### Issue 3: Fusion Mechanism Weakness (Should Fix)

**Problem:** Equal-weighted additive fusion cannot distinguish between reliable and unreliable LLM outputs.

**Evidence:** Figure 6 shows cases where wrong LLM predictions dominate the fused result. Page 7 admits "while Gemini's initial predictions do not match the ground-truth class... the predictions are contextually sensible" — but the fusion has no mechanism to exploit this contextual sensibility while avoiding the wrong classification.

**Required action:** Propose a confidence-weighted fusion variant (e.g., weight each feature by the cosine similarity between the initial prediction embedding and the image feature, or by the LLM's output probability). Show whether this mitigates failure cases.

## Actionable Suggestions
### Must-Fix Items (Publication-Critical)

**S1. Add controlled ablation to isolate the contribution of instance-level descriptions.**
Replace the per-instance LLM queries with a baseline textual input (e.g., a fixed generic description like "An image of an object") while keeping everything else unchanged. If the accuracy drops significantly, this confirms that instance-specific descriptions matter beyond just having more LLM compute. Report the delta on ImageNet and 2-3 additional datasets.

**S2. Report all results with variance.**
Run each experiment 3 times with different random seeds (affecting LLM sampling and any random data ordering). Report mean ± standard deviation. For the main comparison (Table 1), add a significance indicator (bold/asterisk) for results that statistically significantly outperform the CLIP ViT-L/14 baseline under McNemar's test at p<0.05.

**S3. Present total inference cost transparently.**
On Page 8, replace the current computational cost paragraph with: "Each test image requires two Gemini Pro queries (image description and initial prediction) at ~700ms each, totaling ~1.4s per image. On ImageNet (50K images), this corresponds to approximately 19.4 hours of sequential API calls. The CLIP encoding step adds ~15ms per image on an NVIDIA V100."

### Should-Fix Items (Quality Improvement)

**S4. Propose a confidence-weighted fusion.**
Replace equal-weighted sum (Eq. 8) with Xq = α·Xif + β·Xdf + γ·Xpf, where α, β, γ are confidence weights. At minimum, show that using the cosine similarity between Xpf and the closest class label feature as weight γ improves robustness on failure cases (Figure 6).

**S5. Revise the title.**
Replace "WHAT DO YOU SEE? ENHANCING ZERO-SHOT IMAGE CLASSIFICATION WITH MULTIMODAL LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS" with a more informative title such as: "Fusing Visual and LLM-Generated Textual Features for Zero-Shot Image Classification" or "Instance-Level Textual Descriptions from Multimodal LLMs Improve Zero-Shot Classification."

**S6. Restructure the introduction.**
Reorganize the introduction into four clear paragraphs: (1) Problem and significance of zero-shot classification, (2) Limitation of prior work (visual-only features; class-description-only approaches), (3) Proposed solution (fusing instance-level LLM descriptions with visual features), (4) Contribution summary and results preview. The current version's second paragraph spends too much text on generic LLM capabilities.

### Nice-to-Have Items

**S7. Expand fusion ablation to all 10 datasets.**
Table 3 currently tests fusion approaches on only 5,000 ImageNet images. Run the same ablation on all 10 datasets to verify that additive feature fusion is consistently the best choice.

**S8. Add quantitative failure analysis.**
Sample 100-200 misclassified images per dataset and categorize the failure mode: (a) CLIP correct, LLM wrong → fusion wrong, (b) CLIP wrong, LLM correct → fusion wrong, (c) both wrong, (d) LLM produces label not in target set. Report percentages to help readers understand when the method fails.

## Storyline Options + Writing Outlines
### Current Storyline Diagnosis

The current introduction has four paragraph-like segments: (1) a chronological listing of prior zero-shot methods, (2) a transition identifying the visual-features limitation and proposing multimodal LLMs, (3) a generic capabilities list for multimodal LLMs, and (4) contribution claims and roadmap. The narrative problem is that the gap is not sharply formulated, and the solution section spends excessive text on generic LLM praise rather than explaining the technical innovation.

### Recommended Storyline (Candidate A — Best)

Follow a clear **Big Picture → Gap → Solution → Evidence → Contribution** arc:

- **P1 (Big Picture + Gap):** Zero-shot classification is important but existing methods use only visual features at inference. Prior work (CuPL) improves the text side (class descriptions) but ignores instance-level information in the test image.
- **P2 (Proposed solution):** We introduce a method that uses a multimodal LLM to generate per-instance descriptions and initial predictions, then fuses these with visual features. This is simple, uses fixed prompts, and requires no training.
- **P3 (Evidence preview + Contributions):** Our method achieves +4.1 pp average gain over CLIP across 10 benchmarks. We provide ablations showing all three features contribute.

### Abstract Outline (Complete)

**S1 (Problem):** Zero-shot image classification aims to recognize images from classes unseen during training, relying on learned joint embeddings of vision and language.

**S2 (Gap):** Existing methods either (a) use only visual features at inference, missing rich semantic cues, or (b) use LLMs only to describe class labels offline, without accessing instance-level information.

**S3 (Method):** We propose to use a multimodal LLM (Gemini Pro) at inference time to generate two textual representations from each test image — a free-form description and an initial class prediction — which are encoded via CLIP's text encoder and fused with the CLIP image embedding.

**S4 (Key result):** On ten standard benchmarks, our method achieves an average top-1 accuracy improvement of 4.1 percentage points over CLIP (ViT-L/14), with a 6.8 pp gain on ImageNet, using a single fixed prompt set across all datasets.

**S5 (Bounded claim):** These results demonstrate that instance-level textual descriptions provide complementary signal that meaningfully improves zero-shot classification, though the additional LLM inference cost (~1.4s/image) should be weighed against the accuracy gain.

### Research-Theme Gap Diagnosis

- **New Knowledge (weakly supported):** The paper's core hypothesis — that instance-level textual descriptions improve zero-shot classification — is empirically supported but confounded by LLM capacity differences. A clean ablation is missing.
- **Reproducibility (moderately supported):** Code example and algorithm provided, but Gemini Pro is a proprietary API. Results may not be reproducible with future API versions.
- **Change in Practice/Understanding (partially supported):** The paper demonstrates that fusing LLM-generated text with visual features can work, but the computational cost (1.4s/image) limits practical adoption.

### Proposed Research Experiments

**P0 Experiment — Controlled Ablation for Instance-Level Description (P0)**
- **Target Claim:** C1 — Instance-level textual descriptions provide unique value beyond additional LLM capacity.
- **Hypothesis:** Replacing the per-instance LLM query with a fixed generic description will significantly reduce accuracy.
- **Minimal Design:** Replace the per-instance image description prompt with a fixed sentence "An image of an object." Keep all other components (CLIP encoder, fusion, classifier) identical. Compare accuracy on ImageNet and 2 additional datasets.
- **Controls/Baselines:** The current full method serves as the positive control; CLIP zero-shot serves as the negative control.
- **Metrics:** Top-1 accuracy, delta from full method.
- **Success Criterion:** If the fixed-description baseline drops >2 pp vs the full method, the instance-level descriptions are confirmed as beneficial. If the drop is <1 pp, the contribution is primarily from additional LLM capacity.
- **Estimated Cost/Time:** 1-2 days (requires running Gemini Pro on 3 datasets).
- **Expected Paper-Quality Gain:** High — resolves the core fairness concern.

**P1 Experiment — Confidence-Weighted Fusion (P1)**
- **Target Claim:** C1 — Fusion robustness; addressing failure cases.
- **Hypothesis:** Weighting features by their reliability (e.g., cosine similarity to nearest class) reduces the impact of confidently wrong LLM predictions.
- **Minimal Design:** Xq = (w_i·Xif + w_d·Xdf + w_p·Xpf) / ||sum||, where w_p = max(cosine_similarity(Xpf, M)), w_i = w_d = 1 (or learned). Compare with equal-weighted fusion on failure examples.
- **Controls/Baselines:** Equal-weighted fusion (current method).
- **Metrics:** Top-1 accuracy, accuracy on failure-case subset.
- **Success Criterion:** Confidence-weighted fusion achieves >= accuracy of equal-weighted on full set AND higher accuracy on failure cases.
- **Estimated Cost/Time:** 2-3 days.
- **Expected Paper-Quality Gain:** Medium — improves technical depth and addresses a known weakness.

- **Hypothesis:** The reported gains are statistically significant.

- **Controls/Baselines:** N/A (statistical analysis of existing experiment).

- **Success Criterion:** Gains on ImageNet, CIFAR-100, Food-101, SUN397, Cars, DTD, Pets are significant at p<0.05.

- **Expected Paper-Quality Gain:** High — essential for publication credibility.

## Scores
**Final Score:** 5.5/10

**Rationale:** The paper presents a simple, intuitive method with consistent empirical gains across ten diverse benchmarks. The core idea — fusing instance-level textual descriptions from a multimodal LLM with visual features — is sound and practical. However, the score is constrained by three critical factors:

1. **Uncontrolled comparison (validity risk):** The claimed SOTA results are confounded by asymmetric LLM usage between the proposed method and baselines. The contribution of the methodological innovation (instance-level descriptions) cannot be separated from the additional inference-time LLM capacity. This is the single biggest factor preventing a higher score.

2. **Missing statistical rigor:** Without variance or significance reporting, the reliability of the reported gains cannot be assessed. This is a fundamental reproducibility concern.

3. **Novelty unverifiable:** Due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode in this run, external literature verification was not performed. The paper's novelty relative to concurrent work cannot be evaluated here, which introduces significant uncertainty into the assessment.

The empirical gains are real and consistent, which is the paper's strongest asset. The method is clearly described and easy to implement. These strengths prevent the score from falling lower. However, the issues above are serious enough that the paper in its current form would face substantial scrutiny at a top venue.
