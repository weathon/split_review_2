# Final Review Report

## Summary

This paper introduces CONFIDE, a hybrid deep-learning method for inferring PDE coefficient functions from spatio-temporal signal patches in an unsupervised manner. The method combines a context-encoding autoencoder with a finite-difference-based PDE loss, enabling zero-shot generalization to signals generated by PDEs with unseen coefficient values. The approach is demonstrated on three PDE systems (constant-coefficient parabolic PDEs, Burgers' equation, and 2D FitzHugh-Nagumo), comparing favorably against Neural-ODE, FNO, U-Net, and DINO baselines. The paper also provides ablation studies on context ratio, training set size, and autoencoder variants, plus OOD experiments.

**Overall assessment:** The paper addresses a meaningful problem — unsupervised PDE coefficient inference with per-sample varying coefficients — and presents a clean, well-motivated architecture. The empirical results show clear advantages over baselines on synthetic benchmarks. However, the manuscript has several weaknesses that need attention: (1) the mathematical formulation has a discrepancy between Equation (2) and Algorithm 2, (2) the hyper-parameter search is insufficient to support robustness claims, (3) the conclusion over-generalizes beyond what experiments validate, (4) the Related Work section is organized as a list rather than an analytical comparison, and (5) novelty assessment requires external literature verification (deferred in this review run). With targeted revisions, the paper could become a solid contribution.

## Strengths
1. **Well-motivated problem formulation.** The paper identifies a realistic gap in PDE learning: existing methods either assume a single coefficient function across all training samples (e.g., PINN, PDE-NET) or cannot output interpretable coefficients (e.g., FNO, U-Net). The multi-context setting with per-sample varying coefficients is practically relevant for applications like battery modeling and cardiac electrophysiology.

2. **Clean architecture.** The combination of an autoencoder (for context encoding) with a PDE functional loss (for coefficient estimation) is principled and clearly explained. The separation of training (which uses the PDE residual loss without a solver) from inference (which uses an off-the-shelf PDE solver) is a practical design choice that avoids backpropagation through a numerical solver.

3. **Strong empirical results on synthetic benchmarks.** CONFIDE consistently outperforms all baselines (Neural-ODE, FNO, U-Net, DINO) across three PDE families, with the advantage increasing with prediction horizon. The improvements are substantial (3-100x lower MSE at long horizons) and visually convincing in the provided prediction plots. The R²=0.93 for diffusion coefficient estimation on the constant-coefficient task demonstrates good parameter recovery.

4. **Informative ablation studies.** The ablation of the autoencoder component (CONFIDE vs. AE/IC vs. No-AE) cleanly demonstrates the benefit of the initial-conditions-aware decoder, showing ~35% improvement in both prediction and parameter estimation. The train-set-size and context-ratio ablations provide practical guidance for deployment.

5. **OOD experiments.** The inclusion of out-of-distribution tests (shock-wave initial conditions and shifted coefficient distributions) adds credibility. CONFIDE maintains strong performance even under these challenging conditions.

## Weaknesses
1. **Equation (2) — Algorithm inconsistency (Major).** The autoencoder loss in Equation (2) omits the initial condition $u_i(t=0)$ as an input to the decoder, but Algorithm 2 and the text description both include it. This discrepancy undermines reproducibility and suggests incomplete proofreading of mathematical notation.

2. **Insufficient hyper-parameter search for $\alpha$ (Major).** The loss weight $\alpha$ was tested at only two values (0 and 0.5). This is insufficient to determine optimal trade-off or to support conclusions about robustness to hyper-parameters.

3. **Overstated conclusion claims (Major).** The conclusion states the method "is robust to different values of hyper-parameters," but only context ratio and train set size were tested in the ablation — not learning rate, network width, $\alpha$, or optimizer settings. The claim is broader than the evidence.

4. **Related Work is a literature list, not an analytical comparison (Moderate).** The Related Work section presents works in near-chronological order rather than organizing them by decision-relevant axes (e.g., what each method assumes known, what it outputs, whether it supports per-sample varying coefficients). This makes it harder for readers to verify the novelty claim.

5. **Speed comparison uses mismatched baseline (Major, Appendix).** The 44x speedup claim in Appendix E compares CONFIDE to a PINN variant of Neural-ODE, not the Latent-ODE baseline used in the main experiments (Table 2). This is an apples-to-oranges comparison.

6. **Abstract lacks quantitative anchors (Moderate).** The abstract describes "extensive experimentation" and "SOTA approaches" without any concrete numerical results, making it less informative for readers.

7. **CFL stability condition not discussed (Moderate).** The Forward-Time Central-Space scheme used for numerical derivatives and simulation requires CFL stability conditions that are not mentioned anywhere in the paper. This is relevant for reproducibility.

8. **Limited scope of FitzHugh-Nagumo experiment (Minor).** Only 1 out of 3 parameters varies ($k$), and only 1000 signals are used (vs. 10,000 for other experiments). This limits the generality of the 2D PDE results.

9. **Novelty cannot be fully assessed without external retrieval (Deferred).** Due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode in this review run, novelty/comparison conclusions against the strongest related baselines are deferred to manual literature verification.

## Key Issues
### Issue 1 (Major): Equation (2) does not match Algorithm 2
**Evidence:** Page 5 — Equation (2) defines $L_{AE} = \min \sum_i \text{loss}(u^c_i - f_\theta(g_\phi(u^c_i)))$ but Algorithm 2 computes $L_{AE} = (u^c_i - f_\theta(g_\phi(u^c_i), u_i(t=0)))^2$.
**Root cause:** The initial-condition input to the decoder is a key architectural contribution (initial-conditions-aware autoencoder), but the main equation was not updated to reflect it.
**Fix:** Replace Equation (2) with the correct form including $u_i(t=0)$ as decoder input.

### Issue 2 (Major): Hyper-parameter $\alpha$ tested with only 2 values
**Evidence:** Page 16 (Appendix B.2, Hyper-parameters) — "after testing only two different values: 0 and 0.5."
**Root cause:** The search was too sparse to determine the optimal trade-off or to support robustness claims.
**Fix:** Search at least 5 values of $\alpha$ (e.g., 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) and report sensitivity in an appendix figure.

### Issue 3 (Major): Speed comparison uses inconsistent baseline
**Evidence:** Page 20-21 (Appendix E) — The 44x speedup claim compares CONFIDE to a PINN variant of Neural-ODE, not the Latent-ODE used in the main experiments.
**Root cause:** The appendix aims to compare differentiation vs. integration approaches but uses a non-standard Neural-ODE variant.
**Fix:** Either compare against the same Latent-ODE used in Table 2, or clearly state the comparison uses a PINN variant and the speed advantage may differ.

### Issue 4 (Moderate): Related Work lacks analytical organization
**Evidence:** Page 2-3 — Works are listed chronologically rather than grouped by comparison axes.
**Root cause:** The writing prioritizes coverage over structured positioning.
**Fix:** Reorganize by axes: (a) assumed knowledge, (b) per-sample coefficient handling, (c) output type.

### Issue 5 (Moderate): Conclusion overclaims hyper-parameter robustness
**Evidence:** Page 10 — "robust to different values of hyper-parameters" but only context ratio and dataset size were tested.
**Fix:** Bound the robustness claim to the tested parameters and acknowledge other hyper-parameters need investigation.

**Abstract Outline (4 sentences):**
- **S1 (Problem + Domain):** "Many physical phenomena are governed by Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) whose functional form is known but whose coefficient functions are unknown and vary per sample."
- **S2 (Gap):** "Existing deep learning approaches either learn black-box surrogates without explainable coefficients or assume a single coefficient function across all training data — an unrealistic constraint in applications like battery modeling."
- **S3 (Method):** "We introduce CONFIDE, which combines a context-encoding autoencoder with a finite-difference PDE residual loss to infer sample-specific coefficient functions without requiring ground-truth parameters."
- **S4 (Result):** "Across three PDE families, CONFIDE achieves 3-100x lower prediction MSE than baselines while providing accurate coefficient estimates (R²=0.93), and generalizes zero-shot to unseen coefficient values."

**Introduction Outline (5 paragraphs):**

**P1 — Stakes and Motivation (revised):**
"Many scientific and engineering domains use PDEs to model spatio-temporal phenomena. However, in high-stakes applications such as battery management systems, the PDE functional form may be known from first principles while the coefficient functions differ for each individual battery due to manufacturing variability and aging. This creates a pressing need for methods that can infer sample-specific coefficient functions from partial observations."

**P2 — Prior Work Gap (revised):**
"Existing approaches can be grouped into three categories. First, black-box deep learning models (FNO, U-Net, Neural-ODE) achieve fast predictions but lack interpretable coefficients and extrapolate poorly. Second, physics-informed methods (PINN, PDE-NET) incorporate known PDE structure but assume all training samples share the same coefficient function. Third, context-aware methods (CoDA, APHYNITY) handle varying coefficients but either do not output coefficients or assume fixed (non-functional) coefficients. The key missing capability is unsupervised, per-sample coefficient inference for PDEs with functional coefficient dependence on space, time, and state."

**P3 — Proposed Solution (revised):**
"In this paper, we propose CONFIDE, a hybrid approach that overcomes these limitations. Given a partial observation of a signal and the known functional form of its governing PDE, CONFIDE estimates the coefficient functions and uses them to predict the complete evolution. The method trains without ground-truth parameters by combining an autoencoder reconstruction loss with a PDE residual loss computed via finite differences."

**P4 — Technical Highlights (revised):**
"A key innovation is the initial-conditions-aware autoencoder, which encourages the latent representation to encode dynamics rather than initial values. Another is the use of finite differences for the PDE loss, which avoids backpropagation through a numerical solver during training and enables efficient learning across hundreds of PDE samples with different coefficients."

**P5 — Contributions and Outline:**
"Our contributions are: (1) an unsupervised method for per-sample PDE coefficient inference using a finite-difference-based loss, and (2) a context-encoding architecture enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen coefficient values. We demonstrate the method on three PDE families with extensive ablations and OOD tests."

### Research-Theme Gap Diagnosis

1. **New knowledge gap:** The paper demonstrates that unsupervised PDE coefficient inference across varying contexts is feasible. However, the scope is limited to cases where the PDE functional form is exactly known and coefficients vary within bounded uniform distributions. It remains unclear whether the method works when:
   - The PDE form is misspecified beyond additive discrepancies.
   - Coefficient functions have complex nonlinear spatial/temporal structures.
   - The number of varying parameters scales to 5+ (curse of dimensionality for the context encoder).

2. **Reproducibility gap:** The code is stated as "available upon acceptance" — no repository link is provided for reviewer verification. The CFL stability conditions are not documented. The $\alpha$ hyper-parameter choice is supported by only a 2-point search.

3. **Impact on practice gap:** The battery modeling motivation (Page 2) is compelling but no real-world battery data experiments are presented. The paper remains in the synthetic benchmark domain.
