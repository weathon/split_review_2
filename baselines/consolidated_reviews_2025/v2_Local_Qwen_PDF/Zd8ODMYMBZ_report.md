## Summary
This paper proposes FAVICOMP (FAmiliarity-aware EVIdence COMPression), a training-free evidence compression technique for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). The core motivation addresses the "unfamiliarity" gap: compressed evidence generated by a separate compression model may fall outside the target model's high-probability manifold, leading to suboptimal downstream performance. FAVICOMP resolves this by ensembling the decoding probabilities of a compression model and the target model during context generation, effectively using the target model's logits as a soft prior to lower perplexity and integrate parametric knowledge. Experiments on five open-domain QA datasets show that FAVICOMP consistently matches or outperforms recent baselines, including supervised compression methods, while maintaining high compression rates. The paper also provides ablation studies on the ensemble coefficient and analyzes the integration of parametric vs. non-parametric knowledge.

## Strengths
1. **Clear and Well-Motivated Problem Formulation:** The paper identifies a concrete and under-explored gap in compression-based RAG: the distributional mismatch between compression models and target models. The concept of "familiarity" linked to perplexity provides a principled motivation for the proposed method.
2. **Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Design:** FAVICOMP requires no additional training or fine-tuning, making it highly practical and easily integrable into existing RAG pipelines. The ensemble decoding mechanism is simple yet effective, leveraging existing model capabilities without architectural changes.
3. **Comprehensive Empirical Validation:** The method is evaluated across five diverse open-domain QA datasets (single-hop and multi-hop) with multiple target models. The inclusion of ablation studies on the ensemble coefficient $\alpha$ and the Hits=0/Hits=1 analysis provides strong evidence for the claimed integration of parametric and non-parametric knowledge.
4. **Strong Baseline Comparisons:** The paper compares against a wide range of baselines, including reranking, extractive, abstractive, and supervised compression methods. The head-to-head comparison in Appendix B.2 further strengthens the validity of the results by controlling for compression model size.

## Weaknesses
1. **Factual Inaccuracy in Result Analysis:** The main text claims FAVICOMP "outperforms all other baselines across all the datasets," but Table 1 shows CompAct achieves higher accuracy on NQ with Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct (44.1 vs 43.6). This overclaim undermines credibility and requires immediate correction.
2. **Mathematical Notation Inconsistency:** Equation (2) in §2.2 incorrectly equates the generated context sequence $\hat{c}$ with a probability distribution $P_c$. This notational error confuses the distinction between the distribution and the sampled/argmax sequence, reducing technical rigor.
3. **Imprecise Explanation of Ensemble Decoding:** The textual description of token selection implies a simple probability threshold comparison, whereas the actual mechanism maximizes a weighted sum of log probabilities. This ambiguity could mislead implementers regarding the decoding logic.
4. **Lack of Limitation Discussion:** The conclusion presents FAVICOMP as a versatile tool without acknowledging practical constraints, such as the increased inference latency from dual-model decoding or potential failure modes when parametric knowledge is severely outdated.
5. **Baseline Fairness Transparency:** While Appendix B.2 addresses model size disparities, the main text does not explicitly acknowledge that baselines like RECOMP-abstractive use smaller models (T5-large) compared to FAVICOMP's 7B/3B models, which may initially raise fairness concerns for readers.

## Key Issues
1. **Claim-Evidence Mismatch in Main Results (Critical):** The assertion that FAVICOMP outperforms all baselines on all datasets is factually incorrect based on Table 1. This must be corrected to reflect competitive performance rather than universal superiority, with nuanced discussion of cases where supervised methods still lead.
2. **Mathematical Formalization Error (Major):** The definition of $\hat{c}$ in §2.2 conflates a sequence with a probability distribution. Correcting this notation is essential for reproducibility and technical clarity.
3. **Ambiguous Decoding Logic Description (Minor):** The explanation of ensemble decoding should explicitly state that token selection maximizes the weighted log-probability sum, avoiding misleading threshold-based interpretations.
4. **Missing Limitations and Scope Boundaries (Minor):** The conclusion lacks a discussion of compute overhead and parametric reliability assumptions. Adding bounded limitations will improve scientific defensibility and set realistic expectations for deployment.

## Actionable Suggestions
1. **Correct Result Claims:** Revise §4.1 to accurately reflect Table 1. Replace "outperforms all other baselines" with "achieves competitive or superior performance," and explicitly discuss where FAVICOMP leads vs. where it is comparable. Provide a nuanced explanation for the MuSiQue result regarding parametric supplementation.
2. **Fix Mathematical Notation:** Update Eq. (2) in §2.2 to define $\hat{c}$ as the sequence generated from $P_c$, e.g., $\hat{c} \sim P_c(\cdot | x, D)$ or $\hat{c} = \text{argmax}_{c} P_c(c | x, D)$. Ensure $P_t$ and $P_c$ are explicitly defined as probability distributions.
3. **Clarify Decoding Mechanism:** In §2.3, explicitly state that token selection maximizes the weighted sum of log probabilities: $c_i = \text{argmax}_{c_i \in V} ((1-\alpha) \log P_c(...) + \alpha \log P_t(...))$. Remove ambiguous threshold-based phrasing.
4. **Add Limitations Paragraph:** Append a concise limitations section to the conclusion acknowledging the inference overhead of dual-model decoding and the assumption that target model parametric knowledge is generally reliable. Suggest future work on adaptive $\alpha$ or lightweight proxies.
5. **Improve Abstract Structure:** Restructure the abstract into a tight 4-sentence arc: (S1) RAG struggles with noisy multi-evidence. (S2) Existing compressors produce unfamiliar contexts. (S3) FAVICOMP lowers target-model perplexity via ensemble decoding. (S4) Results show consistent gains across five QA datasets. Clarify if the 23.91% gain is absolute or relative.
