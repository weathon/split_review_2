## Summary

This paper introduces the Longitudinal Ensemble Integration (LEI) framework, a novel approach for classifying multimodal longitudinal data, specifically focusing on the task of early dementia detection using data from the Alzheimer’s Disease Prediction of Longitudinal Evolution (TADPOLE) Challenge. The core idea behind LEI is to extend the existing Ensemble Integration (EI) framework by incorporating a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network for temporal modeling of the base predictions. The method operates by first training individual base predictors on each modality of data, such as imaging, genetic, and clinical data. These base predictors, which can include models like logistic regression, support vector machines, or random forests, generate predictions for each time point. These predictions are then fed into an LSTM network, which is trained to classify the longitudinal data and predict future diagnoses. The authors explore different configurations of the base predictors, including time-dependent and time-distributed approaches, and evaluate the performance of the LEI framework using the multi-class F-measure, also known as the argmax measure. The empirical evaluation is conducted on the TADPOLE dataset, where the LEI framework is compared against several LSTM baselines, including both time-distributed and time-distributed classification heads. The results demonstrate that LEI outperforms these baselines and identifies features that are consistent with known biological markers of dementia. The authors also provide an analysis of the identified features, highlighting their temporal dynamics and their relationship to the underlying biological processes of dementia. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach for longitudinal data classification, with a focus on multimodal data and the specific challenge of early dementia detection. The proposed framework is well-motivated, and the empirical results are promising, suggesting that LEI could be a valuable tool for clinical applications. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of its comparison with existing methods, the details of the LSTM implementation, and the discussion of limitations and future research directions.
