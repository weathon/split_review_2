## Summary

This paper introduces a novel framework designed to address what the authors term 'disguaged procedural unfairness' within machine learning models. The central premise is that unfairness can arise not only from biased features but also from changes in the neutral components of the data generating process, which are typically considered to be unaffected by protected attributes. The authors argue that existing approaches, which often focus on constraining model parameters, may not be sufficient to mitigate this form of unfairness. To address this, they propose a framework that leverages causal inference to identify and decouple objectionable components from the data generating process. The core idea is to use reference points and value instantiation rules to ensure that predictions are based solely on the neutral components, effectively removing the influence of protected features and other potentially biased factors. The framework is presented as a way to achieve procedural fairness by directly addressing the underlying data generating process. The authors illustrate their approach using a linear model and the UCI Adult dataset, demonstrating how the framework can be applied in practice. They also connect their work to Rawls' difference principle, highlighting the importance of considering all aspects of the data generating process, not just protected features, when ensuring fairness. The paper's contribution lies in its focus on 'disguaged procedural unfairness' and the proposed framework for decoupling objectionable components. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in its handling of complex scenarios with multiple interacting components and the lack of a clear methodology for identifying which components are objectionable. The empirical evaluation, while present, is limited in scope and could benefit from a broader range of datasets and metrics. Overall, the paper presents an interesting and important perspective on fairness in machine learning, but it requires further development to address its limitations and fully realize its potential.
