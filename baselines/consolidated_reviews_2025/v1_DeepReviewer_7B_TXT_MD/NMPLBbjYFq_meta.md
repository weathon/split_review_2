## Summary

This paper introduces a framework, EconArena, designed to evaluate the strategic reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in competitive economic games. The authors focus on two classic economic games: the beauty contest game and second-price auctions. In the beauty contest game, participants are asked to iteratively guess a number that they believe will be two-thirds of the average of all guesses. In second-price auctions, participants bid on items, with the highest bidder winning the item and paying the second-highest bid. The authors evaluate LLMs by measuring their ability to reach Nash Equilibria, their capacity for strategic reasoning, and their learning from game history. They test a range of LLMs, including GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claude 2, and Llama 2, and find that while these models can achieve Nash Equilibria in these games, they do not consistently demonstrate robust strategic reasoning. Some models show an improvement in their strategies when provided with game history, but this improvement is not universal across all models. The authors use a variety of metrics to quantify the performance of the LLMs, including payoff changes, the difference from the Nash Equilibrium, and strategies over different game configurations and player configurations. The core finding is that while LLMs can achieve Nash Equilibria in these games, they do not consistently demonstrate robust strategic reasoning, and their performance is not always optimal. The authors also explore the impact of providing game history, showing that some models can improve their strategies with this additional information. The paper's contribution lies in the introduction of the EconArena framework, which provides a standardized way to evaluate LLMs in economic games, and the empirical findings that highlight the limitations of current LLMs in strategic reasoning tasks. The study also suggests that LLMs may be able to adapt their strategies with game history, but this adaptation is not always consistent across different models. Overall, the paper provides a valuable contribution to the field by offering a new benchmark for evaluating LLMs in economic contexts and by highlighting the challenges that remain in achieving robust strategic reasoning in these models.

## Weaknesses

While this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field, several limitations warrant careful consideration. A primary weakness is the limited scope of the economic games used in the evaluation. The authors focus exclusively on the beauty contest game and second-price auctions, which, while useful for initial evaluation, do not fully capture the complexities of real-world economic interactions. As the authors themselves state, "At the moment, we provide only single-round games in EconArena, such that the LLMs can be used in an “off-the-shelf” fashion." This narrow focus limits the generalizability of the findings and raises questions about the extent to which the observed results can be extrapolated to other economic scenarios. The beauty contest game, for example, is a highly iterative game, and the fact that the authors only evaluate it in a single round is a significant limitation. Similarly, the second-price auction, while useful for evaluating bidding strategies, is a relatively simple game that does not fully capture the complexities of real-world auctions. The lack of multi-round games and more complex auction designs, such as those with multiple rounds of bidding, reserve price determination, or more complex payoff structures, is a significant oversight. This limitation is further compounded by the fact that the paper does not explore the behavior of LLMs in more complex economic scenarios that involve multiple agents and more intricate strategic interactions. The current games, while useful for initial evaluation, are relatively simple and may not fully capture the complexities of real-world economic systems. For example, incorporating scenarios with multiple rounds of negotiation, or games with more complex payoff structures, could provide a more challenging test of LLMs' strategic reasoning abilities.
