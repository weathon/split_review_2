## Summary

This paper introduces Imprecise Bayesian Continual Learning (IBCL), a novel approach to continual learning (CL) that aims to address the challenge of generating models that balance multiple tasks according to user-defined preferences. Unlike traditional CL methods that focus on a single task or require retraining with all previous tasks, IBCL allows users to specify a preference vector, which dictates the desired trade-off between performance on different tasks. This is achieved through a Bayesian framework, where the model's parameters are represented as random variables, and variational inference is used to update the knowledge base as new tasks are encountered. The core idea is to maintain a knowledge base of task distributions and their convex combinations, enabling the generation of models that satisfy the specified preference vector. The method also incorporates a preference HDR computation to generate models that satisfy the preferences. The authors claim that their method is efficient, with sublinear buffer growth and constant training overhead per task. The empirical evaluation of IBCL is conducted on several CL benchmarks, including 20NewsGroup, Split CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and 20NewsGroup again. The results demonstrate that IBCL outperforms existing CL methods in terms of both accuracy and stability, as measured by peak per-task accuracy and backward transfer. The paper also presents theoretical guarantees for the proposed method, including proofs of probabilistic Pareto-optimality and sublinear buffer growth. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of continual learning by introducing a method that allows for flexible model generation based on user preferences, while also maintaining computational efficiency and scalability. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be improved to strengthen its claims and enhance its impact.
