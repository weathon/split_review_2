## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance the computational efficiency of equivariant neural networks operating on the E(3) group, a crucial framework for modeling 3D data. The core contribution lies in establishing a connection between Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, commonly used in quantum mechanics, and Gaunt coefficients, which are integrals of products of spherical harmonics. This connection allows the authors to reformulate the tensor product of irreducible representations (irreps) as a multiplication between spherical functions represented by spherical harmonics. This transformation is pivotal because it enables the application of the convolution theorem and Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), significantly reducing the complexity of full tensor product computations from O(L^6) to O(L^3), where L is the maximum degree of irreps. The authors propose a 'Gaunt Tensor Product' method, which leverages this transformation to accelerate the computation of tensor products in equivariant operations. The method is evaluated on two primary tasks: the Open Catalyst 2020 (OC20) dataset, a large-scale dataset for predicting the properties of chemical reactions, and the 3BPA dataset, which involves predicting the energy and forces of molecules. The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves substantial speedups compared to baseline implementations, while maintaining competitive accuracy. Specifically, the paper shows that the Gaunt Tensor Product can be integrated into existing equivariant neural network architectures, such as EquiformerV2, and that it leads to improved performance on the OC20 S2EF task. The authors also present a sensitivity analysis, showing that the method is robust to different choices of hyperparameters. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of equivariant neural networks by providing a computationally efficient method for tensor product operations, which is a key bottleneck in many applications. The theoretical connection between Clebsch-Gordan and Gaunt coefficients is a novel insight, and the empirical results demonstrate the practical utility of the proposed method. The work has the potential to enable the development of more complex and accurate equivariant models for a wide range of scientific and engineering applications.

## Weaknesses

Despite the paper's strengths, there are several limitations that warrant careful consideration. A primary concern, independently validated by multiple reviewers, is the paper's exclusive focus on the E(3) group, which represents rotations in 3D space and translations. The method relies on a change of basis to a 2D Fourier basis, which is specific to spherical harmonics and their properties related to rotations. As the reviewers correctly pointed out, the E(3) group is a specific case of the more general SE(3) group, which includes both rotations and translations. The paper does not address the challenges of extending the proposed method to SE(3), which is a more complex and widely used group in many applications. Specifically, the method's reliance on a 2D Fourier basis transformation may not directly translate to the 3D Fourier transform required for SE(3) operations. The authors do not discuss how their approach would handle the additional translational degrees of freedom and whether the current framework can be adapted to incorporate these. This is a significant limitation, as many real-world problems involve SE(3) symmetries. The paper's claim that the method can be applied to various equivariant operations is not fully supported by the experimental evidence. While the experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness on the OC20 S2EF and 3BPA datasets, these tasks are not necessarily representative of the full spectrum of challenging equivariant network architectures and problem domains. As the reviewers noted, tasks involving long-range interactions or complex geometric reasoning could reveal potential limitations of the proposed approach. The current experiments, while promising, do not fully demonstrate the general applicability of the method across diverse equivariant network architectures and problem domains. The paper's experimental evaluation is also limited in terms of the datasets used. While the authors present results on the OC20 S2EF and 3BPA datasets, these datasets are not sufficient to demonstrate the method's robustness and generalizability. The paper would benefit from experiments on a wider range of datasets, including those that are known to be challenging for equivariant networks, such as molecular property prediction or protein structure prediction. The lack of such experiments makes it difficult to assess the method's performance in more complex scenarios. Furthermore, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational benefits of the proposed method. While the authors mention a reduction in complexity from O(L^6) to O(L^3), they do not provide a more detailed breakdown of the computational costs, including the time and memory requirements for different values of L. This would allow readers to better understand the practical implications of the proposed method. The paper also lacks a detailed discussion of the implementation of the proposed method, including the specific choices of basis functions and the numerical techniques used for the FFT. This would allow other researchers to reproduce the results and build upon the work. Finally, the paper does not discuss the potential limitations of the proposed method in terms of memory usage and scalability, especially when applied to large-scale datasets. Addressing these practical considerations would make the paper more complete and useful for the broader research community. In summary, while the paper presents a novel and efficient method for tensor product computations, its limitations in terms of the exclusive focus on E(3), the limited experimental evaluation, and the lack of detailed analysis of computational benefits and implementation details are significant and need to be addressed.
