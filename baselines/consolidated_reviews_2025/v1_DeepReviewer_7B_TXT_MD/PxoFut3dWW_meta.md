## Summary

This paper introduces Wanda, a novel and straightforward pruning method for large language models (LLMs) that does not require retraining or weight updates. The core idea behind Wanda is to leverage a simple yet effective metric that combines the magnitude of a weight with the input activation norms to prune less important connections. This approach is motivated by the observation that, unlike smaller models, LLMs are more difficult to prune directly, even with standard magnitude-based techniques. The authors propose that by considering both the weight magnitude and the corresponding input activation norms, they can more effectively identify and remove less important connections. The method is evaluated on LLaMA and LLaMA-2 models across various sparsity levels and tasks, demonstrating competitive performance compared to existing pruning techniques, particularly SparseGPT. The authors also explore the application of Wanda to structured sparsity, yielding similar results to SparseGPT. The paper highlights the computational efficiency of Wanda, reporting a significant speedup compared to SparseGPT. The authors emphasize that Wanda is a practical solution for pruning large language models, offering a balance between performance and computational cost. The method's simplicity and effectiveness make it a valuable contribution to the field of model compression, providing a practical approach for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained environments. The paper also includes ablation studies on the pruning configuration and robustness to calibration samples, further supporting the method's robustness and practical applicability. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and well-executed study that advances the field of LLM pruning with a simple and effective method.
