### Summary

The paper proposes a novel approach to improve fairness in machine learning models when sensitive attributes are not available. The authors introduce a confidence-based framework that leverages learnable noise and a dual-model system to achieve fair classification. The framework is tested on the COMPAS and New Adult datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of fairness metrics compared to existing methods.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more detailed explanation of the interaction between the dual-model system and the learnable noise. Specifically, the authors should provide a step-by-step description of how the low-confidence generator's knowledge is transferred to the high-confidence generator. This should include a discussion of the specific mechanisms used to update the high-confidence generator using pseudo-labels generated by the low-confidence generator. Furthermore, the paper should include a more rigorous analysis of how the learnable noise alters the feature space. This should include a discussion of the mathematical properties of the noise and how it affects the distribution of the data. The authors should also discuss the potential trade-offs between fairness and accuracy introduced by the noise, and how these trade-offs are managed within the framework. It would be beneficial to include visualizations of the feature space before and after the application of the noise to provide a more intuitive understanding of its effects. Additionally, the authors should consider including a discussion of how the noise is calibrated to ensure that it retains only essential information for predictions, and how this calibration process is integrated into the overall framework.

To address the computational cost concerns, the paper should include a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of each component of the framework. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and memory requirements for each step, such as the VAE training, the dual-model system, and the learnable noise module. The authors should also provide empirical results on training time and memory usage for the datasets used, and discuss how these scale with increasing data size. This analysis should also consider the impact of different hyperparameter settings on the computational cost. Furthermore, the paper should discuss potential strategies for reducing the computational cost of the method, such as using more efficient optimization algorithms or model architectures. This would make the method more practical for large-scale datasets.

Finally, the paper should include a more thorough hyperparameter sensitivity analysis. This analysis should systematically vary the noise variance and the learning rates for the two generators, and evaluate the impact on both accuracy and fairness. The paper should include a sensitivity analysis showing how performance varies with different hyperparameter settings. This analysis should also discuss the optimal hyperparameter settings for different datasets and tasks. The authors should also discuss the potential limitations of the proposed method, such as its applicability to different types of data or its robustness to adversarial attacks. This should include a discussion of whether the method is suitable for high-dimensional data, categorical data, or time-series data. Additionally, the paper should discuss the potential for adversarial attacks that could exploit the learnable noise or the dual-model system to manipulate the fairness outcomes. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses.
