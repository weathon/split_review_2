## Summary

This paper introduces FrAug, a novel frequency-domain data augmentation technique designed to enhance the performance of time series forecasting models, particularly in low-data regimes. The authors begin by critically examining existing time series augmentation methods, highlighting their limitations in capturing fine-grained temporal relationships crucial for forecasting tasks. They argue that methods like cropping, warping, and noise injection, while effective in other time series analysis tasks, disrupt the inherent temporal dependencies necessary for accurate forecasting. To address this, FrAug employs two distinct augmentation strategies: frequency masking, which involves randomly masking segments of the frequency representation of the time series, and frequency mixing, which combines frequency components from different time series. These operations are designed to preserve the semantic consistency of the augmented data-label pairs, ensuring that the frequency components in the labels (look-back window and forecasting horizon) remain aligned with their corresponding time series segments. The core idea is that by operating in the frequency domain, FrAug can manipulate the spectral characteristics of the time series while maintaining the crucial temporal relationships that are often lost in time-domain augmentations. The authors evaluate FrAug across eight widely-used benchmark datasets, employing state-of-the-art forecasting models such as DLinear, FEDformer, and Informer. Their experimental results demonstrate that FrAug significantly improves forecasting accuracy, particularly when the training dataset is small. Notably, FrAug can achieve performance comparable to models trained on full datasets, even when using only 1% of the original training data. This is especially pronounced in scenarios with distribution shifts, where FrAug helps models adapt more quickly to new data patterns. The paper also explores the effectiveness of FrAug in test-time training, where the model is periodically retrained on new data, demonstrating its ability to mitigate performance degradation over time. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by introducing a frequency-domain augmentation technique specifically tailored for time series forecasting, addressing a critical gap in existing methods and offering a practical solution for improving forecasting accuracy, particularly in data-scarce environments.
