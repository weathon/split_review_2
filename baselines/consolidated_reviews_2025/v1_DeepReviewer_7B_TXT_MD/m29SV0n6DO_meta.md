## Summary

This paper introduces Toto, a novel generative pre-training approach for both videos and images, designed to learn robust visual representations through a causal transformer architecture. The authors propose a method of training a model to predict the next patch of tokens in a sequence, drawing inspiration from techniques used in image generation models like iGPT. The core idea is to transform visual data, encompassing both images and video frames, into a sequential format suitable for autoregressive modeling. The model is trained on a large-scale dataset comprising over one trillion visual tokens, sourced from diverse video and image datasets, including ImageNet, Kinetics-600, Ego4D, and HowTo100m. The authors emphasize the importance of handling the variable token lengths of images and videos by using a dVAE tokenizer and a relative positional embedding scheme. The model is trained autoregressively, predicting the next patch of tokens in the sequence. The authors evaluate the learned representations on a variety of downstream tasks, including image recognition, video classification, object tracking, robotic manipulation, and scaling behaviors. They report competitive performance across these tasks, suggesting the efficacy of their generative pre-training approach. The paper also includes ablation studies to understand different design choices and compare their approach to strong baselines across different tasks. The authors use a linear probe for image recognition and video classification, and the last layer for video tracking and object permanence. They also use the learned representations as a backbone for a pre-trained vision encoder and train a controller for robotic manipulation. The paper's main contribution lies in the unified approach to pre-training on both video and image data, the use of a causal transformer for autoregressive modeling, and the demonstration of competitive performance on a range of downstream tasks. The authors also explore the scaling behavior of the model, showing its performance with increasing model size and training data. While the paper presents a compelling approach to generative pre-training, it lacks a detailed theoretical justification for the choice of generative pre-training, and some aspects of the training process and downstream task adaptation are not fully explained, which limits the clarity of the work.
