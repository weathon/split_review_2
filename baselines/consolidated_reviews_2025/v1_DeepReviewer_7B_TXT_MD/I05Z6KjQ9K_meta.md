## Summary

This paper introduces Gradient Regularized Cross-Prompt Attack (GrCPA), a novel method designed to enhance the transferability of adversarial attacks on vision-language models (VLMs). The authors identify a non-stationary phenomenon in the optimization process of VLMs, where adversarial examples generated for one prompt do not effectively transfer to others. This issue, they argue, stems from the complex interplay between visual and textual features within VLMs. To address this, GrCPA employs gradient regularization, specifically by clipping the gradients of both visual and textual features during backpropagation. The core idea is that by limiting the magnitude of gradients, the optimization process becomes more stable, leading to adversarial examples that are more robust and transferable across different prompts. The method is implemented by identifying the k largest and smallest gradient values and setting them to zero, effectively reducing the impact of extreme gradients. Furthermore, the authors hypothesize that regularizing high-level features in LLMs can improve cross-model transferability. The experimental evaluation of GrCPA is conducted on several VLMs, including Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA-1.5, and InstructBLIP, and across various tasks, primarily focusing on Visual Question Answering (VQA). The results demonstrate that GrCPA achieves improved attack success rates and enhanced transferability compared to existing methods, such as Single-P, Multi-P, and CroPA. The paper also includes an analysis of the convergence of GrCPA, showing that it requires fewer iterations to achieve comparable performance. The authors also explore the impact of the proportion of Transformer blocks on regularization, finding that a balance is needed to avoid overfitting. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and empirically supported approach to improving adversarial attacks on VLMs, addressing a significant challenge in the field. The use of gradient regularization is a simple yet effective technique that leads to improved transferability across different prompts, which is a critical aspect for the practical application of adversarial attacks in real-world scenarios. The paper's findings suggest that gradient regularization is a promising direction for enhancing the robustness of VLMs and developing more effective adversarial attack methods.
