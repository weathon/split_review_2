## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to concept forgetting in machine learning models, a critical issue when models inadvertently learn and retain sensitive or irrelevant information from their training data. The authors define concept forgetting as the ability of a model to disregard undesired concepts during its decision-making process, and they propose a Label Annealing (LAN) algorithm to achieve this. The core idea behind LAN is an iterative process involving pseudo-label generation and model fine-tuning. Initially, the algorithm redistributes pseudo-labels to the dataset based on the current model's predictions, effectively encouraging the model to 'forget' the undesired concept. Subsequently, the model is fine-tuned on this modified dataset. This iterative process is designed to gradually shift the model's focus away from the undesired concept while preserving its overall performance on other tasks. The authors also introduce a metric, concept violation, to quantify the extent to which a model has forgotten a specific concept. This metric is based on the total variation distance between the model's predictions with and without the undesired concept. The paper presents experimental results on various image classification datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA, demonstrating the effectiveness of the LAN algorithm in reducing concept violation while maintaining high model accuracy. The experiments compare the proposed method with several baselines from the fairness literature, showing that LAN achieves superior performance in terms of balancing concept forgetting and model accuracy. The authors emphasize the computational efficiency of their approach, making it a practical solution for mitigating concept forgetting in pre-trained models. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by introducing a well-defined problem, a novel algorithm, and a quantitative metric for concept forgetting, which is a crucial step towards developing more robust and responsible machine learning models. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, the paper has some limitations that need to be addressed in future work.
