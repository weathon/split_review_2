## Summary

This paper introduces a novel perspective on the relationship between generalization ability and intrinsic dimension in neural networks, proposing a new metric called 'label sharpness' to quantify the intrinsic dimension of training datasets. The authors argue that the generalization performance of a neural network is negatively correlated with the label sharpness of the training set, and they support this claim with extensive experiments on six medical imaging datasets, comparing them with eleven natural image datasets. The paper's core contributions include the introduction of label sharpness as a metric, the theoretical framework linking generalization to intrinsic dimension, and the empirical validation of these claims through experiments on diverse datasets. The authors also explore the relationship between label sharpness and adversarial robustness, finding that higher label sharpness correlates with greater adversarial vulnerability. The methodological approach involves using a nearest-neighbor based estimator to compute label sharpness, and the theoretical analysis relies on the assumption of Lipschitz continuity of the loss function and the model. The empirical findings demonstrate that the proposed metric can effectively predict the generalization performance of neural networks and that label sharpness is a useful indicator of adversarial robustness. Overall, the paper provides a valuable contribution to the field by introducing a new metric and exploring its relationship with generalization and robustness, although it has several limitations that need to be addressed to enhance its practical applicability and theoretical grounding.
