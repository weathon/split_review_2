## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to low-bit weight-only quantization of Large Language Models (LLMs), focusing on mitigating the impact of activation outliers, which are known to significantly degrade performance. The core idea revolves around per-channel quantization, where weights are quantized per input channel (IC) rather than per output channel (OC), which is the standard practice. The authors argue that this per-channel approach, or per-IC quantization, better isolates the outlier effects, leading to more effective quantization. They further propose an adaptive method, AdaDim, that dynamically selects between per-channel and per-output-channel quantization for each layer based on a reconstruction error metric. The method is evaluated on Llama and WizardLM models, demonstrating improved performance compared to baseline methods like RTN and GPTQ. The paper's key contributions include the introduction of per-IC quantization, the adaptive selection mechanism, and the empirical validation of these techniques on LLMs. The authors' approach is motivated by the observation that outliers in LLM activations tend to affect the entire output dimension, making per-output-channel quantization more sensitive to these outliers. By quantizing weights per channel, they aim to minimize the impact of these outliers on the quantized model. The adaptive mechanism, AdaDim, further refines this approach by selecting the most effective quantization strategy for each layer, based on minimizing the reconstruction error. The empirical results show that the proposed method achieves better performance than baseline methods, suggesting the effectiveness of the per-channel quantization and adaptive selection strategy. However, the paper's analysis and evaluation are limited in several key aspects, which I will discuss in detail. Overall, the paper presents an interesting approach to LLM quantization, but it requires further investigation and clarification to fully validate its claims and contributions.

## Strengths

I found several aspects of this paper to be commendable. First, the core idea of per-channel quantization is both simple and effective. The authors clearly articulate the motivation behind this approach, linking it to the observation that activation outliers affect the entire output dimension, making per-output-channel quantization more susceptible to performance degradation. The proposed per-IC quantization method, which groups consecutive weights within each input channel, is a straightforward yet effective technique for mitigating this issue. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which facilitates understanding of the proposed method. The authors clearly explain the problem they are addressing, the proposed solution, and the experimental setup. Furthermore, the empirical results, while limited in scope, do demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve better performance than baseline methods like RTN and GPTQ. This provides initial evidence for the effectiveness of the per-channel quantization and adaptive selection strategy. The introduction of the adaptive mechanism, AdaDim, is another positive aspect of the paper. This mechanism allows the method to dynamically select the most appropriate quantization strategy for each layer, based on the reconstruction error. This adaptive approach adds a layer of sophistication to the method and allows it to further optimize performance. Finally, the paper's focus on low-bit quantization is highly relevant, given the need to accelerate LLM inference and reduce memory footprint. The authors' work contributes to the growing body of research on model compression techniques for LLMs, and their findings could potentially have a significant impact on the practical deployment of these models.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several significant weaknesses that need to be addressed. First, the novelty of the proposed method is questionable. While the authors introduce per-channel quantization, this technique has been explored in previous works, such as LLM.int8. The paper distinguishes itself by focusing on per-input-channel (per-IC) quantization, but it does not adequately address the trade-offs between this approach and per-group quantization, which is used in LLM.int8. Specifically, the paper does not provide a detailed comparison of per-channel versus per-group quantization, nor does it justify why a simple per-channel approach is preferable to a more sophisticated per-group method, especially considering the additional complexity it introduces. This lack of a thorough comparison makes it difficult to assess the true novelty and contribution of the proposed method. The paper also claims that the proposed method can adaptively choose between per-channel and per-output-channel quantization for each layer. However, the explanation of this adaptive mechanism is insufficient. The paper states that the method minimizes reconstruction error to decide between per-channel and per-output-channel quantization, but it does not clearly define the specific criteria or metrics used to make this decision. The paper lacks a detailed explanation of the adaptive mechanism, including the specific criteria or metrics used to determine when to switch between per-channel and per-output-channel quantization. Without this, the claim of adaptivity is unsubstantiated. Furthermore, the paper's experimental evaluation is limited in scope.
