## Summary

This paper introduces GOODRL, a novel graph-aided offline-online deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework designed to tackle the challenging problem of dynamic workflow scheduling (DWS) in cloud computing environments. The core contribution of this work lies in its innovative approach to effectively manage the inherent complexities of heterogeneous machines, unpredictable workflows, and constantly evolving workloads, which are common in cloud settings. The authors propose a task-specific graph representation and a Graph Attention Actor Network (GAT) for efficient task assignment, coupled with a system-oriented graph representation and a Graph Attention Critic Network (GAC) for managing complex interactions across workflows and machines. This dual-network architecture is further enhanced by an offline-online method that utilizes imitation learning for efficient offline training and gradient control and decoupled high-frequency critic training techniques for online learning. The empirical evaluation of GOODRL, conducted across various online and offline scenarios, demonstrates its superiority over several state-of-the-art DWS algorithms, showcasing significant improvements in mean flowtime. The ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of the proposed components, highlighting the importance of the task-specific and system-oriented graph representations, as well as the specific training techniques. The paper's significance lies in its potential to significantly impact real-world cloud systems by providing an efficient and adaptable workflow scheduling solution. By addressing the challenges of heterogeneous machines, unpredictable workflows, and constantly evolving workloads, GOODRL offers a promising approach to improving resource utilization, reducing task completion times, and enhancing the overall performance of cloud systems. The use of graph representations and deep reinforcement learning techniques to model and optimize the complex interactions between workflows and machines is a notable contribution to the field of cloud computing. The paper's comprehensive evaluation, including both offline and online scenarios, as well as ablation studies, provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the areas of computational complexity analysis, implementation details, and practical implications, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections.
