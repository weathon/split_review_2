## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to interpreting transformer models by applying sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to the attention layer outputs. The authors' core contribution lies in demonstrating that SAEs can effectively decompose these outputs into a set of sparse, interpretable features, which they then analyze to gain insights into model behavior. The methodology involves training SAEs on the concatenated z-vectors from each attention head within a given layer of a transformer model. This approach allows for the identification of distinct feature types, such as long-range context, short-range context, and induction features, which are then used to analyze model behavior. The authors apply their method to attention layer outputs across multiple models, including GPT-2 Small, and use the resulting features to identify polysemantic attention heads, which are attention heads that perform multiple tasks. They further demonstrate the utility of their approach by improving the understanding of the Indirect Object Identification (IOI) circuit in transformer models. The empirical findings reveal that attention heads often perform multiple tasks, highlighting the limitations of individual attention heads and suggesting the need for analysis at the level of attention layers. The paper also shows that attention output SAEs can be used to identify attention heads that are responsible for specific tasks, such as the polysemantic heads identified in the GPT-2 Small model. The authors also introduce Recursive Direct Feature Attribution (RDFA), a technique to trace model computations on arbitrary prompts. The overall significance of this work lies in its ability to provide a fine-grained analysis of transformer models, moving beyond the analysis of individual attention heads to a more holistic view of model computation. This approach offers a new way to understand how transformers process information and makes a valuable contribution to the field of mechanistic interpretability.

## Weaknesses
