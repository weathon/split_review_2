## Summary

This paper introduces a novel framework for iterative minimum-entropy coupling (MEC), a problem with significant relevance in information theory and related fields. The authors propose a unified perspective on existing MEC algorithms, specifically those of Sokota et al. (2022) and Sokota et al. (2023), framing them as special cases of a more general approach based on partition sets. This unification is achieved by introducing the concept of a prefix tree partition set, which allows for the iterative application of Minimum-Entropy Coupling (MEC) between a conditional distribution and the posterior over blocks of a partition associated with the other variable. Building upon this framework, the authors present ARIMEC, a new algorithm that extends the applicability of IMEC to arbitrary large-support distributions by eliminating the factorability assumption. This is achieved by using the prefix tree partition set, which allows for a more flexible and general approach to MEC. Furthermore, the paper introduces a merging technique to improve the robustness of IMEC algorithms, addressing potential issues arising from entropy waste during the coupling process. The authors evaluate their approach in two settings: Markov coding games and steganography. In Markov coding games, they demonstrate that ARIMEC achieves lower joint entropy compared to existing methods, while in steganography, they show that it achieves a lower error rate in decoding. The core contribution of this paper lies in its unifying framework for iterative MEC algorithms, the introduction of ARIMEC, and the empirical validation of the proposed approach in two distinct domains. The authors provide a detailed description of the algorithm and its implementation, along with a thorough discussion of the related work and the limitations of their approach. While the paper presents a compelling framework and a novel algorithm, it lacks a rigorous theoretical analysis of the convergence and optimality of the proposed algorithm, which is a significant limitation. Additionally, the experimental evaluation is limited in scope, and the analysis of the results is somewhat descriptive, which further weakens the overall impact of the paper. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of minimum-entropy coupling and provides a solid foundation for future research.
