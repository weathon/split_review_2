## Summary

This paper introduces a Randomized Linear Embedding (RLE) algorithm, which aims to efficiently compute low-rank approximations of large matrices. The core idea revolves around using a smaller Rademacher matrix and random signs to implicitly generate an embedding matrix, a technique that, while practically useful, the reviewers correctly point out, lacks significant novelty. The authors propose a specific implementation of RLE, detailing its steps and parameters, and then evaluate its performance on single-pass RSVD with streaming data and the randomized Arnoldi process. The theoretical analysis focuses on proving that the proposed RLE algorithm is an (ε,δ,d)-oblivious subspace embedding, which is a key property for ensuring the preservation of geometric properties of the data after embedding. However, the theoretical analysis is not comprehensive, as the proofs for several theorems are missing. The empirical evaluation, while demonstrating the algorithm's performance on the chosen tasks, is limited in scope, lacking experiments on other common machine learning tasks such as linear regression, classification, and clustering. The paper's main contribution lies in its practical implementation and empirical validation of the RLE algorithm, but it falls short in providing a thorough theoretical analysis and a comprehensive experimental evaluation. The authors acknowledge the existing literature on random embeddings and Rademacher matrices, but they do not sufficiently differentiate their approach from prior work, which further contributes to the perception of a lack of novelty. The paper's focus on a specific implementation and its evaluation on a limited set of tasks, without a clear justification for the choice of these tasks, makes it difficult to assess the general applicability and effectiveness of the proposed RLE algorithm. The absence of a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach and its potential drawbacks further weakens the paper's overall contribution. The paper's significance is thus limited by the lack of novelty, the incomplete theoretical analysis, and the narrow experimental evaluation, which collectively hinder a comprehensive understanding of the algorithm's strengths and weaknesses.

## Strengths

While the paper presents a practical implementation of a random embedding algorithm, its strengths are somewhat overshadowed by the identified weaknesses. The most notable strength lies in the practical implementation of the RLE algorithm. The authors have clearly described the steps involved in computing the embedding, including the use of a smaller Rademacher matrix and random signs. This practical approach is valuable for researchers and practitioners who are interested in applying random embeddings to large-scale data. The paper also provides a clear and concise description of the theoretical properties of the proposed algorithm, specifically that it is an (ε,δ,d)-oblivious subspace embedding. This theoretical result is important for ensuring that the geometric properties of the data are preserved after embedding, which is a crucial aspect of many machine learning tasks. The authors have also provided a proof for Theorem 2, which establishes the (ε,δ,d)-oblivious subspace embedding property of the RLE algorithm. This proof, while not comprehensive, does provide a theoretical foundation for the algorithm's performance. Furthermore, the paper includes a detailed description of the experimental setup and results for two specific tasks: single-pass RSVD with streaming data and the randomized Arnoldi process. These experiments provide some empirical evidence of the algorithm's performance in these specific scenarios. The authors also provide a comparison of their algorithm with other existing methods, which helps to contextualize the performance of the proposed RLE algorithm. The paper's clarity in presenting the algorithm's steps and the experimental results is also a strength. The authors have made an effort to explain the algorithm in a way that is easy to understand, which is important for the accessibility of the work. The paper also includes a discussion of the time complexity of the proposed algorithm, which is an important consideration for practical applications. The authors have also provided a detailed description of the parameters used in the algorithm, which is important for reproducibility. Finally, the paper's focus on a specific implementation of the RLE algorithm is a strength in itself, as it allows for a more focused analysis of the algorithm's performance and limitations. While the paper does not explore alternative implementations, its focus on a single, well-defined approach is a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses

The most significant weakness of this paper is the lack of novelty in the proposed RLE algorithm. As pointed out by multiple reviewers, the core idea of using a smaller Rademacher matrix and random signs to implicitly generate an embedding matrix has been explored in prior work. The paper itself acknowledges this, stating in the introduction that "the idea of using a smaller Rademacher matrix and several random signs to implicitly generate an embedding matrix has been proposed in previous work." This lack of novelty is a major concern, as it undermines the paper's contribution to the field. The authors do not adequately differentiate their approach from existing methods, failing to clearly articulate the unique aspects of their implementation. This lack of differentiation makes it difficult to assess the algorithm's true contribution. The paper's theoretical analysis is another significant weakness. While the authors provide a proof for Theorem 2, which establishes the (ε,δ,d)-oblivious subspace embedding property of the RLE algorithm, they fail to provide proofs for other theorems, specifically Theorems 3, 4, and 5. This omission makes it impossible to fully assess the theoretical properties of the algorithm, such as the preservation of vector norms and the orthogonality of vectors after embedding. The absence of these proofs is a major limitation, as it leaves several key aspects of the algorithm unverified. The experimental evaluation is also a major weakness. The authors only conduct experiments on two specific tasks: single-pass RSVD with streaming data and the randomized Arnoldi process. This limited scope makes it difficult to assess the general applicability and effectiveness of the proposed RLE algorithm.
