## Summary

This paper investigates the intriguing relationship between pruning masks and the top Hessian subspaces of neural networks, revealing a striking similarity that emerges early during training and persists throughout the learning process. The authors propose a novel method to quantify this similarity using Grassmannian metrics, which are well-suited for comparing subspaces. Their core finding is that the overlap between pruning masks, which indicate the importance of network parameters, and the top eigenspaces of the Hessian, which capture the curvature of the loss landscape, is significantly larger than what would be expected by chance. This suggests that large parameters in neural networks tend to align with directions of high loss curvature. The authors demonstrate this phenomenon through a series of experiments on a synthetic MLP model, showing that both pruning masks and Hessian subspaces exhibit early emergence and stabilization, and that this similarity is maintained throughout training. The paper's significance lies in its potential to deepen our understanding of how neural networks learn and how pruning can be used to optimize the training process. By linking pruning, a method for identifying and removing less important parameters, with the loss landscape, a fundamental aspect of optimization, the authors provide a new perspective on the interplay between network structure and curvature. This connection could lead to more efficient pruning strategies and a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms of neural network learning. The paper's approach of using Grassmannian metrics to quantify the similarity between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is a novel contribution that provides a new way to analyze the behavior of neural networks. The empirical results, while focused on a synthetic model, provide compelling evidence for the observed phenomena and suggest that these findings may generalize to more complex architectures. The authors' work opens up new avenues for research in the field of neural network optimization and pruning, and it has the potential to significantly impact the development of more efficient and effective deep learning models. The paper's findings are particularly relevant in the context of large-scale deep learning, where understanding the behavior of neural networks during training is crucial for developing efficient training algorithms and reducing computational costs. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by providing a new perspective on the relationship between pruning and the loss landscape, and by demonstrating the potential of Grassmannian metrics for analyzing the behavior of neural networks.

## Strengths

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall impact and significance. First and foremost, the paper introduces a novel and insightful perspective on the relationship between pruning masks and Hessian eigenspaces. By demonstrating that these two seemingly disparate concepts exhibit early emergence and stabilization during training, the authors open up a new avenue for understanding the dynamics of neural network learning. This connection is not only theoretically interesting but also has the potential to lead to practical advancements in neural network optimization. The use of Grassmannian metrics to quantify the similarity between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is another significant strength. This mathematical approach provides a rigorous and quantifiable way to analyze the behavior of neural networks, and it allows the authors to make precise claims about the overlap between these two structures. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling and provide strong evidence for the authors' claims. The experiments, while conducted on a synthetic MLP model, are well-designed and clearly demonstrate the early emergence and stabilization of both pruning masks and Hessian subspaces. The authors also show that the overlap between these two structures is significantly larger than random chance, which further supports their findings. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which makes it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain their methodology and provide sufficient background information to understand the context of their work. The use of visualizations, such as the plots of overlap and approximation accuracy, helps to convey the key findings of the paper in an intuitive way. The paper's focus on the practical implications of its findings is also a strength. While the authors do not delve into specific applications, they clearly articulate the potential of their work to improve model compression and optimization. This focus on practical relevance makes the paper more impactful and demonstrates the potential for real-world applications. Finally, the paper's contribution to the field is significant. By linking pruning and the loss landscape, the authors provide a new perspective on the behavior of neural networks during training. This work has the potential to inspire further research in this area and to lead to the development of more efficient and effective deep learning models. The paper's findings are particularly relevant in the context of large-scale deep learning, where understanding the behavior of neural networks during training is crucial for developing efficient training algorithms and reducing computational costs.

## Weaknesses

Despite its strengths, this paper exhibits several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. The most significant limitation, and one that is consistently raised by multiple reviewers, is the lack of a theoretical explanation for the observed early emergence and stabilization of pruning masks and Hessian subspaces. While the authors demonstrate that these structures exhibit this behavior, they do not delve into the underlying mechanisms that cause this phenomenon. This absence of a theoretical framework leaves a significant gap in our understanding of the observed behavior. The paper focuses on the empirical observation and mathematical formulation, but lacks a theoretical analysis of *why* this early emergence and stabilization occur. This is a critical weakness because it limits our ability to generalize the findings to different architectures and datasets. The paper also fails to explore the potential applications of the proposed method in model compression or optimization. While the authors propose using pruning masks to approximate Hessian eigenspaces, they do not demonstrate how this approximation can be used to improve model performance or reduce computational cost. The experiments focus on evaluating the overlap and approximation accuracy, but do not show how this approximation can be used to achieve better pruning or faster convergence. This lack of practical application limits the impact of the paper and raises questions about the usefulness of the proposed method in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the approximation quality of using pruning masks to estimate the Hessian eigenspaces. While the authors demonstrate that the overlap between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is significantly larger than random chance, they do not quantify the approximation error using standard metrics such as the cosine similarity between the eigenvectors or the Frobenius norm of the difference between the eigenspaces. This lack of a detailed analysis of the approximation quality makes it difficult to assess the practical utility of the proposed method. The authors also do not investigate how the approximation error changes during training or how it varies with different pruning strategies and network architectures. This is a significant oversight because it limits our understanding of the limitations of the proposed method and when it might fail. Finally, the paper does not explore the implications of the observed similarity between pruning masks and top Hessian subspaces for optimization and generalization. The authors do not investigate whether this similarity can be leveraged to improve training efficiency or model generalization. They do not explore whether the pruning masks can be used to guide the optimization process or to select better initializations. This lack of investigation into the practical implications of the observed similarity limits the paper's overall impact and raises questions about the potential of the proposed method to address real-world challenges. The paper's focus on a synthetic MLP model also raises concerns about the generalizability of the findings. While the authors demonstrate the phenomenon on this model, it is unclear whether these results would hold for more complex architectures and datasets. This limitation is significant because it raises questions about the practical applicability of the proposed method in real-world scenarios. ## Summary:

This paper investigates the intriguing relationship between pruning masks and the top Hessian subspaces of neural networks, revealing a striking similarity that emerges early during training and persists throughout the learning process. The authors propose a novel method to quantify this similarity using Grassmannian metrics, which are well-suited for comparing subspaces. Their core finding is that the overlap between pruning masks, which indicate the importance of network parameters, and the top eigenspaces of the Hessian, which capture the curvature of the loss landscape, is significantly larger than what would be expected by chance. This suggests that large parameters in neural networks tend to align with directions of high loss curvature. The authors demonstrate this phenomenon through a series of experiments on a synthetic MLP model, showing that both pruning masks and Hessian subspaces exhibit early emergence and stabilization, and that this similarity is maintained throughout training. The paper's significance lies in its potential to deepen our understanding of how neural networks learn and how pruning can be used to optimize the training process. By linking pruning, a method for identifying and removing less important parameters, with the loss landscape, a fundamental aspect of optimization, the authors provide a new perspective on the interplay between network structure and curvature. This connection could lead to more efficient pruning strategies and a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms of neural network learning. The paper's approach of using Grassmannian metrics to quantify the similarity between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is a novel contribution that provides a new way to analyze the behavior of neural networks. The empirical results, while focused on a synthetic model, provide compelling evidence for the observed phenomena and suggest that these findings may generalize to more complex architectures. The authors' work opens up new avenues for research in the field of neural network optimization and pruning, and it has the potential to significantly impact the development of more efficient and effective deep learning models. The paper's findings are particularly relevant in the context of large-scale deep learning, where understanding the behavior of neural networks during training is crucial for developing efficient training algorithms and reducing computational costs. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by providing a new perspective on the relationship between pruning and the loss landscape, and by demonstrating the potential of Grassmannian metrics for analyzing the behavior of neural networks.

## Strengths:

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall impact and significance. First and foremost, the paper introduces a novel and insightful perspective on the relationship between pruning masks and Hessian eigenspaces. By demonstrating that these two seemingly disparate concepts exhibit early emergence and stabilization during training, the authors open up a new avenue for understanding the dynamics of neural network learning. This connection is not only theoretically interesting but also has the potential to lead to practical advancements in neural network optimization. The use of Grassmannian metrics to quantify the similarity between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is another significant strength. This mathematical approach provides a rigorous and quantifiable way to analyze the behavior of neural networks, and it allows the authors to make precise claims about the overlap between these two structures. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling and provide strong evidence for the authors' claims. The experiments, while conducted on a synthetic MLP model, are well-designed and clearly demonstrate the early emergence and stabilization of both pruning masks and Hessian subspaces. The authors also show that the overlap between these two structures is significantly larger than random chance, which further supports their findings. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which makes it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain their methodology and provide sufficient background information to understand the context of their work. The use of visualizations, such as the plots of overlap and approximation accuracy, helps to convey the key findings of the paper in an intuitive way. The paper's focus on the practical implications of its findings is also a strength. While the authors do not delve into specific applications, they clearly articulate the potential of their work to improve model compression and optimization. This focus on practical relevance makes the paper more impactful and demonstrates the potential for real-world applications. Finally, the paper's contribution to the field is significant. By linking pruning and the loss landscape, the authors provide a new perspective on the behavior of neural networks during training. This work has the potential to inspire further research in this area and to lead to the development of more efficient and effective deep learning models. The paper's findings are particularly relevant in the context of large-scale deep learning, where understanding the behavior of neural networks during training is crucial for developing efficient training algorithms and reducing computational costs.


## Weaknesses:

Despite its strengths, this paper exhibits several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. The most significant limitation, and one that is consistently raised by multiple reviewers, is the lack of a theoretical explanation for the observed early emergence and stabilization of pruning masks and Hessian subspaces. While the authors demonstrate that these structures exhibit this behavior, they do not delve into the underlying mechanisms that cause this phenomenon. This absence of a theoretical framework leaves a significant gap in our understanding of the observed behavior. The paper focuses on the empirical observation and mathematical formulation, but lacks a theoretical analysis of *why* this early emergence and stabilization occur. This is a critical weakness because it limits our ability to generalize the findings to different architectures and datasets. The paper also fails to explore the potential applications of the proposed method in model compression or optimization. While the authors propose using pruning masks to approximate Hessian eigenspaces, they do not demonstrate how this approximation can be used to improve model performance or reduce computational cost. The experiments focus on evaluating the overlap and approximation accuracy, but do not show how this approximation can be used to achieve better pruning or faster convergence. This lack of practical application limits the impact of the paper and raises questions about the usefulness of the proposed method in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the approximation quality of using pruning masks to estimate the Hessian eigenspaces. While the authors demonstrate that the overlap between pruning masks and Hessian subspaces is significantly larger than random chance, they do not quantify the approximation error using standard metrics such as the cosine similarity between the eigenvectors or the Frobenius norm of the difference between the eigenspaces. This lack of a detailed analysis of the approximation quality makes it difficult to assess the practical utility of the proposed method. The authors also do not investigate how the approximation error changes during training or how it varies with different pruning strategies and network architectures. This is a significant oversight because it limits our understanding of the limitations of the proposed method and when it might fail. Finally, the paper does not explore the implications of the observed similarity between pruning masks and top Hessian subspaces for optimization and generalization. The authors do not investigate whether this similarity can be leveraged to improve training efficiency or model generalization. They do not explore whether the pruning masks can be used to guide the optimization process or to select better initializations. This lack of investigation into the practical implications of the observed similarity limits the paper's overall impact and raises questions about the potential of the proposed method to address real-world challenges. The paper's focus on a synthetic MLP model also raises concerns about the generalizability of the findings.
