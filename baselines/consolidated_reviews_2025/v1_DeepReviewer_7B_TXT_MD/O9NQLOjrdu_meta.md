## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to zero-shot image classification by leveraging the power of multimodal large language models (LLMs). The core idea revolves around generating textual descriptions of input images using a multimodal LLM, which are then combined with initial class predictions and image features to enhance classification accuracy. The authors propose a straightforward yet effective method that requires no dataset-specific tuning, making it easily adaptable to various image classification tasks. The method begins by encoding the input image and text prompts into a shared feature space. Then, the LLM is prompted to generate textual descriptions of the input image, which are combined with the initial class predictions and image features. These three sets of features are then fused using a weighted sum approach, and the resulting feature vector is used to classify the image. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through extensive experiments on multiple datasets, showing significant improvements over existing zero-shot classification methods. The experimental results indicate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, highlighting the potential of using multimodal LLMs for zero-shot image classification. The simplicity of the approach, combined with its strong empirical performance, makes it a valuable contribution to the field. The paper also includes an ablation study to analyze the impact of different feature combinations, providing insights into the contribution of each component. The authors also provide a detailed description of the computational resources required for their method, including the time taken for encoding and the memory requirements for using the LLM. Overall, this paper presents a well-motivated, simple, and effective method for zero-shot image classification that leverages the power of multimodal LLMs, and it demonstrates the potential of this approach through comprehensive experimental validation. The paper is well-written and organized, making it easy for readers to follow the methodology and results. The authors also provide a clear explanation of the experimental setup and the evaluation metrics used, which further enhances the reproducibility of their work. The paper's findings suggest that multimodal LLMs can be a valuable tool for zero-shot image classification, and the proposed method provides a practical and effective way to leverage these models for this task.
