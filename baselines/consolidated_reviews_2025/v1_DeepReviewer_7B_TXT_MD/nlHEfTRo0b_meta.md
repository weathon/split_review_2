## Summary

This paper introduces PIDO, a novel physics-informed deep learning solver designed to address the challenges of solving partial differential equations (PDEs) with varying initial conditions, PDE coefficients, and training time horizons. The core innovation of PIDO lies in its approach to learning shared latent representations of solutions, which are then used to model dynamics within a latent space. This is achieved through a combination of an auto-decoder to map solutions into a low-dimensional latent space and a neural ordinary differential equation (ODE) to model the temporal evolution within this latent space. The authors identify two key challenges in physics-informed loss: instability during training and degradation in time extrapolation. To address these issues, they propose two regularization techniques: latent dynamics smoothing and latent dynamics alignment. Latent dynamics smoothing aims to stabilize the latent space dynamics by penalizing rapid changes in the latent variables, while latent dynamics alignment ensures that the latent space representation is consistent across different time steps. The authors evaluate PIDO on a range of benchmark problems, including 1D and 2D combined equations and the Navier-Stokes equations, demonstrating improved performance compared to existing methods. The empirical results show that PIDO exhibits improved training stability and extrapolation performance, as well as enhanced transferability to downstream tasks such as long-term integration and inverse problems. The paper's main contribution is the novel approach of learning shared latent representations of PDE solutions, which allows the model to generalize better across different initial conditions, PDE coefficients, and training time horizons. This is achieved through a combination of physics-informed training in the latent space and the introduction of regularization techniques to improve training stability and extrapolation performance. The authors also demonstrate the transferability of the learned latent representations to downstream tasks, highlighting the robustness and adaptability of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and innovative approach to solving PDEs with deep learning, offering a promising alternative to traditional numerical methods.

## Strengths

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core idea of learning shared latent representations of PDE solutions is both novel and well-motivated. By mapping solutions into a low-dimensional latent space, the authors enable the model to capture the underlying dynamics of the system in a more compact and interpretable manner. This approach allows the model to generalize better across different initial conditions, PDE coefficients, and training time horizons, which is a significant improvement over traditional numerical solvers and existing neural PDE solvers. The introduction of latent dynamics smoothing and latent dynamics alignment regularization techniques is another key strength of this work. These techniques address the challenges of training instability and degradation in time extrapolation, which are common issues in physics-informed neural networks. The latent dynamics smoothing regularization helps to stabilize the latent space dynamics by penalizing rapid changes in the latent variables, while the latent dynamics alignment regularization ensures that the latent space representation is consistent across different time steps. These regularization techniques are crucial for achieving the reported performance improvements. The experimental results presented in the paper are comprehensive and demonstrate the effectiveness of PIDO across a range of benchmark problems. The authors compare PIDO with state-of-the-art methods, including PINN, DINO, and MAD, and show that PIDO achieves improved performance in terms of both accuracy and training stability. The results on 1D and 2D combined equations and the Navier-Stokes equations are particularly compelling, as they demonstrate the method's ability to handle complex PDEs. Furthermore, the paper explores the transferability of PIDO's learned representations to downstream tasks, such as long-term integration and inverse problems, which highlights the robustness and adaptability of the proposed method. The authors clearly articulate the problem, methodology, and results, making the paper easy to follow and understand. The motivation for addressing the limitations of existing methods is well-articulated, and the proposed solutions are presented in a logical and understandable manner. The paper's structure is well-organized, making it easy to follow the authors' line of reasoning and appreciate the significance of their contributions. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of physics-informed neural networks for solving PDEs, offering a novel and effective approach to address the challenges of generalization and stability.
