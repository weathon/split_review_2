## Summary

This paper tackles the increasingly relevant challenge of coexisting multiple deep image watermarking methods within a single image, a problem that arises as digital media grows and the need for diverse data encoding increases. The authors empirically investigate the coexistence of eight different watermarking methods, demonstrating that these methods can indeed be embedded simultaneously without mutual interference. This finding is significant because it allows for the encoding of multiple watermarks within a single image, potentially unlocking new trade-offs between capacity, accuracy, robustness, and image quality. The core methodology involves applying existing watermarking techniques sequentially or in parallel, and then evaluating the resulting watermarked image using a combination of capacity, accuracy, robustness, and image quality metrics. The authors also propose an ensembling technique to enhance overall message capacity and enable new trade-offs between these key factors. This ensembling approach involves averaging the watermarks applied sequentially or applying error-correcting codes to improve the robustness of the combined watermark. The paper's main empirical finding is that different deep image watermarking methods can coexist within the same image without significant mutual interference, and that ensembling these methods can lead to improved overall performance. The authors show that while some methods offer higher accuracy, robustness, and image quality individually, ensembling allows for a combination of these advantages, leading to a more balanced performance. The paper also explores the impact of strength clipping and error-correcting codes on the performance of the ensembled watermarks. Overall, this work provides a valuable empirical contribution to the field of digital watermarking, highlighting the potential for coexisting multiple watermarking methods within a single image and demonstrating the effectiveness of ensembling as a means to improve overall performance. However, the paper lacks a theoretical framework to explain the observed phenomena and does not provide a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed methods in real-world scenarios, which limits the generalizability and practical applicability of the findings.
