### Summary

This paper proposes a new method for molecular graph generation. The main idea is to generate a coarse-grained representation of the molecular graph first, and then generate the atom and bond types given the coarse-grained representation. The coarse-grained representation is generated by a VAE, and the atom and bond types are generated by a transformer. The experiments show that the proposed method can generate more diverse molecules than previous methods.

### Suggestions

The paper introduces an interesting approach to molecular graph generation by generating a coarse-grained representation first, which is then refined to generate atom and bond types. However, the lack of comparison with several state-of-the-art methods significantly weakens the evaluation of the proposed method's performance. Specifically, methods like GeoMol, DiGress, and MolDiff, which employ geometric representations and graph diffusion, respectively, should be included as baselines. Furthermore, the absence of comparisons with shape-based methods like Shape2Vec and motif-based methods like MolDiff and MoLeR leaves a gap in the evaluation. These methods explore different aspects of molecular generation, and a comprehensive comparison is necessary to understand the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed approach. The authors should also consider comparing with other graph-based methods like GraphAF and other shape generation methods like JT-VAE and MiCaM to provide a more complete picture of the method's performance relative to the existing literature.

To strengthen the evaluation, the authors should consider a more detailed analysis of the generated molecules. For example, they could investigate the diversity of the generated molecules in terms of their chemical properties, such as the number of unique atoms, bond types, and functional groups. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of their method compared to the baselines. This would help to understand the trade-offs between the performance and the computational resources required. Furthermore, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the hyperparameter selection process and the sensitivity of the results to different hyperparameter settings. This would help to ensure the reproducibility of the results and to understand the robustness of the method.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the limitations of their method and the potential directions for future research. For example, they could discuss the limitations of the coarse-grained representation and how it might affect the quality of the generated molecules. They could also discuss the potential for extending their method to handle more complex molecules or to incorporate additional information, such as quantum mechanical properties. Addressing these limitations and suggesting future research directions would further enhance the impact of the paper and provide a more complete understanding of the proposed method.
