## Summary

This paper investigates the presence of racial and gender biases in Stable Diffusion, a widely used text-to-image generative model. The authors employ a multi-faceted approach, first developing a classifier to quantify the extent to which Stable Diffusion generates images that reflect these biases across six races, two genders, 32 professions, and eight attributes. The classifier is trained using existing datasets and the embeddings from Stable Diffusion, allowing for efficient inference. The study reveals a significant bias towards generating white, male individuals, often depicting them in stereotypical roles. To address these biases, the authors propose two fine-tuning strategies: SDXL-Inc, which aims to reduce the over-representation of white, male individuals, and SDXL-Div, which encourages facial diversity. SDXL-Inc is achieved by fine-tuning the model using a subset of prompts from the SDXL-Inc fine-tuning dataset and the Flickr-Faces-HQ dataset, while SDXL-Div is created using a similar approach. The authors then conduct a user study to evaluate the impact of exposure to these fine-tuned models, demonstrating that exposure to inclusive AI-generated faces reduces racial and gender biases, while exposure to non-inclusive faces increases them. The core methodology involves training a classifier to predict race and gender from generated images, followed by fine-tuning Stable Diffusion using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The user study provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed solutions in mitigating biases. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of addressing biases in AI-generated content and offers practical solutions to improve the fairness and representativeness of AI-generated images. The study's significance lies in its timely investigation of a critical issue in the field of AI-generated content, providing both a diagnostic tool and a means to mitigate the identified biases.
