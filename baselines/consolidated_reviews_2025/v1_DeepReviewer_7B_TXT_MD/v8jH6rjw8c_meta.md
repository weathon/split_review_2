## Summary

This paper introduces a fairness regularizer designed to enhance the performance of sub-populations in the context of noisy and long-tailed data. The authors propose a method that aims to mitigate the performance disparities often observed in such datasets by explicitly encouraging fairness across different sub-populations. The core idea revolves around incorporating a fairness regularizer into the training objective, which penalizes the model for exhibiting biased performance across these sub-populations. The method is evaluated on several datasets, including CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, both in synthetic and real-world noisy long-tailed settings, as well as on the Clothing1M dataset. The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed fairness regularizer can improve the performance of tail populations, which are often underrepresented and misclassified in standard training scenarios. The authors analyze the impact of the proposed regularizer on model performance, showing that it can lead to more balanced results across different sub-populations. The paper's main contribution lies in the application and analysis of a fairness regularizer within the context of noisy and long-tailed data, rather than in the introduction of a novel regularization technique itself. While the empirical findings are promising, the paper lacks a strong theoretical foundation for the proposed method, and the experimental evaluation could be more comprehensive. The authors demonstrate that their approach can improve performance on tail populations, which is a significant step towards addressing the challenges of training models on imbalanced and noisy data. However, the paper's impact is limited by the absence of a theoretical analysis and comparisons with state-of-the-art fairness-aware learning methods. The paper's focus is on the empirical effectiveness of the proposed regularizer, and while this is a valuable contribution, it would be strengthened by a more rigorous theoretical treatment and a more comprehensive experimental evaluation. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed fairness regularizer can be a useful tool for improving the performance of models on sub-populations, particularly in the challenging setting of noisy and long-tailed data. However, the lack of theoretical justification and limited comparisons with existing methods raise questions about the generalizability and relative performance of the proposed approach. The paper's overall significance is therefore somewhat limited by these shortcomings, despite the promising empirical results.
