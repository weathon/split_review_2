### Summary

This paper introduces a new EEG dataset focused on semantic text relevance, collected from 15 participants who read Wikipedia documents. The dataset captures time-locked EEG responses to words, enabling the prediction of relevance at both word and sentence levels. The authors present benchmark experiments with five machine learning models, providing a foundation for future research in language relevance and psychophysiological studies.

### Suggestions

The authors should consider expanding the dataset to include multiple participants with diverse demographic characteristics, such as age, gender, and reading experience. This would help to assess the generalizability of the findings across different populations. Furthermore, the inclusion of a larger number of participants would increase the statistical power of the analyses, allowing for more robust conclusions to be drawn from the benchmark experiments. The authors should also explore the use of techniques such as cross-validation to further validate the results and ensure their reliability. Additionally, the authors should consider the potential impact of different reading speeds and text complexity on the EEG signals, as these factors could influence the relevance scores.

To address the ethical concerns, the authors should provide a detailed description of the consent process, including how participants were informed about the purpose of the study, how their data would be used, and how their privacy would be protected. The authors should also discuss the potential risks associated with the use of EEG technology and how these risks were mitigated. Furthermore, the authors should consider the potential impact of their research on individuals who may be vulnerable to the use of EEG technology, such as those with cognitive impairments or those who are susceptible to anxiety or stress.
