## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to reinforcement learning (RL) with submodular rewards, a problem that is particularly relevant in scenarios where the goal is to maximize the diversity or coverage of the explored state space. The core contribution of the paper is the development of a submodular policy gradient (SubPO) algorithm, which is a policy gradient-based method adapted for submodular reward functions. The authors begin by establishing the inapproximability of the submodular RL problem, demonstrating that it is NP-hard to approximate within a logarithmic factor. This theoretical result sets the stage for the introduction of the SubPO algorithm, which leverages the gradient of the marginal gain of the submodular reward function to update the policy parameters. The algorithm is designed to be flexible, capable of handling both Markovian and non-Markovian policies, and the authors employ importance sampling and entropy regularization to ensure stable and efficient learning. The empirical evaluation of SubPO is conducted across a range of tasks, including coverage, informative path planning, and Bayesian experimental design, where the algorithm is shown to outperform a modular RL baseline. The paper also explores the relationship between SubPO and standard policy gradient methods, demonstrating that SubPO recovers the standard policy gradient algorithm in the case of additive rewards. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the algorithm's behavior, focusing on the inapproximability result and the convergence properties of the algorithm. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field by addressing the challenging problem of RL with submodular rewards, offering a practical algorithm, and providing theoretical insights into the problem's complexity. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the depth of its theoretical analysis and the scope of its experimental evaluation, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections.
