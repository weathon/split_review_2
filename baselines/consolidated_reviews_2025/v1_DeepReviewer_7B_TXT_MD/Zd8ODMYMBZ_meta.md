## Summary

This paper introduces FAVICOMP, a novel training-free method designed to enhance retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems by making retrieved evidence more familiar to the target model. The core idea behind FAVICOMP is to leverage ensemble decoding, combining the probability distributions from both the compression model and the target model to produce a more aligned output. This alignment is achieved by adjusting the target model's perplexity based on the compressed evidence, effectively integrating both the compressed and original evidence into the final output. The method is presented as a way to improve the performance of RAG systems, particularly in complex tasks where retrieved evidence may be incomplete or noisy. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of FAVICOMP through comprehensive experiments on several open-domain QA datasets, showing that it outperforms existing evidence compression baselines. The experimental results indicate that FAVICOMP achieves up to 23.91% accuracy improvement while maintaining high compression rates, making it a practical solution for reducing the computational cost of RAG systems without sacrificing accuracy. The paper's primary contribution lies in its innovative use of ensemble decoding to integrate compressed evidence with the target model, addressing a key challenge in RAG systems where retrieved evidence may not be fully aligned with the target model's knowledge. The method is presented as a training-free approach, which enhances its applicability in resource-constrained environments. The authors also explore the impact of ensemble coefficients on performance, finding that a balanced approach, where both input sources are equally weighted, yields the best results. This work is significant because it offers a practical and effective way to improve the performance of RAG systems, particularly in scenarios where retrieved evidence is incomplete or noisy, and it does so without requiring additional training. The method's ability to maintain high compression rates while achieving substantial accuracy improvements makes it a valuable contribution to the field of RAG systems.
