### Summary

This paper introduces a novel method called Probability Distribution Estimation (PDE) to enable white-box detection methods to work with proprietary large language models (LLMs). The key idea is to estimate the full probability distribution from partial observations provided by the model API, allowing existing white-box detection techniques to be applied to proprietary models. The authors demonstrate that PDE significantly improves detection accuracy across five different proprietary LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT) and seven languages. The results show that PDE outperforms existing black-box methods and achieves near-perfect detection accuracy on some models, particularly the latest LLMs like GPT-4 and Gemini. The paper also explores the robustness of PDE across different sources and domains, and shows that it consistently provides the highest accuracy. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the results, including ablation studies and comparisons with existing methods, and discuss the potential of PDE for real-world applications.

This is a significant limitation, as it restricts the applicability of the method to scenarios where the detection model has prior knowledge of the potential source models. The paper should acknowledge this limitation and discuss potential solutions for addressing it.

### Suggestions

The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of PDE, including the time and memory requirements for different model sizes and sequence lengths. This analysis should also consider the impact of different estimation algorithms on the computational cost. For example, the authors could compare the performance of PDE using different estimation methods, such as geometric, Zipfian, and MLP models, and discuss the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost. Furthermore, the authors should explore potential optimizations to reduce the computational cost of PDE, such as using more efficient estimation algorithms or parallelizing the computation. This would make the method more practical for real-world applications.

To address the potential for adversarial attacks, the authors should investigate scenarios where an attacker might manipulate the partial observations provided by the model API to deceive the PDE estimation process. For example, the authors could explore the impact of injecting specific tokens or sequences into the partial observations to bias the estimation. The authors should also discuss potential defense mechanisms that could be used to mitigate the impact of adversarial attacks, such as using robust estimation algorithms or incorporating additional information about the model's behavior. This would make the method more robust and reliable in real-world scenarios.

Finally, the authors should discuss the limitations of PDE in detecting text generated by models that are not included in the training data of the detection model.
