## Summary

This paper introduces D2T2, a novel approach designed to enhance the performance of Decision Transformers (DTs) in stochastic environments. The authors identify a key limitation of DTs: their reliance on the growing variance of returns-to-go (RTG) signals, which accumulate over time, leading to suboptimal decision-making in stochastic settings. To address this, D2T2 incorporates a learned steering guidance mechanism derived from temporal difference (TD) learning. This guidance signal, learned through a causal transformer, provides a more stable and reliable signal for DTs to follow, effectively reducing the variance of RTG signals. The core idea is to leverage the strengths of TD learning to provide a more stable guidance signal for the DT, thereby improving its performance in stochastic environments. The method is presented as an extension of the original DT framework, aiming to address the variance issue without requiring the RTG during evaluation, which is a significant practical advantage. The authors evaluate D2T2 on a variety of stochastic tasks, including the stochastic FrozenLake environment and the stochastic NoCrash Benchmark, as well as D4RL datasets, demonstrating that D2T2 outperforms state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning methods. The experimental results show that D2T2 achieves higher success rates and better performance in stochastic settings compared to the original DT and other baselines. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of the performance degradation of DTs in stochastic environments, which serves as the motivation for the proposed method. The authors argue that the accumulation of variance in RTG signals is the primary cause of this degradation, and they use this analysis to justify their approach of incorporating TD learning-based guidance. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and effective method for improving the robustness of DTs in stochastic environments, supported by both theoretical analysis and empirical evidence. The proposed method is simple yet effective, providing a valuable contribution to the field of reinforcement learning.
