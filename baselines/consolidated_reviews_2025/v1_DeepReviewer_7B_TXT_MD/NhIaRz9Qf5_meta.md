## Summary

This paper introduces Self-Aware Knowledge Retrieval (SEARLE), a novel adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) model designed to dynamically determine when and how to retrieve external knowledge. The core idea behind SEARLE is to leverage the internal states of a Large Language Model (LLM) to assess its own uncertainty. Specifically, the model uses the Gram determinant of hidden representations to quantify the LLM's internal uncertainty, which then triggers the knowledge retrieval process. This uncertainty measure is used not only to decide when to retrieve knowledge but also to re-rank the retrieved knowledge based on its reduction of the LLM's uncertainty. The authors argue that this self-aware approach to knowledge retrieval is a more effective strategy than relying on fixed or pre-defined retrieval mechanisms. The method involves a search engine that retrieves knowledge based on the LLM's uncertainty, a large language model that generates content, and a self-aware uncertainty estimator that measures the uncertainty of the LLM. The paper presents experimental results on complex and simple question-answering datasets, demonstrating that SEARLE outperforms existing adaptive RAG methods. The authors use a BERT-based sentence similarity model to determine the relevance of retrieved knowledge and employ a multi-hop reasoning strategy to combine multiple retrieved pieces of knowledge. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel approach to adaptive retrieval, which uses the LLM's internal states to guide the retrieval process, and its empirical demonstration of the effectiveness of this approach. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with the Gram determinant calculation, which is a key component of the SEARLE method. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and innovative approach to adaptive RAG, with promising empirical results.
