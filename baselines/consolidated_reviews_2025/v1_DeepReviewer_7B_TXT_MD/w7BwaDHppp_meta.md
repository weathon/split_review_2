## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to neural radiance field (NeRF) rendering, specifically targeting the challenges of unbounded scenes. The core contribution lies in the development of a geometrically aware mapping function, based on a p-norm distance, which adaptively samples rays based on scene geometry, allowing for a more efficient use of the neural network's capacity. This mapping function aims to address the limitations of existing methods that often struggle with distant objects in unbounded environments. Additionally, the authors propose a new ray parameterization technique that considers the distortion of the embedding space, ensuring more even sampling across different regions. This approach is evaluated on several datasets, including 360° object-centric scenes and free trajectory datasets, demonstrating state-of-the-art results compared to existing methods. The method leverages a point cloud for p-value estimation using RANSAC, which is then used to define an embedding space where rays are parameterized based on their angular distance from the center of projection. The experimental results show that the proposed method achieves superior performance in novel view synthesis, particularly in scenarios with distant objects and complex unbounded scenes. The authors also provide an ablation study to validate the contribution of each component of their method. While the paper presents a compelling approach to unbounded NeRF rendering, my analysis has revealed several areas where further investigation and refinement are needed. Specifically, the lack of a detailed computational analysis and the limited scope of the experimental comparison are significant concerns that need to be addressed to fully understand the practical applicability and limitations of the proposed method. Despite these limitations, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by introducing a novel approach to handling unbounded scenes in NeRF rendering, which has the potential to advance the state-of-the-art in this area.

## Strengths

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core idea of using a p-norm-based mapping function to adaptively sample rays in unbounded scenes is both novel and technically sound. This approach directly addresses a key limitation of existing NeRF methods, which often struggle to effectively capture distant objects. The authors' analysis of existing mapping functions, particularly their identification of limitations in handling unbounded scenes, provides a solid foundation for their proposed method. The introduction of a ray parameterization technique that considers the distortion of the embedding space is another significant contribution. This approach ensures a more even sampling of the scene, which is crucial for accurate rendering, especially in regions with varying levels of detail. The experimental results presented in the paper are compelling, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on challenging unbounded scene datasets. The authors have clearly shown that their method outperforms existing techniques, which provides strong empirical support for their claims. The ablation study, while not fully comprehensive, does provide some evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed mapping function and ray parameterization. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, with a clear presentation of the proposed method and its rationale. The authors provide a thorough explanation of their approach, making it accessible to a wide audience. Overall, the paper presents a technically sound and empirically validated approach to handling unbounded scenes in NeRF rendering, which has the potential to advance the state-of-the-art in this area. The combination of a novel mapping function, a ray parameterization technique, and strong experimental results makes this paper a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, my analysis has identified several significant weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. The most prominent concern is the lack of a detailed computational analysis of the proposed method. Specifically, the adaptive sampling strategy based on the p-norm distance and the angular ray parameterization technique, while effective, may introduce significant computational overhead. The paper does not provide a formal time complexity analysis of these components, nor does it offer empirical runtime comparisons with existing methods. This is a critical omission, as the practical applicability of the method, especially for real-time applications, depends on its computational efficiency. The absence of this analysis makes it difficult to assess the trade-offs between rendering quality and computational cost. My analysis of the method description confirms that the p-norm calculation and the angular parameterization involve additional computations compared to existing methods, and the paper does not provide any evidence to show that these computations are efficient. Furthermore, the paper relies on a RANSAC-based approach to estimate the p-value using a point cloud. While this method is described, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of its sensitivity to the accuracy of the p-value estimation. The paper mentions repeating the process for a given number of iterations, but it does not specify the impact of varying this number or the number of RANSAC iterations. This lack of analysis is a significant concern, as the accuracy of the p-value estimation directly affects the adaptive sampling strategy. If the p-value is not accurately estimated, the method may not perform as intended, leading to suboptimal results. My analysis of the method description confirms that the p-value estimation is a crucial component of the method, and the paper does not provide any evidence to show that it is robust to errors in the p-value estimation. Another significant weakness is the limited scope of the experimental comparison. While the paper demonstrates state-of-the-art results on several datasets, it does not include a comprehensive comparison with other state-of-the-art methods for unbounded scene rendering. The paper primarily compares against a few methods, such as inverted sphere parameterization and F2-NeRF, but it does not include comparisons with methods that employ learned mapping functions or those that use a combination of local and global representations. This omission makes it difficult to fully contextualize the contributions of the proposed method and to understand its strengths and weaknesses compared to other approaches. My analysis of the experimental section confirms that the comparison is limited, and the paper does not provide any evidence to show that it is competitive with other state-of-the-art methods. Finally, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the conclusion briefly mentions some limitations, a more comprehensive discussion in the main body of the paper would provide a more balanced perspective. The paper does not discuss the limitations of the method in handling scenes with complex occlusions or highly dynamic lighting conditions, nor does it discuss the sensitivity of the method to the choice of parameters, such as the p-norm parameter and the ray parameterization parameters. This omission is a significant concern, as it limits the reader's understanding of the practical applicability of the method. My analysis of the paper confirms that the limitations are not discussed in detail, and the paper does not provide any evidence to show that it is robust to different types of scenes and conditions. These weaknesses, taken together, significantly limit my confidence in the practical applicability and generalizability of the proposed method.
