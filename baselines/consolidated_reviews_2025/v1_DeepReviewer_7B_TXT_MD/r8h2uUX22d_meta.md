## Summary

This paper introduces a novel interpretation of the MLP-Mixer architecture, positioning it as a wide Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with Kronecker product weights, which can be further related to a sparse MLP with a hidden layer size of SC. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the implicit regularization of the MLP-Mixer, particularly in the context of linear activations, and conduct experiments to demonstrate the similarity in performance and implicit regularization between the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs. The core contributions of the paper include a new perspective on the MLP-Mixer, a theoretical framework for understanding its implicit regularization, and empirical validation of the proposed interpretation. However, the paper's claims are not fully supported by rigorous quantitative comparisons, and the analysis of implicit regularization and sparsity is limited, which affects the robustness of the empirical findings. Despite these limitations, the paper offers valuable insights into the properties of MLP-Mixers and their relationship to sparse models, which could be significant for future research and practical applications.

## Strengths

I found several strengths in this paper that contribute to its value and novelty. Firstly, the paper provides a novel interpretation of the MLP-Mixer architecture, showing that it can be viewed as a wide MLP with Kronecker product weights. This interpretation is well-supported by the mathematical derivations in Section 3.1, where the authors clearly explain the vectorization operation and the structure of the MLP-Mixer. The authors also draw a connection between the MLP-Mixer and sparse MLPs, which is a significant contribution to the field. The theoretical analysis of the implicit regularization of the MLP-Mixer, particularly in the context of linear activations, is another strength. The authors use the concept of centered kernel alignment (CKA) to compare the hidden features of the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs, providing a quantitative measure of their similarity. This analysis is supported by the experimental results in Tables 1 and 2, which show that the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs perform similarly on several datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and ImageNet-1k. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear structure that facilitates understanding of the complex concepts. The authors also provide a detailed explanation of the experimental setup, which is crucial for reproducibility. Overall, the paper's strengths lie in its innovative interpretation of the MLP-Mixer, its theoretical insights into implicit regularization, and its empirical validation of the proposed interpretation.

## Weaknesses

Despite the paper's strengths, several weaknesses significantly impact the robustness and generalizability of its claims. One of the primary concerns is the lack of a direct quantitative comparison of the performance of the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs. While the paper does present performance comparisons on multiple datasets, it does not provide a detailed analysis of training time, memory usage, and convergence speed. This omission makes it difficult to assess the practical advantages of the proposed interpretation, especially in terms of computational efficiency. For instance, the paper does not compare the training time or memory footprint of the MLP-Mixer with those of sparse-weight MLPs, which is crucial for understanding the trade-offs between performance and efficiency. The confidence level in this issue is high, as the absence of these metrics is a clear gap in the experimental section (Tables 1 and 2). Another significant weakness is the lack of a clear, quantifiable definition of implicit regularization and a direct quantitative comparison of the implicit regularization mechanisms between the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs. The paper uses CKA to compare the hidden features, which is an indirect measure of implicit regularization. However, the authors do not provide a formal definition of the implicit regularization being compared or specific metrics to quantify it. This lack of clarity and quantification makes it challenging to evaluate the claim of similarity in implicit regularization. The confidence level in this issue is medium, as the paper does provide some theoretical analysis of linear activation regularization, but it is not directly compared to the MLP-Mixer's implicit regularization (Section 4.2). Additionally, the paper lacks a quantitative analysis of the sparsity of the weights in the standard MLP-Mixer and the Monarch matrix. The claim that the Monarch matrix has a similar sparsity pattern to the MLP-Mixer is not supported by quantitative evidence. The authors should provide metrics such as the percentage of zero weights or the number of non-zero weights to substantiate this claim. The confidence level in this issue is high, as the paper does not include any such analysis (Section 5.4). The definition of 'wide' MLP is also not clearly defined, and it is unclear how this relates to the number of neurons in the hidden layers. A more precise definition of 'wide' and a quantitative measure of sparsity for the standard MLP-Mixer would strengthen the paper's claims. The confidence level in this issue is high, as the paper's definition of 'wide' is not linked to the number of neurons (Section 3.1). Furthermore, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the experimental results, including a comparison of the performance of the MLP-Mixer and sparse-weight MLPs on different architectures beyond the MLP and SW-MLP. This limits the generalizability of the findings and makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the proposed interpretation. The confidence level in this issue is medium, as the paper does include performance comparisons on multiple datasets but lacks a broader range of architectural comparisons (Tables 1 and 2). These weaknesses collectively suggest that while the paper offers a novel interpretation of the MLP-Mixer, the empirical validation is not as rigorous as it could be, and the claims about implicit regularization and sparsity need more quantitative support.
