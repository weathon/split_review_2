## Summary

This paper introduces Peer Review, an unsupervised evaluation framework for large language models (LLMs) that leverages the capabilities of other LLMs within the same environment. The core idea is to have each LLM evaluate the responses of other LLMs to the same question, creating a network of evaluations that can be used to rank the LLMs. The framework assigns a learnable capability weight to each LLM and optimizes these weights to maximize the consistency of the LLMs' evaluations. This consistency is defined as the agreement between the LLMs' scores and their evaluations of each other's responses. The authors propose three metrics—PEN, CIN, and LIS—to evaluate the alignment between the framework's ranking and human preferences. The experimental results, conducted on multiple datasets and metrics, demonstrate that the proposed framework can effectively rank LLMs in a way that is competitive with, and in some cases superior to, existing unsupervised methods. The authors claim that this approach offers a novel way to evaluate LLMs without the need for human feedback, which is a significant advantage in the current landscape of LLM research.
