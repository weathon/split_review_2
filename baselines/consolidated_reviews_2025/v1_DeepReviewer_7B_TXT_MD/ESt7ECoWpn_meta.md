## Summary

This paper introduces a novel per-instance noise mechanism (NVO) designed to achieve differential privacy (DP) by optimizing noise variances for each data instance. The core idea revolves around formulating the noise optimization problem as a game-theoretic scenario, where each data instance is treated as a player in a common interest game. The goal is to find a Nash equilibrium (NE) in this game, which, according to the authors, ensures that the resulting noise mechanism provides per-instance DP. The authors propose two algorithms to find these NE points: an approximate enumeration (AE) method using a genetic algorithm and a best response dynamics (BRD) method. The AE method explores a discrete set of possible variances, while BRD iteratively refines the noise levels based on best responses. The paper presents theoretical guarantees, demonstrating that the NE points of the NVO game satisfy the conditions for per-instance DP. Empirically, the authors evaluate their method on two datasets, comparing it against a standard Laplace mechanism. The experiments focus on evaluating the utility of the proposed method using metrics such as KL divergence, L1 loss, Jaccard index, and cosine similarity. The results suggest that the NVO game-based approach can achieve better utility compared to the conventional Laplace mechanism while maintaining the same level of privacy. The authors also discuss the limitations of their approach, including the computational cost associated with the discrete search in the AE method and the challenges of applying the method to very large datasets. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to per-instance DP, leveraging game theory to optimize noise variances. However, as I will discuss, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened to provide a more complete and robust contribution to the field.
