## Summary

This paper introduces Predictive Differential Training (PDT), a novel approach to neural network training that leverages the Koopman operator theory to predict future weight updates, thereby accelerating the training process. The core idea behind PDT is to view the training dynamics of neural networks as a dynamical system, allowing for the application of control theory and Koopman operator-based predictions. The method employs Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) to extract linear dynamics from the training trajectory and uses these dynamics to predict future weight updates. A key component of PDT is the masking strategy, which selectively applies these predictions to subsets of parameters exhibiting 'good' prediction performance, while using a standard update for parameters with less predictable dynamics. This selective application of predictions is designed to balance computational efficiency with the benefits of accelerated training. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of PDT across a range of popular neural network architectures, including Fully Connected Networks (FCN), AlexNet, ResNet, and ViT, and across different optimizers, such as SGD and Adam. The experimental results consistently show that PDT achieves faster convergence and higher accuracy with fewer training epochs compared to baseline methods. The paper also includes a thorough analysis of the masking strategy, its impact on performance, and its sensitivity to various hyperparameters, such as the prediction interval and the number of past epochs used to construct the Koopman prediction. The authors claim that the computational load of the Koopman-related calculations is comparable to that of batch-level updates, and that the overhead introduced by the DMD is effectively compensated by the acceleration in loss reduction. Overall, this paper presents a creative combination of control theory and deep learning optimization, offering a promising approach to accelerate neural network training while maintaining or improving performance. The use of Koopman operator theory for weight prediction is a novel contribution, and the masking strategy provides a practical way to implement this approach. The experimental results are compelling, demonstrating the potential of PDT to significantly reduce the computational cost of training deep neural networks. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of a more detailed analysis of the computational costs and a more comprehensive exploration of the method's limitations and scalability.
