## Summary

This paper introduces a novel class of activation functions, termed 'elephant activation functions,' characterized by both sparse representations and sparse gradients. The authors propose that these properties can mitigate catastrophic forgetting in neural networks, a critical challenge in continual learning. The core idea is that by inducing sparsity, the network is encouraged to learn more distinct and less interfering representations for each task, thereby reducing the overlap between task-specific and task-agnostic information. The paper provides a theoretical justification for this claim, demonstrating how sparse representations contribute to sparse gradients and vice versa. Specifically, they show that the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) in a non-linear approximation leads to sparse representations, and they also provide a theorem demonstrating local elasticity, which relates sparse gradients to reduced forgetting. The authors evaluate their approach across a range of continual learning scenarios, including regression, class incremental learning, and reinforcement learning. They compare the performance of models using elephant activation functions against those using classical activation functions and other state-of-the-art continual learning methods. The empirical results, presented in terms of accuracy, suggest that elephant activation functions offer a promising approach to reducing forgetting. The paper's main contribution lies in the introduction of a new class of activation functions and the theoretical analysis that supports their potential for mitigating catastrophic forgetting. While the empirical results are encouraging, the paper also has several limitations, particularly in the areas of computational analysis and comparison to other state-of-the-art methods. Overall, the paper presents an interesting and potentially valuable contribution to the field of continual learning, but it requires further refinement to address the identified weaknesses.
