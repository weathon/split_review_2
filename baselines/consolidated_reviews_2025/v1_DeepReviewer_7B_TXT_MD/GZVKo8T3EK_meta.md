## Summary

This paper introduces PROVCREATOR, a novel graph synthesis framework designed to address the class imbalance problem in system provenance datasets. The core motivation stems from the observation that real-world datasets often exhibit a skewed distribution of program labels, which hinders the performance of machine learning models trained on such data. To tackle this, PROVCREATOR generates synthetic provenance graphs that maintain both structural and attribute fidelity while augmenting the underrepresented classes. The framework operates in two main stages: first, it generates the graph structure using a graph transformer, and then it generates node attributes conditioned on the class label and a context vector derived from the generated structure. The graph transformer is trained using a graph diffusion score-based sampling (GDSS) approach, while the attribute generation model is a transformer-based model. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of PROVCREATOR through a series of experiments on real-world datasets, showing that the generated graphs exhibit higher structural fidelity, attribute fidelity, and downstream utility compared to baseline methods. Specifically, the experiments focus on two security-relevant downstream tasks: program classification and malware detection. The results indicate that models trained on the synthetic graphs generated by PROVCREATOR achieve improved performance in these tasks, suggesting the practical utility of the framework. The paper also includes an analysis of the generated graphs using metrics like Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and BLEU scores, further supporting the claim of improved fidelity. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of graph synthesis for security applications, offering a method to generate realistic synthetic data that can be used to train more robust machine learning models. The authors also provide a detailed description of the proposed method and its evaluation, making it easy to follow and understand. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be improved to further strengthen its claims and impact.
