## Summary

This paper introduces Secure Distributed DP-Helmet, a novel framework for differentially private distributed learning, designed to train models on sensitive data while preserving privacy. The core idea revolves around leveraging a secure sum protocol to aggregate locally trained models, ensuring that the aggregated model converges to the global model. The authors focus on two specific types of machine learning models: Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and Softmax-activated single-layer perceptrons. They demonstrate that these models, when trained with a smooth hinge loss and a smooth softmax-scaled loss, respectively, satisfy the conditions for strong convexity and Lipschitz smoothness. This theoretical foundation allows them to prove that the average of the locally trained models converges to the centralized model. The proposed algorithm operates by having clients locally train their models, then use a secure sum protocol to aggregate these models in a privacy-preserving manner. The paper presents experimental results on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, comparing the performance of Secure Distributed DP-Helmet with DP-SGD-based federated learning. The results indicate that the proposed method achieves comparable utility while providing differential privacy guarantees. The authors also explore the impact of various factors, such as the number of users and the privacy budget, on the performance of their algorithm. The paper's main contribution lies in its application of secure aggregation to differentially private distributed learning, providing a practical approach for training privacy-preserving models in a distributed setting. While the core secure aggregation protocol is not novel, the paper's application to the specific problem of differentially private distributed learning, along with the theoretical analysis of convergence, is a valuable contribution. The authors also provide a comprehensive analysis of the algorithm's performance under various conditions, including non-IID data distributions, which is a crucial aspect of real-world distributed learning scenarios. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and theoretically sound approach to differentially private distributed learning, with empirical validation on standard datasets.
