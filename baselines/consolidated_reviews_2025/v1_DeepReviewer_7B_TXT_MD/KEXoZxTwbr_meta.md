## Summary

This paper introduces a two-stage inverse rendering framework designed to jointly reconstruct explicit geometry, material properties, and lighting from multi-view images. The method begins by extracting an explicit triangular mesh from a neural density field (NeRF2), which serves as a representation of the scene's geometry. This initial stage leverages a neural scene representation, combining a NeRF2-based density field with a NeRF-based color field, and employs a neural density field (NeRF) to extract a coarse mesh. This extracted mesh is then used in the second stage, where a physically-based inverse rendering model, utilizing multi-bounce path tracing and Monte Carlo integration, refines the geometry, material properties, and environment lighting. The authors present experimental results on the TensoIR and Real-World datasets, demonstrating that their method achieves state-of-the-art performance in both decomposition and relighting tasks. The core contribution of this work lies in the explicit representation of geometry, which, according to the authors, avoids issues associated with implicit representations, such as inconsistent optimization during illumination changes, topological inconsistencies, and geometric instability. The method also aims to improve the accuracy of material decomposition and relighting by employing a more realistic physically-based model. The paper emphasizes the efficiency of its approach, claiming a total training time of approximately 4.5 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. The authors position their work as a bridge between traditional implicit methods and the latest advances in explicit scene representation, aiming to provide a more robust and accurate solution for multi-view reconstruction, inverse rendering, and material editing. The overall significance of this work is in its attempt to address the limitations of existing methods by combining the strengths of implicit and explicit representations, and by utilizing a physically-based model for improved accuracy and realism.
