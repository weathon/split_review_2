## Summary

This paper introduces the concept of the 'chunking problem' within the context of continual learning (CL), a setting where data is presented in discrete chunks rather than as a continuous stream. The authors argue that this chunking, even in the absence of task or domain shifts, significantly impacts the performance of CL models, leading to a substantial performance drop compared to offline learning. They demonstrate that current CL methods do not effectively address this chunking problem, often exhibiting behavior akin to simple stochastic gradient descent (SGD) when training on individual chunks. To mitigate this issue, the authors propose a straightforward yet effective method: per-chunk weight averaging. This technique involves training the model on each chunk of data independently and then averaging the learned weights across all chunks. The core argument is that this averaging process helps to stabilize the learning process and reduce forgetting, a common challenge in CL. The empirical findings, presented across standard CL benchmarks such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet, show that the proposed per-chunk weight averaging method significantly improves performance in chunking settings, often surpassing the performance of existing CL methods. Furthermore, the authors demonstrate that this improvement is transferable to full CL settings, suggesting that addressing the chunking problem is a crucial step towards improving the overall performance of CL algorithms. The paper's significance lies in its identification of the chunking problem as a fundamental challenge in CL, its demonstration of its impact on performance, and its proposal of a simple yet effective solution. By highlighting the importance of this problem, the paper opens up new avenues for research in CL, focusing on the development of methods that can effectively handle data presented in discrete chunks. The authors' work underscores the need to move beyond traditional CL methods and consider the unique challenges posed by the chunking setting, ultimately contributing to a more robust and effective approach to continual learning.
