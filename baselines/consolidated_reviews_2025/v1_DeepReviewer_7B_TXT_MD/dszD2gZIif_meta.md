## Summary

This paper introduces Swin4TS, a novel approach for long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) that leverages the Swin Transformer architecture. The authors propose adapting the Swin Transformer, known for its success in image processing, to the unique challenges of time series data. Specifically, they incorporate window-based attention and hierarchical representation techniques to achieve linear computational complexity and capture multi-scale temporal dependencies. The core idea is to treat time series data as if it were patch-based data, allowing the application of windowed attention mechanisms. The method involves dividing the time series into patches, which are then further divided into windows. Attention is then computed within these windows, followed by a hierarchical representation that captures dependencies at different scales. The authors introduce two variants of Swin4TS: Swin4TS/CD, which captures correlations in both the time and channel dimensions, and Swin4TS/CI, which focuses on channel-independent strategies. The empirical evaluation is conducted on eight benchmark datasets, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance compared to existing baselines. The authors provide ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of the different components of their method. The results suggest that the proposed approach is effective in capturing long-range dependencies and achieving superior performance in LTSF tasks. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the methodology and experimental setup. The authors provide a thorough experimental evaluation, including ablation studies and comparisons with various baselines. The use of the Swin Transformer architecture, with its linear complexity, is a significant contribution to the field of time series forecasting. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, the paper has some limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize its potential.

## Strengths

One of the primary strengths of this paper is the successful adaptation of the Swin Transformer architecture to the task of long-term time series forecasting. The authors have effectively leveraged the Swin Transformer's ability to capture long-range dependencies with its linear complexity, which is a significant advantage over traditional Transformer models with quadratic complexity. The use of window-based attention is another notable strength, as it allows the model to focus on local dependencies while still capturing long-range patterns. The hierarchical representation also contributes to the model's ability to capture multi-scale temporal dependencies, which is crucial for long-term forecasting. The paper's empirical results are compelling, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on eight benchmark datasets. The authors have conducted a thorough experimental evaluation, including ablation studies that validate the effectiveness of the different components of their method. The introduction of two variants, Swin4TS/CD and Swin4TS/CI, provides flexibility in adapting the model to different types of time series data. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the methodology and experimental setup. The figures and tables are informative and help to understand the results. The authors have clearly articulated the motivation behind their work and have provided a comprehensive analysis of their proposed method. The paper is a valuable contribution to the field of time series forecasting, and the authors have demonstrated the potential of the Swin Transformer architecture for this task. The use of the Swin Transformer architecture, with its linear complexity, is a significant contribution to the field of time series forecasting. The authors have also provided a thorough experimental evaluation, including ablation studies and comparisons with various baselines. The use of the Swin Transformer architecture, with its linear complexity, is a significant contribution to the field of time series forecasting.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of the paper, there are several weaknesses that need to be addressed. First, the novelty of the proposed method is limited. While the authors effectively apply the Swin Transformer architecture to time series forecasting, the core idea of using Transformers for this task is not new. The paper itself acknowledges that the core idea of applying Swin Transformer to time series forecasting is not new, and the paper does not adequately differentiate itself from existing approaches. The use of window-based attention and hierarchical representation, while beneficial, are not novel contributions in themselves, and the paper does not provide sufficient justification for why these specific techniques are particularly well-suited for time series data compared to other attention mechanisms or hierarchical methods. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of how the proposed method addresses the unique challenges of long-term time series forecasting, such as capturing long-range dependencies and handling non-stationarity, beyond the general applicability of Swin Transformer. This lack of specific adaptation for time series data is a significant limitation. The paper also lacks a comprehensive literature review.
