## Summary

This paper introduces a novel data poisoning attack, termed the 'neural phishing attack,' designed to extract sensitive information from large language models (LLMs). The core idea revolves around poisoning the pre-training dataset with carefully crafted, benign-looking sentences that mimic potential user data. These poisoned sentences are intended to teach the LLM to memorize specific secrets, which can then be extracted during inference. The authors demonstrate that by inserting as few as ten poisoned sentences, the model's ability to regurgitate sensitive information, such as credit card numbers, can be significantly enhanced. The experimental results presented in the paper show a high success rate in extracting these secrets, ranging from 10% to 50%, depending on various factors. The authors explore the impact of factors such as the frequency of duplication, the length of the secret, and the size of the model. They also investigate the durability of the memorized secrets, showing that the attack can persist even after the poisoned data is removed from the training set. The paper's main contribution lies in its practical demonstration of a data poisoning attack that can effectively extract sensitive information from LLMs, highlighting a significant privacy risk. The authors also explore the impact of different poisoning strategies, including random poisoning and handcrafted poisoning, and analyze the effectiveness of the attack under various conditions. The findings underscore the vulnerability of LLMs to data poisoning attacks and the need for robust defense mechanisms to mitigate these risks. While the paper does not delve into the specifics of the LLM architecture or training process, it provides a clear and practical demonstration of how data poisoning can be used to extract sensitive information, which is a crucial step in understanding and addressing the broader ethical concerns associated with LLMs. The paper's focus on practical implementation and empirical validation makes it a valuable contribution to the field of adversarial machine learning, particularly in the context of LLMs.
