## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to weight parameterization in neural networks, termed Neural Representations for Neural Networks (NeRN), which leverages predictor networks to reconstruct model weights. The core idea is to decouple the training process into two distinct stages: reconstruction and distillation. In the reconstruction stage, a predictor network is trained to minimize the reconstruction loss, effectively learning to represent the model weights. Subsequently, in the distillation stage, the predictor network is fine-tuned using a distillation loss, transferring knowledge from a high-performing teacher network to the predictor. This two-stage approach aims to enhance both model accuracy and compression. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method through extensive experiments on various datasets and architectures, showing significant improvements over the original NeRN framework and other state-of-the-art methods in terms of both model accuracy and compression. Specifically, the paper shows that the proposed method can achieve higher accuracy with the same level of compression, or achieve the same accuracy with better compression than existing methods. The authors also explore the relationship between weight smoothing and model generalization, and propose a method to directly alter the original weights to achieve a similar effect without the need to train a predictor model. The paper concludes by discussing the limitations of the proposed method and suggesting potential directions for future research, including the need for methods that can predict weights for diverse architectures and are ideally more efficient when the target model grows in size and complexity. Overall, this paper presents a compelling approach to weight parameterization, offering a new perspective on how to improve model performance and compression. The decoupling of the training process into reconstruction and distillation stages is a significant contribution, and the empirical results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of the analysis of computational cost and the depth of the discussion of related works.
