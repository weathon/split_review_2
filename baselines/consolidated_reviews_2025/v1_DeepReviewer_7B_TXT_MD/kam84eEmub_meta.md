## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to generating directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) by leveraging autoregressive diffusion models and a bipartite graph representation. The core idea revolves around representing DAGs as sequences of bipartite graphs, which allows for a layer-by-layer generation process. This method contrasts with existing generative models in its ability to explicitly capture both node attributes and logical dependencies inherent in DAGs. The authors propose a layer-index-based denoising schedule to enhance the quality-efficiency trade-off, allowing for flexible control over the generation process. Empirically, the method is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-quality DAGs with strong logical constraints. The experimental results show that the proposed method outperforms existing models in terms of generation quality and efficiency, particularly in scenarios where the generation process needs to be controlled. The authors also highlight the potential applications of their method in benchmarking computing systems and creating synthetic datasets for benchmarking deep learning models. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of DAG generation by combining autoregressive diffusion models with a novel graph representation, offering a promising approach for generating complex and realistic DAG structures. The method's ability to capture both node attributes and logical dependencies, along with its flexibility in controlling the generation process, makes it a valuable tool for various applications in system design, testing, and deep learning model development. The paper is well-written and organized, making it easy to follow the proposed methodology and experimental results. The authors provide a clear explanation of the method and its advantages, supported by a thorough evaluation on both synthetic and real-world datasets. The experimental results are presented in a clear and concise manner, with detailed comparisons against existing methods. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed method is a significant step forward in the field of DAG generation, offering a more effective and efficient approach for generating complex and realistic DAG structures. The authors also provide a detailed description of the experimental setup and the evaluation metrics used, which allows for a clear understanding of the results. The paper's overall contribution lies in its novel combination of autoregressive diffusion models and bipartite graph representation, which enables the generation of high-quality DAGs with strong logical constraints. This approach has the potential to significantly impact various fields that rely on DAGs, such as system design, testing, and deep learning model development. The paper's findings and methodology are likely to inspire further research in this area, leading to the development of even more advanced and efficient DAG generation techniques. The authors' work represents a significant advancement in the field, offering a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners alike.

## Weaknesses

Despite the paper's strengths, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. A primary concern, consistently raised by multiple reviewers, is the lack of a detailed computational complexity analysis. While the paper describes the proposed method in detail, it does not provide a formal analysis of the time and space complexity for each step of the algorithm, such as the bipartite graph construction, diffusion process, and sampling. This omission is significant because it leaves the reader without a clear understanding of the method's scalability and efficiency. The paper does not discuss how these complexities scale with the number of nodes and edges in the DAG, nor does it address the suitability of the approach for very large-scale graphs. This lack of analysis is a major limitation, as it prevents a thorough assessment of the method's practical applicability. The absence of a theoretical complexity analysis makes it difficult to compare the proposed method with existing approaches in terms of computational efficiency. Furthermore, the paper does not provide any empirical data on the computational cost of the method, such as the time and memory requirements for different graph sizes. This lack of information makes it challenging to evaluate the method's performance in real-world scenarios. The paper also lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of using a bipartite graph representation for DAGs. While the method relies on a strict layer-wise partitioning, it does not address the potential limitations of this representation for DAGs with complex hierarchical structures or cyclic dependencies. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the trade-off between generation quality and efficiency. The paper does not discuss how the proposed method can be used to generate DAGs with different levels of complexity.
