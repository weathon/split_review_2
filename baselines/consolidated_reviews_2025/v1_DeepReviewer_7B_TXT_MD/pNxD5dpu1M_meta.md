## Summary

This paper introduces a novel multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) model based on the Chung-Lu (CL) random graph model, termed Cooperative Mean Field Games on Chung-Lu Graphs (CLCMFGs). The core contribution lies in adapting the mean-field game (MFG) framework to handle sparse graph structures, which are prevalent in many real-world networks. The authors propose a two-system approximation to address the computational challenges associated with the CLCMFG model, which involves a coarse-grained approximation and a mean-field approximation. They provide theoretical analysis demonstrating the convergence of the empirical mean-field states to the limiting mean-field states under certain conditions. The paper also presents scalable learning algorithms based on the two-system approximation and evaluates the proposed model and algorithms on both synthetic and real-world network datasets. The empirical results suggest that the proposed approach can effectively learn cooperative policies in sparse network environments. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to bridge the gap between mean-field game theory and sparse graph analysis, offering a new perspective on MARL in networked multi-agent systems. However, the paper's presentation and analysis could benefit from greater clarity and a more thorough discussion of its limitations and practical implications.
