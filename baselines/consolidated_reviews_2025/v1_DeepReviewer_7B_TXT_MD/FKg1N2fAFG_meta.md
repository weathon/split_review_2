## Summary

This paper tackles the critical issue of architecture overfitting in dataset distillation, a phenomenon where models trained on distilled datasets perform poorly when tested on different network architectures. The authors propose a series of methodological adjustments to mitigate this overfitting, including the use of DropPath, a modified residual shortcut connection, knowledge distillation, periodic learning rate scheduling, a stronger optimizer (Lion), and enhanced data augmentation. The core idea is to enhance the generalization capabilities of models trained on distilled datasets across various network architectures. The authors evaluate their approach using CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets, comparing their method against established dataset distillation techniques like Feature Regression with Pooling (FRePo) and Matching Training Trajectories (MTT). The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed methods significantly improve the performance of distilled datasets across different network architectures, particularly when the number of instances per class (IPC) is limited. The paper's main contribution lies in its empirical demonstration of the effectiveness of these combined techniques in addressing architecture overfitting, although the individual components are not entirely novel. The authors argue that their approach is efficient and generic, and can be applied to different model architectures and training schemes. While the paper presents a comprehensive set of experiments, it also suffers from a lack of novelty in its core methods and a limited scope in its experimental evaluation. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed techniques can improve the robustness of distilled datasets, but the lack of a thorough analysis of computational costs and the limited scope of the experiments raise questions about the practical applicability and generalizability of the approach. Overall, the paper provides a valuable contribution to the field of dataset distillation, but it also highlights the need for further research to address the limitations of existing methods and to explore more efficient and scalable solutions.
