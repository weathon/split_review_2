### Summary

The paper presents a graph generation method for generating synthetic system provenance graphs. The method is based on a graph diffusion score-based sampling (GDSS) model for generating graph structures, and a transformer-based model for generating node attributes. The transformer-based model takes the node embeddings generated by the graph transformer as input, and generates node attributes conditioned on the class label of the process represented by the node. The authors demonstrate that their method can generate synthetic graphs that are more similar to the original graphs than the graphs generated by GDSS, and that the generated graphs improve the performance of machine learning models on downstream tasks.

Furthermore, the evaluation of the generated attributes is limited to a comparison with randomly sampled attributes, which does not provide a comprehensive assessment of the quality of the generated attributes.
- The paper does not provide a detailed explanation of how the generated graphs are evaluated, and how the evaluation metrics are calculated. The paper mentions that the similarity between the generated graphs and the original graphs is measured using MMD, but it does not specify the exact implementation details, such as the kernel used, the bandwidth parameter, or the number of random samples used for estimation. The paper also does not provide a detailed explanation of how the generated graphs are used to improve the performance of machine learning models on downstream tasks, and how the performance is measured.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit significantly from a more detailed explanation of the graph transformer architecture and the node embedding generation process. Specifically, the authors should provide a clear description of the number of layers, the size of the hidden states, the attention mechanism used (e.g., multi-head attention), and the activation functions. Furthermore, the paper should clarify how the node embeddings are aggregated to represent the entire graph structure for the attribute generation model. For example, are the node embeddings concatenated, averaged, or aggregated using a pooling layer? A detailed explanation of these aspects would greatly enhance the clarity and reproducibility of the proposed method. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the attribute generation process, including the exact input format to the transformer model, the number of layers, the activation functions, and the loss function used for training. The paper should also include a more comprehensive evaluation of the generated attributes, beyond just comparing them to randomly sampled attributes. For example, the authors could evaluate the semantic similarity of the generated attributes to the original attributes using metrics such as BLEU or ROUGE scores, or by using human annotators to assess the quality of the generated attributes.

To improve the evaluation of the generated graphs, the authors should provide a detailed explanation of how the MMD metric is calculated, including the specific kernel used, the bandwidth parameter, and the number of random samples used for estimation. It would also be beneficial to include a visualization of the generated graphs and the original graphs to provide a qualitative assessment of the similarity between them. Furthermore, the paper should provide a more detailed explanation of how the generated graphs are used to improve the performance of machine learning models on downstream tasks. For example, the authors could describe the specific machine learning models used for the downstream tasks, the training procedure, and the evaluation metrics. The paper should also include a comparison of the performance of the machine learning models trained on the generated graphs with the performance of the models trained on the original graphs. This would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their approach and potential directions for future research. For example, the authors could discuss the computational complexity of the proposed method, the sensitivity of the method to different hyperparameters, and the potential for bias in the generated graphs. The authors could also discuss how their method could be extended to handle more complex graph structures, such as heterogeneous graphs with multiple node and edge types. Addressing these limitations and suggesting future research directions would further strengthen the paper and provide a more complete picture of the proposed method.
