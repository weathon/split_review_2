### Summary

This paper studies the look-ahead capabilities of the Leela Chess Zero model. It extends the analysis of Jensen et al. (2024) to 5- and 7-step look-ahead. The authors use a combination of activation patching, probing, and ablation studies to investigate how the model encodes information about future board states and move sequences. They find that the model's look-ahead behavior is highly context-dependent, varying significantly based on the specific puzzle set and whether the sequence leads to checkmate. They also find evidence that the model processes some future moves using similar concrete internal mechanisms, particularly through specialized attention heads. These mechanisms appear to be pattern-sensitive rather than timing-specific, suggesting the model has learned some general strategies for processing look-ahead information.
