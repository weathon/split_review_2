## Summary

This paper introduces STRATEGIST, a novel framework designed to enhance strategic decision-making in complex, partially observable environments by integrating the strengths of large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL). The core idea revolves around a dual-level approach: a high-level strategic abstraction learning level, facilitated by an LLM, and a low-level policy execution level, driven by RL. The LLM is employed to generate and refine strategic abstractions, which are represented as interpretable text, capturing high-level strategic insights. These abstractions are then executed by a low-level RL mechanism, which is trained through self-play and evolutionary strategies. This dual-level approach aims to address the limitations of traditional RL methods, which often struggle with the exploration of large action spaces and the adaptation to dynamic environments. The authors evaluate STRATEGIST in two challenging, multi-turn games: Game of Pure Strategy (GOPS) and Avalon. The experimental results demonstrate that STRATEGIST outperforms both traditional RL methods and other LLM-based agents in terms of win rates and adaptability. Specifically, the paper shows that STRATEGIST achieves higher win rates and adapts more effectively to dynamic environments compared to baseline methods. The framework's ability to learn from high-level strategic abstractions and execute them efficiently through self-play and evolutionary strategies is presented as a promising direction for enhancing strategic decision-making in complex environments. The paper's contribution lies in its innovative integration of LLMs and RL, its dual-level approach to strategic decision-making, and its empirical validation in two complex games. The authors argue that this approach addresses the limitations of traditional RL methods and offers a more efficient and adaptable solution for strategic decision-making. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed framework has the potential to significantly advance the field of AI for strategic games and beyond, by leveraging the strengths of both LLMs and RL to achieve superior performance in complex, partially observable environments. The authors also highlight the potential for generalization of the proposed approach to other types of strategic environments, although this aspect is not explored in detail in the paper. Overall, the paper presents a compelling case for the use of LLMs in strategic decision-making, while also acknowledging the need for further research to address the limitations of the proposed approach.
