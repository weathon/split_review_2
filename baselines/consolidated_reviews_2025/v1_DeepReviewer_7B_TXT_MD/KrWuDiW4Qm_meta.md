## Summary

This paper introduces MetaPhysiCa, a novel meta-learning framework designed to enhance the out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness of physics-informed machine learning (PIML) models, particularly in the context of dynamical systems forecasting. The core problem addressed is the poor generalization performance of existing PIML methods when faced with OOD scenarios, where both initial conditions and system parameters deviate from the training data. The authors propose a two-pronged approach: first, leveraging causal structure discovery to learn the underlying ODE structure from multiple training trajectories, and second, employing invariant risk minimization to adapt the model to new OOD test tasks. The method operates by meta-learning a shared causal structure across multiple training tasks, using a predefined set of basis functions to represent the ODE. The causal structure is learned through a combination of causal discovery techniques and a V-REx regularization term, which encourages sparsity in the learned graph. This learned causal structure is then used to adapt the model to new tasks, where the initial conditions and parameters are OOD. The paper evaluates MetaPhysiCa on three ODE-based systems: a damped pendulum, a predator-prey model, and an epidemic model. The results demonstrate significant improvements over existing methods in OOD scenarios, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors use a meta-learning framework to learn the ODE structure and adapt to new OOD test tasks. The method is evaluated on three different ODE-based systems, and the results show significant improvements over existing methods in OOD scenarios. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, and the experimental results are convincing. However, the paper does have some limitations, particularly in its assumptions about the fixed ODE structure and the limited scope of the experimental evaluation. Despite these limitations, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of physics-informed machine learning by addressing the critical issue of OOD robustness in dynamical systems forecasting.

## Weaknesses

As stated in Section 4.1, the method relies on a predefined set of basis functions and assumes that the true ODE structure can be represented by a linear combination of these basis functions. This assumption is problematic because, in many real-world scenarios, the ODE structure can vary, or it may not be fully known.
