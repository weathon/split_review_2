## Summary

This paper introduces LUMA, a novel multimodal dataset designed to facilitate research in multimodal learning under uncertainty. The dataset comprises 101,000 images, 135,000 audio recordings, and 6,000 text samples across 50 classes. The authors emphasize the inclusion of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties, which are crucial for developing robust and trustworthy multimodal models. To support research on this dataset, they also provide baseline models using three uncertainty quantification methods and a Python package that enables the generation of multiple variants of the dataset with controllable uncertainties. The core methodology involves extracting deep features from each modality and using inverse distances to the center of each class to control data diversity. The authors generate uncertainties through various methods, including adding Gaussian noise, label noise, and bias. The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the dataset's properties, including the distribution of data points across classes, the impact of label and sample noise, and the performance of baseline models with different uncertainty quantification methods. The experimental results demonstrate the dataset's utility in evaluating model robustness, particularly in the presence of label and sample noise. The authors also explore the impact of out-of-distribution (OOD) samples and bias on model performance. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by providing a new benchmark dataset for multimodal learning under uncertainty, along with baseline models and a tool for generating variations of the dataset. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the lack of detailed information on uncertainty generation and a comprehensive comparison with existing multimodal uncertainty benchmarks. These limitations, which I will detail in the following sections, need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of the proposed dataset.

## Weaknesses

While the paper presents a valuable contribution in the form of the LUMA dataset, several weaknesses need to be addressed to fully realize its potential. The most significant weakness is the lack of clarity in the description of how uncertainties were generated, particularly for the audio modality. As noted by multiple reviewers, the paper mentions adding various types of noise to the audio, such as Gaussian, shot, impulse, defocus blur, frosted glass blur, motion blur, zoom blur, snow, frost, fog, changing brightness, contrast, elasticity, pixelating, and JPEG compression (Section 3.2). However, the paper fails to specify the parameters used for these noise generation processes. For example, the type of Gaussian noise (e.g., white noise, colored noise) and the signal-to-noise ratio for adding Gaussian noise are not detailed. Similarly, the parameters for the blur types, such as the kernel size for defocus blur or the aperture disk size for frosted glass blur, are missing. This lack of detail makes it difficult to assess the validity and reproducibility of the uncertainty modeling, as other researchers cannot replicate the exact noise conditions used in the dataset.
