## Summary

This paper introduces Diffusion-guided Safe Policy Optimization (DSPO), a novel approach to offline safe reinforcement learning (RL) that aims to learn safe policies directly from offline datasets without relying on explicit cost labels. The core idea behind DSPO is to leverage the power of diffusion models to generate safe trajectories, which are then used to train a policy via behavior cloning. The method is motivated by the limitations of traditional offline safe RL methods that often require task-relevant cost functions, which can be difficult to define or obtain in many real-world scenarios. Instead, DSPO employs a two-stage optimization process. First, a return-agnostic discriminator is trained to derive trajectory-wise safety signals. This discriminator is trained using a transformer architecture, which allows it to capture complex, non-Markovian safety patterns that might be missed by traditional cost-based approaches. The key innovation here is the use of a return-agnostic loss, which ensures that the discriminator's output is indicative of safety rather than just a proxy for return. Second, a conditional diffusion model is trained to generate safe trajectories conditioned on trajectory return and the safety signals derived from the return-agnostic discriminator. The diffusion model is conditioned on both the return and the safety signal to ensure that the generated trajectories are both high-reward and safe. Finally, a policy is derived by behavior cloning (BC) from these generated safe trajectories. The authors evaluate their approach on various tasks from SafetyGym, BulletGym, and MetaDrive, and a variety of baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of their method in achieving safe policies with high returns. The results show that DSPO outperforms several existing offline safe RL methods in terms of both safety and return. The paper also includes an analysis of the limitations of the return-agnostic loss, showing that it can assign high weights to unsafe trajectories even with the return-agnostic loss. Overall, this paper presents a promising approach to offline safe RL, addressing a critical challenge in the field by learning safety directly from demonstrations without explicit cost labels. The use of a transformer-based return-agnostic discriminator and a conditional diffusion model for trajectory generation is a novel contribution that shows potential for addressing complex safety problems. However, the paper also has several limitations that need to be addressed to make the method more robust and practical for real-world applications.
