## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to fluid simulation that learns physical properties from visual observations, enabling the simulation of unseen scenarios. The core idea is to infer hidden physical parameters of a fluid from a single 3D video, and then use these inferred parameters to adapt a probabilistic fluid simulator to novel scenes. The method is structured into three distinct stages: pretraining, inference, and transfer. In the pretraining stage, the model learns a probabilistic fluid simulator on a dataset of particle-based fluid simulations with varying physical properties. This stage aims to establish a prior distribution over possible fluid dynamics. During inference, the model adapts the simulator to new scenes by estimating the latent posteriors of the fluid properties from visual inputs. This adaptation is achieved through a visual posterior inference module. Finally, in the transfer stage, the model fine-tunes the simulator using the adapted latent variables to enable novel scene simulations. The authors validate their method on synthetic datasets, demonstrating its ability to generalize to unseen geometries, boundary conditions, and fluid types. The results show that the proposed method can accurately simulate fluid dynamics in novel scenarios, outperforming existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy. The significance of this work lies in its ability to bridge the gap between visual observations and physical simulations, offering a new paradigm for fluid simulation that does not require explicit knowledge of physical parameters. This approach has the potential to be applied to various real-world scenarios, such as autonomous driving and robotics, where understanding and predicting fluid behavior is crucial. However, the paper's primary focus on synthetic data and the lack of detailed analysis on computational cost and real-world applicability raise important questions about the practical utility of the proposed method.
