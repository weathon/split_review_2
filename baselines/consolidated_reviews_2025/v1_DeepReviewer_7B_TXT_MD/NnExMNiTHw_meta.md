## Summary

This paper introduces SpecDec++, an adaptive version of speculative decoding designed to dynamically adjust the candidate length based on the probability of rejection by the target model. The core idea is to move beyond fixed candidate lengths, which can be suboptimal, by formulating the candidate length selection as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The authors prove that the optimal policy for this MDP is a threshold policy, meaning that the speculation should stop when the probability of at least one rejected token exceeds a certain threshold. To implement this policy, they propose training an acceptance prediction head on top of the draft model, which predicts the conditional probability of each token being accepted by the target model. This head is trained using a weighted binary cross-entropy loss, and the overall training process is designed to be computationally efficient. The experimental results, conducted on a few datasets including Alpaca, HumanEval, and GSM8K, demonstrate that SpecDec++ achieves better speedup compared to the baseline method, SpecDec, while maintaining comparable accuracy. The authors also show that SpecDec++ can be easily integrated with other speculative decoding methods. The significance of this work lies in its principled approach to adaptive candidate length selection, which has the potential to improve the efficiency of large language model inference. By dynamically adjusting the candidate length based on the target model's acceptance probability, SpecDec++ offers a more flexible and efficient alternative to fixed-length speculative decoding. The theoretical grounding of the method, based on the MDP framework, adds a layer of rigor to the approach, and the empirical results provide evidence of its effectiveness. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the areas of training cost analysis and generalizability, which I will discuss in more detail in the following sections. Overall, I find the paper to be a valuable contribution to the field of speculative decoding, offering a novel and promising approach to adaptive candidate length selection.
