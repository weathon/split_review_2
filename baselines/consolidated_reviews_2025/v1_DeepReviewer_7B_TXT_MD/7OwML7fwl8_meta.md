## Summary

This paper introduces Reckoner, a novel framework for achieving fairness in classification tasks when sensitive attributes are not available during training. The core idea revolves around a dual-model system that leverages a 'high-confidence' generator, trained on a subset of data with high confidence scores, to guide the training of a 'low-confidence' generator on the remaining data. This is coupled with a learnable noise module, applied to the input of the high-confidence generator, to enhance both accuracy and fairness. The method is motivated by an analysis of fairness in the COMPAS and New Adult datasets, which reveals disparities in model performance across different confidence levels. The authors propose that by training the high-confidence generator on high-confidence samples and using the low-confidence generator to augment the training data, they can achieve a more robust and fair model. The framework employs a variational autoencoder (VAE) for the high-confidence generator and a simple two-layer MLP for the low-confidence generator. The learnable noise module is a key component, designed to introduce perturbations to the input data, which the authors hypothesize will lead to a more robust feature space and improved fairness. The training process involves a pseudo-learning approach, where the low-confidence generator is trained using the pseudo-distributions generated by the high-confidence generator. The authors evaluate their method on the COMPAS and New Adult datasets, demonstrating that Reckoner achieves competitive accuracy while maintaining competitive fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds and Demographic Parity. The experimental results show that the proposed method outperforms several baseline approaches, including Distributed Robust Optimization (DRO), ARL, FairRF, and Chai et al. (2022). The paper also includes an ablation study to analyze the impact of the learnable noise and the dual-model system on both accuracy and fairness. Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to fairness in the absence of sensitive attributes, combining a dual-model system with a learnable noise module to achieve both accuracy and fairness. The empirical results suggest that the proposed method is effective in improving fairness metrics while maintaining competitive accuracy. However, the paper also has several limitations, including a lack of detailed analysis of the interaction between the dual-model system and the learnable noise, and a lack of discussion of the computational cost of the proposed method. The paper also does not address the limitations of the proposed method, such as its applicability to different types of data or its robustness to adversarial attacks. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of fair machine learning, offering a new approach to achieving fairness in the absence of sensitive attributes.
