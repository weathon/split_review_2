## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to the problem of catastrophic forgetting in large language models (LLMs) during model refinement, a critical challenge in the practical application of these models. The core idea revolves around forecasting which training examples will be forgotten after the model is updated with new data. To achieve this, the authors propose two distinct methods: a logit-based forecasting model and a black-box representation-based forecasting model. The logit-based method is inspired by the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory, analyzing the changes in logit outputs of upstream examples when the model is updated with new data. Specifically, it posits that changes in pre-softmax logits of upstream examples resemble those of online learned examples. The black-box method, on the other hand, uses inner products of example representations to predict forgetting, offering a computationally efficient alternative. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of these methods through extensive experiments on three different LLMs (BART0, FLAN-T5, and BART0) using the MMLU and P3-Test datasets. Their results show that the proposed forecasting methods can be integrated into existing model refinement pipelines to reduce overall forgetting when used to guide model updates. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to address a fundamental problem in LLMs, providing a partially interpretable approach to model refinement that can potentially improve the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning. By focusing on predicting forgetting, the authors offer a proactive approach to model updating, rather than simply mitigating it, which is a valuable contribution to the field. The paper also provides a theoretical analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed methods, which is a useful addition to the empirical results. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and executed study that addresses an important problem in LLMs, with the potential to improve the practical application of these models.
