## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to evaluating large-scale Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) over encrypted data using fully homomorphic encryption (FHE), addressing a significant challenge in privacy-preserving machine learning. The core contribution lies in the development of an overflow-aware activity regularization (OAR) technique, designed to mitigate the overflow issue that arises from the large domain sizes inherent in FHE operations. This overflow issue, where intermediate values during RNN computation exceed the modulus range, can lead to incorrect results and reduced model performance. The authors propose OAR as a method to guide pre-activations towards correct overflow regions, effectively restoring lost accuracy. The method is evaluated on the MNIST dataset, demonstrating improved accuracy and latency compared to plaintext and other FHE-based evaluations. Specifically, the paper leverages the CGGI scheme for FHE, which allows for one ciphertext per input/activation, aiming to reduce inference latency. The experimental results show that the proposed OAR technique, when combined with CGGI, achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both accuracy and latency on the MNIST dataset. The authors also explore the impact of different parameter settings on the performance of the OAR method. The paper's significance lies in its potential to enable privacy-preserving machine learning applications, particularly in scenarios where RNNs are required for processing sensitive data. By addressing the overflow issue, the authors make FHE-based RNN evaluation more practical and efficient, paving the way for broader adoption of homomorphic encryption in real-world applications. However, the paper's evaluation is limited to the MNIST dataset, and it lacks a detailed analysis of computational overhead and a thorough comparison with existing FHE-based RNN evaluation methods. These limitations, which I will discuss in detail, restrict the generalizability of the findings and the overall impact of the work.
