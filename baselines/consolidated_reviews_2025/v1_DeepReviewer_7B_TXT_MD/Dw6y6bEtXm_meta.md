## Summary

This paper introduces Physics-Informed Coarse-grained data Learning (PICL), a novel framework designed to address the challenges of modeling physical systems using coarse-grained data. The core idea behind PICL is to integrate physics-based constraints into the training of machine learning models, specifically by employing a two-stage training process. The framework consists of an encoding module and a transition module. The encoding module is responsible for generating a learnable fine-grained state from a given coarse-grained input, while the transition module predicts the subsequent state. A key aspect of PICL is its ability to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data, which is a common scenario in many real-world applications where obtaining fine-grained data is expensive or infeasible. The authors propose a two-stage training process that utilizes physics-informed loss in the first stage and fine-tuning with unlabeled data in the second stage. This approach aims to improve the model's predictive accuracy and data efficiency. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of PICL on three partial differential equation (PDE) problems: the wave equation, the linear shallow water equation, and the nonlinear shallow water equation. The experimental results show that PICL outperforms existing methods in terms of data efficiency and predictive accuracy, particularly when dealing with coarse-grained data. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the proposed method, including the encoding and transition modules, and the two-stage training process. The authors also conduct extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of PICL across different PDEs, demonstrating its robustness and generalizability. The paper's contribution lies in its innovative approach to integrating physics-based constraints into the training of machine learning models for physical systems, and its ability to handle coarse-grained data, which is a significant challenge in many scientific and engineering applications. The use of unlabeled data and physics-informed loss in a two-stage training process is a novel approach that shows promising results. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors provide a clear explanation of the proposed method and its underlying principles, which is essential for understanding and reproducing the results. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of physics-informed machine learning and has the potential to impact various scientific and engineering disciplines.
