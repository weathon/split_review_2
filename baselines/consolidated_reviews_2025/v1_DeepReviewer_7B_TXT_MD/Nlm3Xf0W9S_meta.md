## Summary

This paper introduces a novel watermarking framework specifically designed for order-agnostic language models, a significant advancement given the limitations of traditional watermarking methods that rely on sequential token generation. The authors address the challenge of embedding and detecting watermarks in non-sequential generation models by employing a Markov-chain-based key sequence generation method and a statistical pattern-based detection algorithm. This approach allows for the watermark to be embedded without requiring access to the original prompt, a crucial feature for practical applications. The core idea involves generating a key sequence using a Markov chain, which is then used to alter the statistical properties of the generated text, effectively embedding the watermark. The detection algorithm then analyzes the generated text to identify the statistical patterns indicative of the watermark. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their framework through experiments on protein generation and machine translation tasks, showing improved detection efficiency, generation quality, and robustness compared to baseline methods. The experimental results indicate that the proposed method outperforms existing watermarking techniques in terms of both detection accuracy and the quality of the generated text. Specifically, the paper evaluates the framework's performance under random token modification and ChatGPT paraphrasing attacks, demonstrating its resilience against these common text transformations. The authors also analyze the impact of different parameters on the detection efficiency, providing insights into the optimal configuration of their method. The paper's contribution lies in its novel approach to watermarking order-agnostic models, which are increasingly important in various applications. By addressing the limitations of existing methods, the authors provide a valuable tool for ensuring the integrity and authenticity of generated text in these models. The framework's ability to embed and detect watermarks without requiring the original prompt makes it particularly useful in scenarios where access to the prompt is restricted or unavailable. The paper's findings suggest that this method has the potential to enhance the security and reliability of text generation systems, especially in the context of order-agnostic models. The authors' work opens up new avenues for research in watermarking for non-sequential generation models and provides a solid foundation for future developments in this area. The paper's clear presentation and comprehensive evaluation make it a valuable contribution to the field of watermarking for language models.
