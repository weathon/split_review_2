## Summary

This paper introduces a novel algorithm for fast constrained sampling in pre-trained diffusion models, addressing a critical need for efficient inference in these complex models. The core idea revolves around a numerical approximation of gradients, which circumvents the computationally expensive backpropagation process typically required in such tasks. This approach is motivated by the observation that image-conditioned generation relies more on local pixel correlations than on the intricate semantic relationships captured by text-to-image models. The authors propose that by focusing on these local correlations, they can achieve efficient and effective constrained sampling. The method is evaluated on standard image inpainting and super-resolution tasks, demonstrating comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods while significantly reducing inference time. Furthermore, the authors introduce a new task called 'image layer inference,' which aims to decompose an image into two layers, each representing different aspects of the image. This task is presented as an additional demonstration of the method's versatility. The experimental results, primarily on the ImageNet dataset, show that the proposed method achieves competitive performance in terms of PSNR, LPIPS, and FID scores, while requiring less computational time than existing methods. The authors emphasize the computational efficiency of their approach, highlighting the avoidance of backpropagation and the use of a numerical gradient approximation. The paper concludes by positioning the proposed method as a significant advancement in the field, offering a fast and high-quality constrained sampling algorithm for pre-trained diffusion models. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to efficient constrained sampling, with a focus on practical applicability and computational efficiency. However, as I will discuss, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened through more detailed analysis and experimentation.
