## Summary

This paper delves into the impact of weight decay on the update dynamics of individual neurons within neural networks, offering a novel perspective by analyzing these dynamics through a simplified random walk model. The authors' core contribution lies in their theoretical derivation of the equilibrium norm and angular update of neuron weights under this model, revealing how weight decay interacts with gradient descent to balance the rotation of weight vectors across neurons. This interaction, they argue, leads to a balanced rotational equilibrium, which is crucial for stable and efficient training. The paper further proposes a rotational variant of AdamW, termed AdamW-RV, which constrains the angular updates of weights, aiming to maintain this balanced equilibrium. The authors validate their theoretical findings through a series of experiments on various datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1k, and OpenWebText, and using different network architectures such as ResNet-20 and ResNet-50. These experiments demonstrate that the proposed AdamW-RV optimizer can achieve comparable performance to standard AdamW while potentially offering advantages in terms of training stability and convergence. The overall significance of this work lies in its attempt to provide a deeper understanding of the role of weight decay in neural network training, moving beyond traditional views that focus on the overall loss landscape. By analyzing the update dynamics at the neuron level, the authors offer a fresh perspective on how weight decay influences the optimization process. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, the paper's reliance on a simplified model and the limited scope of its experimental validation raise important questions about the generalizability and practical implications of its findings. The paper's theoretical analysis and the proposed AdamW-RV optimizer represent a valuable contribution to the field, but further research is needed to fully validate the claims and explore the potential of these ideas in more complex and realistic settings.
