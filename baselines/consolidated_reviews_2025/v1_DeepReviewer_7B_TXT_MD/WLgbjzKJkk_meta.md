## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to end-to-end Transformer-based multi-object tracking (MOT), addressing limitations in existing methods, particularly the label assignment strategy. The authors propose a cooperative and competitive label assignment (COLA) method, which allows tracked objects to be reassigned to detection queries in intermediate decoders, enhancing the flexibility and robustness of the tracking process. Additionally, they introduce a shadow set strategy, where each tracking query is augmented with multiple shadow queries, aiming to mitigate the one-to-one assignment problem and improve generalization, especially in crowded scenes. The core idea behind COLA is to move beyond the traditional approach of assigning only newborn objects to detection queries, allowing existing tracked objects to be revisited by detection queries in earlier layers. This is achieved by maintaining a set of shadow queries for each tracking query, which are trained to represent different aspects of the tracked object, thus providing redundancy and robustness. The authors also employ a one-to-set label assignment strategy, where each object is tracked by a set of queries rather than a single query, further enhancing the model's ability to handle occlusions and tracking failures. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several challenging MOT benchmarks, including DanceTrack, BDD100K, and MOT17. The authors also provide an efficiency comparison, showing that their method achieves comparable performance to other state-of-the-art methods while maintaining reasonable computational costs. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and effective approach to end-to-end MOT, with a clear focus on addressing the limitations of existing label assignment strategies and improving the robustness of the tracking process. The introduction of the shadow set strategy and the cooperative label assignment are significant contributions to the field, offering a promising direction for future research in end-to-end MOT.
