## Summary

This paper introduces Federated Dual Prompt Tuning (Fed-DPT), a novel approach to federated learning designed to address the challenges of domain shift and communication efficiency, particularly in the context of heterogeneous data. The core idea behind Fed-DPT is to leverage a pre-trained vision-language model, specifically CLIP, and apply both visual and textual prompt tuning to facilitate domain adaptation across decentralized data. The method operates by training domain-specific prompts for each client, which are then aggregated at the server. A key aspect of Fed-DPT is its parameter-efficient design, which aims to minimize communication costs by transmitting only the prompt parameters rather than the entire model. The authors evaluate their method on the DomainNet dataset, comparing it against several baselines, including standard CLIP, single-domain tuning, and other federated learning algorithms. The experimental results demonstrate that Fed-DPT achieves superior performance compared to these baselines, particularly in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The paper also explores the method's robustness under different data heterogeneity scenarios, including a decentralized setup where each domain is treated as a separate client. The authors emphasize the method's ability to adapt to diverse data distributions while maintaining communication efficiency, making it a practical solution for real-world federated learning applications. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to domain adaptation in federated learning, leveraging the power of pre-trained models and parameter-efficient tuning to achieve strong empirical results. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of experimental evaluation and analysis.
