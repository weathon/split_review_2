## Summary

This paper introduces MaskComp, a novel approach for object completion that leverages a mask denoiser and a segmentation model within an iterative refinement framework. The core idea revolves around refining both the mask and the generated object simultaneously, aiming to produce high-quality, complete objects from partial inputs. The method begins with an initial mask, often derived from the input image, and an initial object mask, typically a full mask. The process then iteratively refines these masks and the generated object through a combination of a generation and segmentation stage. Specifically, the generation stage employs a conditional diffusion model, where the conditioning is based on the current mask, to generate a refined object. Simultaneously, a segmentation model, such as a U-Net, is used to refine the mask based on the generated object. This iterative process continues until a desired level of completion is achieved or a maximum number of iterations is reached. The authors evaluate MaskComp on two datasets, AHP and DYCE, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to several baselines, including ControlNet, Kandinsky 2.1, and Stable Diffusion 1.5. The experimental results, measured using metrics like FID, show that MaskComp achieves competitive performance. The paper also includes an ablation study that explores the impact of different design choices, such as the number of diffusion steps and the degree of segmentation errors. The authors emphasize the importance of jointly modeling the generation and segmentation processes, arguing that this approach leads to better results than methods that focus solely on generation conditioned on the input image. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to object completion, combining a novel iterative refinement strategy with a well-established generative model. However, as I will discuss in detail, the paper has several limitations that need to be addressed to fully assess its potential.
