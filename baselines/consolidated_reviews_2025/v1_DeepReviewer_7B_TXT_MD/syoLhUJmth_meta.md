## Summary

This paper introduces COMM, a novel framework designed to enhance the visual capabilities of multi-modal large language models (MLLMs). The authors' core contribution lies in their investigation of the effectiveness of different visual encoders, specifically focusing on the shallow and deep layers of CLIP and DINOv2. Through a series of experiments, they observe that shallow layers of CLIP capture fine-grained details, which are crucial for tasks like grounding and region understanding, while deep layers of DINOv2 exhibit superior global understanding. Based on this analysis, they propose COMM, a feature fusion strategy that combines the strengths of both encoders. COMM integrates CLIP and DINOv2 features by concatenating and aligning features from shallow and deep layers using a multi-level feature merging (MFM) strategy. This approach is designed to leverage the detailed information from shallow layers and the global understanding from deep layers. The authors conduct extensive experiments across various benchmarks, including Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), Referring Expression Generation (REG), Object Hallucination (OH), and Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Image Captioning (IC). The results demonstrate that COMM outperforms existing methods, highlighting the effectiveness of their proposed fusion strategy. The paper's significance lies in its detailed analysis of visual encoders for MLLMs and the introduction of a simple yet effective feature fusion method that improves visual understanding. The authors' findings suggest that a more nuanced approach to visual encoding, considering both shallow and deep layers, can lead to substantial performance gains in MLLMs. The paper also provides a valuable insight into the behavior of different visual encoders, which can inform future research in this area. The authors' work contributes to a better understanding of how to effectively integrate visual information into language models, which is a critical aspect of multi-modal learning. The paper's empirical findings are supported by a comprehensive set of experiments, which validate the effectiveness of the proposed COMM framework. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the feature representations learned by different layers of the visual encoders, which is a valuable contribution to the field. Overall, this paper presents a well-motivated and empirically supported approach to improving the visual capabilities of MLLMs, which is a significant contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, there are several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. The most significant limitation is the lack of a detailed analysis of the underlying reasons why DINOv2's deep layers are better at global understanding than shallow layers. While the authors correctly point out that DINOv2's pre-training objective is to learn representations invariant to different views of the same image, they do not delve into the specifics of why this leads to better global understanding. The paper states that DINOv2's deep layers contain more fine-grained visual information, but it does not explain why this is the case. It is unclear whether this is due to the model learning to capture more abstract features at deeper layers, or if it is simply a consequence of the self-supervised pre-training objective. A more in-depth analysis of the feature representations at different layers, perhaps using techniques like t-SNE or UMAP, would be beneficial. This would allow for a visual inspection of how the features evolve from shallow to deep layers and whether the features in deeper layers cluster more according to semantic categories. Without this analysis, the claim that DINOv2's deep layers are better at global understanding is not fully supported by the evidence presented in the paper. This lack of analysis is a critical weakness, as it leaves a key question unanswered and weakens the overall argument for the proposed method.
