## Summary

This paper introduces AutoLoRa, a novel framework designed to enhance the adversarial robustness of pre-trained feature extractors (FEs) through a robust fine-tuning (RFT) process. The core contribution of AutoLoRa lies in its innovative approach to disentangling the optimization of natural and adversarial objectives. Instead of simultaneously optimizing both objectives through the FE, AutoLoRa employs a low-rank (LoRa) branch that focuses solely on natural objectives while the FE optimizes adversarial objectives. This disentanglement is achieved through a carefully designed loss function that incorporates a KL divergence term to transfer natural data knowledge to the FE, ensuring that the FE learns robust features while maintaining standard generalization capabilities. A key aspect of AutoLoRa is its automated hyperparameter scheduling, which eliminates the need for manual tuning of learning rates and scalars, making the framework more practical and accessible. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of AutoLoRa through extensive experiments across various downstream tasks, showing state-of-the-art adversarial robustness without requiring any manual hyperparameter tuning. The experimental results indicate that AutoLoRa achieves significant improvements in adversarial robustness compared to existing methods, including vanilla RFT and TWINS, while maintaining competitive performance on clean data. The paper's focus on disentangling the optimization process and automating hyperparameter tuning represents a significant step forward in the field of robust fine-tuning, offering a practical solution for enhancing the security of deep learning models against adversarial attacks. The use of a low-rank branch for disentangling objectives is a novel approach, and the empirical results are compelling, demonstrating the potential of the proposed method. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the lack of a comprehensive discussion of related work and a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of robust fine-tuning and offers a promising direction for future research.
