## Summary

This paper introduces a novel retrieval-augmented test-time adaptation (RA-TTA) method for vision-language models (VLMs). The core idea is to leverage external knowledge from a web-scale image database to enhance the model's adaptability to test data distributions. The method operates in two distinct stages: image-to-text retrieval and text-to-image retrieval. In the first stage, the method retrieves relevant text descriptions for a given test image by comparing its embeddings with those of the descriptions in the external database. From these retrieved descriptions, the most relevant ones are selected based on cosine similarity. In the second stage, the method retrieves external images that align with the selected text descriptions, again using cosine similarity. The final prediction is adjusted on the fly using a relevance score based on the semantic gap between the retrieved information and the test image. This relevance score is calculated by comparing the distances between the test image and the retrieved images in the embedding space. The authors conduct extensive experiments on 17 datasets, demonstrating that RA-TTA outperforms state-of-the-art test-time adaptation methods by 2.49-8.45% on average. The paper also includes ablation studies and hyperparameter sensitivity analyses to validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The authors argue that by incorporating external knowledge, RA-TTA can bridge the gap between pre-training data and test data, thereby improving the model's robustness and generalization. The method is presented as a simple yet effective approach to address the limitations of existing test-time adaptation techniques, which often rely solely on internal knowledge encoded within the model parameters. The paper's contribution lies in its practical application of retrieval-based methods to enhance the adaptability of VLMs, offering a promising direction for improving the performance of these models in real-world scenarios. However, as I will discuss, the paper has some limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize its potential.
