## Summary

This paper introduces CoPrompt, a novel fine-tuning method designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of vision-language models in few-shot learning scenarios. The core idea behind CoPrompt is to enforce a consistency constraint between the trainable components of the model and a pre-trained, frozen encoder. This is achieved by minimizing the cosine distance between the embeddings of the same image generated by both the trainable and pre-trained encoders. The authors argue that this consistency regularization helps to prevent overfitting, a common issue in few-shot learning where models often fail to generalize well to unseen data. In addition to the consistency constraint, CoPrompt incorporates adapters and prompts to further regularize the model and improve generalization. The adapters are used to fine-tune the pre-trained model, while the prompts are used to guide the model's behavior. The authors conduct extensive experiments across various datasets and tasks, including base-to-novel generalization, cross-dataset evaluation, and domain generalization. The results demonstrate that CoPrompt achieves state-of-the-art performance in these challenging scenarios, outperforming existing methods such as MaPLe, CoOp, and PromptSRC. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the contribution of each component of the proposed method, providing insights into the design choices. Overall, CoPrompt presents a promising approach to few-shot learning by combining consistency regularization with adapters and prompts, leading to improved generalization and performance on various benchmarks. The authors provide a clear motivation for their approach and explain the technical details of their method in a straightforward manner. The experimental results are comprehensive and well-designed, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed method. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are some areas where the paper could be improved, particularly in terms of the depth of analysis and the clarity of presentation.
