## Summary

This paper introduces a novel Q-learning algorithm, termed RegQ, designed to address the divergence issue that often arises when applying linear function approximation in reinforcement learning. The core idea behind RegQ is to incorporate a regularization term into the standard Q-learning update rule, aiming to stabilize the learning process and ensure convergence under linear function approximation. The authors motivate this approach by drawing parallels between Q-learning and off-policy temporal difference (TD) learning, suggesting that the regularization term helps to align the update with the principles of TD-learning regularization. The proposed algorithm is presented as a modification of the standard Q-learning update, where a regularization term, specifically an L2 norm of the parameter vector, is added to the update. This regularization term is inspired by similar techniques used in off-policy TD-learning algorithms. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of RegQ, demonstrating its convergence under certain conditions. Their analysis employs a switching system framework, which is a common approach in the analysis of reinforcement learning algorithms with function approximation. The theoretical results include a convergence guarantee for the algorithm and an upper bound on the approximation error of the learned value function. The empirical evaluation of RegQ is conducted on three sets of experiments. The first set compares RegQ with standard Q-learning and Greedy GQ on a Baird's counterexample, a well-known benchmark for evaluating the stability of reinforcement learning algorithms. The results show that RegQ converges faster than the baselines in this example. The second set of experiments compares RegQ with standard Q-learning and Greedy GQ on a tabular grid world environment. Again, the results indicate that RegQ converges faster than the baselines. The third set of experiments compares RegQ with standard Q-learning and Greedy GQ on a linearly approximated environment. The results show that RegQ converges faster than the baselines in this case as well. Overall, the paper aims to contribute a simple yet effective modification to the standard Q-learning algorithm that addresses a known limitation of Q-learning with linear function approximation. The authors provide both theoretical and empirical evidence to support their claims, although, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are some limitations to these claims. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to provide a practical solution to a common problem in reinforcement learning, but the lack of a detailed analysis of the proposed regularization term and the limited scope of the experimental evaluation raise questions about the generalizability and robustness of the proposed approach.
