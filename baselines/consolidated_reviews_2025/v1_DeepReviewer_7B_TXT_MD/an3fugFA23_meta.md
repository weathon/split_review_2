## Summary

This paper introduces Probability Distribution Estimation (PDE), a novel method designed to enhance the detection of machine-generated text from proprietary Large Language Models (LLMs). The core challenge addressed is the limited access to internal workings of these models, which typically only provide partial outputs from the model API, making traditional detection methods inapplicable. PDE tackles this by estimating the full probability distribution of an LLM given partial observations from the API. The authors propose three specific estimation algorithms: Geometric, Zipfian, and an MLP-based model, each aiming to approximate the full distribution from the partial data. The method is evaluated across five different proprietary LLMs, including GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT, and seven diverse languages, demonstrating significant improvements in detection accuracy compared to existing black-box methods. The empirical results show that PDE, particularly when applied to the latest LLMs like GPT-4 and Gemini, achieves near-perfect detection accuracy on some models and significantly outperforms existing methods on others. The paper also explores the robustness of PDE across different sources and domains, showing consistent performance gains. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the results, including ablation studies and comparisons with existing methods, and discuss the potential of PDE for real-world applications. The paper's significance lies in its ability to enable white-box detection methods to work with proprietary LLMs, addressing a critical gap in the field of AI security and text detection. By estimating the full probability distribution from partial observations, PDE offers a practical approach to detecting machine-generated text without requiring access to the internal workings of the model. This work contributes a valuable technique to the ongoing effort to ensure the responsible and ethical use of LLMs.
