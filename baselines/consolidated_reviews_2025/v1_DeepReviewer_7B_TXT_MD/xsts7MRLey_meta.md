## Summary

This paper introduces a comprehensive benchmark for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) in time series classification, addressing a critical gap in the existing literature. The authors present a novel benchmark comprising seven new time series datasets, covering diverse domain adaptation scenarios, and integrate them with seven established benchmark datasets, resulting in a total of twelve datasets. This benchmark is designed to facilitate fair and standardized comparisons between various UDA methods, which often lack a common evaluation framework. The paper also explores the impact of hyperparameter tuning on UDA performance, evaluating three different methods for selecting the best hyperparameters: Target Risk, Source Risk, and Importance Weighted Cross Validation (IWCV). The empirical evaluation encompasses nine UDA algorithms, including both adversarial and non-adversarial approaches, and examines the performance of three different model backbones. The results highlight the strengths and limitations of different UDA methods under various domain adaptation challenges, providing valuable insights into their effectiveness and robustness. The authors emphasize the importance of their benchmark for advancing research in UDA for time series data, offering a valuable resource for both researchers and practitioners. The paper also delves into the crucial aspect of hyperparameter tuning criteria, which is an important point in UDA, given the absence of labeled data in the target domain. The authors present a detailed analysis of the performance of different UDA methods under various domain adaptation scenarios, providing a comprehensive evaluation of their strengths and limitations. The paper's contribution lies in the creation of a standardized benchmark that allows for a more rigorous and fair comparison of UDA methods, which is essential for driving progress in this field. The authors also provide a thorough analysis of the impact of hyperparameter tuning on UDA performance, which is a critical aspect of practical application. The paper's findings offer valuable insights into the behavior of different UDA methods under various conditions, which can inform the development of more effective and robust techniques for time series classification in the presence of domain shifts.
