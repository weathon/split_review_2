## Summary

This paper introduces MultiIoT, a large-scale benchmark dataset designed to advance machine learning research in the Internet of Things (IoT) domain. The dataset comprises over 1.15 million samples across 12 diverse sensory modalities, including IMU, thermal, GPS, depth, audio, and others, collected from various real-world IoT devices. The authors aim to address the challenges of learning from multimodal data, capturing fine-grained interactions across long temporal sequences, and handling the inherent heterogeneity of real-world sensors. To this end, they curated 8 distinct tasks, ranging from activity recognition and gaze estimation to event detection and classification, all grounded in practical IoT applications such as personal wellness, healthcare, and smart cities. The paper also presents an empirical comparison of several modeling paradigms on this benchmark, including domain-specific models, unimodal models, unimodal multi-task models, multisensory models, and multisensory multitask models. The experiments demonstrate the performance of these models across different modalities and tasks, highlighting the importance of leveraging multimodal data for improved accuracy and robustness. The authors emphasize the scale and diversity of their dataset as key contributions, arguing that it provides a valuable resource for the community to develop and evaluate novel machine learning techniques for IoT applications. The paper serves as a benchmark for future research, providing a standardized platform for evaluating different modeling approaches in the context of IoT data. The authors also provide a detailed description of the data collection process, the preprocessing steps, and the experimental setup, which allows for reproducibility and further research. The paper concludes by discussing the potential impact of the benchmark on real-world IoT applications and the need for future work to address the limitations of the dataset and the models evaluated.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant attention. First, while the authors present MultiIoT as a novel benchmark, it primarily aggregates existing datasets from various modalities.
