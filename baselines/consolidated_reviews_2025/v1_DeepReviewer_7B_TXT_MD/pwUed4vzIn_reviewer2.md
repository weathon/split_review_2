### Summary

The authors present a computational model that investigates the role of spontaneous muscle activations (SMAs) in the development of flexible motor representations. The model is tested in three different scenarios: one where SMAs are not introduced, one where they are introduced before training, and one where they are introduced intermittently during training. The results show that intermittent SMAs lead to better performance and higher representational variability, suggesting that this approach enhances the adaptability and flexibility of motor representations.
