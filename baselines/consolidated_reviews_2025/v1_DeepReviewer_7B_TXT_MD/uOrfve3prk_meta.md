## Summary

This paper introduces a unified encoder-decoder framework designed to enhance the interpretability of large language models (LLMs) by enabling interventions on interpretable features. The core idea is to map intermediate representations within the language model to human-interpretable features, allowing for targeted interventions on these features to steer the model's behavior. The authors propose a framework that can accommodate several existing interpretability methods, including sparse autoencoders, logit lens, tuned lens, and probing, by viewing them through the lens of encoding and decoding. They introduce two new evaluation metrics: intervention success rate and coherence-intervention tradeoff, to quantify the effectiveness of these interventions. The intervention success rate measures whether increasing the activation of a feature increases the feature in the model's output, while the coherence-intervention tradeoff measures the model's coherence after intervention. The authors conduct experiments using three models—GPT2-small, Llama2-7b, and Gemma3-2b—on a dataset of 210 prompts related to various topics. The empirical findings suggest that simpler methods like logit lens and tuned lens generally outperform sparse autoencoders and probes in intervention capability. The study highlights the potential of the proposed framework for understanding and manipulating the internal workings of LLMs, offering a unified perspective on existing interpretability techniques. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to provide a common framework for evaluating and comparing different interpretability methods, which could facilitate further research in this area. However, the paper also reveals some limitations, particularly in the generalizability of the findings and the lack of a strong theoretical foundation for the proposed metrics. The authors acknowledge some of these limitations, but further work is needed to address them.
