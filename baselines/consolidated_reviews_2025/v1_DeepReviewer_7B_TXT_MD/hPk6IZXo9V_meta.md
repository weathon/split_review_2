## Summary

This paper introduces Neuro2Semantic, a novel two-phase framework designed to decode continuous language from intracranial neural signals. The core contribution lies in its innovative approach to aligning neural data with pre-trained text embeddings, enabling the reconstruction of semantic content without the need for predefined vocabularies or constrained text generation. The framework operates in two distinct phases: first, an LSTM adapter is trained to align neural signals with pre-trained text embeddings, effectively projecting neural data into a semantic space; second, a Vec2Text corrector, trained through a generative modeling approach, generates coherent text from these aligned embeddings. This two-phase training process allows the model to bypass the need for extensive labeled data, a significant advantage in the field of neural decoding. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Neuro2Semantic using a dataset collected from three subjects listening to podcast-like conversations. Their results show that the model achieves remarkable performance with as little as 30 minutes of neural data, significantly outperforming a baseline method in low-data settings. Furthermore, the model exhibits promising zero-shot generalization capabilities, successfully reconstructing semantic content from out-of-domain stories, highlighting its potential for real-world applications in brain-computer interfaces and neural decoding technologies. The authors also explore the impact of data scaling on model performance, showing that larger datasets lead to more accurate text reconstruction, and that the model's performance scales linearly with increasing data. The paper also includes a discussion of the model's limitations, acknowledging the high-dependence on pre-trained text embeddings and the Vec2Text corrector, as well as the potential for bias in the training data. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of neural decoding, offering a practical and efficient approach to translating neural signals into understandable text, with potential implications for brain-computer interfaces and neural decoding technologies.
