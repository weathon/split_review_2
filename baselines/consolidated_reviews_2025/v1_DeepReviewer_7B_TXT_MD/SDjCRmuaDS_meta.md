## Summary

This paper introduces MolMiner, a transformer-based model designed for multi-objective inverse design of molecules, specifically focusing on generating molecules that satisfy desired properties such as log-solubility, redox potential, and synthetic accessibility. The core idea behind MolMiner is a step-by-step, fragment-based generation process, where molecules are built up from a set of predefined fragments. This approach allows for the incorporation of chemical rules and constraints, ensuring the generated molecules are chemically valid. The model employs a semi-order-agnostic autoregressive architecture, which enables the generation of molecules in discrete steps, and integrates 3D information into the generation process. The model's architecture is inspired by order-agnostic autoregressive models, and it utilizes a transformer network with specific modifications to accommodate the fragment-based generation and 3D information. The model is trained on the RedDB dataset of organic compounds, and the training process involves maximizing the probability of selecting the true next fragment to be docked. The authors evaluate the model's performance by comparing it to other property prediction models, including AqSolPred (SARCOS), QuantumDeepField, and a custom model (FFNN). The results demonstrate that MolMiner can generate molecules that satisfy the desired properties, and the authors also perform a detailed analysis of the model's performance on a real-world dataset, including a calibration analysis. While the paper presents a novel approach to molecule generation, it also has some limitations, particularly in terms of the novelty of the core architecture, the choice of baselines for comparison, and the lack of a detailed computational cost analysis. The paper's main contribution lies in the specific combination of fragment-based generation, 3D information integration, and the geometry-weighted attention mechanism, which allows for the generation of chemically valid molecules with desired properties. However, the paper could be strengthened by addressing the identified limitations and providing a more comprehensive evaluation of the model's performance.
