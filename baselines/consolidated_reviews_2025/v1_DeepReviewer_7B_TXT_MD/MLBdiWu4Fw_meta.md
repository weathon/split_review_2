## Summary

This paper introduces InterVid, a large-scale video-text dataset comprising over 7 million YouTube videos, each annotated with detailed descriptions of actions and scenes. The authors aim to address the scarcity of high-quality video-language data by providing a resource for multimodal learning and generation. The dataset is curated from YouTube videos and includes detailed captions and temporal descriptions, which are generated using a combination of models, including CLIP and BLIP2. The authors also propose a video-language model, Video-CLIP, trained on InterVid, which achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot action recognition performance and competitive fine-tuned results. The paper explores various applications of InterVid, including video generation and dialogue systems, highlighting its potential for advancing research in multimodal video understanding and generation. The core methodology involves a multi-scale approach to caption generation, leveraging both short and long video segments to capture diverse temporal dynamics. The authors also introduce a spatiotemporal modeling approach for video-language modeling, which is used to train Video-CLIP. The empirical findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed dataset and model, showcasing their potential for improving performance in video-related tasks. The overall significance of this work lies in the creation of a large-scale, high-quality video-text dataset that can facilitate further research in multimodal learning and generation, particularly in the context of action recognition and video understanding.
