## Summary

This paper introduces a novel graph attention mechanism, termed Robust Graph Attention (RGA-IB), designed to enhance the robustness of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) against graph adversarial attacks. The core idea is to explicitly minimize the Information Bottleneck (IB) loss within the GNN's attention layers. The authors posit that by doing so, the attention mechanism will learn to focus on the most relevant features of the graph, while discarding noise or less pertinent details, thereby improving the overall robustness of the GNN. The proposed RGA-IB layer is designed to be a general and interesting contribution to the existing literature on graph attention mechanisms. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the relationship between graph attention and the IB loss, and it empirically demonstrates that RGA-IB exhibits lower IB loss and improved node classification accuracy compared to existing robust GNNs and robust attention-based GNNs on semi-supervised node classification under graph adversarial attacks. The authors evaluate their method on multiple datasets, showing consistent improvements in both robustness and accuracy. The paper's primary contribution lies in its explicit attempt to connect the information-theoretic concept of the IB loss with the practical problem of adversarial robustness in GNNs, and the empirical validation of this connection. While the paper presents a compelling approach, it also has some limitations that warrant further investigation, such as the lack of a formal proof for the core assumption and the need for a more thorough comparison with existing methods that also aim to minimize the IB loss. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field, but it also highlights the need for further research to address the identified limitations and to fully understand the theoretical underpinnings of the proposed method.
