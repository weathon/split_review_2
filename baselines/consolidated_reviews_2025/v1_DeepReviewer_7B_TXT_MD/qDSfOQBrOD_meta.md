## Summary

This paper introduces VChangeCodec, a novel neural speech codec designed for real-time voice-changing applications, such as online streaming services. The core contribution of this work lies in its integration of voice-changing functionality directly into the codec's architecture, enabling seamless switching between the original voice mode and customized voice change mode. This is achieved through a lightweight causal projection network that adapts timbre at the token level, allowing for high-quality voice conversion with minimal latency. The authors leverage a pre-trained neural codec as the backbone, freezing its encoder and decoder parameters and only training the lightweight projection network for timbre adaptation. This approach significantly reduces the computational overhead, making it suitable for real-time applications. The proposed method employs a scalar quantization technique to mitigate the high complexity of neural codecs, and it incorporates a token commitment loss to ensure high-quality timbre adaptation. The experimental results demonstrate that VChangeCodec outperforms state-of-the-art codecs in terms of timbre adaptation and speech quality, achieving an ultra-low 40ms latency. The system is designed to be suitable for bandwidth-constrained environments, such as online streaming, and the authors emphasize its potential for voice-changing applications in these contexts. The paper also highlights the system's ability to maintain a limited parameter size, which is crucial for real-time deployment. The authors provide a detailed description of the system architecture, including the encoder, decoder, and the causal projection network, and they also discuss the use of metadata, specifically openSMILE features, to extract acoustic information for timbre adaptation. The paper presents both objective and subjective evaluations of the proposed method, including comparisons with several state-of-the-art codecs and a voice-changing baseline. The results show that VChangeCodec achieves superior performance in terms of timbre adaptation and speech quality, while also maintaining a low computational cost. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in real-time voice-changing applications by integrating voice-changing functionality directly into the codec and achieving high-quality results with minimal latency and computational overhead. The proposed method has the potential to enable more immersive and personalized voice communication experiences in various online platforms.
