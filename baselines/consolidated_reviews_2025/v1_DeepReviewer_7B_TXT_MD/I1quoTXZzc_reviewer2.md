### Summary

The paper proposes Energy-based Concept Bottleneck Models (ECBMs), which unify concept-based prediction, concept correction, and conditional interpretation as conditional probabilities under a joint energy formulation. ECBMs use a set of neural networks to define the joint energy of candidate (input, concept, class) tuples. With such a unified interface, prediction, concept correction, and conditional interpretation are naturally represented as conditional probabilities $p(\cdot | \cdot)$, which are generated by composing different energy functions. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach on three datasets.

### Suggestions

The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with the proposed approach. The use of multiple neural networks to define the joint energy function likely increases the training and inference time compared to simpler concept bottleneck models. A thorough comparison of the computational complexity, including the number of parameters, training time, and inference time, would be beneficial. This analysis should also consider the scalability of the approach to larger datasets and more complex models. Furthermore, it would be helpful to provide a breakdown of the computational cost associated with each component of the model, such as the concept embedding network, the class embedding network, and the joint energy network. This would allow for a more precise understanding of the computational bottlenecks and potential areas for optimization.

To address the concern about the limited number of datasets, the authors should consider evaluating the proposed approach on a more diverse set of datasets. This could include datasets with different modalities (e.g., text, audio, time series), different sizes, and different levels of complexity. For example, evaluating the method on a dataset with a large number of concepts or a dataset with a high degree of concept entanglement would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's capabilities. Additionally, it would be beneficial to compare the performance of the proposed approach with other state-of-the-art concept bottleneck models on these additional datasets. This would provide a more robust evaluation of the method's effectiveness and generalizability. The authors should also consider reporting the performance of the proposed approach on each dataset separately, rather than just reporting the average performance across all datasets. This would allow for a more detailed analysis of the method's strengths and weaknesses on different types of data.

Finally, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach. While the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the approach on the selected datasets, it is important to acknowledge the potential limitations and challenges. For example, the paper should discuss the sensitivity of the approach to the choice of hyperparameters, the potential for overfitting, and the computational cost of training and inference. The authors should also discuss the assumptions made by the model and the potential impact of these assumptions on the performance of the model. A thorough discussion of the limitations would provide a more balanced and realistic assessment of the proposed approach.
