## Summary

This paper introduces Dual-Prism (DP), a novel graph data augmentation method grounded in spectral graph theory. The core idea revolves around selectively modifying the high-frequency components of a graph's Laplacian matrix while preserving its low-frequency components. This approach is motivated by the observation that low-frequency components of the Laplacian matrix are closely tied to crucial graph properties, such as connectivity and diameter, while high-frequency components are more sensitive to noise and perturbations. The authors propose two specific augmentation techniques: DP-Noise, which adds noise to the selected high-frequency eigenvalues, and DP-Mask, which sets selected high-frequency eigenvalues to zero. The method is presented as a spectral alternative to existing spatial-based graph augmentation techniques. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the relationship between graph properties and the graph spectrum, arguing that their method selectively modifies graph properties while ensuring diversity in augmented graphs. The empirical evaluation of the proposed method is conducted across a range of graph classification and transfer learning tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to several baseline augmentation methods. The experiments include comparisons with methods like DropEdge, DropNode, and Subgraph sampling, as well as more recent graph contrastive learning techniques. The results suggest that DP-Noise and DP-Mask can improve the performance of GNNs on various datasets. The paper's overall contribution lies in its novel spectral perspective on graph augmentation and its empirical validation of the proposed method's effectiveness. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in its lack of comparison with certain relevant graph augmentation methods and in the depth of its discussion on the limitations of the proposed method. Despite these shortcomings, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of graph data augmentation by introducing a novel spectral approach and demonstrating its potential through empirical results.

## Strengths

The authors clearly articulate the motivation behind their approach, connecting the spectral properties of the Laplacian matrix to key graph characteristics. This spectral perspective is a novel contribution, offering a different lens through which to view graph augmentation. The theoretical analysis, while not exhaustive, provides a solid foundation for the proposed method, demonstrating a clear understanding of the relationship between graph properties and the graph spectrum. The authors also present a thorough experimental evaluation of their method, comparing it against several relevant baselines across a range of graph classification and transfer learning tasks. The results consistently show that DP-Noise and DP-Mask can improve the performance of GNNs, which is a significant empirical achievement. The simplicity of the proposed method is also a strength. The implementation of DP-Noise and DP-Mask is straightforward, making it relatively easy to adopt and integrate into existing GNN frameworks. The paper's focus on preserving low-frequency components while selectively modifying high-frequency components is a key strength, as it allows the method to maintain the core structural properties of the graph while introducing diversity in the augmented graphs. This approach is well-aligned with the goal of creating effective graph augmentations that can improve the performance of GNNs. Finally, the paper's clear articulation of the method's workflow, including the selection of top N_a eigenvalues and the subsequent augmentation process, makes it easy to understand how the method is applied in practice. The inclusion of a workflow diagram further enhances the clarity of the method's implementation. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated approach to graph data augmentation, making a valuable contribution to the field.
