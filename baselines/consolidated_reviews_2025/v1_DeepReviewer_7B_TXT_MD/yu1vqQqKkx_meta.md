## Summary

This paper introduces LICO, a novel approach that leverages large language models (LLMs) for black-box optimization (BBO) in molecular optimization. The core idea is to adapt LLMs, typically used for natural language processing, to the domain of molecular optimization by incorporating molecular data into the LLM's input and output. Specifically, LICO extends the capabilities of LLMs by adding embedding and prediction layers for molecules and their associated scores. This is achieved by training the LLM on a combination of intrinsic molecular properties, such as molecular weight, and synthetic functions generated by Gaussian Processes. The training process is designed to enable in-context learning, allowing the LLM to perform molecular optimization tasks without the need for explicit task-specific training. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of LICO on the Practical Molecular Optimization (PMO) benchmark, showing that it outperforms several state-of-the-art BBO methods in terms of sample efficiency and accuracy. The key methodological innovation lies in the adaptation of LLMs to handle molecular data and the use of a semi-synthetic training strategy that combines intrinsic and synthetic functions. This approach allows the LLM to generalize to unseen molecular properties, a crucial aspect for practical applications. The empirical findings suggest that LICO achieves superior performance compared to existing methods on the PMO benchmark, highlighting its potential for advancing molecular optimization tasks. The overall significance of this work lies in its ability to bridge the gap between the power of LLMs and the specific requirements of molecular optimization, offering a new perspective on how LLMs can be used to tackle complex scientific problems. The paper is well-written and organized, making it easy to follow the methodology and results. The authors provide a clear explanation of the LICO framework, including the embedding and prediction layers, and the semi-synthetic training process. The empirical evaluation is thorough, comparing LICO against several state-of-the-art methods on the PMO benchmark. The ablation studies further provide insights into the method's behavior and performance. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of LICO.
