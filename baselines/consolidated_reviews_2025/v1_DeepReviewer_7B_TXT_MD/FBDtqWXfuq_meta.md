## Summary

This paper introduces Modality-Collaborated Federated Learning (MCFL), a novel framework designed to address the challenges of training models when clients possess data from only a single modality, while the overall goal is to benefit from data from multiple modalities. The authors propose FedCola, a method that leverages a modality-agnostic transformer to facilitate knowledge sharing across these single-modality clients. The core idea is to enable each client to contribute to the training of a global model, even when they only have data from one modality. This is achieved through a combination of attention sharing, modality compensation, and a temporal modality arrangement strategy. The attention sharing mechanism aims to enhance the performance of the transformer by allowing different modalities to attend to each other's features, even when they are not directly available to each client. The modality compensation strategy addresses the issue of varying numbers of trained parameters across modalities by prepending modality-specific parameters to the global model's weights. Finally, the temporal modality arrangement strategy is used to optimize the training process by dynamically adjusting the order of modalities. The authors evaluate FedCola on a variety of datasets, including CIFAR-100, AGNEWS, and MS-COCO, demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving performance gains for each modality and its efficiency in terms of communication and resource requirements. The empirical results show that FedCola outperforms existing methods such as Uni-FedAVG and CreamFL in the proposed MCFL setting. The paper's contribution lies in its introduction of a new federated learning setting and a corresponding framework that enables collaboration among clients with diverse data modalities, addressing a practical challenge in real-world applications. The authors also provide a resource analysis, comparing the computational and communication costs of FedCola with the baselines. Overall, the paper presents a significant step towards enabling federated learning in scenarios where clients have limited modality diversity, while still benefiting from the knowledge contained in other modalities.
