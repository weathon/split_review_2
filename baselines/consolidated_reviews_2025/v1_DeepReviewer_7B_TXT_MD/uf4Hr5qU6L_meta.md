## Summary

This paper introduces Problem Representation Enhanced Chain-of-Thought (PreCoT), a novel prompting framework designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). The core idea behind PreCoT is to explicitly extract and structure the initial and goal states from a given problem before applying the chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting technique. This approach is motivated by cognitive psychology, where problem-solving often involves identifying the initial state, goal state, and the steps needed to transition between them. The authors argue that by providing LLMs with this structured problem representation, they can guide the reasoning process more effectively, leading to improved performance on various reasoning tasks. The methodology involves a two-stage process: first, extracting the initial and goal states from the problem description, and second, using these representations to guide the LLM's reasoning through a CoT approach. The paper evaluates PreCoT on a wide range of benchmarks, including arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks, using two different LLMs: PaLM 2 and GPT-3. The experimental results demonstrate that PreCoT generally outperforms standard CoT prompting methods in both few-shot and zero-shot settings across these diverse tasks. The authors also conduct a manual error analysis on a subset of problems, comparing the types of errors made by PreCoT with those made by standard CoT. This analysis reveals that PreCoT tends to make fewer structural reasoning errors, suggesting that the explicit problem representation is beneficial. The paper's findings suggest that incorporating structured problem representations into the prompting process can be a promising direction for improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the areas of computational cost analysis and the exploration of the sensitivity of PreCoT to different problem representations. Despite these limitations, the work represents a valuable contribution to the field, offering a new perspective on how to leverage LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.
