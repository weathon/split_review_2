## Summary

This paper introduces CONCORD, a novel dataset distillation method that leverages large language models (LLMs) to enhance instance-level conceptual completeness during the diffusion-based sample generation process. The core idea is to use LLMs to extract fine-grained concepts from category labels, which are then used to guide the diffusion model in generating high-quality, diverse samples. This approach aims to address the limitations of existing dataset distillation methods that often produce generic and less detailed samples. The authors propose a concept-informed diffusion process, where the extracted concepts are incorporated into the diffusion denoising process, allowing the generated images to reflect the detailed characteristics derived from the LLMs. The method is evaluated on multiple benchmarks, including ImageNet-1K and its subsets, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and offering a promising direction for more controllable and effective dataset distillation. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components and hyperparameters, providing valuable insights into the method's behavior and performance. The paper's main contribution lies in its innovative use of LLMs to enhance instance-level conceptual completeness, which is a significant step forward in the field of dataset distillation. By focusing on instance-level details, the method addresses a critical gap in existing approaches, which often prioritize overall dataset quality at the expense of fine-grained details. The experimental results, which show consistent improvements over baseline methods, further validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the impact of different components and hyperparameters, which is crucial for understanding the method's behavior and for practical applications. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in dataset distillation, offering a more controllable and effective approach for generating high-quality, diverse samples.
