## Summary

This paper introduces Prompt Gradient Projection (PGP), a novel method for continual learning that combines prompt-tuning with gradient projection to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The core idea behind PGP is to leverage the strengths of both prompt-tuning, which allows for parameter-efficient adaptation to new tasks, and gradient projection, which offers theoretical guarantees against forgetting. The authors propose a specific approach to integrate these two techniques, focusing on the orthogonal condition of anti-forgetting for prompt gradient. They achieve this by conducting a Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on an element-wise sum space between input space and prompt space, which allows them to obtain the gradient projection matrix in an efficient manner. The method is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, demonstrating improved performance compared to existing continual learning methods. The empirical results show that PGP can effectively reduce forgetting and achieve a better balance between stability and plasticity. The authors also provide an ablation study to analyze the impact of different gradient projection manners and the distinct threshold used in their approach. The paper claims that PGP requires less memory space and avoids privacy leakage, as it does not rely on exemplar-based rehearsal methods. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to continual learning by integrating prompt-tuning and gradient projection, offering a new direction for research in this field. However, the paper lacks a detailed theoretical analysis of the proposed method, a thorough analysis of the computational cost, and a comprehensive discussion of the limitations and potential directions for future research. The experimental results, while promising, could be further strengthened by a more in-depth analysis of the forgetting behavior on a per-task basis and a discussion of the cases where the method performs poorly. The paper also lacks a detailed explanation of the experimental setup, including the specific hyperparameters used and the training procedure, making it difficult to reproduce the results and assess the robustness of the method. Despite these limitations, the paper provides a valuable contribution to the field of continual learning and opens up new avenues for future research.
