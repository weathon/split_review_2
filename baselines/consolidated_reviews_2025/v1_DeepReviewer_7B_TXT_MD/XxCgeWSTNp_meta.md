## Summary

This paper introduces a novel sampling algorithm for Lévy-Itô diffusion models, which are characterized by the use of α-stable noise instead of the Gaussian noise typically found in standard diffusion models. The core contribution lies in the introduction of a parameter, ηt, which allows for a flexible control over the amount of noise added at each reverse diffusion step. This parameter enables the algorithm to interpolate between standard deterministic sampling and stochastic sampling methods, offering a potentially more efficient approach, particularly when using a limited number of function evaluations (NFE). The authors derive a new stochastic differential equation (SDE) that incorporates this parameter, allowing for a more flexible reverse diffusion process. The method is presented as an extension of existing techniques, building upon the understanding that standard Itô diffusion models can be approximated by α-stable noise. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through experiments on image generation tasks, comparing their method against established techniques like DDIM and Euler-Maruyama. They show that their proposed method achieves improved performance with fewer NFE, suggesting a more efficient sampling process. Furthermore, the paper explores the applicability of the proposed method to imbalanced datasets, showcasing its potential for handling real-world data with varying sample sizes. The authors claim that their method is applicable to any diffusion model that uses an SDE to model the forward process, including various types of diffusion models, such as those used in image generation, audio synthesis, and video generation. However, the paper primarily focuses on image generation and text-to-speech modeling, leaving the generalizability of the method to other domains as an open question. The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, including experiments on image generation and text-to-speech modeling, and compares its performance against existing approaches. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the results, including ablation studies and visualizations, which helps to understand the behavior of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of diffusion models by introducing a novel sampling algorithm that offers a more flexible and efficient approach for generating samples. The method's ability to control the amount of noise added at each reverse diffusion step is a key innovation, which allows for a more nuanced exploration of the sample space. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, and the authors provide a thorough analysis of the results, which helps to understand the behavior of the proposed method. The paper's focus on Lévy-Itô diffusion models is also a significant contribution, as it opens up new possibilities for exploring the properties of these models and their potential applications. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in terms of the computational cost of the proposed method and its sensitivity to the choice of hyperparameters.
