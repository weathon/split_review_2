## Summary

This paper introduces a novel adaptive confidence distillation method, termed AdaptConf, designed to enhance the performance of student models in both weak-to-strong and strong-to-strong distillation scenarios. The core idea behind AdaptConf is to dynamically adjust the supervision provided by a teacher model based on the discrepancy between the teacher's soft labels and the student's hard predictions. Specifically, the method employs a weighting mechanism that adaptively balances the influence of the teacher's soft labels and the student's hard labels, aiming to leverage the strengths of both while mitigating the weaknesses. The authors argue that this adaptive approach allows the student model to learn more effectively, particularly in situations where the teacher model's predictions are not entirely reliable. The method is evaluated across a range of image classification tasks, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, and also includes experiments on noisy label learning and transfer learning. The empirical findings suggest that AdaptConf can outperform strong-to-strong distillation methods and improve the performance of teacher-student pairs with different architectures. The authors also explore the impact of various hyperparameters on the performance of the proposed method. While the paper presents a seemingly effective approach to knowledge distillation, my analysis reveals several critical limitations that need to be addressed. The core mechanism of AdaptConf bears a striking resemblance to Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (DKD), and the paper lacks a strong theoretical justification for its specific formulation. Furthermore, the experimental evaluation is limited to image classification tasks and does not include object detection or semantic segmentation, which are important vision tasks. The absence of experiments on large-scale datasets, such as LAION, also raises concerns about the scalability and practical applicability of the proposed method. These limitations, which I will discuss in detail, significantly impact the overall contribution and validity of the paper's claims.

## Weaknesses

My analysis has revealed several critical weaknesses that significantly impact the paper's contribution and validity. The most significant concern is the lack of novelty of the proposed method compared to Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (DKD). The core mechanism of AdaptConf, which involves a weighted combination of soft labels from a strong model and hard labels from a weak model, is very similar to the principles behind DKD. The paper introduces an adaptive weighting mechanism based on the confidence level of the strong model's predictions, but this mechanism does not fundamentally alter the underlying approach from DKD. The primary distinction is that AdaptConf uses the strong model's output as a proxy for a superior target, while DKD decouples the distillation process by using the teacher's soft probabilities and the student's hard predictions.
