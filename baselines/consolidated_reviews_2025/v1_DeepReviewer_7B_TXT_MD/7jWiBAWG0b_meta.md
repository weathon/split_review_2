## Summary

This paper delves into the realm of non-convex pairwise learning, a domain that has garnered considerable attention due to its relevance in various machine learning applications. The authors tackle a critical challenge in this area: the presence of heavy-tailed gradient noise, which often renders traditional analyses inadequate. The core contribution of this work lies in establishing stability-based learning guarantees for non-convex pairwise learning under heavy-tailed gradient noise, a setting that is more realistic than the commonly assumed bounded gradient scenario. The authors introduce the concept of 'pairwise $\ell_1$ on-average model stability,' a novel measure tailored to the pairwise loss function, which is central to their analysis. This stability notion allows them to derive generalization error bounds and optimization error bounds for non-convex pairwise learning. A significant achievement is the extension of these analyses to the minibatch case, which is a crucial step for practical applications. The paper's theoretical findings are supported by empirical results on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors also claim that their derived bounds are nearly comparable to existing results in the literature, which is a notable achievement. The paper's significance lies in its ability to provide a more robust theoretical foundation for non-convex pairwise learning, particularly in the presence of heavy-tailed noise, which is a common occurrence in real-world data. By relaxing the bounded gradient assumption and providing stability-based guarantees, this work advances our understanding of the generalization properties of pairwise learning algorithms. The extension to the minibatch case further enhances the practical relevance of the findings, making them applicable to large-scale learning scenarios. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience of researchers in machine learning and optimization. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by addressing a critical gap in the theoretical analysis of non-convex pairwise learning.
