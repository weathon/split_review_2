## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) by leveraging publicly available proxy datasets instead of relying solely on original data (OD) for quantization. The core idea is that proxy data, which is readily accessible and diverse, can guide the quantization process, potentially leading to improved accuracy. The authors propose a method to select the most suitable proxy data based on batch normalization statistics (BNS). Specifically, they define a distance metric using BNS to measure the similarity between a candidate proxy dataset and the original data. The proxy dataset with the smallest BNS distance to the original data is selected as the most appropriate proxy. The method is implemented within the FDDA framework, and the selected proxy data is used to replace the original data in the quantization process. The authors conduct extensive experiments on various computer vision datasets, including ImageNet-1K, CIFAR, and SVHN, using different model architectures such as ResNet and RegNet. The results demonstrate that the proposed method, termed ZeroP, achieves state-of-the-art performance in most cases, outperforming existing zero-shot quantization methods. The paper also includes an ablation study to analyze the impact of different proxy datasets and the BNS-based selection method. The authors claim that their approach can achieve competitive performance on multiple computer vision datasets, and that the proposed method can be generalized to different model architectures. Overall, the paper presents a simple yet effective method for improving zero-shot quantization by utilizing proxy data, and provides empirical evidence to support its claims. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the theoretical justification of the proposed method and the lack of a comprehensive analysis of computational costs and the impact of domain gaps between proxy and original data.
