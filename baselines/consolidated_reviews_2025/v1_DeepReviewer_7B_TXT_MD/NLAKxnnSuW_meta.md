## Summary

This paper introduces MEGA, a novel memory-efficient framework for 4D Gaussian Splatting, designed to address the high memory consumption associated with dynamic 3D scenes. The core contribution of MEGA lies in its innovative memory-efficient 4D Gaussian representation, which significantly reduces storage requirements without compromising rendering quality. This is achieved by decoupling the color attribute into a per-Gaussian direct current (DC) component and a shared lightweight alternating current (AC) color predictor. This approach effectively eliminates the need for spherical harmonics coefficients, which are computationally expensive and memory-intensive. Furthermore, MEGA incorporates an entropy-constrained Gaussian deformation technique, which expands the action range of each Gaussian and integrates a spatial opacity-based entropy loss to limit the number of Gaussians, thus enhancing the utilization rate of each Gaussian and reducing the overall memory footprint. The method's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on the Technicolor and Neural 3D Video datasets, where MEGA achieves significant storage reductions, exceeding 190x on the Technicolor dataset and 125x on the Neural 3D Video dataset, compared to the original 4DGS. These reductions are achieved while maintaining comparable rendering speeds and scene representation quality. The paper presents a clear and well-structured methodology, detailing the DC-AC color representation and the entropy-constrained Gaussian deformation technique. The experimental results, presented in tables and figures, showcase the superior performance of MEGA compared to the original 4DGS and other state-of-the-art methods. The paper also includes ablation studies that validate the effectiveness of the proposed components. Overall, MEGA presents a compelling approach to memory-efficient 4D Gaussian Splatting, offering a practical solution for deploying dynamic scene rendering in resource-constrained environments. The authors have successfully translated their theoretical insights into a practical framework that achieves significant memory savings without sacrificing rendering quality, making a valuable contribution to the field of neural rendering.
