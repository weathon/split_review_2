## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to example-based explanation for neural classifiers, leveraging the Kernelized Stein Discrepancy (KSD) to identify training samples that provide the best predictive support for a given test point. The core idea is to use KSD, a measure of discrepancy between probability distributions, to quantify how well a training sample explains a test point. The authors propose that the KSD between the test point and a training sample can be used to rank the training samples, with the top-ranked samples being considered the most influential. This method, termed HD-Explain, is presented as a way to generate more accurate and effective explanations compared to existing methods. The authors provide a theoretical justification for using KSD in this context, arguing that it captures the discrepancy between the model's predictions and the underlying data distribution. Empirically, the paper presents results on several image classification datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN, demonstrating the effectiveness of HD-Explain in generating explanations that are accurate and effective in terms of their fidelity, consistency, and computation efficiency. The paper also includes a quantitative evaluation of the method using metrics such as Hit Rate, Coverage, and Run Time, and qualitative evaluations that showcase the visual quality of the generated explanations. The authors compare HD-Explain against several baseline methods, including Influence Functions, TracIn, and RPS, and show that their method achieves superior performance in terms of these metrics. Overall, the paper presents a compelling new direction for example-based explanation by connecting the theoretical properties of KSD to the practical task of identifying influential training samples. The proposed method is well-motivated, and the empirical results provide evidence of its effectiveness. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are some limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this approach.
