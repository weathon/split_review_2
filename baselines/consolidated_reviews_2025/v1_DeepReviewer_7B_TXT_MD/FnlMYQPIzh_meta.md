## Summary

This paper introduces ConvINT, a novel semi-structured intention framework designed to enhance the understanding of user intentions in conversational AI by organizing them into four key aspects: situation, emotion, action, and knowledge. This framework aims to address the limitations of existing structured methods, which often struggle to capture the complexity and context-dependence of human intentions. To facilitate the generation of ConvINT data, the authors propose the Weakly-supervised Reinforced Generation (WeRG) method. WeRG leverages a combination of coarse-to-fine labels and weak supervision signals to efficiently expand ConvINT annotations across large datasets. The method employs a reinforcement learning approach to fine-tune a model, using a reward mechanism that incorporates the different levels of intention granularity. The experimental results, obtained on two conversational datasets, DuRecDial and ESConv, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed ConvINT framework and WeRG method in improving downstream tasks such as response generation and task completion. Specifically, the authors show that integrating ConvINT with WeRG leads to significant performance gains compared to baseline methods. The core contribution of this work lies in the introduction of a more nuanced and context-aware approach to intention understanding, coupled with an efficient data generation method that can scale to large datasets. The paper presents a well-structured methodology, with clear descriptions of the ConvINT framework and the WeRG algorithm. The experimental results are presented in a clear and concise manner, with comparisons against relevant baselines. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of conversational AI, offering a promising approach for enhancing the understanding of user intentions and improving the performance of conversational systems. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of practical applications, limitations, and comparisons with existing methods.
