## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance out-of-distribution (OOD) detection using normalizing flows, addressing a critical limitation identified in prior research: the tendency of these models to assign higher likelihoods to OOD samples characterized by low complexity. The authors propose a method that incorporates synthetic outliers during training and employs a softplus-based penalty for OOD samples during the training process. This approach aims to mitigate the bias towards low-complexity OOD samples, thereby improving the model's ability to distinguish between in-distribution and OOD data. The core idea is rooted in the hypothesis that normalizing flow models become more sensitive to variations in the latent space when processing less complex inputs, leading to the observed bias. To counter this, the authors generate synthetic outliers, which are designed to challenge the model's assumptions about data complexity, and penalize the likelihood of OOD samples using a softplus function, which is more effective than a simple L2 penalty. The method is evaluated on a range of image and text datasets, demonstrating improved performance in OOD detection compared to baseline methods. The authors also show that their approach increases the Lipschitz constant, which aligns with the hypothesis that a higher Lipschitz constant enhances robustness in OOD detection. The experimental results, while promising, are primarily focused on image and text data, and the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of OOD detection by addressing a specific bias in normalizing flows, but it also has some limitations that need to be addressed in future work. The authors provide a clear motivation for their approach, supported by a discussion of the underlying hypothesis, and the experimental results show a clear improvement over baseline methods. However, the lack of a detailed analysis of the computational cost and the limited range of datasets used in the evaluation are areas that need further investigation. The paper's significance lies in its ability to improve the robustness of normalizing flow models for OOD detection, which is a crucial step towards deploying these models in real-world applications where reliability and robustness are paramount. The authors' approach of using synthetic outliers and a softplus-based penalty is a novel contribution that has the potential to be applied to other types of models and tasks. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed method is a promising direction for future research in OOD detection, and it provides a solid foundation for further exploration of the bias in normalizing flows and its implications for model robustness.
