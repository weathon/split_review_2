## Summary

In this paper, I found a novel and well-organized approach to modeling interacting dynamical systems, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The authors introduce PGODE, a method that integrates object-level and system-level contexts using representation disentanglement and GNN prototypes within a graph ODE framework. This approach aims to enhance model expressivity and generalization, allowing for better handling of complex dynamics and system changes. The paper is well-structured and provides a comprehensive set of experiments that effectively demonstrate the performance of PGODE, showcasing its superiority over existing methods in both in-distribution (ID) and OOD settings. The theoretical analysis of the proposed model is also a strong point, enhancing the credibility of the paper. However, the paper could benefit from a more in-depth discussion of its limitations, a thorough comparison with existing methods in terms of computational aspects, and more detailed explanations of the experimental setup and results. These additions would provide a more complete understanding of the practical applicability and robustness of PGODE.

## Strengths

One of the key strengths of this paper is its well-organized and clearly written structure, which makes it easy to follow and understand. The authors have done an excellent job in presenting a novel approach, PGODE, that addresses the challenges of modeling interacting dynamical systems, especially in OOD scenarios. The integration of object-level and system-level contexts using representation disentanglement and GNN prototypes within a graph ODE framework is innovative and well-motivated. The paper's comprehensive set of experiments effectively demonstrates the performance of PGODE, showing significant improvements over existing methods in both ID and OOD settings. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed model, which adds to the credibility of the paper. The empirical validation is thorough, with results presented across multiple datasets, including physical and molecular dynamics simulations. The use of Mean Squared Error (MSE) as the primary evaluation metric is appropriate for the tasks at hand, and the authors clearly articulate the metrics used to assess the performance of PGODE. The paper's contributions are substantial, as it not only introduces a new method but also provides a detailed comparison with state-of-the-art techniques, highlighting the advantages of PGODE in terms of accuracy and generalization.

## Weaknesses

Despite the paper's strengths, there are several verified limitations that need to be addressed. First, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors thoroughly highlight the strengths of PGODE, a balanced perspective requires an exploration of potential weaknesses. For instance, the computational complexity of training and inference, especially when dealing with large-scale dynamical systems, is not discussed. This is a significant concern, as the scalability of the model with respect to the number of interacting entities and the length of the time series is crucial for practical applications. The paper should consider discussing the memory requirements and the time needed to train and inference, which would provide a more complete understanding of the practical applicability of PGODE. Additionally, the sensitivity of the model to hyperparameter tuning and the potential for overfitting, particularly when the amount of training data is limited, are not addressed. This analysis should be compared against the computational costs of other state-of-the-art methods, such as those that use different graph neural network architectures or ODE solvers. Understanding the robustness of PGODE in such scenarios would be valuable. 2. How does the model perform in scenarios with a large number of interacting entities? The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of PGODE on datasets with a moderate number of entities, but it would be beneficial to see how the model scales with an increasing number of interacting entities. 3. How does the model perform in scenarios with noisy or incomplete data? Real-world datasets often contain noise and missing values, and it is important to understand how PGODE handles such data. What are the specific parameter settings used in the experiments, and how do these settings affect the performance of PGODE?
