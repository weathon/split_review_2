## Summary

This paper introduces the "Tree-of-Table" (ToT) method, a novel approach designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) when dealing with complex tabular data. The core idea behind ToT is to structure table understanding as a hierarchical reasoning process, mimicking human cognitive approaches to problem-solving. The method operates in three key stages: table condensation, tree-based decomposition, and table-tree execution. Initially, the table is condensed to reduce its size and complexity, making it more manageable for LLMs. Subsequently, the condensed table is decomposed into a tree-based representation, where the original table is viewed as a proxy for the entire table-tree structure. This decomposition is based on the context of the question and any additional evidence provided. Finally, the table-tree is executed in a breadth-first manner, allowing the LLM to iteratively solve the sub-tables and synthesize the results to derive the final answer. The authors evaluate their approach on several benchmark datasets, including WikiTQ, TableFact, FeTaQA, and BIRD, demonstrating improved performance over existing methods. The experimental results suggest that ToT is particularly effective in handling large and complex tables, highlighting its potential for advancing table understanding in real-world applications. The paper also includes an efficiency analysis, showing that ToT achieves its results with fewer generated samples compared to other methods. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to table understanding, combining existing techniques in a novel way to achieve improved performance and efficiency. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of its theoretical grounding and the depth of its analysis.
