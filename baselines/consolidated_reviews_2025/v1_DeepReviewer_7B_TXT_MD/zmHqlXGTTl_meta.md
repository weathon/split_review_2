## Summary

This paper introduces a novel task, Layout-aware Scientific Poster Generation (LayoutSciPG), which aims to automatically generate visually appealing and informative scientific posters from existing research papers. The core idea is to move beyond simply extracting key information and instead focus on the holistic layout design, a crucial aspect of effective scientific communication. To address this challenge, the authors have made a significant contribution by constructing a large-scale dataset, SciPG, comprising over 10,000 pairs of scientific papers and their corresponding posters. This dataset serves as a valuable resource for future research in this area. The proposed methodology centers around a multimodal extractor-generator framework. The extractor is responsible for identifying and extracting key elements from the scientific papers, including both text and image content. This is achieved through a combination of a multimodal encoder, which leverages both text and image features, and a bidirectional long short-term memory (Bi-LSTM) network. The extracted elements are then fed into an interactive generator, which is designed to seamlessly integrate the paraphrased extracted elements with the layout generation process. A key component of this generator is an adaptive memory mechanism, which aims to handle long-term dependencies and improve the overall performance of the layout generation. The generator itself is based on a Transformer architecture, which is known for its ability to model complex relationships between different parts of the input sequence. The paper presents both qualitative and quantitative evaluations of the proposed approach. The quantitative evaluation focuses on metrics such as F1 score for text extraction, recall rate for image extraction, and overlap and coverage for layout generation. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, with comparisons against several baseline methods. The authors also conduct a topic-aware evaluation to assess the model's performance across different scientific domains. The findings suggest that the model is particularly effective at extracting images from specific topics, but it also highlights the challenges of cross-topic generalization. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of scientific communication by introducing a new task, a large-scale dataset, and a novel framework for automatic scientific poster generation. The work has the potential to significantly improve the efficiency and impact of scientific research by making poster creation more accessible and effective.
