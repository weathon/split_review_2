## Summary

This paper introduces STLLM, a novel framework designed for urban computing tasks, which integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) with a cross-view mutual information maximization paradigm. The core idea is to leverage the LLM's ability to capture implicit spatio-temporal dependencies and preserve point-of-interest (POI) information across urban space. The proposed method employs a graph neural network (GNN) to model spatial relationships and uses the LLM to generate semantic node representations based on text-derived features, including region information, POI data, spatial context, and temporal context. These LLM-based representations are then aligned with the GNN-based structural embeddings through a cross-view mutual information maximization objective. The framework is evaluated on three distinct urban computing tasks: crime prediction, traffic flow forecasting, and property price prediction. The experimental results demonstrate that STLLM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across various prediction tasks, highlighting its effectiveness in capturing complex spatio-temporal patterns and preserving POI information. The authors also conduct ablation studies to validate the efficacy of the proposed method, robustness analysis to assess performance under different data conditions, and efficiency studies to evaluate computational and memory requirements. The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, demonstrating its potential for addressing real-world urban computing challenges. The authors also include a case study to illustrate the model's ability to capture geographical semantics and its robustness to missing data. Overall, the paper presents a novel approach that effectively combines the strengths of LLMs and GNNs for urban computing tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple prediction tasks.
