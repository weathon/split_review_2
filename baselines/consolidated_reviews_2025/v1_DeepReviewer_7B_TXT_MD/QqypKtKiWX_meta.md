## Summary

This paper introduces SimBOL, a data augmentation method designed to enhance the localization of early ventricular activation origin (SoA) in electrocardiogram (ECG) signals. The core idea behind SimBOL is to leverage the physical characteristics of ECG signals by performing resampling based on the onset of pacing signals. This approach aims to increase the diversity of the training data without significantly increasing its size, which is particularly important in medical applications where data is often limited. The authors propose a small-scale 1D convolutional neural network (CNN) to classify the location of the SoA, using the augmented data to train the model. The method is evaluated on a pacing-site dataset, where the goal is to predict the location of the pacing site from the ECG signal. The authors demonstrate that SimBOL achieves a localization error of 9.83 mm, which is below the clinical acceptable error of 10 mm. This result is presented as a significant improvement over existing methods. The paper also includes an analysis of the impact of different data augmentation techniques and the effect of varying the amount of training data. The authors show that SimBOL is robust to changes in the amount of training data and is less sensitive to input data structure. The paper also discusses the clinical relevance of their method and its potential for real-world applications. The authors emphasize the balance between model complexity and the amount of training data, arguing that their approach effectively mitigates overfitting. Overall, the paper presents a simple yet effective data augmentation method for SoA localization, supported by empirical evidence and a thorough analysis of the impact of different components. The authors also discuss the limitations of their method and potential future work, which is a sign of a well-rounded and thoughtful study. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are some limitations that need to be addressed to strengthen the paper's claims and impact.
