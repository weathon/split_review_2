## Summary

This paper investigates the in-context learning (ICL) capabilities of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, a class of models that has been underexplored in this area compared to decoder-only models. The authors identify a gap in the literature and embark on a systematic exploration of ICL in seq2seq models, proposing two primary methodological approaches: prompt design and fusion-based methods. The prompt design approach focuses on aligning prompts with the pretraining objectives of the models and designing prompts that suit the task, while the fusion-based approach explores different ways to combine examples during the ICL process. Specifically, the authors investigate early-fusion and late-fusion strategies, where early-fusion processes the encoder outputs and the decoder inputs jointly, and late-fusion processes the decoder outputs. The core empirical findings demonstrate that seq2seq models, when properly aligned with their pretraining objectives and using effective prompt designs, can achieve performance comparable to or even surpassing decoder-only models in few-shot learning tasks. The authors conduct extensive experiments on a variety of tasks, including natural language understanding (NLU) tasks from the SuperGLUE benchmark and generation tasks from the XSum and WebNLG datasets. The results consistently show that the proposed methods significantly improve the few-shot learning ability of seq2seq models. The paper also includes an analysis of the permutation bias of the fusion-based approaches, showing that the order of examples does not significantly affect performance. The authors further demonstrate that the proposed methods are effective in reducing the standard deviation of performance across different permutations. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution by highlighting the potential of seq2seq models for ICL and by proposing practical methods to enhance their few-shot learning capabilities. The findings suggest that seq2seq models, when properly aligned and prompted, can be a viable alternative to decoder-only models for ICL tasks, and the proposed fusion-based methods offer a promising direction for further research in this area. The paper's focus on both theoretical and empirical aspects of ICL in seq2seq models makes it a valuable contribution to the field, despite some limitations in the analysis of computational cost and the exploration of failure cases.
