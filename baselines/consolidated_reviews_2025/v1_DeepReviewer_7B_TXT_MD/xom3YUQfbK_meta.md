## Summary

This paper introduces the Model Manager framework, a novel approach designed to facilitate the comparison of machine learning models by generating natural language descriptions of their differences. The core idea is to serialize model outputs and inputs into a JSON format, which is then used as a prompt for an LLM to produce verbalizations of the models' behaviors. The authors propose that these verbalizations, which capture the distinctions between models, can serve as a means of model comparison, potentially enhancing transparency and explainability in machine learning. The methodology involves selecting a representative sample of input instances, obtaining corresponding model outputs, and serializing this data along with the models' internal structures (when available) into a JSON format. This JSON data is then fed into an LLM, which generates a textual description of the models' differences. The authors evaluate their framework on three classification datasets—Blood, Diabetes, and Car—using Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, and K-Nearest Neighbors models. They employ a zero-shot prompting strategy, where the LLM is prompted to generate verbalizations without fine-tuning. The evaluation focuses on the accuracy of the LLM's verbalizations in predicting the outputs of one model based on the descriptions of another. The results indicate that the framework performs well in verbalizing differences between Logistic Regression models, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieving the best performance. However, the framework faces challenges when comparing Decision Trees and K-NN models. The paper concludes by discussing the potential of the Model Manager framework and suggests future research directions, such as exploring more sophisticated prompting strategies and expanding the framework's applicability to other machine learning tasks. Overall, the paper presents an interesting approach to model comparison, but it also reveals several limitations that need to be addressed for the framework to be practically useful.
