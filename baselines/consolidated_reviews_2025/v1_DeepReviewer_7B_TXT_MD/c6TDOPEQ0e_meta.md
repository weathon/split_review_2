## Summary

This paper introduces LASP-2, a novel sequence parallelism (SP) approach designed to enhance the efficiency of training linear attention models, particularly for handling very-long input sequences. The core contribution of LASP-2 lies in its reorganization of computations and communication, aiming to minimize latency and improve overall throughput. The method achieves this by overlapping communication operations with computation, thereby reducing the time spent on data movement. Specifically, LASP-2 employs a single all-gather collective communication to transfer key states across devices, which is designed to be more efficient than traditional all-reduce operations. The paper also introduces LASP-2H, an extension that integrates LASP-2 with standard attention modules, enabling the model to handle both linear and standard attention layers within a unified framework. The experimental results, conducted on a Llama3 model with 1B parameters, demonstrate that LASP-2 and LASP-2H achieve significant throughput improvements compared to existing SP methods like LASP-1 and Ring Attention, particularly when dealing with very long sequences. The authors present a theoretical analysis of the communication and computation costs of LASP-2, further supporting its efficiency claims. The paper's significance lies in its potential to address the computational bottlenecks associated with long sequences in linear attention models, making it a valuable contribution to the field of large language model training. However, the paper's evaluation is limited to a single model architecture and hardware setup, which raises questions about the generalizability of the findings. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a well-motivated and clearly explained approach to sequence parallelism, with promising empirical results that warrant further investigation.
