### Summary

This paper presents a new method for fair classification when sensitive attributes are not available. The method is based on a VAE architecture, where a high confidence generator is trained on high confidence data, and a low confidence generator is trained on low confidence data. The high confidence generator is trained with ground truth labels, while the low confidence generator is trained with pseudo labels generated by the high confidence generator. Experiments on two datasets show that the proposed method outperforms state of the art fair classification methods.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more thorough analysis of the computational demands of the proposed method. Specifically, a detailed breakdown of the time complexity for each stage of the algorithm, including the VAE training, the pseudo-label generation, and the final classification, should be provided. This analysis should not only consider the asymptotic complexity but also provide empirical measurements of training time and memory usage on the datasets used in the experiments. Furthermore, a comparison of these metrics against existing fair classification methods would be crucial to understand the practical trade-offs of using the proposed approach. For example, it would be useful to know if the method's computational cost is primarily driven by the VAE training or the pseudo-label generation, and how these costs scale with the size of the dataset. This would allow readers to better assess the method's applicability to different problem settings.

In addition to the computational analysis, a more comprehensive sensitivity analysis is needed to understand the robustness of the proposed method. The paper should explore how the performance of the method varies with different values of key hyperparameters, such as the confidence threshold used to split the data into high and low confidence sets, the learning rate for training the VAE and the generators, and the number of training epochs for each component. For each hyperparameter, the paper should present a range of values and show how the performance metrics (e.g., accuracy, fairness metrics) change. This analysis should not only focus on the average performance but also on the variance across different runs. It would be beneficial to identify the optimal hyperparameter settings for each dataset and to discuss the sensitivity of the method to deviations from these settings. This would provide practical guidance for users of the method and help them understand the trade-offs involved in choosing specific hyperparameter values.

Finally, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. This discussion should not only focus on the theoretical limitations but also on the practical challenges that may arise when applying the method to real-world datasets. For example, the paper should discuss the potential impact of the choice of the confidence threshold on the performance of the method, and how to choose an appropriate threshold for a given dataset. It should also discuss the potential limitations of the VAE architecture and how these limitations may affect the performance of the method. Furthermore, the paper should discuss the potential for bias in the pseudo-labels generated by the high confidence generator and how this bias may propagate to the final classification. A thorough discussion of these limitations would provide a more balanced view of the method and help readers understand its potential drawbacks.
