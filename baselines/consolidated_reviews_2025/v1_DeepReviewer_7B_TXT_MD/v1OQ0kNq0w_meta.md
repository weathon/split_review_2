## Summary

This paper introduces MotionRL, a novel approach to text-to-motion generation that leverages multi-reward reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize for a combination of text adherence, motion quality, and human preferences. The authors address a critical gap in current text-to-motion generation research, where human perception is often overlooked. Unlike traditional methods that primarily focus on optimizing motion quality and text alignment, MotionRL integrates human perception models to capture nuanced human preferences, enabling the generation of motions that are more natural and human-like. The core of MotionRL lies in its multi-objective optimization strategy, which approximates Pareto optimality within each batch of samples. This is achieved through a batch-wise Pareto selection strategy, which allows the model to generate motions that effectively balance the trade-offs between the three objectives. The method employs a reward function that combines text encoder, motion encoder, and human perception model components, with learnable weights that regulate the strength of each reward signal. The authors evaluate MotionRL on the HumanML3D dataset, demonstrating superior performance in terms of both traditional metrics and human perceptual model scores. The experimental results show that MotionRL achieves better performance in terms of human alignment, which is a critical factor for real-world applications. The paper also includes a qualitative comparison with other methods, visually demonstrating the improved alignment of MotionRL with human preferences. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of text-to-motion generation by introducing a method that explicitly considers human perception and balances multiple objectives using a novel batch-wise Pareto selection strategy. The results suggest that MotionRL is a promising approach for generating human-like motions that are both textually aligned and perceptually natural. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the areas of computational cost analysis, generalizability, and detailed comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods, which I will discuss in more detail in the following sections.
