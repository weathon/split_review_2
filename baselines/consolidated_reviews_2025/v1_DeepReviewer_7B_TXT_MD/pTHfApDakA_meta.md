## Summary

This paper introduces a novel self-checking method designed to enhance the accuracy of multi-step reasoning in large language models (LLMs). The core idea revolves around a four-stage process: target extraction, information collection, step regeneration, and result integration. In the target extraction stage, the LLM identifies the core action within each step. The information collection stage then filters out irrelevant information to focus on the essential components of the step. Next, the step regeneration stage uses the LLM to create an independent alternative solution based on the previous steps. Finally, the result integration stage compares the original solution with the generated alternative to determine the correctness of each step. The method's effectiveness is evaluated on three datasets: GSM8K, MathQA, and MATH, demonstrating improved final answer accuracies compared to baseline methods. The authors also explore the method's robustness by varying the number of generated solutions and analyzing the verification performance. The key finding is that by using the LLM's ability to extract the target and generate alternative solutions independently, they can improve the overall accuracy of LLMs on multi-step reasoning tasks. This approach is particularly noteworthy because it does not require additional training data or external resources, making it a zero-shot method. The authors also show that their method can be used to improve the performance of LLMs even when the LLM's initial reasoning is not as accurate as other LLMs. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the method's performance, including ablation studies and comparisons with other verification techniques. Overall, this work presents a promising approach for improving the reliability of LLMs in complex reasoning tasks, offering a practical and efficient solution for enhancing their performance without the need for extensive training or external resources.
