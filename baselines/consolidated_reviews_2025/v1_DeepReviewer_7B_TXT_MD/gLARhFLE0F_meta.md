## Summary

This paper introduces LUT-GEMM, a novel kernel designed to accelerate inference in large language models (LLMs) by leveraging quantized weights without the need for dequantization. The core idea revolves around utilizing binary-coded quantization (BCQ) to represent both weight and activation matrices, enabling efficient matrix multiplication through a lookup table (LUT). The authors propose an extended BCQ format that supports both uniform and non-uniform quantization, offering flexibility in the compression ratio and accuracy trade-off. The LUT-GEMM kernel is constructed by pre-computing the results of binary multiplications and lookups, which are then used to perform matrix multiplication at a lower computational cost. The authors demonstrate that LUT-GEMM can achieve a 2.1x speedup over existing methods like OPTQ while maintaining comparable accuracy. The experimental evaluation primarily focuses on the OPT-175B model and LLaMA models, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method in accelerating token generation latency. The paper also explores the impact of different quantization levels on the final performance, showing that LUT-GEMM can achieve comparable accuracy with lower quantization levels. The authors claim that LUT-GEMM reduces memory footprint and improves overall system performance by eliminating the need for dequantization, which is a common bottleneck in quantized matrix multiplication. While the paper presents a promising approach for accelerating LLM inference, it lacks a comprehensive analysis of the method's limitations, particularly concerning its applicability to different model architectures and hardware configurations. The evaluation is primarily focused on a single type of GPU, and it is unclear how the method would scale to multi-GPU setups or different hardware platforms. Additionally, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the memory footprint of the proposed method, nor does it provide a thorough comparison with other state-of-the-art quantization techniques. Despite these limitations, the paper introduces a novel approach that has the potential to significantly improve the efficiency of LLM inference.
