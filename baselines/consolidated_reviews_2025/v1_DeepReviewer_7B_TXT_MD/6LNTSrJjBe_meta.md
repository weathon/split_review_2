## Summary

This paper introduces Language Agent Tree Search (LATS), a novel framework designed to enhance the reasoning and planning capabilities of language models (LMs) by synergizing their acting and planning processes. The core idea behind LATS is to leverage Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to explore multiple action trajectories, incorporating external feedback and self-reflection to guide the search process. The framework operates by expanding a tree of possible actions, where each node represents a state comprising the original input, action sequence, and observation sequence. A language model is then used to evaluate the value of each state, combining both a self-generated LM score and a self-consistency score. This value function is crucial in guiding the search process, allowing the model to prioritize promising trajectories. Furthermore, upon encountering an unsuccessful terminal node, the model is prompted to provide a verbal self-reflection, summarizing errors and proposing alternative actions. This self-reflection mechanism is a key aspect of LATS, enabling the agent to learn from its mistakes and adapt its behavior. The authors evaluate LATS across diverse domains, including programming, interactive question-answering (QA), web navigation, and mathematical problem-solving, demonstrating its versatility and performance. The empirical results show that LATS achieves competitive performance, often outperforming baseline methods, particularly in tasks requiring complex reasoning and planning. The paper also includes ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of each component of the framework, such as the self-reflection mechanism and the tree-based search. Overall, LATS presents a promising approach to enhancing the decision-making capabilities of language models by integrating planning, acting, and self-reflection within a unified framework. The framework's ability to learn from feedback and adapt its behavior makes it a valuable contribution to the field of language model-based agents.
