## Summary

This paper introduces DPFormer, a novel approach for training Transformers with differential privacy (DP), addressing two key challenges: the computational overhead associated with per-sample gradient clipping and the issue of attention distraction within the attention mechanism. The authors propose two main techniques: Phantom Clipping and Re-Attention. Phantom Clipping is designed to efficiently compute per-sample gradient norms for parameters that are shared across the input and output embeddings in a Transformer, thereby reducing the computational burden of DP-SGD. This method leverages the parameter sharing to avoid instantiating the embedding layer twice, leading to a more efficient gradient computation. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the memory and time complexity of Phantom Clipping, demonstrating its potential for significant speedups compared to standard per-sample gradient clipping. The second technique, Re-Attention, aims to mitigate the attention distraction phenomenon, which occurs when the attention mechanism overemphasizes tokens with high variance, regardless of their relevance. To address this, the authors propose a method to correct the attention scores based on an error multiplier derived from the variance of the noise introduced by DP-SGD. This correction is intended to steer the attention mechanism towards more relevant tokens. The authors present experimental results on two real-world datasets, MovieLens and Amazon, demonstrating the effectiveness of DPFormer in improving the performance of DP Transformers. The experiments compare DPFormer against vanilla Transformers, Transformers without parameter sharing, and other baseline methods like GRU and LSTM. The results show that DPFormer achieves better performance than these baselines, suggesting that the proposed techniques are effective in improving the performance of DP Transformers. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to make DP training of Transformers more practical and efficient, which is a crucial step towards deploying privacy-preserving machine learning models in real-world applications. By addressing the computational bottlenecks and the attention distraction issue, the authors contribute to the growing body of research on differentially private deep learning, particularly for sequence models.
