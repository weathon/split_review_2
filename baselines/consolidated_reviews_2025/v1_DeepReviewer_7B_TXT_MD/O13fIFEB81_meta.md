## Summary

This paper introduces a novel, training-free watermarking method for diffusion models, aiming to embed watermarks directly into the latent space, thereby minimizing visual quality impact and enhancing robustness against adversarial attacks. The authors propose a unified framework that identifies and connects key design principles of existing watermarking methods, focusing on three critical dimensions: the distribution of individual elements, the specification of watermark regions within each channel, and the choice of channels for watermark embedding. This framework is presented as a systematic approach to understanding the strengths and limitations of existing techniques. The core of the proposed method involves embedding watermarks directly into the latent space of diffusion models, which the authors argue reduces computational overhead and avoids the need for extensive model retraining. The method employs a distribution-preserving watermarking technique, utilizing a random Gaussian watermarking with Redundant and Dispersed Watermarks to minimize visual quality impact. The watermark embedding strategy is a hybrid approach that combines the strengths of different methods, dynamically selecting the watermarking technique based on element distribution and channel selection. The method is evaluated on both text-to-image and image-to-image diffusion models, demonstrating superior performance in terms of image quality and robustness against a range of adversarial attacks, including geometric transformations, noise addition, and cropping. The empirical results, measured using FID and CLIP scores, suggest that the proposed method maintains high image quality while providing robust watermarking performance. The authors claim that their method is training-free and operates directly on the latent space of diffusion models, which reduces computational overhead and avoids the need for extensive model retraining.
