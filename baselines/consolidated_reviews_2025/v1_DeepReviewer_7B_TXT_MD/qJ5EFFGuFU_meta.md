## Summary

This paper introduces a semantic-aware implicit representation (SAIR) method for image inpainting, aiming to address the limitations of traditional implicit neural representation (INR) approaches that primarily focus on appearance information. The authors propose a two-module architecture: a semantic implicit representation (SIR) module and an appearance implicit representation (AIR) module. The SIR module, built upon a modified CLIP model, infers semantic embeddings for arbitrary pixel coordinates, effectively integrating semantic information into the inpainting process. The AIR module then reconstructs the color of these pixels based on the inferred semantic embeddings and the original image's appearance. The authors evaluate their method on the CelebAHQ and ADE20K datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing INR-based inpainting methods across several quantitative metrics, including PSNR, SSIM, L1, and LPIPS. The core idea is that by incorporating semantic information, the inpainting process can better handle degraded images and produce more coherent results. The paper also includes an analysis of the SIR module's ability to reconstruct semantic features, showing improved performance on semantic segmentation tasks. While the paper presents a seemingly effective approach to semantic-aware inpainting, my analysis reveals several limitations, particularly concerning the novelty of the method and the scope of the experimental evaluation. The method's reliance on existing INR and CLIP architectures, while combined effectively, raises questions about its fundamental innovation. Furthermore, the primary focus on image inpainting, without exploring other downstream tasks, limits the assessment of the method's true potential. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a clear and well-written description of the proposed method and its evaluation, providing a solid foundation for future research in this area. However, the identified weaknesses need to be addressed to fully validate the claims made by the authors and to establish the method's true novelty and impact.
