## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to panoramic image-to-image (Pano-I2I) translation, addressing the challenges of geometric distortions and limited datasets by leveraging pinhole images as a target domain. The authors propose a versatile encoder and spherical positional embedding to bridge the gap between panoramic and pinhole images, along with a distortion-free discrimination technique and panoramic rotation augmentation to handle large domain gaps and discontinuities. The method is validated on the StreetLearn and Init datasets, demonstrating superior results in both quantitative and qualitative assessments. The core idea revolves around using pinhole images, which are readily available, as a target domain for training, which helps in learning the mapping from the more challenging panoramic images. The authors employ a two-stage training process, where the content encoder and style encoder are pre-trained using panorama-to-pinhole projection. The proposed method incorporates a novel distortion-free discrimination technique to ensure that the generated panoramic images are geometrically consistent with the input pinhole images. Additionally, the method uses panoramic rotation augmentation to improve the robustness of the model to large domain gaps. The experimental results, including both quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations, suggest that the proposed method effectively translates between panoramic and pinhole images, outperforming existing methods. The authors also conduct a user study to evaluate the perceptual quality of the generated images, further supporting their claims. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to Pano-I2I translation, with promising results on real-world datasets. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are some limitations in the practical applications and computational cost that need to be addressed.
