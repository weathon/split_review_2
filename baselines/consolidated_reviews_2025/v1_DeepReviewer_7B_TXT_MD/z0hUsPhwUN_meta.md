## Summary

This paper introduces Control-GIC, a novel image compression framework that builds upon the VQGAN architecture. The core innovation lies in its ability to control the bitstream rate by leveraging the entropy of image patches. Specifically, the method employs a granularity-informed encoder that maps image patches into a hierarchical structure of three granularities, determined by the entropy of the patches. This allows for a flexible bitrate control by selectively including finer-grained representations based on a probability derived from the entropy. The hierarchical granularities are then fed into a probabilistic conditional decoder, which reconstructs the image by aggregating the multi-granularity features in a conditional probability manner. This decoder predicts the features of the encoder by searching for the codebook, conditioned on the selected multi-granularity representations. The authors present experimental results on the Kodak dataset, demonstrating that Control-GIC achieves better rate-distortion performance compared to several state-of-the-art image compression methods. The method aims to address the limitations of existing generative image compression methods by enabling more precise and variable bitrate control. The authors also provide an analysis of the inference time, comparing the encoding and decoding times of their method with several baselines. The paper's main contribution is the integration of entropy-based granularity control within a VQGAN framework, which allows for flexible and efficient image compression. The authors also introduce a probabilistic conditional decoder that aims to improve the realism of the reconstructed images. While the paper presents a promising approach, several limitations need to be addressed to fully realize its potential. These include the lack of comparison with state-of-the-art diffusion-based image compression methods, a limited discussion of the method's limitations, and a lack of detailed analysis of the computational complexity and memory usage. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of image compression and provides a solid foundation for future research.
