## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to quantifying synergistic interactions in semi-supervised multi-modal learning settings, a problem of significant importance given the increasing prevalence of multi-modal data. The core contribution lies in the development of lower and upper bounds for multi-modal synergy, which are derived from the mutual information between modalities and the disagreement between unimodal classifiers. The authors propose a method that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data, addressing a practical challenge in semi-supervised learning. The theoretical framework is grounded in information theory, with a focus on estimating the shared information between modalities and its relationship to downstream task performance. The authors validate their approach through experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of their bounds in estimating multi-modal model performance, guiding data collection, and selecting appropriate multi-modal models. The empirical findings suggest that the proposed bounds can be used to make informed decisions about data acquisition and model selection, potentially leading to more efficient and effective multi-modal learning. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to bridge the gap between information-theoretic measures and practical applications in multi-modal learning, offering a new perspective on how to quantify and utilize synergistic interactions between modalities. The authors also explore the relationship between interaction estimates and downstream task performance, assuming optimal multimodal classifiers are trained on labeled multimodal data, and provide a theoretical framework for predicting multimodal model performance before even training the model itself. The paper also discusses the limitations of the proposed approach, such as the assumption of accurate classifiers and the potential impact of noisy or missing data. Overall, this work presents a valuable contribution to the field of multi-modal learning by providing a novel method for quantifying and utilizing synergistic interactions between modalities, with potential implications for a wide range of applications.
