## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to imitation learning (IL) by leveraging behavior foundation models (BFMs) based on the forward-backward (FB) framework. The core idea is to pre-train a BFM using successor measures, which allows for the rapid imitation of expert policies from a few demonstrations. The authors propose FB-IL, a method that leverages this pre-trained BFM to perform imitation learning without requiring extensive reinforcement learning (RL) training. This is achieved by utilizing the BFM's ability to estimate the distribution of optimal policies, enabling the computation of forward-backward discrepancies that guide the learning process. The method is evaluated on a range of tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite, demonstrating competitive performance against state-of-the-art offline IL algorithms while achieving significantly faster training times. The key innovation lies in the ability to perform fast and efficient IL from few expert demonstrations by leveraging the pre-trained BFM, which bypasses the need for online interaction or complex RL procedures. The authors demonstrate that FB-IL can recover imitation policies in seconds, which is a substantial improvement over existing offline IL methods that often require hours of training. The paper provides a theoretical foundation for the method, showing that it satisfies the three properties required for effective IL: it can be pre-trained on unsupervised data, it can implement different IL principles, and it can perform imitation in a few seconds. The experimental results show that FB-IL achieves comparable or better performance than state-of-the-art offline IL baselines, while also being significantly faster. The authors also explore the use of FB-IL for different IL principles, such as reward-based and goal-conditioned imitation, demonstrating the versatility of the approach. Overall, this work presents a compelling method for fast and efficient imitation learning, addressing a critical need in the field by reducing the time required to learn policies from demonstrations. The use of BFMs provides a promising direction for developing more efficient and scalable IL methods, and the empirical results demonstrate the potential of this approach.
