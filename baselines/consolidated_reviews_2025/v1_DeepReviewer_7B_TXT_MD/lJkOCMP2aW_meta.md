## Summary

This paper introduces Pathformer, a novel multi-scale transformer architecture designed for time series forecasting. The core idea behind Pathformer is to address the limitations of existing transformer models in capturing diverse temporal patterns by employing a multi-scale approach. The authors propose a multi-scale division module that divides the input time series into patches of varying sizes, allowing the model to capture both local and global temporal dependencies. This division is followed by a multi-scale transformer block, which utilizes both intra-patch and inter-patch attention mechanisms to model temporal correlations at different scales. Furthermore, the model incorporates an adaptive pathway mechanism that dynamically selects the optimal patch sizes for each time series, based on the temporal dynamics of the input data. This adaptive selection is achieved through a multi-scale router, which extracts periodicity and trend patterns from the data and uses these patterns to determine the most suitable patch sizes. The authors evaluate Pathformer on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to existing time series forecasting models. The experimental results show that Pathformer achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy, suggesting that the proposed multi-scale approach is effective in capturing complex temporal patterns in time series data. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the contribution of different components of the model, such as the multi-scale router and the adaptive pathways. Overall, the paper presents a well-structured and clearly written contribution to the field of time series forecasting, offering a promising approach to multi-scale modeling with transformers. However, as I will detail in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened through more detailed explanations and analyses.
