## Summary

This paper introduces a cascade framework designed to enhance the cost-efficiency of reasoning tasks using large language models (LLMs). The core idea is to leverage a weaker, less computationally expensive LLM for easier questions and a stronger, more expensive LLM for more difficult ones. The authors propose two methods for determining the difficulty of a question: a vote-based method and a verification-based method. The vote-based method assesses whether the weaker LLM can consistently answer the question across multiple sampling paths, while the verification-based method checks if the answers from the weaker LLM are consistent with the outputs of a stronger LLM. The authors conduct experiments on six reasoning datasets, comparing their proposed cascade approach against using only the stronger LLM. The results demonstrate that the proposed cascade framework achieves comparable task performance to using only the stronger LLM, while significantly reducing the computational cost, requiring only 40% of the token cost. The paper's primary contribution lies in its practical approach to balancing cost and accuracy in LLM-based reasoning, offering a cost-effective solution for complex tasks. However, the paper's novelty is limited by its similarity to existing methods that use consistency for routing decisions, and the evaluation is limited to reasoning tasks, which may not fully capture the general applicability of the proposed method. The paper also lacks a thorough comparison with other cost-efficient methods, such as prompt engineering or ensemble methods, and does not include a baseline where only the weaker LLM is used for all tasks. These limitations, along with the lack of analysis on the limitations of the proposed method, such as its performance when the weaker LLM consistently makes errors or when its consistency is low, raise questions about the robustness and generalizability of the approach. Overall, while the paper presents a practical and cost-effective solution for reasoning tasks, it requires further investigation to address the identified limitations and to fully establish its novelty and contribution.

## Strengths

I found several aspects of this paper to be commendable. The core idea of using a cascade framework to leverage the strengths of both weaker and stronger LLMs is both intuitive and practical. The authors clearly articulate the motivation behind their approach, which is to reduce the computational cost of using expensive LLMs for all tasks while maintaining acceptable performance. The proposed methods, vote-based and verification-based, are simple and easy to understand, making the approach accessible and implementable. The experimental results are compelling, demonstrating that the proposed cascade framework achieves comparable task performance to using only the stronger LLM, while significantly reducing the computational cost. Specifically, the paper reports that the cascade approach achieves comparable task performance with only 40% of the token cost of using the stronger LLM alone. This cost reduction is a significant achievement and highlights the practical value of the proposed method. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which enhances its readability and accessibility. The authors clearly explain their methodology and provide a thorough description of their experimental setup. The use of multiple sampling paths to assess the consistency of the weaker LLM is a reasonable approach, and the authors provide a clear explanation of their consistency measures. The paper also includes a detailed analysis of the results, which provides valuable insights into the performance of the proposed method. Overall, I believe that the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of LLM-based reasoning, offering a practical and cost-effective solution for complex tasks.

## Weaknesses

Despite its strengths, this paper has several notable weaknesses that I have verified through careful examination. First, the novelty of the proposed method is limited. While the authors frame their approach as a cascade framework, the core idea of using a weaker LLM for easier tasks and a stronger LLM for harder tasks is not entirely new. As noted by multiple reviewers, methods like 'Self-Consistency rectifies itself' also leverage consistency for routing decisions. The paper lacks a thorough comparison with these existing methods, particularly those that also use consistency for routing decisions. Specifically, the authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the differences in the consistency mechanisms used by their approach and existing methods, nor do they analyze how these differences impact the routing decisions and overall performance. This lack of comparison makes it difficult to assess the novelty and specific contributions of the proposed method in the context of consistency-based routing. The paper also does not compare against prompt engineering or ensemble methods, which are other cost-efficient reasoning techniques. This omission makes it difficult to understand the relative advantages and disadvantages of the proposed approach compared to these alternatives. The paper's evaluation is also limited in scope. The authors only evaluate their proposed method on reasoning datasets, which may not fully capture the general applicability of the approach. The paper does not include experiments on other types of tasks, such as translation or summarization, which would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's strengths and weaknesses. Furthermore, the paper does not include a baseline where only the weaker LLM is used for all tasks. This is a critical omission, as it makes it difficult to assess the cost-performance trade-off of the proposed method accurately. The authors compare their method against using only the stronger LLM, but they do not demonstrate that their method provides a better cost-performance trade-off than simply using the cheaper LLM for all tasks. This comparison is essential for evaluating the practical utility of the proposed approach. The paper also lacks a discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. The authors do not address scenarios where the weaker LLM consistently makes errors or when its consistency is low due to ambiguous or poorly phrased questions. This could lead to the cascade framework incorrectly routing questions to the weaker LLM, even when the stronger LLM is needed. The paper does not discuss how the method would perform in such cases, which is a significant limitation. Finally, the paper does not include a detailed analysis of the failure modes of the proposed method. The authors do not investigate scenarios where the proposed method performs worse than the cheaper LLM, and they do not explore the cases where the proposed method is less effective. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to understand the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method and its potential for real-world applications. In summary, while the paper presents a practical and cost-effective solution for reasoning tasks, it requires further investigation to address the identified limitations and to fully establish its novelty and contribution. The lack of comparison with existing consistency-based routing methods, the limited task diversity, the absence of a baseline using only the weaker LLM, and the lack of analysis of failure modes are all significant weaknesses that need to be addressed.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct a more rigorous comparison with existing methods that utilize consistency for routing decisions, such as 'Self-Consistency rectifies itself'. This comparison should not only focus on the final performance but also on the computational cost and the number of LLM calls required. A detailed analysis of how the proposed method's consistency measure differs from existing methods and how these differences impact the routing decisions and overall performance is crucial. This would help to clarify the novelty and specific contributions of the proposed method in the context of consistency-based routing. Second, the authors should include a baseline where only the weaker LLM is used for all tasks. This is crucial for understanding the trade-offs between cost and performance. The authors need to demonstrate that their method provides a better cost-performance trade-off than simply using the cheaper LLM, even if it means sacrificing some accuracy. This comparison is essential for evaluating the practical utility of the proposed approach. Third, the authors should expand the evaluation of their method to include a more diverse set of tasks beyond reasoning. This could include tasks such as translation, summarization, or question answering on different domains. This would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's general applicability and robustness. The authors should also analyze the performance of their method on tasks that require different types of reasoning or knowledge. This analysis should include a discussion of the types of tasks where the proposed method performs well and the types of tasks where it is less effective. Fourth, the authors should include a more detailed analysis of the failure modes of the proposed method. Specifically, the authors should investigate scenarios where the weaker LLM consistently makes errors or when its consistency is low due to ambiguous or poorly phrased questions. This analysis should include a discussion of the types of questions where the proposed method is most effective and the types of questions where it is less effective. Furthermore, the authors should explore the possibility of using the cheaper LLM for tasks where it performs well and the more expensive LLM for tasks where it excels, potentially leading to a more cost-effective and accurate system. Fifth, the authors should compare their method with other cost-efficient reasoning methods, such as prompt engineering or ensemble methods. This would provide a more complete picture of the advantages and disadvantages of the proposed method. The authors should analyze the cost of prompt optimization to optimize prompts for the weaker LLM versus the cost of using the proposed cascade framework. Similarly, the authors should compare the cost of using ensemble methods to combine the predictions of multiple LLMs versus the cost of using the proposed cascade framework. Finally, the authors should explore the use of more sophisticated consistency measures. The current approach relies on a simple majority vote or a consistency check based on the output of the weaker LLM. However, there may be more advanced consistency measures that could provide more reliable routing decisions. For example, the authors could explore the use of techniques from the field of uncertainty quantification to estimate the confidence of the weaker LLM's predictions. This could involve using Bayesian methods or other techniques to model the uncertainty of the weaker LLM's predictions. This would allow for more informed routing decisions, and potentially improve the overall performance of the cascade framework. By addressing these points, the authors can significantly strengthen their paper and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the proposed method.
