### Summary

This paper proposes a novel fairness approach that does not rely on sensitive attributes. It uses a confidence-based framework to analyze the COMPAS dataset and identifies disparities in model fairness across different confidence levels. The paper introduces a dual-model system where a high-confidence generator is trained on a subset of data with high confidence scores, while a low-confidence generator is trained on the remaining data. The high-confidence generator is used to generate pseudo-labels for the low-confidence generator, which is then updated using these pseudo-labels. The paper also introduces learnable noise into the dataset to retain only essential information for predictions, thereby improving both accuracy and fairness. The proposed method is evaluated on the COMPAS and New Adult datasets and is shown to outperform state-of-the-art baselines in terms of fairness metrics while maintaining competitive accuracy.
