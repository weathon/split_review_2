## Summary

This paper introduces State Decoupling with Q-Supervised Contrastive Representation (SDQC), a novel framework designed to enhance the safety of offline reinforcement learning (RL) in safety-critical scenarios. The core idea behind SDQC is to decouple global observations into reward-related and cost-related representations, thereby improving the generalization capability of safe offline RL. This decoupling is achieved through a Q-supervised contrastive learning method, which clusters states based on their similarity in Q-values, enabling the agent to make safer decisions in unseen environments. The authors provide a theoretical foundation for their approach, demonstrating that SDQC generates a coarser representation while preserving the optimal policy. Empirically, the paper evaluates SDQC on the DSRL benchmark, showing that it outperforms other baseline algorithms, particularly in terms of achieving near-zero violations in more than half of the tasks. The paper's contributions are significant, as they address the challenge of out-of-distribution (OOD) states in offline safe RL, a critical issue for real-world applications. However, the paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of its limitations, particularly regarding scenarios where reward and cost signals are highly entangled, and a more comprehensive comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods.

## Weaknesses

Despite its strengths, the paper has several verified limitations that warrant attention. One of the primary concerns is the lack of a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed decoupling approach. Specifically, the paper does not explore scenarios where the reward and cost signals are highly entangled and cannot be easily decoupled based on Q-value similarity. In such environments, the reward and cost representations might not be easily separable, leading to suboptimal performance. For instance, in complex dynamics or sparse reward/cost functions, the reward and cost signals might not be clearly distinguishable, and the method's ability to handle these challenges is not discussed.
