## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach, termed Counterfactual Regression with Delayed Feedback (CFR-DF), for estimating heterogeneous treatment effects (HTEs) in the presence of delayed feedback. The core contribution lies in explicitly modeling the time it takes for an outcome to be observed after treatment, a critical aspect often overlooked in traditional HTE estimation methods. The authors formulate the problem by defining potential outcomes and response times, and they provide theoretical proofs of the identifiability of these causal estimands under certain assumptions. The proposed method employs a modified Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm to simultaneously predict potential outcomes and response times, leveraging counterfactual regression techniques. The empirical evaluation of CFR-DF is conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving HTE estimation accuracy compared to existing methods. The paper's significance stems from its ability to address a common challenge in many real-world applications, such as online marketplaces and healthcare, where delayed feedback is prevalent. By explicitly modeling the response time, the proposed method offers a more realistic and accurate approach to HTE estimation in these settings. The authors' approach is particularly relevant in scenarios where the timing of the observed outcome is as important as the outcome itself, and where the treatment effect may not be immediately apparent. The paper's theoretical contributions, including the identifiability proofs, provide a solid foundation for the proposed method, enhancing its credibility and applicability. The empirical results, while promising, highlight the potential of the method to improve HTE estimation in practical settings. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field of causal inference by addressing the important problem of delayed feedback in HTE estimation, and it provides a solid foundation for future research in this area.
