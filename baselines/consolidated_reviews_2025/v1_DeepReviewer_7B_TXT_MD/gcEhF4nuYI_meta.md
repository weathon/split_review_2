## Summary

This paper introduces a fine-grained token pruning approach for large language models (LLMs), aiming to reduce computational costs without requiring retraining. The authors propose a method that selectively skips token computation within each block of the model, leveraging a learnable router to dynamically determine which tokens are less important and can be spared. This router is trained using a combination of static and dynamic factors, including token position, attention scores, and gradient information. The core idea is to prune tokens at the end of each block, which the authors claim makes their approach more fine-grained compared to block-wise or neuron-wise pruning methods. The method is evaluated on several LLMs, including Llama2-7B, Llama2-13B, and Qwen1.5-7B, across various benchmarks, and the results demonstrate that the proposed method, termed FTP, outperforms existing pruning techniques in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The authors also explore the compatibility of their method with the key and value (KV) cache, showing that it can further enhance inference speed. The paper's main contribution lies in the proposed token-wise pruning strategy and the dynamic router training framework. The experimental results suggest that this approach can achieve significant speedups during inference while maintaining competitive accuracy. However, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational overhead introduced by the dynamic router itself, and it does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other token pruning methods, particularly those based on attention scores or gradients. Additionally, the paper does not fully explore the limitations of the proposed method, such as its performance on tasks requiring long-range dependencies, and it does not discuss potential future research directions. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a promising approach to token pruning that warrants further investigation.
