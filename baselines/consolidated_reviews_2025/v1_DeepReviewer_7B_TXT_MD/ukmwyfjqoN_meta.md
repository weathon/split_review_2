## Summary

This paper introduces a novel video enhancement network, termed Recurrent Bottleneck Mixer Network (ReBotNet), designed for real-time processing of surveillance videos. The authors address the limitations of existing methods, which often suffer from high computational costs and latency, by proposing an architecture that leverages a dual-branch structure operating at both spatio-temporal and image levels. The core innovation lies in the recurrent training setup, where the prediction from the previous frame is used as an additional input to enhance temporal consistency. This approach aims to capture temporal redundancy inherent in real-world videos, thereby improving the efficiency of the enhancement process. The authors also curate two new datasets, PortraitVideo and FullVideo, specifically designed to evaluate the method's performance under various real-world conditions, including multiple degradations such as blur, noise, and compression artifacts. The experimental results, conducted on these datasets, demonstrate that ReBotNet achieves state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced computational cost and inference time compared to existing methods. The paper emphasizes the practical applicability of their method for live video enhancement, highlighting its potential for real-time video conferencing and surveillance applications. The authors also explore the use of a recurrent training setup to further improve the temporal consistency of the enhanced videos. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to real-time video enhancement, with a focus on practical applicability and performance on real-world datasets.
