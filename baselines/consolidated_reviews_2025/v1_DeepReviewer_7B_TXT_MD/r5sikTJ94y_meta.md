## Summary

This paper introduces Reshape and Adapt for Output Quantization (RAOQ), a novel approach designed to mitigate the impact of ADC quantization errors in In-Memory Computing (IMC) systems. The core motivation stems from the inherent challenges of ADC quantization, particularly its detrimental effect on signal-to-quantization-noise ratio (SQNR) in analog implementations of IMC. The authors propose a two-pronged strategy to address this issue, encompassing both algorithmic and training-time optimizations. At the algorithmic level, RAOQ incorporates two key techniques: Activation Shifting (A-shift) and Weight Reshaping (W-reshape). A-shift aims to shift activations to avoid zero, thereby maximizing the 2nd moment of the activation distribution and improving SQNR. W-reshape, on the other hand, involves increasing the variance of weights to facilitate effective training. These algorithmic modifications are intended to shape the data distributions in a way that is more resilient to the distortions introduced by ADC quantization. Furthermore, RAOQ includes a Bit Augmentation (BitAug) method during training, which adapts to ADC quantization by augmenting the input data distribution. The method is implemented through a two-stage training process, where first, QAT is performed for activations and weights only, and then RAOQ is applied in another stage with ADC quantization introduced. The authors conduct extensive experiments across various tasks, including image classification (using ResNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNet models), object detection (using YOLOv5s), and natural language processing (using BERT-base and BERT-base/large). The empirical results demonstrate that RAOQ achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across these tasks, even at practical ADC bit precisions, thus highlighting its effectiveness in addressing the challenges of ADC quantization in IMC. The paper also includes an analysis of the energy efficiency of IMC, modeling it from existing works and comparing it with digital accelerators. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and empirically supported approach to a critical problem in the field of IMC, offering a practical solution for maintaining accuracy in the presence of ADC quantization errors.
