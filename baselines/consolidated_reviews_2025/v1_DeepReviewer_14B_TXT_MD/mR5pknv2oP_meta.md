## Summary

This paper introduces SECToR (Self-Education via Chain-of-Thought Reasoning), a novel self-learning method designed to enable language models to acquire new skills without relying on human-generated training data. The core idea behind SECToR is to leverage chain-of-thought reasoning as a policy improvement operator, drawing a parallel to how Monte-Carlo Tree Search is used in AlphaZero. The method begins with an initial supervised fine-tuning phase, where the model is trained on simple addition problems with a limited number of digits. Subsequently, the model enters a self-training loop. In this loop, the model is prompted to solve addition problems using chain-of-thought reasoning. The solutions generated during this process are then used to fine-tune the model further, allowing it to solve similar problems directly, without the need for explicit chain-of-thought reasoning. This iterative process continues, with the model progressively tackling more complex problems. The authors demonstrate that this approach allows the model to autonomously learn to add numbers with up to 29 digits, starting from a base knowledge of only 6-digit additions. The paper's central hypothesis is that chain-of-thought reasoning can act as a policy improvement operator, enabling the model to improve its problem-solving capabilities through self-generated solutions. The authors present a detailed description of the method, including the self-training loop and the use of self-consistency checks to mitigate error propagation. The results, while promising, are limited to the task of addition, and the paper does not include comparisons to other existing self-learning methods. The authors acknowledge that the method is computationally expensive, requiring significant resources to generate the training data for each iteration. Overall, the paper presents an interesting approach to self-learning, but its limitations in terms of generalizability and lack of comparative analysis need to be addressed in future work. The potential of this method to reduce reliance on human-generated data is significant, but further research is needed to validate its effectiveness across a broader range of tasks and to improve its computational efficiency.
