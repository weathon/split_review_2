The authors present a neural organoid simulation framework (NOSF) to simulate the interaction experiments with real organoids. The NOSF uses neural computing models to enable stimulation, response, and learning. The authors also present a benchmark to evaluate the NOSF.

The authors propose a neural organoid simulation framework to simulate the interaction experiments with real organoids. However, the motivation of the paper is unclear. The authors do not explain why we need to simulate the interaction experiments with real organoids. The authors also do not explain the benefits of the proposed framework.

### Suggestions

The paper needs to clearly articulate the specific limitations of current experimental methods for studying organoid-machine interactions that the proposed simulation framework aims to address. For example, are current methods too slow, too expensive, or lack the necessary control for systematic exploration? The authors should provide a detailed explanation of how the simulation framework overcomes these limitations. This should include a discussion of the specific parameters and variables that the simulation can manipulate, and how these relate to real-world experimental conditions. Furthermore, the authors should provide concrete examples of how the simulation can be used to generate hypotheses that can then be tested experimentally. This would help to demonstrate the practical utility of the framework and its potential to accelerate research in this area.

The authors should also provide a more detailed explanation of the neural computing models used in the simulation framework. What type of neural networks are used? How are these models trained? What are the inputs and outputs of these models? The authors should also discuss the limitations of these models. For example, how well do these models capture the complex dynamics of real neural organoids? What are the potential sources of error in the simulation? A thorough discussion of these points will help to establish the validity and reliability of the simulation framework. It is also important to clarify how the simulation framework handles the stochastic nature of biological systems. Are random variations in the simulation controlled, and how does this affect the reproducibility of the results?

Finally, the authors need to provide a more detailed explanation of the benchmark used to evaluate the simulation framework. What are the specific metrics used in the benchmark? How do these metrics relate to the performance of the simulation framework? The authors should also provide a comparison of the simulation results with experimental data to validate the accuracy of the simulation. This comparison should include a discussion of the limitations of the benchmark and the potential for future improvements. The authors should also discuss the computational cost of the simulation framework and how this might limit its applicability. A clear understanding of these points is essential for assessing the practical value of the proposed framework.
