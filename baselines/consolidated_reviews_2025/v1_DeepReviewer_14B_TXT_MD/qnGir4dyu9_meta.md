## Summary

This paper introduces RACCooN, a novel two-stage framework for video editing that operates through a video-to-paragraph (V2P) process followed by a paragraph-to-video (P2V) process. The core idea is to leverage the power of large language models (LLMs) to generate detailed textual descriptions of video content, which are then used to guide a video diffusion model for editing tasks. In the V2P stage, the framework employs a multi-granular spatiotemporal pooling strategy to capture both holistic and fine-grained details of the video, generating structured natural language descriptions. This stage uses a visual encoder, a multimodal projector, and an LLM to produce detailed captions of both the overall video and specific local objects. The P2V stage then takes these descriptions and allows users to refine them, providing precise control over the video editing process. This stage uses a video diffusion model, specifically an inpainting model, to modify the video based on the textual prompts. The framework is trained on a newly created dataset called VPLM, which contains detailed video-paragraph descriptions and object-level captions with masks. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of RACCooN through a series of experiments, showing its capabilities in various video editing tasks such as object removal, addition, and modification. The results indicate that RACCooN achieves superior performance compared to existing models in video captioning, object layout planning, and video editing. The paper's main contribution lies in the novel two-stage framework, the multi-granular spatiotemporal pooling strategy, and the introduction of the VPLM dataset. The framework's ability to generate detailed video descriptions and use them for precise editing represents a significant advancement in the field of video manipulation. The authors also highlight the user-friendliness of their approach, as it allows for intuitive text-based editing of videos. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to video editing that leverages the strengths of both visual and language models, offering a versatile and effective tool for video content creation and modification.
