### Summary

This paper proposes a retrieval augmented reasoning method, FiDeLiS, which enhances knowledge graph question answering by anchoring responses to structured, verifiable reasoning paths. FiDeLiS uses a keyword-enhanced retrieval mechanism that fetches relevant entities and relations from a vector-based index of KGs to ensure high-recall retrieval. The method constructs candidate reasoning paths which are then refined using a stepwise beam search. This ensures that all the paths we create can be confidently linked back to KGs, ensuring they are accurate and reliable. A distinctive feature of our approach is its blend of natural language planning with beam search to optimize the selection of reasoning paths. Moreover, we redesign the way reasoning paths are scored by transforming this process into a deductive reasoning task, allowing the LLM to assess the validity of the paths through deductive reasoning rather than traditional logit-based scoring. This helps avoid misleading reasoning chains and reduces unnecessary computational demand.

### Suggestions

The paper's strengths are somewhat overstated, particularly regarding the novelty and computational efficiency. While the method is presented as a training-free approach, this is not a unique contribution, as many existing KGQA methods also operate without explicit training. The claim of lower computational cost needs more rigorous justification. The paper should provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of each step in the proposed method, including the retrieval, path ranking, and beam search components. This analysis should be compared against the computational costs of the baseline methods, with a clear breakdown of the time spent on each operation. Furthermore, the generality claim needs to be supported by experiments on a wider range of datasets, including those with different characteristics and complexities. The current evaluation is limited to three datasets, which may not be sufficient to demonstrate the method's robustness and generalizability.

The paper's presentation of the Path-RAG and DVBS components could be improved. The description of Path-RAG, particularly the scoring function in Equation 3, lacks sufficient detail. The roles of the terms S_rel(r), S_ent(e), and the max operator are not clearly explained, making it difficult to understand how the method selects the top-m reasoning steps. The paper should provide a more intuitive explanation of the scoring function, perhaps with examples illustrating how different terms contribute to the final score. Additionally, the connection between the Path-RAG module and the subsequent DVBS process is not clearly established. It is unclear how the reasoning paths generated by Path-RAG are used as input to the DVBS module, and how the beam search is guided by the deductive verification process. A more detailed explanation of the interaction between these two modules is needed, including a clear description of the input and output of each module.

Finally, the paper should address the limitations of the proposed method more explicitly. While the authors mention that the method is limited to multi-hop questions with a single answer, this limitation should be discussed in more detail. The paper should explore the potential challenges of extending the method to handle single-hop questions, questions with multiple answers, and questions requiring complex reasoning beyond simple deductive steps. The authors should also discuss the limitations of the keyword-enhanced retrieval mechanism, particularly its reliance on the quality of the keywords extracted from the question. The paper should also consider the potential impact of noisy or ambiguous keywords on the performance of the method. Addressing these limitations would provide a more balanced and realistic assessment of the proposed approach.

### Questions

1. In the Path-RAG component, the scoring function in Equation 3 is not very clear. What is the meaning of S_rel(r) and S_ent(e)? What is the meaning of the max operator? How to select the top-m reasoning steps based on the score in Equation 3?
2. The connection between Path-RAG and DVBS is not very clear. How to use the reasoning paths generated by Path-RAG in the DVBS process?
3. In the Deductive Verification subsection, the authors use the term “deductive verification” without a clear explanation of its meaning or how it is implemented in the context of the proposed method. A more detailed explanation of the deductive verification process, including the criteria used to determine the validity of a reasoning path, would be beneficial.
4. In the introduction, the authors mention that the entities in KGs are often represented as machine identifiers (MIDs), which lack semantic richness. However, the proposed method also relies on entity and relation embeddings, which may also lack semantic richness, especially for unseen or rare entities and relations. How does the proposed method address this issue?
5. The proposed method seems to be limited to multi-hop questions with a single answer. How to extend the proposed method to handle single-hop questions, questions with multiple answers, and questions requiring complex reasoning beyond simple deductive steps?
