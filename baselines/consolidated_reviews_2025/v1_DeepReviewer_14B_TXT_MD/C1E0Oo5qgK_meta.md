## Summary

This paper introduces Compress Guidance, a novel method designed to enhance the efficiency of conditional diffusion models by addressing the issue of model-fitting during the sampling process. The authors observe that applying guidance at every timestep can lead to samples that are overly tailored to the guidance model's parameters, rather than generalizing to the desired condition. To mitigate this, Compress Guidance reduces the number of timesteps where guidance is applied, concentrating the guidance steps in the early stages of the sampling process and skipping them in later stages. This approach is motivated by the observation that the guidance loss is primarily active in the early stages of sampling, and becomes less significant as the image structure emerges. The core idea is that by reducing the number of guidance steps, the model is less likely to overfit to the guidance model and more likely to generate images that are faithful to the desired conditions. The method is presented as a way to improve both the quality and diversity of generated images while also reducing the computational cost associated with the sampling process. The authors provide a theoretical justification for their approach, arguing that reducing guidance steps mitigates the model-fitting issue. They also present extensive experimental results on various datasets, including ImageNet and MSCOCO, and across different tasks, such as label-conditional generation and text-to-image generation. These experiments demonstrate that Compress Guidance improves image quality and diversity while also reducing the required guidance timesteps by nearly 40%. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different parameters, such as the distribution of guidance timesteps. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by identifying and addressing a key limitation of current guidance methods in diffusion models, and by proposing a simple yet effective solution that improves both the performance and efficiency of conditional generation.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper primarily focuses on improving the efficiency of single-condition generation, and it is not clear how well the proposed method would scale to more complex scenarios. Specifically, the paper does not explore how the method would perform when multiple conditions are applied sequentially or when the generation process requires multiple iterations to converge to a high-quality image. This limitation is evident in the experimental setup, which primarily focuses on single-label conditional generation and text-to-image generation, without explicitly testing scenarios with multiple label conditions applied sequentially or iterative refinement. The method description and equations also focus on a single sampling pass, further highlighting this limitation. This lack of exploration limits the applicability of the method to more complex real-world scenarios, where multiple conditions or iterative refinement are often necessary. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is clearly supported by the paper's scope and experimental design. Second, while the authors demonstrate improvements in image quality, the paper lacks a thorough analysis of the potential trade-offs between efficiency gains and image quality, particularly when generating high-resolution images or images with fine-grained details. The experiments are conducted on ImageNet datasets with resolutions up to 256x256, which is reasonable, but it does not cover extremely high-resolution images. Furthermore, the paper does not explicitly analyze the performance on images requiring very fine-grained details. The ablation study on the parameter 'k' explores different distributions of guidance timesteps but does not directly address the trade-off between compression rate and image quality at varying resolutions or detail levels. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the robustness and limitations of the method under challenging conditions. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is supported by the limited resolution of the datasets used and the lack of specific analysis on fine-grained details. While the method is presented as potentially applicable to other guidance types, there is no empirical evidence within the paper to support this claim. This lack of comparison makes it difficult to contextualize the method's performance and highlight its advantages and disadvantages compared to existing approaches. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is supported by the absence of such comparisons in the paper.
