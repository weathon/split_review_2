## Summary

This paper introduces OptBatch, a novel data selection method designed to enhance the instruction tuning of large language models (LLMs). The core idea behind OptBatch is to focus on the learnability of entire batches of data, rather than individual samples, during the training process. This is achieved through a three-pronged approach: first, an online loss-probability based stratified sampling algorithm is employed to prioritize batch selection methods that exhibit higher diversity. Second, Hessian gradient optimization is used to guide the data selection strategy for subsequent batches, aiming to capture global gradient characteristics and improve the stability of the training process. Finally, the method is evaluated on three diverse downstream datasets, demonstrating its ability to maintain comparable performance while reducing computational costs. The authors conduct extensive experiments on various datasets and tasks, including multi-turn dialogue, multilingual translation, and question-answering, using models such as LLaMa3 and ChatGLM3. The results suggest that OptBatch can achieve similar or better performance compared to full dataset training while using less data, thereby reducing computational costs by 20-40%. The paper also includes evaluations using GPT-4 scores and other metrics, consistently demonstrating OptBatch's optimal performance. The authors motivate their approach by highlighting the importance of data diversity and coverage, and they propose that their method addresses the limitations of existing data selection techniques by considering the learnability of whole batch data. The paper is well-organized and clearly written, providing sufficient background information and motivation for their research. However, as I will detail in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be improved, particularly in terms of the clarity and completeness of the methodological explanations and the analysis of computational complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a novel approach to data selection for instruction tuning, and the empirical results suggest that it has the potential to be a valuable contribution to the field.
