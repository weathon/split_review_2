## Summary

This paper presents a novel investigation into the relationship between parameter pruning masks and Hessian eigenspaces in deep neural networks, focusing on their behavior during the training process. The authors introduce a method to quantify the similarity between these subspaces using Grassmannian metrics, specifically the overlap metric. This approach allows for a rigorous analysis of how these subspaces evolve and stabilize over the course of training. The core finding of the paper is that the subspaces spanned by the largest magnitude parameters, which are often used for pruning, exhibit a significant and consistent overlap with the top Hessian eigenspaces throughout training, and not just at convergence. This overlap is shown to be well above what would be expected by chance, suggesting a fundamental connection between parameter magnitudes and the curvature of the loss landscape. The authors demonstrate that this overlap is particularly pronounced during the early stages of training, indicating an early stabilization of both the pruning masks and the Hessian eigenspaces. This observation challenges the notion that these subspaces only align at the end of training. The paper also discusses the potential implications of these findings, suggesting that the magnitude-based pruning masks could be used to approximate the top Hessian subspace, which is typically computationally expensive to obtain. This could lead to more efficient methods for network pruning and optimization. The authors support their claims with extensive experiments on a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained on a subsampled version of the MNIST dataset, providing empirical evidence for the early stabilization phenomenon and the significant overlap between the subspaces. Overall, the paper offers a fresh perspective on the connection between pruning and the loss landscape, highlighting the importance of considering training dynamics when analyzing these phenomena. The use of Grassmannian metrics provides a rigorous framework for quantifying the relationship between pruning masks and Hessian eigenspaces, and the empirical findings suggest a deeper connection between parameter magnitudes and loss curvature than previously understood. The paper's focus on the early stages of training and the stabilization of these subspaces provides a valuable contribution to the field, opening up new avenues for research in network pruning and optimization.
