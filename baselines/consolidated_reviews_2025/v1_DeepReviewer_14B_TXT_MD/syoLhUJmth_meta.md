## Summary

This paper introduces COMM, a novel multi-level feature merging strategy designed to enhance the visual capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The core idea revolves around integrating the strengths of CLIP and DINOv2, two prominent vision encoders, by leveraging their shallow and deep layer features, respectively. The authors observe that shallow layer features of CLIP are beneficial for fine-grained tasks, while DINOv2, despite not being pre-trained with text-image alignment, demonstrates promising performance when equipped with a simple MLP layer for alignment. This observation motivates the development of COMM, which merges multi-level features from both models to achieve a more comprehensive visual representation. The proposed method is evaluated on a range of tasks, including visual grounding, visual question answering (VQA), and image captioning. The empirical results demonstrate that COMM achieves superior performance compared to existing methods on several benchmarks, particularly in fine-grained tasks. The authors conduct extensive experiments, including ablation studies, to validate the effectiveness of their approach. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The primary contribution of this work lies in the innovative integration of CLIP and DINOv2 features using a multi-level merging strategy, which is shown to be effective in enhancing the visual understanding of MLLMs. The authors also highlight the importance of shallow layer features for fine-grained tasks, which is a valuable insight for the community. The paper's findings suggest that combining different types of visual features can lead to improved performance in various vision-language tasks. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of MLLMs by proposing a simple yet effective method for enhancing visual representations. However, there are some limitations that need to be addressed, such as the limited exploration of different vision backbones and the performance on general VQA tasks, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper's reliance on the ViT-Large backbone for both CLIP and DINOv2 is a significant limitation. As the authors explicitly state in the 'Method' section under 'Feature Extraction', 'The visual encoders of CLIP and DINOv2 are based on ViT-Large.' The experimental section lacks any exploration of different backbone sizes, such as ViT-Huge. This is a crucial omission because the performance of COMM may be dependent on the specific architecture of the vision encoder. Different backbones may exhibit varying performance characteristics, and the effectiveness of the multi-level feature merging strategy could be impacted by the scale of the model. For instance, larger backbones might benefit more from the fine-grained features of DINOv2, while smaller backbones might be more sensitive to the computational overhead. The absence of experiments with different backbones limits the generalizability of the findings and makes it unclear whether the proposed method's performance is robust across different model scales. This is a crucial aspect to consider for practical applications and further research. The lack of such exploration undermines the robustness of the conclusions drawn from the experiments. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is directly supported by the paper's explicit mention of ViT-Large and the absence of any experiments with other backbones.

The experiments should include a comparison of the performance of COMM with different backbones on all the tasks evaluated in the paper. This would help to determine whether the performance gains observed with ViT-Large translate to larger models, or if the method is specifically tailored to the ViT-Large architecture.
