### Summary

This paper introduces a new task called layout-aware scientific poster generation (LayoutSciPG), which aims to generate flexible posters from scientific papers through integrated automatic content extraction and layout design. To achieve this, they first build a large-scale dataset containing over 10,000 pairs of scientific papers and their corresponding posters. Then they propose a multimodal extractor-generator framework that employs a multimodal extractor to retrieve key text and image elements from the papers and designs an interactive generator with an adaptive memory mechanism to paraphrase the extracted content and generate a structured layout.

### Suggestions

The paper introduces a novel and important task, layout-aware scientific poster generation, and provides a large-scale dataset, which are significant contributions. However, the generated posters' visual complexity needs improvement. The current layouts appear simplistic, lacking the nuanced arrangements and design elements found in professional scientific posters. Future work should explore more sophisticated layout generation techniques, potentially incorporating principles of graphic design and visual communication. This could involve exploring different panel shapes, sizes, and arrangements, as well as the integration of more complex visual elements such as custom icons, color gradients, and varied typography. The goal should be to move beyond basic grid layouts to create visually engaging and informative posters that effectively communicate complex scientific information. This could also involve exploring methods to allow for more user control over the final layout, perhaps through a set of parameters or a visual interface.

Furthermore, the evaluation of the proposed method needs to be more comprehensive. While the paper mentions some evaluation metrics, it lacks a detailed analysis of the quality of the generated posters from both a content and a design perspective. The evaluation should include more quantitative metrics that capture the visual appeal and layout quality of the generated posters. For example, metrics that measure the balance, alignment, and contrast of visual elements could be considered. Additionally, the evaluation should include a more thorough comparison with existing baseline methods. The paper should explore a wider range of content extraction and layout generation techniques, and provide a detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method compared to these baselines. This would provide a more robust assessment of the method's effectiveness and identify areas for further improvement. It would also be beneficial to include a user study to assess the perceived quality of the generated posters by domain experts.

Finally, the paper should address the limitations of the proposed method more explicitly. While the authors acknowledge that the generated posters are relatively simple, they should provide a more detailed discussion of the challenges involved in generating complex and visually appealing scientific posters. This discussion should include the limitations of the current dataset, the challenges of capturing the nuances of human design, and the computational complexity of generating high-quality posters. The paper should also outline potential future directions for research in this area, such as exploring the use of more advanced generative models, incorporating user feedback into the generation process, and developing more sophisticated evaluation metrics. By addressing these limitations and outlining future directions, the paper can provide a more complete and nuanced understanding of the challenges and opportunities in the field of layout-aware scientific poster generation.
