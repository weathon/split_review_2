### Summary

This paper proposes a new method to generate readable SVG code. The authors propose three losses to improve the readability of the generated SVG code, including structural consistency loss, element appropriateness loss, and redundancy reduction loss. The experiments demonstrate that the proposed method can improve the readability of the generated SVG code while keeping the visual accuracy.

### Suggestions

The paper would significantly benefit from a more thorough qualitative analysis of the generated SVG code. While the quantitative metrics provide a high-level overview of the method's performance, they fail to capture the nuances of code readability. The authors should include a detailed comparison of the SVG code generated by their method and the baseline, highlighting specific differences in structure, element usage, and redundancy. For example, they could show how their method avoids unnecessary nested groups or how it chooses more semantically appropriate SVG elements compared to the baseline. This would provide a much clearer understanding of the practical impact of their proposed losses. Furthermore, the authors should consider including examples that showcase the limitations of their approach, such as cases where the generated code is still not easily understandable or where the method struggles with complex shapes. This would provide a more balanced view of the method's capabilities and limitations.

To further strengthen the evaluation, the authors should consider incorporating a user study to assess the readability of the generated SVG code. While the proposed metrics are a good starting point, human evaluation is crucial for assessing the practical usability of the generated code. The study could involve asking participants to perform tasks such as modifying or debugging the generated SVG code. This would provide a more direct measure of the code's readability and would help to identify areas where the method could be further improved. The authors should also consider using a more diverse set of images for evaluation, including more complex shapes and scenes. This would help to assess the robustness of the method and its ability to generalize to different types of images. The current evaluation is limited to relatively simple shapes, which may not be representative of real-world scenarios.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of their method. While the paper mentions that the method is efficient, it lacks a quantitative comparison of the runtime and memory usage of the proposed method and the baseline. This information is crucial for assessing the practical applicability of the method, especially for real-time applications. The authors should also discuss the potential for optimizing the method to further reduce its computational cost. This would make the method more accessible to a wider range of users and would increase its impact. The authors should also consider providing the source code for their method to facilitate further research and development in this area.
