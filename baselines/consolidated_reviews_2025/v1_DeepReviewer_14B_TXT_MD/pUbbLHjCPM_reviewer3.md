### Summary

This paper introduces Progressive Thought Refinement (PTR), a framework that enables LLMs to progressively refine their responses. The authors propose a weak and strong model collaborative selection strategy to build a high-quality progressive refinement dataset. They also design a training structure to mask the "thought" and adjust loss weights to encourage LLMs to refine prior thought. Experimental results show that PTR significantly enhances LLM performance across ten diverse tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

### Strengths

1. The authors propose the Progressive Thought Refinement (PTR) method to stimulate models' PR abilities and enhance generalization across various tasks without additional task-specific fine-tuning.
2. The authors design an efficient weak-strong model collaborative selection strategy to construct high-quality PTR datasets without extra feedback.
3. The authors introduce a novel weighted thought-mask fine-tuning method to instill general PR capabilities in LLMs.

#### comment

1. The authors claim that PTR significantly enhances LLM performance across ten diverse tasks (avg. from 49.6% to 53.5%). However, the improvement is marginal. It is better to report the average performance and standard deviation on each task across multiple runs. The reported average improvement of 3.9% is not substantial, and the lack of standard deviations makes it difficult to assess the statistical significance of these results. Furthermore, the performance gains appear inconsistent across different tasks, with some tasks showing minimal improvement and others showing more substantial gains. This variability raises concerns about the robustness of the proposed method.
2. The authors claim that the weak-strong model collaborative selection strategy ensures the quality of the final answer surpasses that of the previous thoughts. However, the authors do not provide any evidence to support this claim. It is better to report the quality of thoughts and answers generated by the weak and strong models, respectively, using some evaluation metrics. The paper lacks a clear definition of 'quality' in this context, and it is unclear how the collaborative selection strategy guarantees that the strong model's output is indeed a refinement of the weak model's initial thought. Without quantitative metrics, it is difficult to validate this core claim.
3. The authors claim that the weighted thought-mask fine-tuning encourages LLMs to improve responses based on previous thoughts and ensuring logical consistency between the thought process and the final answer. However, the authors do not provide any evidence to support this claim. It is better to conduct an ablation study to investigate the impact of each component of the loss function on the performance of the proposed method. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the weights in the loss function are determined, nor does it offer any analysis of the individual contributions of each term in the loss function. Without this analysis, it is difficult to understand the mechanism by which the method achieves its results.

### Suggestions

The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the performance improvements achieved by their method. Specifically, they should report the average performance and standard deviation on each task across multiple runs to establish the statistical significance of their results. This would help to clarify the robustness of the method and address the concern that the improvements are marginal. Furthermore, a more detailed analysis of the performance gains across different tasks is needed. The authors should investigate why some tasks show minimal improvement while others show more substantial gains. This could involve analyzing the characteristics of the tasks and the types of reasoning required, which could provide valuable insights into the strengths and limitations of the proposed method. The authors should also consider reporting results on a per-instance basis to better understand the variance in performance.

To validate the claim that the weak-strong model collaborative selection strategy ensures the quality of the final answer surpasses that of the previous thoughts, the authors should provide quantitative metrics for evaluating the quality of thoughts and answers generated by both the weak and strong models. This could involve using metrics such as BLEU score, ROUGE score, or other relevant metrics for the specific tasks. The authors should also provide a clear definition of 'quality' in the context of their work. This would help to clarify the criteria used for selecting the final answer and provide a more objective measure of the effectiveness of the collaborative selection strategy. Furthermore, the authors should provide examples of the thoughts and answers generated by the weak and strong models to illustrate the refinement process.

To support the claim that the weighted thought-mask fine-tuning encourages LLMs to improve responses based on previous thoughts and ensures logical consistency, the authors should conduct a thorough ablation study to investigate the impact of each component of the loss function on the performance of the proposed method. This would involve systematically removing or modifying each term in the loss function and evaluating the resulting performance. The authors should also provide a clear explanation of how the weights in the loss function are determined and why these specific weights are chosen. This would help to clarify the mechanism by which the method achieves its results and provide a more solid foundation for the proposed approach. Additionally, the authors should provide examples of the thought process and the final answers to illustrate the logical consistency between them.
