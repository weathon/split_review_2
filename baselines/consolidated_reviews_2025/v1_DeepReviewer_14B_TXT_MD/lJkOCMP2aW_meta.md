## Summary

This paper introduces Pathformer, a novel multi-scale Transformer model designed for time series forecasting. The core contribution of Pathformer lies in its integration of both temporal resolution and temporal distance into a multi-scale modeling framework. The model employs a dual attention mechanism, which operates on patches of varying sizes, allowing it to capture both local details and global correlations within time series data. Furthermore, Pathformer incorporates adaptive pathways, which dynamically adjust the multi-scale modeling process based on the input data's temporal dynamics. This adaptive mechanism aims to enhance the model's accuracy and generalization capabilities by selecting optimal patch sizes and attention mechanisms for each layer. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Pathformer through extensive experiments on eleven real-world datasets, showing that it achieves state-of-the-art performance and exhibits strong generalization abilities under various transfer scenarios. The model's architecture is designed to address the limitations of existing multi-scale modeling approaches in Transformers, which often suffer from incompleteness and fixed modeling processes. By adaptively selecting the appropriate scales for different time series, Pathformer aims to overcome these limitations and provide a more robust and accurate forecasting model. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed approach is a promising direction for time series forecasting, offering a unique way to integrate multi-scale information and adapt to the diverse characteristics of real-world time series data. The experimental results show that Pathformer not only outperforms existing models but also demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, making it a valuable contribution to the field. The authors emphasize the importance of considering both temporal resolution and temporal distance in multi-scale modeling, and their approach provides a concrete way to achieve this. The adaptive pathways mechanism is a key innovation, allowing the model to dynamically adjust its architecture based on the input data, which is a significant step forward in the field of time series forecasting. The paper's overall significance lies in its novel approach to multi-scale modeling and its empirical demonstration of improved performance and generalization.

## Weaknesses

While the paper presents a novel approach to time series forecasting, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further consideration. Firstly, the motivation, while present, lacks sufficient depth and clarity. The authors claim that the incompleteness of multi-scale modeling and the fixed multi-scale modeling process are the two main challenges in Transformers for time series forecasting. However, the paper does not fully explain why these are the *main* challenges and how they specifically impact forecasting performance. While the introduction mentions that simply changing temporal resolutions cannot emphasize temporal dependencies in various ranges explicitly and efficiently, and that considering different temporal distances enables modeling dependencies from different ranges, it does not provide concrete examples of time series data where these limitations are particularly problematic. For instance, the paper could have shown examples of time series with rapid local fluctuations that a purely global model fails to capture, or examples where a fixed multi-scale model misses important temporal dynamics that vary across the time series. The concepts of temporal resolution and temporal distance, while defined, could also be explained more rigorously, perhaps with mathematical formulations, to clarify their role in the proposed multi-scale modeling approach. The paper states that temporal resolution is achieved by dividing the time series into patches of varying sizes, and temporal distance is modeled through the dual attention mechanism, but a more detailed explanation of how different patch sizes relate to different temporal resolutions and how the dual attention mechanism captures both local and global dependencies at these resolutions would be beneficial. The lack of a more detailed explanation of the motivation and the underlying concepts weakens the paper's foundation. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does touch upon these points, but lacks the necessary depth and concrete examples. 

Secondly, the technical contribution, while novel in its combination, is limited in its individual components. The multi-scale division and dual attention mechanisms are similar to existing methods in CNNs and Transformers. The multi-scale division, where the input time series is divided into patches of varying sizes, is a standard technique. The dual attention mechanism, which performs intra-patch and inter-patch attention, is also based on standard self-attention mechanisms. The main novelty seems to stem from the adaptive pathways, but the details of how they work are not very clear. The paper provides the formula for pathway weight generation, which uses a softmax function with a noise term, and mentions that a top-K selection is performed to introduce sparsity. The paper does not compare the proposed adaptive pathway mechanism with other adaptive methods, such as those using reinforcement learning or evolutionary algorithms.
