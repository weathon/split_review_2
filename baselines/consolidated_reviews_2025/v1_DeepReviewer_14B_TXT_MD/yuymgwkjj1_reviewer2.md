### Summary

This paper addresses the issue of normalizing flows assigning higher likelihoods to out-of-distribution (OOD) samples with low complexity, which undermines the effectiveness of likelihood-based OOD detection methods. The authors propose a method to correct this bias by incorporating synthetic outliers during training, which guides the model to assign lower likelihoods to OOD samples. Additionally, they introduce a specialized training objective that leverages the softplus function for OOD data, ensuring a smooth and effective training process. The proposed approach is evaluated on both image and text datasets, and the results demonstrate that it significantly enhances OOD detection accuracy, achieving performance comparable to models trained with a limited number of real outliers.

#### comment

1. The paper lacks a clear explanation of the intuition behind the proposed method. Specifically, the authors do not provide a detailed explanation of why the synthetic outliers are generated in the way they are, and how this generation process helps to correct the bias in the normalizing flow models. The connection between the specific transformations applied to create outliers and the resulting bias correction is not well-established.
2. The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing methods for OOD detection using normalizing flows. For example, the authors should compare their approach with methods like [1] and [2], which also use invertible models for anomaly detection. The lack of such comparisons makes it difficult to assess the relative performance and advantages of the proposed method.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. The authors should provide an analysis of the time and memory requirements of their approach, especially when dealing with high-dimensional data. This analysis should include a breakdown of the computational cost associated with the outlier synthesis process and the modified training objective, and how these scale with increasing data dimensionality and model complexity.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit significantly from a more detailed explanation of the synthetic outlier generation process. The authors should elaborate on the specific transformations used to create these outliers and provide a clear rationale for why these transformations are effective in correcting the bias of normalizing flows. For instance, if the outliers are generated by adding noise or perturbing the input data, the authors should explain how the magnitude and type of perturbation are chosen, and how these choices relate to the desired bias correction. A visualization of the generated outliers, along with an analysis of their distribution relative to the in-distribution data, would also be helpful in understanding the method's behavior. Furthermore, the authors should discuss the sensitivity of their method to the choice of outlier generation parameters and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate values.

To strengthen the paper's contribution, a more thorough comparison with existing OOD detection methods using normalizing flows is essential. The authors should not only compare their method with [1] and [2], but also include other relevant approaches in the field. This comparison should go beyond simply reporting performance metrics; it should also include an analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of each method, and a discussion of the specific scenarios where the proposed method is expected to perform better or worse. For example, the authors could analyze the robustness of their method to different types of OOD data, or its sensitivity to the choice of hyperparameters. A detailed comparison would help to establish the novelty and practical value of the proposed approach.

Finally, a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method is needed. The authors should provide a breakdown of the time and memory requirements of each step in their approach, including the outlier synthesis process and the modified training objective. This analysis should consider the impact of data dimensionality, model complexity, and other relevant factors. The authors should also discuss the scalability of their method and its suitability for different types of applications. For example, they could analyze the computational cost of training and inference on large-scale datasets, and discuss any potential bottlenecks or limitations. This analysis would help to assess the practical feasibility of the proposed method and guide its application in real-world scenarios.

### Questions

1. Could the authors provide more details on how the synthetic outliers are generated? What criteria are used to ensure that these outliers are representative of OOD samples?
2. How does the proposed method compare to other state-of-the-art OOD detection methods for normalizing flows, such as those mentioned in the references [1] and [2]?
3. What is the computational complexity of the proposed method, especially when dealing with high-dimensional data? How does it compare to the computational complexity of standard normalizing flow training?
