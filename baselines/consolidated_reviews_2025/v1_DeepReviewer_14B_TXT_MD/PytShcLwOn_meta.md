## Summary

This paper introduces a novel task, termed Single-Image Conditioned Generation of Hand Trajectories (SIGHT), which aims to generate realistic and diverse 3D hand trajectories from a single image of an object, either with or without a hand interacting with it. The authors propose a method called SIGHT-Fusion, which leverages a conditional motion generation diffusion model, specifically repurposing the MDM architecture. The core idea is to extract visual features from the input image, including both object-centric and part-centric features, to guide the diffusion model in generating plausible hand motion sequences. The object features are extracted using CLIP, while part features are derived from high-level CLIP 2D feature grids, focusing on the contact points between the hand and the object. The method is trained on video data with hand trajectory annotations, without explicit action labels, and evaluated on two datasets: FPHAB and HOI4D. The empirical results demonstrate that SIGHT-Fusion outperforms several baselines, including image-based and text-based versions of MDM, in terms of accuracy, diversity, and fidelity. The paper also explores the generalization capabilities of the proposed method by evaluating its performance on unseen object instances. The authors emphasize the potential of their approach in various applications, such as robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution by introducing a new task and a corresponding method for generating hand trajectories from a single image, addressing a gap in the existing literature. The use of a diffusion model and the incorporation of object and part features are key methodological choices that contribute to the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The empirical findings, particularly the superior performance compared to baselines and the ability to generalize to unseen objects, highlight the potential of the method. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to further improve the robustness and applicability of the proposed approach.

## Strengths

The primary strength of this paper lies in its introduction of a novel and challenging task: generating 3D hand trajectories from a single image. This is a significant contribution, as it addresses a gap in the existing literature, which primarily focuses on generating hand motions from text or action labels. The proposed method, SIGHT-Fusion, is a well-designed pipeline that effectively combines visual features with a conditional diffusion model to generate realistic and diverse hand trajectories. The use of CLIP for object feature extraction and the incorporation of part features, derived from high-level CLIP 2D feature grids, are innovative approaches that contribute to the method's success. The part features, in particular, are shown to be crucial for disambiguating different actions and affordances, as demonstrated in the ablation study. The empirical results are compelling, with SIGHT-Fusion outperforming several baselines, including image-based and text-based versions of MDM, on both the FPHAB and HOI4D datasets. The paper also demonstrates the generalization capabilities of the method by evaluating its performance on unseen object instances, which is a crucial step towards real-world applicability. The authors' decision to use a diffusion model, specifically repurposing the MDM architecture, is also a strength, as it allows them to leverage the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models for this new task. The paper is also well-written and clearly explains the proposed method and the experimental setup. The inclusion of an ablation study, which analyzes the effectiveness of different components of the proposed method, provides valuable insights into the design choices. The authors also acknowledge the limitations of their approach and suggest potential directions for future research, which is a sign of a mature and well-considered study. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field, with a novel task, a well-designed method, and compelling empirical results.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. Firstly, the authors should conduct a more thorough analysis of the hand and object detection pipeline. This should include a detailed discussion of the specific algorithms used for detection, their performance characteristics (e.g., precision, recall, F1-score), and how they are adapted to handle challenges such as occlusions, varying lighting conditions, and complex backgrounds. The authors should also provide a quantitative evaluation of the detection performance on a held-out test set and discuss how detection errors impact the overall performance of the trajectory generation. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to include a qualitative analysis of failure cases, showing examples of images where the detection fails and how this affects the generated trajectories. This would provide a more complete understanding of the limitations of the detection module and its impact on the overall system. Secondly, to improve the discussion of generalization to unseen objects, the authors should conduct a more thorough analysis of the model's performance on objects that are not present in the training data. This could involve creating a separate test set of unseen objects with varying characteristics (e.g., shape, size, texture, affordances) and evaluating the model's performance on this set. The authors should also discuss the factors that influence the model's generalization ability, such as the diversity of the training data and the complexity of the objects. It would be helpful to include a visualization of the generated trajectories for unseen objects and to compare these trajectories with those generated for seen objects. This would provide a more concrete understanding of the model's generalization capabilities and its limitations. Thirdly, to address the lack of information on computational efficiency, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the model's inference time, memory usage, and computational requirements. This should include a breakdown of the time spent on different parts of the pipeline, such as feature extraction, trajectory generation, and rendering. The authors should also discuss the potential for optimizing the model for real-time performance, such as using model compression techniques or parallel processing. It would be beneficial to compare the computational requirements of the proposed method with those of other relevant methods and to discuss the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency. This would provide a more complete understanding of the practical feasibility of deploying the method in real-world scenarios. Fourthly, to address the limited improvement in accuracy, the authors should explore more sophisticated methods for incorporating object features into the diffusion model. Instead of simply concatenating or adding the object feature to the text embedding, they could investigate attention mechanisms or learnable fusion layers that allow the model to dynamically weigh the importance of object features at different stages of the generation process. Furthermore, the authors should consider incorporating temporal constraints or priors into the model to ensure the generated hand pose sequences are smooth and physically plausible. This could involve using a temporal convolutional network or a recurrent neural network to model the temporal dependencies between hand poses. The authors should also explore the use of adversarial training to improve the realism of the generated hand pose sequences. Fifthly, to address the inherent ambiguity of generating motion from a single image, the authors could explore methods that incorporate temporal information or multiple viewpoints. This could involve using a sequence of images or a video clip as input, which would provide more context for the motion generation process. Alternatively, the authors could explore methods that incorporate prior knowledge about hand-object interactions, such as using a database of pre-defined motion patterns. Finally, to address the limitations of the evaluation metrics, the authors should include a more thorough analysis of failure cases and a qualitative assessment of the generated trajectories. This could involve visualizing the generated trajectories and comparing them to ground-truth trajectories, as well as conducting a user study to assess the naturalness and plausibility of the generated motions. The authors should also consider using more sophisticated evaluation metrics that capture the nuances of hand pose sequence generation, such as metrics based on the smoothness of the hand motion or the physical plausibility of the generated poses.
