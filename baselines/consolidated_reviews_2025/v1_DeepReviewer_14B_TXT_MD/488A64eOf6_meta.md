## Summary

This paper introduces DAEEMON, a novel decoding framework designed to align generated text with human-like qualities across multiple dimensions simultaneously. The core idea is to formulate decoding as a constrained optimization problem, where the objective is to minimize the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the model's distribution and the distribution of human-written text. This is achieved by optimizing the model's output to match specific metric scores observed in human text, such as repetition, coherence, diversity, and information content. The authors provide a theoretical foundation for their approach, demonstrating that minimizing the reverse KL divergence leads to an improvement in perplexity on human texts. They derive an analytical solution for the decoding distribution and employ a Sampling-Importance-Resampling (SIR) technique to enable tractable sampling from this distribution. The method involves estimating coefficients that weight the importance of each metric, using a development set to match the expected metric scores of generated text to those of human text. The experimental results, conducted on Wikipedia and News domains, demonstrate DAEEMON's superiority over strong baselines in both automatic and human evaluations. The authors evaluate the generated text using a suite of automatic metrics, including measures of repetition, coherence, diversity, and information content, and also conduct human evaluations to assess the overall quality of the generated text. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel approach to decoding, which moves beyond traditional methods that often focus on a single objective or rely on heuristics. By framing decoding as a constrained optimization problem and optimizing for multiple metrics simultaneously, DAEEMON offers a more comprehensive approach to generating human-like text. The theoretical grounding and the empirical results presented in the paper provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several limitations and areas for improvement that need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this approach.

## Strengths

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First and foremost, the paper presents a novel approach to decoding by framing it as a constrained optimization problem. This is a significant departure from traditional decoding methods that often focus on a single objective or rely on heuristics. The idea of simultaneously optimizing for multiple metrics that capture different aspects of human-like text is a compelling contribution. The authors' decision to minimize the reverse KL divergence, rather than the forward KL divergence, is also a notable strength. As they argue, the reverse KL divergence encourages mode-seeking behavior, which is more appropriate for decoding, where the goal is to generate high-quality text samples. The theoretical grounding of the method is another significant strength. The authors provide a rigorous proof that their approach improves perplexity on human texts, which adds credibility to their method. The derivation of an analytical solution for the decoding distribution is also a valuable technical contribution. Furthermore, the use of the Sampling-Importance-Resampling (SIR) technique to enable tractable sampling from the globally normalized decoding distribution is a crucial innovation that makes the proposed method practical. Without SIR, sampling from the decoding distribution would be computationally intractable. The experimental results presented in the paper are also compelling. The authors conduct comprehensive evaluations on two different domains, Wikipedia and News, and demonstrate that DAEEMON outperforms strong baselines in both automatic and human evaluations. The use of both automatic metrics and human evaluations provides a well-rounded assessment of the method's effectiveness. The ablation study, which analyzes the impact of varying the number of samples used in SIR, is also a valuable contribution, as it provides insights into the trade-off between computational cost and generation quality. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated, theoretically sound, and empirically validated approach to decoding, which represents a significant advancement in the field.
