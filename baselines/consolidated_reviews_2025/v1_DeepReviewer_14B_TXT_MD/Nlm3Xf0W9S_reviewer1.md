### Summary

This paper presents a watermarking scheme for order-agnostic language models, which are commonly used in tasks such as protein generation and machine translation. The scheme embeds watermarks based on a Markov chain with a transition matrix $A$, and uses a statistical detection algorithm to detect these watermarks. The authors provide a dynamic programming algorithm to compute the probability distribution of pattern occurrences in the generated sequences. The experimental results demonstrate that this method outperforms the Soft watermark, Multikey watermark, and Unigram watermark in terms of detection efficiency.

### Strengths

1. The dynamic programming algorithm computes the probability distribution of pattern occurrences in sequences generated by a Markov chain, and the time complexity of this algorithm is $O(n^2l^m)$, where $n$ is the sequence length, $l$ is the size of the key space, and $m$ is the pattern length.

2. Experimental results show that this method outperforms the Soft watermark, Multikey watermark, and Unigram watermark in terms of detection efficiency.

### Weaknesses

#### comment

1. The method relies on a Markov chain model, which may not be suitable for capturing the complex dependencies in the output of language models. Specifically, the assumption that the next state depends only on the current state might be too simplistic for language models, where long-range dependencies are crucial. This could lead to the watermark being easily removed or altered without significantly affecting the output distribution, thus compromising the robustness of the watermarking scheme.

2. The method requires the recovery of the key sequence from the generated content for watermark detection. The effectiveness of the method depends on the accuracy of this recovery process. The paper does not provide sufficient detail on the key recovery mechanism, making it difficult to assess its reliability. If the recovery process is prone to errors, especially in the presence of noise or adversarial attacks, the overall performance of the watermarking scheme will be significantly degraded.

3. The method requires prior knowledge of the vocabulary split and the transition matrix of the Markov chain used for watermark generation. This could limit its applicability in practical scenarios where this information is not available. In real-world applications, the attacker might not have access to the exact vocabulary split or the transition matrix, making it challenging to implement the watermarking scheme effectively. This reliance on specific internal parameters makes the method less flexible and potentially vulnerable to changes in the model or its configuration.

### Suggestions

The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the limitations of using a Markov chain model for watermarking language model outputs. It would be beneficial to explore alternative models that can capture longer-range dependencies, such as recurrent neural networks or transformers, and compare their performance with the proposed Markov chain approach. The authors should also investigate the impact of model complexity on the watermark's robustness and detection accuracy. Furthermore, a theoretical analysis of the watermark's robustness against various attacks, such as paraphrasing or adversarial modifications, would be valuable. This analysis should consider the trade-off between watermark robustness and the quality of the generated content.

The paper needs to provide a more detailed explanation of the key recovery process, including the specific algorithms used and their computational complexity. It is crucial to evaluate the performance of the key recovery process under various conditions, such as different noise levels and adversarial attacks. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of the key recovery process to errors in the generated content. For example, how does the recovery rate change when a small percentage of tokens are modified or replaced? It would be beneficial to provide a quantitative analysis of the relationship between the accuracy of the key recovery and the overall detection performance of the watermarking scheme. This analysis should include a discussion of the error propagation from key recovery to watermark detection.

To enhance the practical applicability of the method, the authors should explore techniques that reduce the reliance on prior knowledge of the vocabulary split and the transition matrix. One possible approach is to use a more robust watermarking scheme that is less sensitive to variations in these parameters. Another approach is to develop methods for estimating these parameters from the generated content itself. The paper should also discuss the potential impact of changes in the model or its configuration on the watermarking scheme's performance. For example, how does the method perform if the vocabulary or the model architecture is updated? Addressing these practical concerns will make the proposed method more relevant and useful in real-world scenarios.
