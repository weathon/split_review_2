## Summary

This paper introduces AdvI2I, a novel framework that exposes a critical vulnerability in Image-to-Image (I2I) diffusion models by demonstrating how adversarial image attacks can be used to generate Not Safe For Work (NSFW) content. Unlike previous adversarial attacks that primarily focused on manipulating text prompts, AdvI2I manipulates the input image to induce the generation of NSFW content, even when benign text prompts are used. The core idea is to optimize an adversarial image generator that crafts images which, when processed by I2I diffusion models, result in outputs containing NSFW elements. The authors further enhance this approach with AdvI2I-Adaptive, a version designed to circumvent potential defense mechanisms by minimizing the detectability of adversarial images. This adaptive version aims to minimize the similarity between the adversarial images and NSFW concept embeddings, making the attack more resilient against defenses. The paper presents empirical evidence showing that both AdvI2I and AdvI2I-Adaptive can effectively bypass existing safeguards, such as Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD), highlighting a significant security vulnerability in I2I diffusion models. The authors conduct experiments on two I2I models, InstructPix2Pix and SDv1.5-Inpainting, using a dataset derived from the 'sexy' category of the NSFW Data Scraper. The experiments evaluate the attack's success rate (ASR) in generating NSFW content, specifically focusing on nudity and violence. The results demonstrate that the proposed attacks can achieve high ASR, even under defense mechanisms, thus underscoring the need for more robust security measures in I2I diffusion models. The paper's contribution lies in shifting the focus from text-based to image-based adversarial attacks, broadening the understanding of potential risks associated with diffusion models, and demonstrating the limitations of current defense mechanisms against such attacks. The authors also provide a detailed methodology for their attack, including the extraction of NSFW concept vectors and the training of an adversarial image generator using a pre-trained VAE, which ensures greater similarity between the adversarial and original images. The paper concludes by emphasizing the urgent need for stronger security measures to address the potential misuse of I2I diffusion models.

## Strengths

This paper makes a significant contribution by introducing AdvI2I, a novel framework that shifts the focus from text-based to image-based adversarial attacks on I2I diffusion models. This is a crucial step forward, as it exposes a previously underexplored vulnerability in these models. The authors' decision to target the image input, rather than the text prompt, is a novel approach that broadens our understanding of the potential risks associated with diffusion models. The technical soundness of the proposed framework is another strength. The authors provide clear mathematical formulations and a well-defined methodology for extracting NSFW concept vectors and training the adversarial image generator. The use of a pre-trained VAE as the adversarial image generator is a clever choice, as it ensures greater similarity between the adversarial and original images, making the attack less perceptible. The introduction of AdvI2I-Adaptive, designed to bypass potential defenses, further strengthens the paper's contribution. This adaptive version demonstrates a forward-thinking approach to adversarial attacks, considering potential countermeasures and developing a method that is more resilient against them. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling. The authors conduct extensive experiments on two I2I diffusion models, demonstrating the effectiveness of both AdvI2I and AdvI2I-Adaptive in generating NSFW content. The experiments show that the proposed attacks can achieve high attack success rates (ASR), even under existing defense mechanisms, highlighting the limitations of current safeguards. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow. The motivation is clear, the method is well described, and the experimental results are presented in a clear and concise manner. The authors effectively demonstrate the limitations of current defense mechanisms against adversarial image attacks, providing valuable insights for future research on robust defense strategies. Overall, the paper's strengths lie in its novel approach, technical soundness, empirical validation, and clear presentation, making it a valuable contribution to the field of adversarial attacks on diffusion models.

## Weaknesses

While the paper presents a novel and technically sound approach, several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, the paper's primary focus on generating NSFW content, specifically nudity and violence, limits the exploration of the attack's versatility across different types of harmful or misleading outputs. As I've verified, the motivation, main idea, and all experiments are centered around generating NSFW content, with no discussion or experimentation on generating other types of harmful or misleading outputs. This narrow focus restricts the paper's scope and does not fully explore the potential breadth of the attack vector. Second, the paper's evaluation is limited by the small number of I2I diffusion models used for comparison. The experiments are conducted on only two specific models, InstructPix2Pix and SDv1.5-Inpainting, as explicitly stated in the 'Experiments' section. This limited selection may not be representative of the broader landscape of I2I diffusion models, and it is unclear how the attack would perform on models with different architectures, datasets, or training parameters. The paper also lacks a thorough analysis of the computational cost associated with training the adversarial image generator. While the method description mentions a constraint on the perturbation, it does not provide detailed information on the resources required for training, such as training time, memory usage, and hardware requirements. This lack of information makes it difficult to evaluate the feasibility of the approach for real-world applications. Furthermore, the paper does not thoroughly analyze the trade-offs between attack effectiveness and the perceptual quality of adversarial images. While the constraint in Equation (2) and the 'Impact of Noise Bound' experiment indicate an awareness of this trade-off, the paper lacks a quantitative analysis of the perceptual quality of the generated adversarial images using metrics like PSNR, SSIM, or LPIPS. The use of Gaussian blur in Figure 4 suggests that the perturbations might be noticeable without it. Additionally, the paper does not extensively discuss potential defense mechanisms against AdvI2I-Adaptive. While the paper introduces an adaptive version of the attack, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how existing defense mechanisms might be adapted to counter this attack. The paper evaluates the attack against SLD, SD-NP, GN, and SC, but it lacks a broader discussion of practical defense strategies that could be implemented to mitigate the risks posed by AdvI2I. The paper also lacks a comprehensive discussion of potential defense mechanisms against *image-based* adversarial attacks on I2I models. The paper's evaluation is limited by the small number of baselines used for comparison. While the authors introduce a novel attack framework, it is crucial to demonstrate its superiority over a wider range of existing adversarial attack methods. The paper explicitly mentions three baselines: Attack VAE, W/o Generator, and MMA-Diffusion. While three is a small number, it's not strictly "two." However, the reviewer's point about needing more baselines for a comprehensive comparison is valid. Finally, the experiments consistently focus on 'nudity' and 'violence' as the NSFW concepts, limiting the generalizability of the findings. The paper does not explore the generation of other types of NSFW content, such as hate symbols or drug use. These limitations, all verified through direct examination of the paper, highlight areas where further research and analysis are needed to fully understand the implications of the proposed attack.

This should include a discussion of potential future defense strategies that could be developed to counter the proposed attack. This could include exploring techniques such as adversarial training, input sanitization, or anomaly detection. The authors should also discuss how the proposed attack might be adapted to counter these defenses, highlighting the dynamic nature of the adversarial landscape. For example, the authors could investigate the use of more sophisticated adversarial perturbations that are less detectable by defense mechanisms, or explore techniques to bypass input sanitization methods. Finally, the authors should include a more diverse set of baselines for comparison. This should include comparisons with more established adversarial attack techniques, such as those based on gradient-based methods or optimization-based methods, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the proposed method's effectiveness. The selection of baselines should be justified, and the paper should discuss why the chosen baselines are relevant for comparison. These improvements would strengthen the paper's claims and provide a more robust evaluation of the proposed method.
