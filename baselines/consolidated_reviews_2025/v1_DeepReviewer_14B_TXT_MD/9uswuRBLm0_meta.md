## Summary

This paper introduces Cyclic Neural Networks (Cyclic NNs), a novel architecture that departs from the traditional layer-by-layer design of artificial neural networks (ANNs). Inspired by the complex, interconnected structure of biological neural networks, Cyclic NNs allow for cyclic connections between neurons, enhancing information flow and model expressiveness. The authors propose the Graph Over Multi-layer Perceptron (GOMLP) as a concrete model based on this new paradigm. They demonstrate the effectiveness of Cyclic NNs through experiments on widely tested datasets, showing that they outperform traditional Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)-based neural networks in most cases. The paper also highlights the Forward-Forward training algorithm's superior performance compared to the Back-Propagation algorithm when used with Cyclic NNs. The core contributions of this work include the introduction of a transformative design for ANNs, the development of a localized training approach, and the empirical validation of the proposed architecture on multiple datasets. The methodological approaches involve the use of graph structures to represent neuron connections and the application of the Forward-Forward algorithm for training. The main empirical findings show that Cyclic NNs, particularly when using the Forward-Forward algorithm, achieve better performance on tasks such as image and text classification. The overall significance of this work lies in its potential to open new avenues for neural network design and to provide a more biologically realistic model of computation in ANNs.

## Strengths

One of the most compelling strengths of this paper is its introduction of a transformative design paradigm for ANNs that moves beyond the traditional layer-by-layer, Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) structure. This is a significant contribution to the field, as it opens up new possibilities for neural network architectures. The concept of Cyclic NNs, which allows for cycles in the network structure, is a novel idea that could lead to more biologically realistic AI systems. The paper provides a detailed model, the Graph Over Multi-layer Perceptron (GOMLP), as a concrete example of the Cyclic NN design paradigm. This model serves as a practical implementation that demonstrates the feasibility of the proposed architecture. The experimental validation on widely tested datasets, such as MNIST, NewsGroup, and IMDB, demonstrates the practical advantages of Cyclic NNs over traditional DAG-based neural networks. The results show that Cyclic NNs, trained with the Forward-Forward algorithm, outperform traditional Back-Propagation trained models in most cases, which is a significant finding. The paper also introduces the concept of localized training, where each neuron is optimized with its own local loss function, which is a novel approach that frees the network from the need for DAG dependencies. This localized training method is a key innovation that could lead to more efficient and flexible training of neural networks. The authors provide a clear and concise overview of the relevant concepts, making the paper accessible to a broad audience. The motivation for the problem and the challenges of cyclic dependencies and localized training are well-articulated, which helps in understanding the importance of the proposed solution.
