### Summary

The paper introduces a framework that combines LLMs and MCTS. The LLM is used to generate strategies which are refined by MCTS. The authors show that this method outperforms RL and other LLM-based methods in complex games like GOPS and Avalon.

### Strengths

The main strength of the paper is the solid evaluation of the proposed method. The authors compared their method with a wide range of baselines and conducted human evaluation. The proposed method is also more computationally efficient than the deep RL baselines.

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

The proposed method is a combination of LLM and tree search which is not novel. As mentioned in the paper, the high-level strategies generated by the LLM are refined by MCTS. This is similar to the approach taken by AlphaGo. The authors should make this distinction clearer in the paper. 

The presentation of the paper can be improved. Section 2 is hard to understand. The authors should make it clearer that they are focusing on multi-agent environments and provide a detailed example of the environment (e.g., Avalon) when introducing the notation. The current description lacks clarity on how the agents interact within the defined environment, and the notation is not sufficiently grounded in a concrete example. Specifically, the roles of different agents, their action spaces, and the transition function are not clearly delineated, making it difficult to grasp the practical implications of the proposed framework.

### Suggestions

To address the lack of novelty, the authors should more clearly articulate the specific differences between their approach and existing methods like AlphaGo. While AlphaGo uses a value network trained by deep RL to guide MCTS, the authors use an LLM to generate strategies which are then refined by MCTS. The authors should emphasize that their method does not require extensive training of a value network, and that the LLM is used in a distinct manner, not as a replacement for a value function but as a generator of high-level strategies. This distinction should be made explicit in the introduction and methodology sections. Furthermore, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of their approach compared to deep RL methods, including the number of LLM calls and the depth of the MCTS search. This would help to clarify the practical advantages of their method.

To improve the presentation of Section 2, the authors should begin by explicitly stating that they are focusing on multi-agent environments. They should then provide a detailed example of the environment, such as Avalon, when introducing the notation. This example should include a clear description of the roles of different agents, their action spaces, and the transition function. The authors should also provide a concrete example of how the LLM generates high-level strategies for a specific scenario in Avalon, and how these strategies are then refined by MCTS. This would help to ground the notation in a practical example and make the section more accessible to readers. The authors should also consider using diagrams or flowcharts to illustrate the interaction between the LLM and MCTS, as well as the flow of information within the environment.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of how the LLM generates strategies. What is the input to the LLM, and what is the format of the output? How are the strategies represented, and how are they refined by MCTS? The authors should also discuss the limitations of their approach, such as the potential for the LLM to generate suboptimal strategies or the computational cost of MCTS. Addressing these limitations would help to provide a more balanced and comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method.
