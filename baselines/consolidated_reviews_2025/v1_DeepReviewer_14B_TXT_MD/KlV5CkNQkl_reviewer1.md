### Summary

This paper presents a new example-based explanation method, HD-Explain, for neural network classifiers. For a given test point, it finds training points that maximize the Kernelized Stein Discrepancy (KSD) as the explanation. It conducts extensive experiments to show the superiority of HD-Explain in terms of quality, consistency, and computation efficiency.

#### comment

1. The proposed method is not well-motivated. The paper does not provide an intuition of why using KSD is a good choice. It shows the KSD can be used to measure the discrepancy between distributions, but can not explain why it can find training samples that provide the best predictive support to a test point. Figure 1 only shows that KSD is large when the test point is from a different distribution, so it can not show why it is a good choice to find training samples that are most similar to a test point.
2. The paper makes a strong assumption that $P_D$ is uniformly distributed, which is unrealistic.
3. The proposed method is only applicable to generative models that can provide $P(y|x)$, but existing example-based explanation methods are applicable to any black-box models.
4. The paper only shows the results of explaining the test samples. It is more important to explain the misclassified samples.

### Suggestions

The paper needs to provide a more compelling justification for using Kernelized Stein Discrepancy (KSD) as the basis for selecting training examples. While the paper demonstrates that KSD can distinguish between distributions, it fails to articulate why maximizing KSD between a test point and training points would identify training samples that provide the best predictive support. The connection between KSD and the notion of 'predictive support' is not clear. The authors should provide a more detailed explanation of how the KSD kernel captures the relationship between a test sample and training samples in the context of a trained model. Specifically, it would be beneficial to show how the KSD kernel relates to the model's learned representation and decision boundaries. A more thorough analysis of the properties of the KSD kernel in this specific context is needed to strengthen the motivation for the proposed method. The paper should also include a discussion of the limitations of using KSD, particularly in comparison to other distance metrics or similarity measures.

The assumption of a uniform data distribution, $P_D$, is a significant limitation that needs to be addressed. While the authors argue that this assumption is valid in the context of explanation, it is still a strong simplification that may not hold in real-world scenarios. The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the impact of this assumption on the performance of the proposed method. It would be beneficial to explore how the method behaves when the data distribution is not uniform, and to consider potential modifications to the method to make it more robust to non-uniform distributions. The authors should also discuss the potential biases that may arise from this assumption and how they might affect the explanations generated by the method. Furthermore, the paper should provide a more detailed explanation of why this assumption is necessary for the method to work and if there are any alternatives.

Finally, the paper should address the limitation that the proposed method is only applicable to generative models. The authors should discuss the potential for extending the method to other types of models, such as discriminative models, which are widely used in practice. The paper should also provide a more detailed comparison of the proposed method with existing example-based explanation methods, particularly in terms of their applicability to different types of models. The authors should also consider the practical implications of this limitation and discuss potential workarounds or alternative approaches for applying the method to non-generative models. Additionally, the paper should include a more comprehensive evaluation of the method, including an analysis of its performance on misclassified samples, as these are often the most important cases to explain.
