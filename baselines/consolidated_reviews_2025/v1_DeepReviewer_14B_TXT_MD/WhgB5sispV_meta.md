## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to dynamic scene representation using 4D Gaussian splatting. The core idea is to extend the concept of 3D Gaussian splatting, which has proven effective for static scene rendering, into the temporal domain. The authors propose a 4D Gaussian representation, where each Gaussian is defined by a mean vector and a covariance matrix that encompasses both spatial and temporal dimensions. This 4D Gaussian is constructed by combining two isotropic rotations and a scaling matrix, allowing for the modeling of complex spatiotemporal dynamics. Furthermore, the paper introduces 4D spherindrical harmonics to capture the time-evolving appearance of each Gaussian, enabling the representation of view-dependent effects and changes in appearance over time. The method leverages a rasterization-based rendering pipeline, which is known for its efficiency, to achieve real-time rendering of dynamic scenes. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through experiments on both multi-view and monocular dynamic scene datasets, showing state-of-the-art rendering quality and significant speed improvements compared to existing methods. Specifically, the method shows impressive results on the Plenoptic Video dataset and the D-NeRF dataset, achieving high rendering quality and real-time performance. The paper's main contribution lies in the novel 4D Gaussian representation and its integration with 4D spherindrical harmonics, which allows for a coherent and efficient modeling of dynamic scenes. The method's ability to achieve real-time rendering while maintaining high visual quality is a significant advancement in the field of dynamic scene representation. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the proposed method and its advantages. The experimental results and ablation studies further support the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of dynamic scene modeling, offering a promising approach for real-time rendering of complex dynamic scenes.

## Strengths

The primary strength of this paper lies in its innovative extension of 3D Gaussian splatting to the temporal domain, resulting in a novel and effective 4D Gaussian representation. The formulation of the 4D Gaussian, using two rotations and a scaling matrix, is both elegant and technically sound, allowing for the modeling of complex spatiotemporal dynamics. This approach is a significant departure from previous methods that often treat time as a separate dimension or rely on implicit representations. The introduction of 4D spherindrical harmonics to model the time-evolving appearance of the Gaussians is another notable contribution. This allows the method to capture view-dependent effects and changes in appearance over time, which is crucial for realistic rendering of dynamic scenes. The use of a rasterization-based rendering pipeline further contributes to the method's efficiency, enabling real-time rendering of complex dynamic scenes. The experimental results presented in the paper are compelling, demonstrating state-of-the-art rendering quality and significant speed improvements compared to existing methods. The method's performance on both multi-view and monocular dynamic scene datasets highlights its versatility and generalizability. The ablation studies provide further evidence of the effectiveness of the proposed components, such as the 4D Gaussian representation and the 4D spherindrical harmonics. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the proposed method and its advantages. The authors have successfully integrated several complex concepts into a coherent and effective framework. The ability to achieve real-time rendering while maintaining high visual quality is a significant achievement, making this method a promising approach for various applications, including virtual and augmented reality. The paper's technical soundness, combined with its empirical success, makes it a valuable contribution to the field of dynamic scene modeling. The method's ability to handle complex dynamic scenes with high rendering quality and real-time performance is a significant advancement, showcasing the potential of the proposed 4D Gaussian representation.

## Weaknesses

While the paper presents a compelling approach to dynamic scene representation, several weaknesses need to be addressed. A significant concern, consistently raised by multiple reviewers, is the lack of a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed method. The paper does not explore potential failure cases or scenarios where the method might struggle. For instance, the paper does not discuss how the method handles scenes with very thin structures, high-frequency textures, or extremely rapid motion. The Plenoptic Video dataset, while diverse, contains scenes with complex occlusions and lighting changes, and it is unclear how the proposed method performs in these challenging situations. The absence of such a discussion makes it difficult to fully understand the method's boundaries and potential areas for improvement. This is a critical oversight, as it prevents a complete assessment of the method's robustness and generalizability. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. While the authors claim real-time rendering, they do not provide specific training and inference times or a detailed comparison of computational costs with other methods. This is a crucial omission, as it is essential to understand the practical implications of the method's computational requirements. The paper does not provide a breakdown of the time spent on different stages of the pipeline, such as Gaussian parameter optimization, 4D spherindrical harmonics computation, and rendering. This lack of detail makes it difficult to assess the method's scalability and identify potential bottlenecks. Furthermore, the paper does not discuss the memory footprint of the Gaussian parameters and the harmonics coefficients, which is important for practical applications where memory resources might be limited. The paper also presents surprisingly low LPIPS values in Table 2, particularly on the D-NeRF dataset, compared to other methods. While the results are presented, the paper lacks a deeper analysis to explain this significant improvement. It is important to understand the underlying reasons for this performance gain and whether it is due to the method's inherent capabilities or other factors. The paper does not provide a per-scene breakdown of the LPIPS scores or discuss potential biases in the metric. Additionally, the paper does not provide sufficient details on the densification and pruning strategy for 4D Gaussians. While the authors mention that they adapt the strategy from 3D Gaussian Splatting, they do not provide specific details on how the splitting and pruning thresholds are determined in the 4D case or the exact mechanism of "joint spatial and temporal position sampling." This lack of detail makes it difficult to assess the effectiveness of the method and hinders reproducibility. The paper also lacks a discussion of the sensitivity of the method to the initialization of the Gaussian parameters. It is unclear whether the method converges to a good solution regardless of the initial parameters or if specific initialization strategies are required for optimal performance. Finally, the paper does not discuss the potential for artifacts or inaccuracies in the reconstructed scene and how these might manifest in the rendered output. This is a critical aspect that needs to be addressed to fully understand the method's limitations. These weaknesses, particularly the lack of discussion on limitations, the insufficient analysis of computational cost, and the lack of detail on the densification and pruning strategy, significantly impact the overall assessment of the method. The absence of this information makes it difficult to fully understand the method's capabilities and limitations and hinders its practical application.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the paper should include a dedicated section discussing the limitations of the proposed method. This section should explore potential failure cases and scenarios where the method might struggle, such as scenes with very thin structures, high-frequency textures, or extremely rapid motion. The authors should provide qualitative examples of these failure cases, perhaps with visualizations of the reconstructed Gaussian splats, to give a more complete picture of the method's capabilities and limitations. This discussion should also include an analysis of the method's sensitivity to the initialization of the Gaussian parameters and how this might affect the final reconstruction quality. Second, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time spent on different stages of the pipeline, such as Gaussian parameter optimization, 4D spherindrical harmonics computation, and rendering. The authors should also compare the memory footprint of the proposed method with other dynamic scene representation techniques, including the number of Gaussians used and the memory usage of the harmonics coefficients. This analysis should also discuss the scalability of the method with respect to the complexity of the scene and the number of frames. Third, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the surprisingly low LPIPS values. This analysis should include a per-scene breakdown of the LPIPS scores and a discussion of potential biases in the metric. The authors should also investigate whether the improved LPIPS score is correlated with improvements in other metrics, such as PSNR or SSIM. Fourth, the paper should provide a more detailed explanation of the densification and pruning strategy for 4D Gaussians. This explanation should include a step-by-step description of how the 3D densification and pruning methods are adapted for the 4D case, including the specific modifications made and the rationale behind them. The authors should also discuss how the method handles the temporal dimension during densification and pruning and how the splitting and pruning thresholds are determined in the 4D case. Fifth, the paper should include a discussion of the potential for artifacts or inaccuracies in the reconstructed scene and how these might manifest in the rendered output. This discussion should include an analysis of the per-pixel errors and identify any patterns or biases in the reconstruction. Finally, the paper should discuss the potential for future improvements and extensions of the proposed method. For example, the authors could explore the possibility of combining the method with other techniques, such as neural rendering or implicit representations, to further improve the rendering quality or efficiency. The authors could also explore the potential for using the 4D Gaussian representation for other tasks, such as scene editing or animation. By addressing these points, the paper can provide a more complete and nuanced understanding of the proposed method and its potential for future research.
