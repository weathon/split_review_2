## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to end-to-end Transformer-based multi-object tracking (MOT), termed CO-MOT, which aims to address limitations in existing methods, particularly concerning the label assignment strategy during training. The authors identify that current end-to-end MOT methods often suffer from unbalanced training, where detection queries receive fewer positive samples compared to tracking queries. To mitigate this, they propose a coopetition label assignment strategy (COLA), which allows tracked objects to be reassigned to detection queries in intermediate decoder layers. This strategy is designed to enhance the detection performance of the model by providing more positive samples for detection queries. Furthermore, the paper introduces a shadow set concept, where each query is augmented with multiple shadow queries, which are slightly perturbed versions of the original query. This one-to-set matching strategy aims to improve the robustness of the model to noisy predictions by optimizing the most challenging query within each set. The method is evaluated on multiple MOT benchmarks, including DanceTrack, BDD100K, and MOT17, where it achieves superior performance compared to several baselines, without incurring additional computational costs. The authors also provide ablation studies and visualizations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed components. The core idea behind COLA is to leverage the tracked objects to provide additional training data for the detection queries, thus addressing the issue of imbalanced training. The shadow set concept is inspired by similar techniques in object detection, but adapted for the MOT setting. The authors claim that their method can be applied as a plugin to any end-to-end MOT method, making it a versatile contribution to the field. The experimental results demonstrate that CO-MOT achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple datasets, showcasing its effectiveness in improving tracking accuracy. The paper also includes an efficiency comparison, showing that the proposed method does not significantly increase computational overhead. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and effective approach to end-to-end Transformer-based MOT, with a clear focus on addressing the limitations of existing methods through novel label assignment and query augmentation techniques. The authors provide a thorough evaluation of their method, including ablation studies and visualizations, which supports their claims of improved performance and efficiency.
