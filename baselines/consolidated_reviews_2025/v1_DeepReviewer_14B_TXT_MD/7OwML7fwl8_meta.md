## Summary

This paper introduces Reckoner, a novel framework designed to enhance fairness in machine learning models when sensitive attributes are unavailable. The core idea revolves around a confidence-based hierarchical structure of variational autoencoders (VAEs). The method begins by training a linear classifier to obtain confidence scores for each data point, which are then used to split the dataset into high-confidence and low-confidence subsets. Two VAEs are trained on these subsets, respectively, and a knowledge-sharing mechanism is employed to transfer information between them. A key component of the framework is the introduction of learnable noise, which aims to extract essential information for predictions while mitigating bias. The high-confidence VAE is updated using a pseudo-learning process, incorporating knowledge from the low-confidence VAE, and the process is iterated. The authors evaluate Reckoner on the COMPAS and New Adult datasets, using Equalised Odds and Demographic Parity as fairness metrics, and accuracy as a performance metric. The experimental results demonstrate that Reckoner consistently outperforms several state-of-the-art baselines in terms of both fairness and accuracy. The paper's main contribution lies in its innovative approach to fairness learning without relying on sensitive attributes, which is particularly relevant in the context of increasing data privacy concerns and regulatory restrictions. The authors provide an analysis of the distribution of non-sensitive attributes across demographic groups at different model confidence levels, which sheds light on the relationship between predictability and fairness. The proposed framework, with its dual-model system, learnable noise, and knowledge-sharing mechanism, presents a creative solution to the challenge of achieving fairness in the absence of sensitive information. The experimental results, while limited to two datasets, demonstrate the practical utility of the framework, showing that it can achieve improved fairness without sacrificing predictive performance. Overall, the paper addresses a significant and timely issue in machine learning, offering a promising approach to mitigate bias in scenarios where sensitive attributes are not accessible.
