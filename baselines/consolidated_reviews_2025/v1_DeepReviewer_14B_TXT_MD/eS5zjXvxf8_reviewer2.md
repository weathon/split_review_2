### Summary

This paper introduces a new benchmark for multimodal learning on IoT data. The benchmark includes 12 different modalities and 8 different tasks. The paper also provides several baseline results on the benchmark using a variety of existing multimodal learning techniques. The goal of the benchmark is to facilitate the application of multimodal learning in the IoT domain.

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

1. The benchmark is essentially a collection of existing datasets with a somewhat far-fetched IoT theme. The datasets were not originally collected with the intention of being used for multimodal learning, and the connection to IoT is not very clear. The tasks defined also seem arbitrary. I'm not sure if this benchmark will be useful for the community.
2. The paper provides a collection of datasets and tasks without providing any specific insights or analysis that could guide future research directions. The paper lacks a clear demonstration of how the proposed benchmark enables the development of new multimodal learning methods that would not be possible without it. The baseline results, while comprehensive, do not offer significant insights into the challenges or potential of the benchmark.
3. The paper is not well-written. The language is redundant, and the figures are of low quality. For example, Figure 1 is blurry and difficult to read. The descriptions of the datasets and tasks are not clear and concise, making it hard to understand the specifics of the benchmark.

### Suggestions

The paper needs to provide a more compelling justification for the proposed benchmark. The authors should clearly articulate the specific challenges that this benchmark addresses and how it differs from existing multimodal datasets. The connection to IoT should be made more explicit, demonstrating how the benchmark reflects real-world IoT scenarios. For example, the authors could provide use cases that show how the different modalities and tasks are relevant to practical IoT applications. The tasks should be more clearly motivated, with a discussion of why these specific tasks are important for the IoT domain and how they can be used to evaluate different aspects of multimodal learning. Furthermore, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the baseline results. Instead of just presenting the numbers, the authors should discuss the performance of different methods on different tasks and modalities, highlighting the challenges and limitations of existing methods. This analysis should provide insights into the specific difficulties of multimodal learning in the IoT domain and suggest potential research directions. For example, the authors could analyze the performance of different fusion strategies or the impact of missing modalities on the task performance. The paper should also explore the potential of the benchmark for zero-shot or few-shot learning, which would be particularly relevant for IoT applications where labeled data is often scarce. Finally, the presentation of the paper needs significant improvement. The figures should be of higher quality, and the descriptions of the datasets and tasks should be more clear and concise. The writing should be more precise, avoiding redundant language. The authors should also consider reorganizing the paper to improve the flow of information. For example, the description of the datasets and tasks could be moved to the main body of the paper, rather than being relegated to the appendix. The paper should also include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the benchmark and potential future directions for research.
