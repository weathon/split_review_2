### Summary

The paper introduces LUMA, a novel benchmark dataset designed for learning from uncertain and multimodal data. LUMA combines audio, image, and textual data across 50 classes, extending the well-known CIFAR-10/100 dataset with audio samples from three audio corpora and text data generated using the Gemma-7B Large Language Model (LLM). A key feature of LUMA is the ability to inject controlled amounts of uncertainty, allowing for tailored experiments and benchmarking initiatives. The dataset is provided as a Python package, enabling users to control data diversity, noise levels for each modality, and the inclusion of out-of-distribution samples. Baseline models and three uncertainty quantification methods are also provided to support the development and evaluation of trustworthy multimodal deep learning approaches. The authors anticipate that LUMA will aid the ICLR community in designing more robust machine learning approaches for safety-critical applications.

### Weaknesses

#### comment

1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the dataset's characteristics, such as the distribution of data across different classes and modalities. Specifically, the paper lacks a thorough investigation into potential imbalances or biases within the dataset that could affect model training and evaluation. For instance, are there certain classes that are overrepresented in specific modalities, and how might this impact the performance of multimodal models? A more granular analysis, including visualizations of class distributions and modality-specific feature spaces, is needed to fully understand the dataset's properties.
2. The paper does not compare LUMA with other existing multimodal datasets, which would help to contextualize its contributions and limitations. The absence of a comparative analysis makes it difficult to assess the unique value proposition of LUMA relative to other available resources. A detailed comparison should include aspects such as dataset size, modality types, annotation quality, and the availability of uncertainty annotations or mechanisms for injecting uncertainty. This would help researchers understand where LUMA excels and where it might fall short compared to other datasets.
3. The paper does not provide a thorough evaluation of the baseline models and uncertainty quantification methods on the LUMA dataset. The evaluation lacks a comprehensive exploration of different model architectures and training strategies. For example, the paper does not explore the impact of different fusion techniques on the performance of multimodal models, nor does it investigate the sensitivity of uncertainty quantification methods to various hyperparameter settings. A more rigorous evaluation should include a wider range of models and uncertainty quantification techniques, along with a detailed analysis of their performance under different conditions.
4. The paper does not discuss the potential biases or ethical considerations associated with the dataset, which is important for ensuring responsible use of the data. The paper should address potential biases in the data collection process, annotation, and the inherent biases that might be present in the text data generated by the LLM. It is crucial to discuss how these biases could affect the performance of models trained on LUMA and how researchers can mitigate these risks. Furthermore, the paper should consider the ethical implications of using this dataset in various applications, particularly in safety-critical domains.

### Suggestions

To address the lack of detailed dataset analysis, the authors should include a comprehensive statistical analysis of the LUMA dataset. This should include, but not be limited to, detailed class distributions for each modality, visualizations of the feature spaces for each modality using techniques like t-SNE or UMAP, and an analysis of the correlation between modalities. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the presence of any potential biases in the dataset, such as gender or racial biases, and report these findings transparently. This analysis should also include a discussion of the limitations of the dataset, such as the potential for overfitting to specific data distributions or the lack of diversity in certain modalities. This detailed analysis will provide a better understanding of the dataset's characteristics and help researchers use it more effectively.

To contextualize the contributions of LUMA, the authors should provide a detailed comparison with existing multimodal datasets. This comparison should not only focus on the size and modality types but also on the quality of annotations, the availability of uncertainty annotations, and the mechanisms for injecting uncertainty. For example, the authors could compare LUMA with datasets like CMU-MOSI, which focuses on audio-visual sentiment analysis, or with datasets used in multimodal machine learning benchmarks. This comparison should highlight the unique features of LUMA, such as its ability to inject controlled uncertainty, and discuss how these features make it a valuable resource for the research community. The authors should also discuss the limitations of LUMA compared to other datasets, such as the lack of real-world complexity or the potential for biases in the generated text data.

Finally, to improve the evaluation of baseline models and uncertainty quantification methods, the authors should conduct a more thorough evaluation using a wider range of models and techniques. This should include exploring different fusion techniques for combining modalities, such as early fusion, late fusion, and attention-based fusion. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of uncertainty quantification methods to different hyperparameter settings and data conditions. Furthermore, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the performance of these methods under different levels of injected uncertainty, including both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. This evaluation should also include a discussion of the limitations of the baseline models and uncertainty quantification methods, and suggest directions for future research. The authors should also address the potential biases and ethical considerations associated with the dataset, including a discussion of how these biases could affect the performance of models trained on LUMA and how researchers can mitigate these risks.
