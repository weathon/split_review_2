## Summary

This paper introduces a novel prompt-driven mixture of experts (MoE) framework for universal anomaly detection in multi-modal, multi-organ medical images. The core idea is to leverage natural language prompts to guide the selection of specialized expert decoders, each trained to identify anomalies within specific imaging modalities and anatomical contexts. The proposed architecture comprises a vision encoder, a text encoder, a routing network, and multiple hallucination-aware expert decoders. The vision encoder processes the input medical images, while the text encoder transforms the natural language prompts into task-specific feature representations. The routing network then dynamically selects the most suitable expert decoders based on these representations. A key innovation is the introduction of hallucination-aware expert decoders, designed to minimize false positives by explicitly modeling and mitigating reconstruction errors in normal regions. The authors curated a dataset of 12,153 images spanning five modalities (X-ray, MRI, OCT, ultrasound, and CT) and four anatomical structures (lung, brain/head, retina, and breast). The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms both single-task and universal anomaly detection models across multiple datasets, achieving higher AUC, F1 score, and accuracy. The authors emphasize that the use of natural language prompts enhances model interpretability and user interaction. The framework's ability to handle diverse medical images within a single model, coupled with its improved accuracy and interpretability, represents a significant contribution to the field of medical anomaly detection. The authors also plan to make the code and data publicly available, furthering the impact of their work. The paper's approach to integrating multi-modal and multi-organ data within a single model, along with the novel use of hallucination-aware experts, offers a promising direction for advancing the field of medical image analysis. The framework's potential to improve the accuracy and reliability of anomaly detection in clinical settings is substantial, potentially leading to better diagnostic tools and patient outcomes. The authors' focus on interpretability is also crucial, as it can help build trust in AI-driven medical systems and facilitate their adoption by healthcare professionals. Overall, this work presents a well-designed and thoroughly evaluated approach to a challenging problem, with significant potential for real-world impact.
