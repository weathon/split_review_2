### Summary

This paper introduces LUMA, a multimodal dataset designed to benchmark uncertainty quantification in deep learning models. It combines image, audio, and text data from 50 classes, extending CIFAR-10/100 with audio from three audio corpora and text generated by a large language model. LUMA allows controlled injection of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties, providing a Python package to manipulate noise levels and add out-of-distribution samples. Baseline models and three uncertainty quantification methods are included to support the development of robust, trustworthy multimodal models, particularly for safety-critical applications.

1. The dataset is limited to a subset of CIFAR, which may restrict the complexity and diversity of the data. The use of CIFAR-10/100 as the image base limits the resolution and complexity of visual scenes, potentially hindering the development of models that can generalize to more realistic scenarios. This lack of complexity might not fully challenge the uncertainty quantification methods, especially when dealing with more intricate real-world data.

2. The audio samples consist only of class labels pronounced by different speakers, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world audio data. This simplified audio setup, focusing solely on clean speech of class labels, lacks the nuances of environmental sounds, background noise, and other real-world audio complexities. This could lead to an overestimation of model performance in practical applications where audio data is often noisy and varied.

3. The text data is generated by a large language model without validation, which may introduce factual inaccuracies and biases. The reliance on a single LLM for text generation, without subsequent validation, raises concerns about the potential for factual errors, inconsistencies, and biases inherent in the LLM's training data. This could lead to models learning spurious correlations or exhibiting biased behavior, which is particularly problematic in safety-critical applications.

### Suggestions

The authors should consider expanding the dataset to include more complex image data, such as higher-resolution images with more diverse scenes and objects. This could involve incorporating datasets like ImageNet or COCO, which offer a greater variety of visual content. Additionally, the authors should explore methods to generate or collect more realistic audio data, including environmental sounds, background noise, and variations in speech. This could involve using datasets like AudioSet or UrbanSound8K, which provide a more comprehensive representation of real-world audio. Furthermore, the text data should be validated and augmented to reduce biases and factual inaccuracies. This could involve manual verification of a subset of the generated text, or using multiple LLMs with different training data to generate text and then cross-validating the outputs. The authors should also consider incorporating techniques to measure and mitigate biases in the generated text.

To address the limitations of the audio data, the authors could explore techniques such as data augmentation to introduce noise and variations in the audio samples. This could include adding background noise, varying the pitch and speed of the speech, and simulating different acoustic environments. Additionally, the authors could consider incorporating more complex audio tasks, such as audio classification or audio event detection, to better evaluate the performance of uncertainty quantification methods in more realistic scenarios. The authors should also investigate the impact of different types of noise on the performance of the models, and how the uncertainty quantification methods can be adapted to handle these different types of noise.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the limitations of the dataset and the potential impact of these limitations on the evaluation of uncertainty quantification methods. This analysis should include a discussion of the potential biases and inaccuracies in the text data, and how these biases could affect the performance of the models. The authors should also discuss the limitations of the audio data, and how the simplified audio setup could lead to an overestimation of model performance in real-world applications. This analysis should be included in the paper to provide a more complete understanding of the dataset and its limitations.
