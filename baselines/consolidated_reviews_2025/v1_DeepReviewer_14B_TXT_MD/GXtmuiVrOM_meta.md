## Summary

This paper introduces DORAEMON, a novel domain randomization method designed to enhance the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of reinforcement learning agents. The core idea behind DORAEMON is to automatically shape the distribution of dynamics parameters during training in simulation, without requiring any real-world data. This is achieved by maximizing the entropy of the training distribution while ensuring that the policy maintains a certain level of success. The method operates by gradually increasing the diversity of sampled dynamics parameters, as long as the probability of success of the current policy remains sufficiently high. This approach aims to strike a balance between exploration and exploitation, leading to more robust and generalizable policies. The authors validate their approach through a series of simulation experiments and a real-world robotic manipulation task. In simulation, DORAEMON is evaluated on six benchmark tasks from the OpenAI Gym MuJoCo environment, demonstrating superior performance compared to several baseline domain randomization techniques. The method's ability to adapt the dynamics distribution is shown to be effective in achieving a good trade-off between exploration and exploitation. The real-world evaluation focuses on a robotic arm pushing a box with an unknown center of mass, a task chosen to highlight the method's ability to transfer learned policies to real-world scenarios. The results indicate that DORAEMON achieves successful zero-shot transfer, outperforming the baseline methods in this setting. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel approach to domain randomization, which automatically adapts the training distribution based on the policy's success rate. This method offers a promising avenue for improving the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of reinforcement learning agents, potentially reducing the need for expensive real-world data collection. The empirical results, both in simulation and the real world, provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach. However, the paper also acknowledges certain limitations, such as the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameter choices and the need for a well-defined success indicator function. These limitations, along with the limited scope of the real-world evaluation, suggest avenues for future research and improvement. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of sim-to-real transfer, offering a practical and effective method for enhancing the robustness and generalizability of reinforcement learning agents.

## Strengths

The primary strength of this paper lies in its introduction of DORAEMON, a novel and effective domain randomization method. The core idea of maximizing the entropy of the training distribution while ensuring a certain level of policy success is both intuitive and powerful. This approach allows for automatic adaptation of the dynamics distribution, eliminating the need for manual tuning or real-world data. The method's ability to gradually increase the diversity of sampled dynamics parameters, as long as the policy maintains a sufficient success rate, is a key innovation. This mechanism ensures a balance between exploration and exploitation, leading to more robust and generalizable policies. The empirical results presented in the paper provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of DORAEMON. The method consistently outperforms several baseline domain randomization techniques across a range of simulation tasks, demonstrating its ability to achieve a good trade-off between exploration and exploitation. The experiments in Section 5.2, which analyze the impact of different hyperparameter settings, further highlight the method's adaptability. The real-world evaluation, although limited to a single task, provides compelling evidence for the method's sim-to-real transfer capabilities. The successful zero-shot transfer in the PandaPush environment, where a robotic arm learns to push a box with an unknown center of mass, is a significant achievement. This result demonstrates the practical potential of DORAEMON for real-world robotic applications. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making the method accessible to a wider audience. The authors clearly explain the core concepts and provide sufficient details for reproducibility. The inclusion of a toy problem in Section 4.2 helps to build intuition for the method's behavior. The analysis of the method's performance in Section 5.2, including the trade-off between success rate and entropy, provides valuable insights into its inner workings. The paper's focus on a practical problem, sim-to-real transfer, and its proposal of a novel solution, DORAEMON, make it a valuable contribution to the field of reinforcement learning. The method's ability to automatically adapt the dynamics distribution, coupled with its strong empirical performance, positions it as a promising approach for enhancing the robustness and generalizability of reinforcement learning agents.

## Weaknesses

While the paper presents a compelling approach to domain randomization, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. A primary concern, independently validated by multiple reviewers, is the limited scope of the real-world experimental validation. The paper dedicates Section 5.3 to a single sim-to-real experiment, the 'PandaPush' task, involving a 7-DoF robotic arm pushing a box. While this task demonstrates the method's potential, it is insufficient to fully assess its generalizability to diverse real-world scenarios. As the reviewers correctly point out, the pushing task, while useful as a starting point, does not capture the intricacies of more complex robotic tasks, such as manipulation of deformable objects, navigation in cluttered environments, or tasks involving intricate contact dynamics. The lack of diversity in the real-world evaluation raises concerns about the method's robustness and its ability to transfer to more challenging scenarios. This limitation is further exacerbated by the absence of a detailed analysis of the transfer gap, which would examine how performance degrades when moving from simulation to real-world scenarios, and identify potential failure modes. The paper's reliance on a single, relatively simple real-world task significantly limits the confidence in its general applicability. Another significant weakness, also highlighted by multiple reviewers, is the sensitivity of DORAEMON to hyperparameter choices. The paper acknowledges this sensitivity, particularly concerning the in-distribution success rate (α) and the success indicator function (σ(τ)). Section 5.2 includes experiments analyzing the impact of different α values and success thresholds, demonstrating how these choices affect the performance and the resulting dynamics distribution. However, the paper does not provide a thorough analysis of how to choose these hyperparameters for new tasks and environments. The lack of clear guidelines for selecting appropriate hyperparameter values limits the practical applicability of the method. The sensitivity to the success indicator function is particularly concerning, as it may be difficult to define for some tasks. The paper does not fully explore the challenges of defining appropriate success criteria for all types of tasks, and the sensitivity analysis in Section 5.2, while insightful, does not fully address this issue. The absence of a detailed discussion on the practical difficulties of defining the success indicator function for complex tasks is a significant limitation. Furthermore, the paper lacks a comprehensive discussion of the method's limitations. While the conclusion briefly touches upon some limitations, such as the potential need for a KL constraint and biasing distribution growth, it does not provide a thorough analysis of the method's sensitivity to environmental differences, potential failure modes, and computational costs. The experimental results hint at some limitations, such as performance drops in some environments as the distribution widens, but these are not explicitly discussed in the context of generalizability to vastly different environments. The absence of computational cost analysis is a significant omission for practical applicability. The paper does not quantify the computational overhead of the proposed method, especially in terms of memory and training time, which is crucial for practical deployment. Finally, the paper lacks a formal theoretical analysis of the method. While the intuition behind entropy maximization for generalization is explained, there are no theorems or proofs regarding the convergence of the algorithm or formal guarantees about the generalization performance. The analysis is primarily empirical, and the absence of a theoretical framework limits the understanding of the method's behavior and its potential limitations. The lack of a theoretical analysis also makes it difficult to compare the proposed method with other domain randomization techniques on a more fundamental level. These weaknesses, particularly the limited real-world evaluation, the sensitivity to hyperparameters, the lack of a detailed discussion on limitations, and the absence of a theoretical analysis, significantly impact the overall conclusions of the paper and suggest avenues for future research.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First and foremost, the paper needs a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method across a wider range of real-world tasks. The current evaluation, limited to a single pushing task, is insufficient to demonstrate the method's generalizability. I suggest evaluating DORAEMON on more complex robotic tasks, such as manipulation of deformable objects, assembly tasks, or locomotion tasks on different terrains. These tasks would provide a more rigorous assessment of the method's capabilities and limitations. Furthermore, the evaluation should include a detailed analysis of the transfer gap, examining how performance degrades when moving from simulation to real-world scenarios, and identifying potential failure modes. This analysis should also explore the method's sensitivity to variations in environmental parameters, such as friction, mass, and sensor noise. Second, the paper needs a more thorough analysis of the method's sensitivity to hyperparameter choices. I recommend conducting a more systematic investigation of how different hyperparameter settings affect the performance of the method, including the learning rate, the entropy regularization coefficient, and the parameters related to the dynamics distribution. This analysis should not only focus on the final performance but also on the training stability and convergence speed. Furthermore, the authors should investigate methods to reduce this sensitivity, such as adaptive hyperparameter tuning or robust optimization techniques. The paper should also provide clear guidelines for selecting appropriate hyperparameter values for new tasks and environments. Third, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the method's limitations. This should include an analysis of the method's sensitivity to environmental differences, potential failure modes, and computational costs. The authors should investigate how the method's performance degrades as the difference between the training and testing environments increases. They should also analyze the computational overhead of the method in terms of memory usage and training time, and compare it to other domain randomization techniques. This would provide a more complete picture of the method's strengths and weaknesses and help potential users understand its practical limitations. The authors should also consider providing guidelines for selecting appropriate hyperparameter values for different tasks and environments. Fourth, the paper should include a more detailed comparison with existing domain randomization techniques. While the paper mentions that DORAEMON outperforms other baselines, it does not provide a thorough analysis of the differences between these methods. A more detailed comparison should include an analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of each method, as well as a discussion of the specific scenarios where each method performs best. This would help to better understand the contributions of DORAEMON and its potential advantages over existing approaches. The authors should also consider comparing DORAEMON with more recent domain randomization methods, which may have addressed some of the limitations of the baselines used in the paper. Finally, while a full theoretical analysis may be challenging, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the method's theoretical properties. For instance, it would be useful to explore the relationship between the entropy of the randomized parameters and the generalization performance of the learned policy. It would also be helpful to provide some insights into the convergence properties of the algorithm, and to discuss the conditions under which the method is expected to perform well. Even a qualitative analysis of the method's behavior, based on existing theoretical results in related areas, would significantly strengthen the paper. These suggestions, if implemented, would significantly enhance the paper's impact and address the identified weaknesses.
