## Summary

This paper introduces a novel pruning framework for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), aiming to enhance their energy efficiency by leveraging the inherent sparsity of neuromorphic computing. The core contribution lies in the simultaneous application of unstructured weight pruning and unstructured neuron pruning, a departure from traditional methods that primarily focus on weight pruning alone. The authors propose an energy consumption model that quantifies the energy savings achieved through pruning, using the number of synaptic operations (SOPs) as a key metric. This model is then integrated into the training process, guiding the pruning of both weights and neurons. The method employs a re-parameterization technique, using a scaled sigmoid function to approximate binary masks, which are then applied to the weights and neurons. The training process involves a gradual scheduling approach for the scale factor of the sigmoid function, followed by a finalization step using the Heaviside step function. The empirical findings demonstrate significant energy efficiency gains, with the pruned networks achieving comparable accuracy to the original dense networks while requiring substantially fewer SOPs. Specifically, the authors report a 91-fold increase in energy efficiency on the CIFAR-10 dataset, with only a minor accuracy loss. The experiments are conducted on three datasets: CIFAR-10, DVS-CIFAR10, and ImageNet, using SpikingVGG and SpikingResNet architectures. The results indicate that the proposed pruning framework can effectively reduce the computational cost of SNNs, making them more suitable for deployment on resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware. The paper's overall significance lies in its potential to bridge the gap between the theoretical energy efficiency of SNNs and their practical implementation, by addressing the redundancy present in deep SNN models. By combining unstructured weight and neuron pruning, the authors offer a promising approach to maximize the utilization of sparsity in neuromorphic computing, thereby paving the way for more energy-efficient SNNs. However, the paper also has limitations, particularly in the lack of detailed comparisons with existing energy consumption models and the absence of specific implementation details, which I will elaborate on in the following sections. Despite these shortcomings, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of SNN research, offering a novel and effective approach to enhance their energy efficiency.

## Strengths

The primary strength of this paper lies in its novel approach to pruning SNNs by combining unstructured weight and neuron pruning. This is a significant departure from traditional methods that primarily focus on weight pruning, and it aligns well with the goal of maximizing sparsity in neuromorphic computing. The authors' decision to target both weights and neurons directly addresses the redundancy present in deep SNN models, leading to substantial energy efficiency gains. The proposed energy consumption model, while not extensively compared to existing models, provides a useful framework for quantifying the energy savings achieved through pruning. The use of synaptic operations (SOPs) as a key metric is also a sensible choice, given the event-driven nature of SNNs. The empirical results are compelling, demonstrating that the pruned networks can achieve comparable accuracy to the original dense networks while requiring significantly fewer SOPs. The reported 91-fold increase in energy efficiency on the CIFAR-10 dataset is particularly impressive. The experiments are conducted on three different datasets, including CIFAR-10, DVS-CIFAR10, and ImageNet, which provides a reasonable assessment of the method's generalizability. The use of SpikingVGG and SpikingResNet architectures also adds to the credibility of the results. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly articulate their motivation, methodology, and experimental results. The use of equations and diagrams helps to clarify the technical details of the proposed framework. The paper's focus on energy efficiency is also a significant strength, as it addresses a critical challenge in the practical deployment of SNNs on resource-constrained hardware. The authors' emphasis on leveraging the inherent sparsity of neuromorphic computing is also a key strength, as it aligns well with the potential of SNNs for low-power applications. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of SNN research, offering a novel and effective approach to enhance their energy efficiency. The combination of unstructured weight and neuron pruning, the use of an energy consumption model, and the compelling empirical results all contribute to the paper's strengths.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, several weaknesses need to be addressed. First, and most significantly, the paper lacks a detailed comparison of its proposed energy consumption model with existing models. While the authors introduce their model in Section 3.1, they do not adequately discuss how it differs from or improves upon other energy consumption models for SNNs. The paper mentions the basis of the model and cites Furber (2016), but it does not delve into a comparative analysis, failing to articulate the limitations of other models that its model aims to address. This omission makes it difficult to assess the novelty and impact of the proposed model. The paper's justification for the chosen model is brief and does not address potential limitations of other approaches. This lack of comparative analysis is a critical weakness, as it undermines the foundation upon which the pruning framework is built. Second, the paper lacks sufficient implementation details, making it difficult to reproduce the results. While the core idea of the pruning framework is presented, crucial details about the training process and hyperparameter settings are missing. For instance, the paper mentions using backpropagation through time (BPTT) in Section 5.1, but it does not specify the number of time steps used during training. The type of encoder used to convert input data into spike trains is also not explicitly mentioned in the main paper. Furthermore, specific details about the optimization algorithm, learning rate, batch size, and other hyperparameters are not explicitly provided. This lack of detail hinders reproducibility and makes it challenging to assess the practical applicability of the method. The paper also lacks a dedicated section discussing the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors present compelling results, they do not address the computational overhead of the pruning process itself. The sensitivity of the method to different network architectures is also not thoroughly explored. The paper focuses on SpikingVGG and SpikingResNet architectures, but it does not discuss how the method might perform on other types of SNN architectures. This lack of discussion on limitations makes it difficult to assess the robustness and generalizability of the proposed approach. The paper also lacks a discussion of potential challenges in deploying the method on real neuromorphic hardware. While the authors emphasize energy efficiency, they do not address the compatibility of the pruning strategy with specific hardware architectures or the impact of hardware constraints on the method's performance. This omission is a significant weakness, as it limits the practical relevance of the work. Finally, the paper does not provide sufficient details about the hardware and software environment used for the experiments. While the paper mentions using PyTorch, it does not specify the type of CPU/GPU used, the version of PyTorch, or any other specific libraries or dependencies. This lack of information makes it difficult to verify the reported performance gains and hinders the reproducibility of the results. These weaknesses, particularly the lack of comparison with existing energy models, the insufficient implementation details, the missing discussion of limitations, and the lack of hardware/software information, significantly impact the paper's overall contribution and need to be addressed.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should include a detailed comparative analysis of their proposed energy consumption model with existing models. This analysis should not only highlight the differences in the mathematical formulations but also discuss the underlying assumptions and the specific scenarios where each model is most applicable. For instance, the paper should discuss whether existing models fail to account for the sparsity introduced by pruning, or if they make simplifying assumptions about the hardware architecture that limit their accuracy. A table summarizing the key features of each model, including their strengths and weaknesses, would be beneficial. Furthermore, the paper should provide a more in-depth explanation of how the proposed model captures the energy savings achieved through unstructured pruning, and how this is validated through experimental results. This would strengthen the claims of the paper and provide a more solid foundation for the proposed method. Second, the authors need to provide a step-by-step description of how the pruning method is integrated into the training process. This should include a clear explanation of how the pruning masks are initialized, updated, and applied during training. The paper should also discuss the specific optimization techniques used to train the pruned network, and how these differ from standard training procedures. For example, it should clarify whether the pruning process is performed jointly with the weight optimization, or if it is a separate step. Furthermore, the paper should provide a detailed analysis of the hyperparameter settings used in the experiments, including the learning rate, batch size, and the parameters specific to the pruning method. A sensitivity analysis of these hyperparameters would also be valuable, as it would help to understand the robustness of the method and its applicability to different datasets and network architectures. The paper should also include a discussion of the computational cost of the pruning process, and how it compares to the cost of training the original network. Third, the authors should include a dedicated section discussing the limitations of the proposed method. This discussion should include an analysis of the computational overhead of the pruning process, the sensitivity of the method to different network architectures, and the potential for further optimization. For example, the paper should discuss whether the proposed method is applicable to recurrent SNNs, or if it is limited to feedforward networks. The paper should also explore the potential for combining the proposed method with other optimization techniques, such as quantization or knowledge distillation. Furthermore, the paper should suggest specific directions for future research, such as exploring the application of the method to different types of SNNs, or developing more efficient algorithms for pruning SNNs. This would help to position the work within the broader context of SNN research and highlight its potential for future impact. Fourth, the authors should provide a detailed description of the hardware and software environment used for the experiments. This should include the specific type of CPU/GPU used, the version of the deep learning framework, and any specific libraries or dependencies. This information is crucial for other researchers to replicate the results and build upon this work. Finally, the authors should consider releasing their code to further enhance reproducibility. By addressing these points, the authors can significantly strengthen their paper and make it more impactful for the SNN research community.

## Questions

Several key questions arise from my analysis of this paper. First, regarding the energy consumption model, I am curious about the specific assumptions made in its development. How does the model account for the complexities of real neuromorphic hardware, and what are its limitations in capturing the energy consumption of different types of operations? Specifically, how does the model handle the energy costs associated with memory access and data movement, which can be significant in neuromorphic systems? Second, concerning the implementation details, I would like to know more about the specific optimization techniques used during training. What is the learning rate schedule, and how are the hyperparameters of the pruning method, such as the scale factor of the sigmoid function, chosen? How sensitive is the method to these hyperparameters, and what is the impact of different choices on the final performance and energy efficiency? Third, regarding the pruning process, I am interested in the computational overhead associated with applying the masks during training and inference. How does this overhead compare to the energy savings achieved through pruning, and what are the trade-offs involved? Also, how does the method handle the dynamic nature of SNNs, where the spiking activity can change over time? Fourth, regarding the limitations, I would like to know more about the potential challenges in deploying the method on real neuromorphic hardware. What are the specific hardware constraints that might affect the performance of the pruned networks, and how can these constraints be addressed? Are there specific types of neuromorphic architectures where the proposed method would be more or less effective? Finally, regarding the generalizability of the method, I am curious about its performance on different types of SNN architectures, such as recurrent SNNs or SNNs with more complex neuron models. How does the method handle the variability in spiking patterns and temporal dynamics that might be present in these different architectures? Addressing these questions would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the proposed method and its potential for practical applications.
