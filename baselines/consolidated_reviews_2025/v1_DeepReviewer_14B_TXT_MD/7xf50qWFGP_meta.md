## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to online Laplacian-based representation learning within the context of reinforcement learning (RL). The core idea is to simultaneously update the state representation, modeled as a graph, alongside the policy being learned by the RL agent. This is a significant departure from traditional methods that often rely on fixed representations or offline computation of Laplacian embeddings, which can be computationally infeasible in large or continuous state spaces. The authors propose the Asymmetric Graph Drawing Objective (AGDO) as a means to learn the Laplacian representation in an online fashion. They provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating the ergodic convergence of running online projected gradient descent on AGDO, under the assumption of a bounded policy drift. This theoretical result is a key contribution, as it provides a formal guarantee for the convergence of the proposed online learning method. The empirical validation of the approach is conducted through simulation studies in grid world environments. These experiments compare the proposed AGDO method with the Augmented Lagrangian method of Gomez et al. (ALLO), showing that AGDO can effectively converge to the true Laplacian representation. The authors also explore the impact of different policy learning algorithms on the accuracy of the learned representation, highlighting the importance of the bounded policy drift assumption. Overall, the paper makes a valuable contribution by addressing the challenge of online Laplacian representation learning in RL, providing both theoretical guarantees and empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method. The work opens up new avenues for leveraging graph-based representations in online RL settings, where the state space is dynamic and the policy is constantly evolving. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the scope of the empirical evaluation and the depth of the computational analysis, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections.
