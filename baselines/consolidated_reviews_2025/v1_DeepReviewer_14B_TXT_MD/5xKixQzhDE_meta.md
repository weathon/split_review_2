## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to dataset condensation, specifically tailored for hyperparameter optimization (HPO). The core idea revolves around creating a synthetic validation dataset that preserves the performance ranking of different hyperparameter configurations. The authors propose a method called Hyperparameter-Calibrated Dataset Condensation (HCDC), which achieves this by aligning the hyperparameter gradients computed on the original and synthetic datasets. This alignment is achieved through implicit differentiation and an efficient inverse Hessian approximation using the Neumann series. The authors demonstrate that this alignment is equivalent to preserving the performance ranking of hyperparameters, which is a key contribution of the paper. The method is evaluated on both image classification tasks using CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, and graph node classification tasks using Cora, Citeseer, Ogbn-arxiv, and Reddit datasets. The experimental results show that HCDC effectively maintains the validation-performance rankings of models and speeds up hyperparameter/architecture search. The authors also show that the method can be applied to neural architecture search (NAS), demonstrating its versatility. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel perspective on dataset condensation, focusing on hyperparameter optimization and introducing the concept of hyperparameter calibration. By aligning hyperparameter gradients, the authors provide a method that is both theoretically grounded and empirically effective. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed method, demonstrating its effectiveness in preserving the performance rankings of different hyperparameters. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of dataset condensation and hyperparameter optimization, offering a practical and efficient approach for accelerating these processes.

## Strengths

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the formulation of dataset condensation for hyperparameter search is both novel and interesting. The authors introduce the concept of hyperparameter calibration, which ensures that the performance rankings of different hyperparameters are preserved between the original and condensed datasets. This is a significant departure from traditional dataset condensation methods that focus primarily on preserving model performance. The idea of aligning hyperparameter gradients as a means to achieve this calibration is also a novel contribution. The authors demonstrate that this alignment is equivalent to preserving the performance ranking, which is a key theoretical finding. Second, the authors' use of implicit differentiation and efficient inverse Hessian approximation is a notable technical innovation. This allows for efficient computation of hypergradients, which is crucial for the practical application of the method. The use of the Neumann series approximation for the inverse Hessian is a clever way to reduce the computational burden. Third, the experimental results are comprehensive and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The authors show that HCDC effectively maintains the validation-performance rankings of models and speeds up hyperparameter/architecture search for tasks on both images and graphs. The experiments on both image and graph data provide a good range of evaluation. The fact that the method can be applied to both hyperparameter optimization and neural architecture search (NAS) further highlights its versatility and practical value. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors clearly explain the motivation behind their work, the details of their method, and the results of their experiments. The theoretical analysis is also well-presented and supports the claims made in the paper. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of dataset condensation and hyperparameter optimization, offering a practical and efficient approach for accelerating these processes.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, while the paper introduces the concept of a continuous extension of the hyperparameter search space, it does not fully explore the practical implications of this assumption, particularly when dealing with discrete hyperparameters. Although the authors propose a method to handle discrete hyperparameters by constructing trajectories in an extended search space (Section 5.2), the paper lacks a thorough analysis of how this continuous relaxation impacts the final performance. For instance, while learning rate can be continuously extended, it's unclear how the method handles the discrete nature of other hyperparameters like batch size, number of layers, or nodes. The paper does not provide any experiments that compare the performance of the proposed method with and without this continuous relaxation, which makes it difficult to assess the impact of this assumption. This is a partially valid concern, as the paper does address discrete hyperparameters, but the analysis is not thorough enough. My confidence level in this issue is high. Second, the paper lacks a detailed computational complexity analysis of the proposed method. While the authors mention using implicit differentiation and inverse Hessian approximation for efficiency (Section 5.1), they do not provide a rigorous analysis of how the computational cost scales with the size of the hyperparameter space, the size of the condensed dataset, and the number of training epochs. The paper does not provide a breakdown of the time complexity for each step of the algorithm, such as the computation of hypergradients and the inverse Hessian approximation. This makes it difficult to assess the practical feasibility of the method, especially when compared to other dataset condensation techniques. This is a valid concern, and my confidence level is high. Third, the experimental evaluation is limited in scope. The image classification experiments are conducted on relatively small datasets like CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (Section 6). The paper lacks experiments on larger image datasets, such as ImageNet, which would be necessary to demonstrate the scalability of the proposed method. This is a valid concern, and my confidence level is high. Furthermore, the paper primarily focuses on image classification and graph node classification tasks. It would be beneficial to evaluate the proposed method on other types of tasks, such as natural language processing or time series analysis, to demonstrate its generalizability. The current evaluation is limited to image and graph data, and it is unclear how the method would perform on tasks with different data modalities and characteristics. This is a valid concern, and my confidence level is high. Fourth, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method compared to other dataset condensation methods. While the paper shows a reduction in search time in the NAS experiments (Table 3), it does not provide a comprehensive breakdown of the computational overhead of HCDC itself or a direct comparison with other dataset condensation techniques. This makes it difficult to assess the practical benefits of the proposed approach. This is a valid concern, and my confidence level is high. Finally, the paper does not include a sensitivity analysis of the HCDC method's performance to its own hyperparameters. The paper mentions hyperparameters like learning rates for hyperparameter updates, but it does not analyze the impact of different hyperparameter settings on the performance of the proposed method. Without a sensitivity analysis, it is difficult to determine the robustness of the method and provide practical guidance for its use. This is a valid concern, and my confidence level is high.

Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct a more thorough investigation into the implications of the continuous hyperparameter space assumption. Specifically, they should perform experiments that compare the performance of the proposed method with and without the continuous relaxation of discrete hyperparameters. This analysis should include a discussion of the potential limitations of this approach and how it might affect the final performance. The authors could also explore alternative methods for handling discrete hyperparameters directly, such as using combinatorial optimization techniques, to avoid the potential issues associated with continuous relaxation. Second, the authors should provide a detailed computational complexity analysis of the proposed method. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time complexity for each step of the algorithm, such as the computation of hypergradients and the inverse Hessian approximation. The analysis should also consider the impact of various factors, such as the size of the hyperparameter space, the size of the condensed dataset, and the number of training epochs, on the overall computational cost. The authors should also compare the computational cost of HCDC with other dataset condensation methods, including both training time and memory usage. This comparison should be performed on a range of datasets and hyperparameter settings to provide a comprehensive understanding of the practical applicability of the proposed method. Third, the authors should expand the experimental evaluation to include larger and more diverse datasets. This should include experiments on larger image datasets, such as ImageNet, to demonstrate the scalability of the proposed method. Furthermore, the authors should evaluate the method on other types of tasks, such as natural language processing and time series analysis, to demonstrate its generalizability. These additional experiments would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's strengths and limitations. Fourth, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method compared to other dataset condensation methods. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and memory requirements of the proposed method, as well as a comparison with existing techniques. This would allow for a more informed assessment of the trade-off between computational cost and performance. Finally, the authors should conduct a sensitivity analysis of the HCDC method's performance to its own hyperparameters. This analysis should include a discussion of the optimal range for each hyperparameter and the trade-offs between different settings. The authors should also provide guidelines for selecting appropriate hyperparameter values based on the characteristics of the dataset and the task. This would make the method more robust and easier to use in practice. These changes would significantly strengthen the paper and make it more impactful.
