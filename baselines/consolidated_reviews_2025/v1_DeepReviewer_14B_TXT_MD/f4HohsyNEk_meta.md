## Summary

This paper introduces NeuManifold, a novel two-stage approach for converting implicit neural representations into explicit, high-quality, watertight manifold meshes with neural textures. The method leverages the strengths of both neural volumetric fields and differentiable rasterization. In the first stage, a neural field method, such as NeuS or TensoRF, is employed to initialize the geometry and appearance of the scene. This initialization is crucial for providing a good starting point for the subsequent mesh optimization. The second stage involves the use of Differentiable Marching Cubes (DiffMC) to extract an initial mesh from the density grid. This mesh is then refined through differentiable rasterization using nvdiffrast, which optimizes both the geometry and the appearance of the mesh. The authors demonstrate that their method achieves competitive performance in terms of both mesh quality and novel view synthesis compared to previous volume rendering methods, while also achieving significantly faster rendering speeds. The resulting mesh-based representation is compatible with existing graphics pipelines, enabling downstream 3D applications such as simulation. The core contribution of this work lies in the combination of neural volumetric fields for initialization, DiffMC for mesh extraction, and differentiable rasterization for mesh optimization, resulting in a method that produces high-quality, watertight meshes with efficient rendering capabilities. The paper presents extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors also highlight the potential of their method for various applications, including geometry editing, appearance editing, and physical simulation, demonstrating the versatility of the generated mesh-based representation. Overall, NeuManifold presents a compelling approach for bridging the gap between implicit neural representations and explicit mesh-based models, offering a practical solution for high-quality 3D reconstruction and rendering.

The visual results presented in the paper are impressive, demonstrating the method's ability to recover high-quality geometry with faithful appearance. ## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. Firstly, the authors should investigate and incorporate color calibration techniques to address the color shifts observed in real-world examples. This could involve using a color chart during image acquisition or employing white balancing algorithms to ensure consistent color representation across different views. Furthermore, they should consider incorporating color consistency constraints directly into the optimization process, potentially by adding a loss term that penalizes color discrepancies between the rendered and input images, using a perceptual color difference metric. This would encourage the model to learn a more accurate color representation while maintaining the fidelity of the reconstructed geometry. Additionally, exploring different neural texture representations that are more robust to color variations could also be beneficial. Secondly, the authors should conduct experiments on the DTU dataset, a standard benchmark for multi-view 3D reconstruction. This would allow for a direct comparison with state-of-the-art multi-view stereo methods and provide a more robust evaluation of the method's generalizability. The evaluation should include quantitative metrics such as root mean squared error (RMSE) of the point cloud, as well as qualitative comparisons of the reconstructed meshes. Furthermore, the authors should analyze the method's performance on different scenes within the DTU dataset, as this would provide insights into its robustness across varying levels of geometric complexity and texture richness. Thirdly, the authors should provide a more thorough comparison with different neural field representations, particularly the original NeRF implementation. This comparison should include both quantitative and qualitative evaluations, focusing on aspects such as rendering speed, geometric accuracy, and visual fidelity. The authors should also analyze the trade-offs between these different neural field representations, highlighting the specific advantages and disadvantages of their proposed approach. Fourthly, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the computational cost of each stage of their pipeline, including the time spent in neural network inference, mesh extraction using DiffMC, and differentiable rasterization. This analysis should not only include the total time but also the time per triangle or per vertex, which would be more informative for understanding the scalability of the method. Furthermore, the memory footprint of each stage should be analyzed, especially when dealing with high-resolution meshes or complex scenes. This analysis should be performed on a variety of hardware configurations to understand the method's performance across different platforms. Fifthly, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the method's limitations when dealing with specular areas. The paper should elaborate on the specific types of specularities that cause issues, such as high-frequency reflections or anisotropic reflections. Additionally, it would be helpful to analyze how the method's performance degrades as the specular component becomes more prominent in the scene. The authors could also consider providing visualizations of the reconstructed meshes in specular regions, which would help to illustrate the limitations of the method. Furthermore, the authors should discuss potential strategies for addressing these limitations, such as incorporating inverse rendering techniques or using more advanced neural field representations that can better capture specularities. Sixthly, the authors should provide more details on the implementation of the proposed method, including the exact architecture of the TensoRF network, the specific form of the rendering loss, and other relevant hyperparameters. This level of detail is crucial for reproducibility and for understanding the method's behavior. Seventhly, the authors should conduct more comprehensive ablation studies, including the impact of different differentiable rasterization techniques and the number of training views. This would provide a better understanding of the contribution of each component of the proposed method. Finally, the authors should provide a more thorough discussion of the limitations of their method, including its performance on scenes with complex topologies or thin structures, the limitations of the neural texture representation, and how the method could be extended to handle dynamic scenes or varying lighting conditions. This would provide a more complete understanding of the method's capabilities and limitations and help guide future research in this area.
