## Summary

This paper introduces LR0.FM, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the zero-shot classification performance of visual-language foundation models (FMs) when subjected to low-resolution (LR) images. This is a critical area of investigation, as real-world applications often involve images with varying degrees of resolution degradation, yet the robustness of FMs to such conditions remains underexplored. The authors conduct a comprehensive evaluation of 10 foundation models, spanning 66 backbones and 15 datasets, providing a broad perspective on the current state of FM robustness to low resolution. A key contribution of this work is the introduction of a new metric, Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR), which aims to provide a more balanced evaluation of model performance across different resolutions and datasets compared to existing metrics. The authors also propose a simple yet effective method, LRTK0, to enhance model robustness against low-resolution inputs without altering the pre-trained weights. This method involves introducing low-resolution-specific tokens that learn to bridge the gap between high-resolution and low-resolution feature spaces. The core findings of the paper reveal that larger models tend to exhibit greater robustness to resolution degradation, and that the quality of the pre-training dataset is more crucial than its size. Furthermore, the authors observe that fine-tuned models are generally less robust against low-resolution images compared to their base counterparts. The LRTK0 method demonstrates consistent improvements in robustness across various models and datasets, highlighting its potential for practical applications. Overall, this paper makes a significant contribution by systematically evaluating the impact of low resolution on FM performance, introducing a new metric for robustness evaluation, and proposing a simple yet effective method for enhancing robustness. The findings provide valuable insights into the limitations of current FMs and offer a practical approach for improving their performance in real-world scenarios with low-resolution images.

## Strengths

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the introduction of the LR0.FM benchmark is a significant contribution. It addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of foundation models by focusing on their performance under low-resolution conditions, a factor that is often overlooked but highly relevant in real-world applications. The comprehensive nature of the benchmark, encompassing 10 foundation models, 66 backbones, and 15 datasets, provides a broad and robust evaluation of the current state of FM robustness to low resolution. This extensive evaluation allows for more reliable and generalizable conclusions. Second, the proposed LRTK0 method is a notable strength. It offers a simple yet effective approach to enhance model robustness against low-resolution inputs without requiring any modification to the pre-trained weights. This is a practical and valuable contribution, as it allows for the adaptation of existing models to low-resolution scenarios with minimal overhead. The method's effectiveness is demonstrated across various models and datasets, highlighting its potential for real-world applications. The use of low-resolution-specific tokens to bridge the gap between high-resolution and low-resolution feature spaces is a clever and intuitive approach. Third, the paper provides several key insights that are valuable for the research community. The finding that larger models exhibit greater robustness to resolution degradation is an important observation that can guide future model development. Similarly, the finding that the quality of the pre-training dataset is more crucial than its size is a significant insight that can inform data curation strategies. The observation that fine-tuned models are generally less robust against low-resolution images is also an important finding that warrants further investigation. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the methodology, experiments, and results. The figures and tables are helpful and easy to understand, making the paper accessible to a broad audience. The authors also provide a clear rationale for their choices and discuss the limitations of their work, which is a sign of good scientific practice.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, while the paper introduces the Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR) metric, it lacks a clear and detailed explanation of how the weights are calculated. The paper states that WAR is a weighted average of relative robustness across datasets, but the specific method for calculating these weights is not explicitly provided in the main text. This lack of clarity makes it difficult to fully understand the metric and to assess its advantages over existing metrics. While the paper does provide the formula for relative robustness and mentions that WAR is a weighted average of this, the absence of the explicit weighting formula is a significant limitation. This makes it difficult to reproduce the metric and to compare it with other robustness measures. Second, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the performance differences between LRTK0 and Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT). While the paper presents a comparison of LRTK0 and VPT in Table 4, it does not delve into the underlying reasons for the observed performance differences. The paper mentions that VPT concatenates tokens before the first block, while LRTK0 adds tokens, but it does not provide a detailed mechanistic explanation of why these differences lead to the observed performance variations. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to fully understand the strengths and weaknesses of each method and to determine the scenarios where each method is most effective. Third, the paper does not explore the potential of data augmentation techniques using low-resolution images to improve the robustness of fine-tuned models. The authors claim that fine-tuned models are less robust against low-resolution images, but they do not investigate whether this limitation can be mitigated by augmenting the training data with low-resolution images. This is a significant oversight, as data augmentation is a common and effective technique for improving model robustness. The absence of experiments with low-resolution data augmentation makes it difficult to assess the true potential of fine-tuned models in low-resolution scenarios. Fourth, the paper does not compare the proposed LRTK0 method against a baseline that involves generating low-resolution images using generative models and then fine-tuning the foundation models on these generated images. While the paper does compare against super-resolution methods, it does not explore this alternative approach, which could be a more straightforward way to address the low-resolution problem. This lack of comparison makes it difficult to assess the relative effectiveness of LRTK0 against a more intuitive approach. Fifth, the paper focuses solely on zero-shot classification performance under low-resolution conditions and does not explore the impact of low resolution on other tasks that foundation models are capable of, such as object detection, image segmentation, or image generation. This limits the scope of the study and its generalizability to other tasks. The analysis is confined to classification, and it remains unclear how the observed robustness trends would translate to tasks with different output structures and requirements. Sixth, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost and efficiency of the proposed LRTK0 method. While the authors mention that LRTK0 has a minimal parameter gain, they do not provide detailed measurements of inference time or memory usage. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method, especially in resource-constrained environments. Finally, the paper does not explore the impact of other image quality issues, such as noise, blur, or compression artifacts, on the performance of foundation models. These factors often co-occur with low resolution in real-world scenarios, and their combined effect on model performance is not addressed. This limits the practical relevance of the study, as real-world images often suffer from multiple types of degradation.

## Suggestions

Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several improvements to this paper. First, the authors should provide a clear and detailed explanation of how the weights in the Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR) metric are calculated. This should include the explicit formula for calculating the weights, as well as a rationale for why these weights are appropriate. This would make the metric more transparent and reproducible, and it would allow for a more meaningful comparison with other robustness metrics. Second, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the performance differences between LRTK0 and VPT. This should include a discussion of the underlying reasons for the observed performance variations, as well as an analysis of the architectural differences between the two methods. This would provide a more complete understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of each method and would help to determine the scenarios where each method is most effective. Third, the authors should explore the potential of data augmentation techniques using low-resolution images to improve the robustness of fine-tuned models. This should include experiments with various data augmentation strategies, such as random resizing and cropping, or the application of Gaussian blur, to determine the impact of these techniques on model robustness. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the potential of fine-tuned models in low-resolution scenarios. Fourth, the authors should compare the proposed LRTK0 method against a baseline that involves generating low-resolution images using generative models and then fine-tuning the foundation models on these generated images. This would provide a more complete picture of the landscape of methods for addressing low-resolution robustness and would help to contextualize the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Fifth, the authors should expand the scope of the study to include other tasks that foundation models are capable of, such as object detection, image segmentation, and image generation. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the robustness of foundation models under low-resolution conditions and would increase the generalizability of the findings. Sixth, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost and efficiency of the proposed LRTK0 method. This should include measurements of inference time and memory usage, as well as a comparison of the computational overhead of LRTK0 against other methods for improving robustness. This would help to determine the practical applicability of the method, especially in resource-constrained environments. Finally, the authors should explore the impact of other image quality issues, such as noise, blur, and compression artifacts, on the performance of foundation models. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the robustness of the proposed method and would help to identify its limitations. This could involve adding Gaussian noise or motion blur to the low-resolution images, or simulating compression artifacts using JPEG compression. Furthermore, the authors should explore whether LRTK0 can be combined with other techniques, such as denoising or deblurring methods, to further improve robustness under these challenging conditions.

## Questions

Based on my analysis, I have several questions that I believe would be beneficial to address. First, how does the performance of foundation models under low-resolution conditions affect their ability to generalize to real-world applications? Are there specific domains or tasks where the impact of low-resolution images is more pronounced? This question is important for understanding the practical implications of the findings and for guiding future research in this area. Second, the paper introduces the LRTK0 method to enhance the robustness of models without altering pre-trained weights. However, it does not explore the potential of other techniques, such as data augmentation or adversarial training, to improve robustness under low-resolution conditions. How do these techniques compare to LRTK0 in terms of effectiveness and efficiency? This question is important for understanding the relative strengths and weaknesses of different approaches and for identifying the most promising avenues for future research. Third, the paper focuses on the impact of low-resolution images on zero-shot classification performance. However, it does not explore the impact of other image quality issues, such as noise, blur, or compression artifacts, on the performance of foundation models. How do these factors interact with low-resolution to affect model performance? This question is important for understanding the robustness of foundation models under more realistic conditions. Fourth, the paper provides insights into the relationship between model size, pre-training dataset quality, and robustness to low-resolution images. However, it does not provide specific recommendations for designing and training foundation models that are more robust to low-resolution conditions. What are the key considerations for developing more robust foundation models? This question is important for guiding future model development and for ensuring that foundation models are more robust to real-world conditions. Finally, what are the limitations of the proposed LRTK0 method, and under what conditions might it fail to provide significant improvements in robustness? This question is important for understanding the scope of the method and for identifying potential areas for future improvement.
