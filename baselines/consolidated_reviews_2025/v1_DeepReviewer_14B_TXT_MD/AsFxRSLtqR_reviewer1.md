### Summary

This paper introduces LR0.FM, a benchmark for evaluating the zero-shot classification performance of visual-language foundation models on low-resolution images, and assesses 10 such models across 15 datasets. The authors also propose a novel metric called Weighted Aggregated Robustness to better evaluate model performance across resolutions and datasets. Finally, the authors introduce a simple strategy, LRTK0, to enhance robustness of models without compromising their pre-trained weights.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, figures and tables are helpful and easy to understand. 
- The study includes a comprehensive set of 10 foundation models and 15 datasets, providing a broad evaluation.
- The proposed LRTK0 method is simple yet effective in enhancing model robustness against low-resolution inputs without altering pre-trained weights.
- The authors provide insights into how different factors such as model architecture, pre-training dataset quality, and input resolution affect model robustness.

### Weaknesses

#### comment

 - The authors mention that fine-tuned models are less robust against LR images, but no fine-tuned models are evaluated in the benchmarking process. It would be better to include some fine-tuned models in the evaluation to verify this claim.
- The authors propose the LRTK0 method, but it is not the most straightforward method to address this issue. A simpler method would be to train a low-resolution image generator using generative models and then use these generated images to fine-tune the foundation models. This simpler method is not compared with the LRTK0 method in the paper.
- The paper lacks a clear explanation of the Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR) metric. It is not sufficient to simply state that it addresses the limitations of existing metrics; the authors must clearly explain how WAR is calculated and why it is better than existing metrics.
- The paper does not provide a clear explanation of the performance differences between LRTK0 and VPT (Visual Prompt Tuning). The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of why LRTK0 performs differently from VPT, especially considering that both methods involve training additional parameters.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more thorough evaluation of fine-tuned models. The claim that fine-tuned models are less robust to low-resolution images is a significant assertion that requires empirical validation. The authors should include a diverse set of fine-tuned models in their benchmark, using different fine-tuning strategies and datasets. This would not only verify their claim but also provide a more comprehensive understanding of how fine-tuning affects robustness to low-resolution inputs. Furthermore, the evaluation should include a comparison of fine-tuned models with and without low-resolution data augmentation to determine the impact of such techniques on robustness. This would provide a more nuanced view of the trade-offs involved in fine-tuning for low-resolution scenarios.

Regarding the LRTK0 method, while simplicity is a virtue, the authors should acknowledge and compare their approach with more straightforward methods, such as fine-tuning with low-resolution images generated by generative models. The authors should implement and evaluate such a baseline, using a state-of-the-art generative model to create low-resolution images and then fine-tune a foundation model on these images. This comparison is crucial to demonstrate the effectiveness of LRTK0 against a more intuitive approach. The evaluation should also consider the computational cost and data requirements of both methods. A detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of each approach would provide a more complete picture of the landscape of low-resolution robustness techniques. The authors should also explore different generative models and their impact on the fine-tuning process.

Finally, the paper needs to provide a more detailed explanation of the Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR) metric. The authors should clearly define the mathematical formulation of WAR, including the specific weights used and how they are derived. They should also provide a clear rationale for why these weights are appropriate and how they address the limitations of existing metrics. A comparison of WAR with other metrics, such as simple averaging, should be included, with a discussion of the advantages and disadvantages of each. Furthermore, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the performance differences between LRTK0 and VPT. This analysis should include a discussion of the architectural differences between the two methods and how these differences might affect their performance on low-resolution images. The authors should also investigate the impact of different training parameters and datasets on the performance of both methods.

### Questions

- Could the authors provide more details on how the Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR) metric is calculated and why it is better than existing metrics?
- How does the LRTK0 method compare to other methods for improving model robustness, such as data augmentation techniques?
- Could the authors elaborate on the potential real-world applications of their findings and how they might impact the deployment of foundation models in practical scenarios?
