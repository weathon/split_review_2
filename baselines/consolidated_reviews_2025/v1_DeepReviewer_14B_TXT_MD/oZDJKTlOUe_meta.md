## Summary

This paper introduces LURE, a novel post-hoc method designed to mitigate object hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The core idea behind LURE is to train a 'hallucination revisor' that can identify and correct hallucinated objects in the descriptions generated by LVLMs. The authors' approach is grounded in a statistical analysis of the key factors contributing to object hallucinations, which they identify as co-occurrence, uncertainty, and object position. They propose that these factors, when considered together, can help pinpoint instances where LVLMs are likely to generate incorrect object descriptions. To train the revisor, the authors first generate a dataset of hallucinatory descriptions by modifying accurate captions using GPT-3.5. This process involves introducing co-occurring objects, replacing uncertain or late-occurring objects with a special '[IDK]' token, and then using this modified data to fine-tune a LVLM, which acts as the revisor. The authors evaluate LURE on six open-source LVLMs, including MiniGPT-4, LLaVa, MMGPT, LLaMA-Adapter, mPLUG-Owl, and InstructBLIP, using both automated metrics like CHAIR, BLEU, and CLIP score, as well as human evaluations. Their results demonstrate that LURE significantly reduces object hallucinations compared to existing methods, outperforming the previous best approach in both automated metrics and human evaluations. The authors also provide a theoretical explanation for the effectiveness of LURE, linking it to the statistical properties of object hallucinations. They argue that by addressing the key factors of co-occurrence, uncertainty, and object position, LURE can effectively reduce the likelihood of hallucinated objects in the generated descriptions. The paper also includes ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of the three key factors and a robustness analysis to show that the method can be applied to different LVLM backbones. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and empirically validated approach to addressing a critical issue in LVLMs, with a clear methodology and strong experimental results. The authors' approach of using a post-hoc revisor is particularly appealing due to its potential applicability to a wide range of existing LVLMs without requiring retraining of the base models. The statistical analysis of the causes of hallucination is also a valuable contribution, providing a deeper understanding of the problem and guiding the development of effective mitigation strategies. The paper's findings suggest that LURE is a promising tool for improving the reliability and accuracy of LVLM-generated descriptions, which is crucial for their deployment in real-world applications.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, there are several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. One of the most significant limitations is the lack of a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with LURE. As all three reviewers who addressed this point (Reviewers 2, 3, and 4) correctly noted, the paper does not provide any information on the training time, inference time, or memory requirements for the hallucination revisor. This omission is a major drawback, as it makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method, especially in resource-constrained environments. Without this information, it is impossible to determine whether the performance gains achieved by LURE justify the additional computational overhead. This is a critical piece of information that is essential for anyone looking to implement or deploy the method in real-world scenarios. The absence of this analysis significantly limits the paper's practical value and raises questions about the scalability of the approach. Another significant weakness, highlighted by multiple reviewers (Reviewers 2, 3, and 4), is the lack of discussion and experimentation regarding the limitations of LURE. The paper does not adequately address how the method would perform on more complex or ambiguous images, or how it would handle cases where the hallucinated objects are actually present in the image but are difficult to detect. This is a crucial limitation, as real-world images often present such challenges. The paper also fails to explore the sensitivity of LURE to the quality of the initial captions. The method relies on accurate descriptions to generate the hallucinatory dataset, but it does not investigate how noisy or erroneous initial captions might affect the performance of the revisor. Furthermore, the paper does not explore the potential for LURE to introduce new errors or biases during the hallucination mitigation process. This is a critical oversight, as any post-hoc correction method has the potential to introduce new problems while trying to solve existing ones. The lack of discussion and experimentation regarding these limitations significantly undermines the robustness and reliability of the proposed method. Additionally, as noted by Reviewer 1, the paper lacks specific implementation details of the proposed method, particularly regarding the architecture and hyperparameters of the hallucination revisor. While the paper mentions that the revisor is a fine-tuned LVLM, it does not specify the type of model, the number of layers, the hidden dimensions, or the activation functions used. Similarly, the paper states that hyperparameters were selected via cross-validation but does not list the specific values used. This lack of detail makes it difficult to reproduce the results and understand the complexity of the revisor. This is a critical omission that hinders the reproducibility and transparency of the research. Furthermore, Reviewer 1 correctly pointed out that the paper lacks a specific ablation study on the '[IDK]' token. While the paper includes ablation studies on the three key factors (co-occurrence, uncertainty, and object position), it does not isolate the contribution of the '[IDK]' token itself. Without this analysis, it is difficult to understand the specific impact of this component on the overall performance of LURE. This is a significant oversight, as the '[IDK]' token is a core component of the method. Finally, while the paper provides a theoretical explanation for the effectiveness of LURE, as noted by Reviewer 1, the connection between this theoretical analysis and the practical implementation of the method is not as strong as it could be. The theoretical explanation focuses on the statistical properties of object hallucinations, but the method uses these factors as heuristics for generating training data rather than a direct application of the theoretical framework. This disconnect between theory and method weakens the overall argument and raises questions about the theoretical grounding of the approach. In summary, the paper suffers from a lack of computational cost analysis, a failure to address the limitations of the method, insufficient implementation details, a lack of ablation studies on the '[IDK]' token, and a weak connection between theory and method. These weaknesses significantly undermine the paper's overall impact and raise concerns about the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed method. My confidence in these identified weaknesses is high, as they are consistently supported by the evidence in the paper and the independent analyses of multiple reviewers.
