### Summary

This work aims at improving KGQA by proposing a retrieval-augmented reasoning method, which consists of two major components: Path-RAG, which retrieves chain of entities and relations from KGs; and DVBS, which generates multiple reasoning paths leading to final answers.

### Weaknesses

#### Some Related Works

[1] GraphPath: Guided Path Generation for Knowledge Graph Question Answering
[2] Think-on-Graph: Deep and Responsible Reasoning of Large Language Model on Knowledge Graphs
[3] Deductive Verification of Large Language Model Predictions

#### comment

1. The novelty of this work is somewhat limited. The proposed method is more like a combination of some existing methods. For example, the Path-RAG is quite similar to GraphPath [1], which also generates the path for retrieval. The main difference is that GraphPath generates the path from the answer entity to the start entity, while the proposed work generates the path from the start entity to the answer entity. However, the core idea of generating a path for retrieval is the same. For DVBS, it combines the beam search with some verification process, which is quite similar to ToG [2]. The authors claim that the main difference is the verification process, which is based on the deductive reasoning process. However, the idea of using some verification process for KGQA is not new. For example, ToG [2] also uses some verification process to check whether the selected entity is related to the question. The authors do not clearly articulate how their deductive verification process differs fundamentally from existing verification methods beyond the specific prompting strategy.
2. The authors do not provide the code for their work. So, the reproducibility is hard to check.
3. The authors do not provide some details of their work. For example, how to generate the planning steps? The description of the planning step generation is vague, lacking details on the specific prompts or algorithms used. It is unclear how the LLM is guided to produce effective planning steps, and what happens if the planning steps are incorrect or incomplete. This lack of detail makes it difficult to assess the robustness of this component.
4. The authors do not provide some analysis for their work. For example, what is the performance of vanilla DVBS (i.e., without Path-RAG)? It is crucial to understand the contribution of Path-RAG independently. Without this ablation, it's hard to determine if the retrieval mechanism is truly beneficial or if the performance gains are primarily due to other components. Furthermore, the analysis should include a comparison of the quality of paths retrieved by Path-RAG versus those generated by other methods, such as a simple beam search without the retrieval component.

### Suggestions

The authors should provide a more detailed comparison with existing methods, particularly GraphPath [1] and ToG [2]. The discussion should go beyond superficial differences in path generation direction and focus on the core algorithmic distinctions. For example, a detailed analysis of the information retrieval process, including the specific embedding models used, the retrieval metrics, and the impact of different retrieval strategies, would be beneficial. The authors should also clarify how their deductive verification process differs from existing verification methods, providing a more rigorous analysis of the verification process and its impact on the overall performance. A more detailed explanation of the prompting strategy used for deductive verification is needed, including examples of the prompts and the reasoning process.

To improve the reproducibility of the work, the authors should provide the code and the detailed implementation details of the proposed method. Specifically, the authors should provide a clear description of how the planning steps are generated, including the specific prompts used and the reasoning process behind them. The authors should also provide a detailed analysis of the impact of different planning strategies on the overall performance. Furthermore, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive ablation study, including an evaluation of the vanilla DVBS (i.e., without Path-RAG) to isolate the contribution of the Path-RAG component. This ablation should also include a comparison of the quality of paths retrieved by Path-RAG versus those generated by other methods, such as a simple beam search without the retrieval component. This analysis should include metrics such as path coverage, path accuracy, and the impact of path quality on the final answer accuracy. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of the failure cases, including examples of where the method fails and the reasons for these failures. This analysis should help to identify the limitations of the method and suggest directions for future research.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method, including the time and memory requirements for each component. This analysis should include a comparison of the computational cost of the proposed method with existing methods. The authors should also discuss the scalability of the proposed method to larger knowledge graphs and more complex questions. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to different hyperparameters, including the beam size, the number of reasoning steps, and the retrieval threshold. This analysis should help to identify the optimal hyperparameter settings for different datasets and tasks.
