## Summary

This paper introduces Prediction of Functionals from Masked Latents (PFML), a novel self-supervised learning (SSL) algorithm tailored for time-series data. The core idea behind PFML is to predict statistical functionals of masked input signals based on unmasked embeddings, aiming to circumvent the issue of representation collapse commonly encountered in SSL methods. The authors argue that by predicting these functionals, they reduce the complexity of the pre-training task compared to methods that attempt to reconstruct the raw input signal. The method involves masking embeddings derived from the input time series, and then using a transformer network to predict statistical functionals of the masked portions based on the unmasked embeddings. These functionals include mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, minimum and maximum values, zero-crossing rate, and the mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis of the autocorrelation function. The authors validate PFML across three diverse real-life classification tasks: infant posture and movement classification using multi-sensor inertial measurement unit (IMU) data, emotion recognition from speech data, and sleep stage classification from electroencephalogram (EEG) data. The experimental results demonstrate that PFML achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art SSL methods, such as data2vec, and outperforms a conceptually similar method, MAE, without suffering from representation collapse. The authors claim that PFML's approach of predicting statistical functionals simplifies the pre-training task and makes it more robust to the dimensional collapse issue. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the proposed method and its advantages, making it accessible to researchers in the field of time-series analysis and self-supervised learning. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the lack of theoretical analysis and the limited scope of the experimental evaluation, which I will discuss in detail in the following sections. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to self-supervised learning for time-series data, but further work is needed to fully understand its capabilities and limitations.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, a significant limitation is the lack of a rigorous theoretical analysis explaining why PFML avoids representation collapse. While the authors provide an intuitive explanation based on the inherent variance of the target functionals, this explanation is not supported by a formal mathematical proof or a deeper theoretical framework. The paper states that "good model predictions that lead to low prediction loss values also inherently contain variance," but this is an assumption, not a proven fact. The absence of a theoretical analysis weakens the paper's claims and leaves open the question of whether PFML's approach is truly robust to representation collapse in all scenarios.
