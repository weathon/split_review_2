## Summary

This paper introduces S-ViLM, a novel video-language pre-training framework designed to learn fine-grained structures within video and language data. The core contribution of S-ViLM lies in its ability to simultaneously capture region-object correspondences and temporal-aware features through two innovative mechanisms: inter-clip spatial grounding and intra-clip temporal grouping. The inter-clip spatial grounding component aims to align visual regions within a video with corresponding object entities mentioned in the associated text, while the intra-clip temporal grouping mechanism seeks to cluster video frames based on their temporal relationships, enabling the model to better understand the temporal dynamics within the video. The authors pre-train their model on a large-scale video-text dataset and then evaluate its performance on four downstream tasks: text-video retrieval, video question answering, video action recognition, and temporal action localization. The empirical results presented in the paper demonstrate that S-ViLM outperforms existing approaches on these tasks, suggesting the effectiveness of the proposed framework in learning more expressive video-language representations. Specifically, the model shows improvements in retrieval accuracy, question answering accuracy, action recognition accuracy, and temporal localization precision. The authors argue that their approach provides a more comprehensive understanding of video content by integrating both spatial and temporal information, which is crucial for various video-language understanding tasks. The paper is well-written and provides a clear explanation of the proposed framework and its components, making it accessible to a wide audience. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to further strengthen the paper's contributions. These limitations primarily concern the scope of the experimental evaluation, the lack of detailed computational analysis, and the need for more thorough ablation studies. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of video-language pre-training by introducing a novel framework that effectively integrates spatial and temporal information.

This limitation is significant because the field of action recognition is rapidly evolving, and a more thorough comparison with the latest methods is necessary to establish the true contribution of S-ViLM. My confidence in this assessment is high, as the absence of 2023 baselines is clearly evident in Table 3. While the authors mention that pre-training takes about one day, they do not provide a breakdown of the computational requirements for each component of the model, such as the video encoder, text encoder, and the grouping blocks. Furthermore, there is no discussion of the number of parameters, training time per epoch, or inference time for a single video or text input. This lack of computational analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical feasibility of the method, especially when considering large-scale datasets and complex models. Without this information, it is hard to determine the computational overhead introduced by the novel intra-clip temporal grouping and inter-clip spatial grounding mechanisms. This is a significant limitation because the computational cost is a crucial factor in the real-world applicability of any machine learning model. Finally, the paper does not provide a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed framework. ## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive evaluation of S-ViLM, particularly in the context of action recognition. This should include comparisons with state-of-the-art methods from 2023, such as those published in recent conferences. Specifically, the authors should consider evaluating their model on more challenging benchmarks like Kinetics-400 or Kinetics-700, which are widely used in the action recognition community. This would provide a more accurate assessment of the model's performance and allow for a more meaningful comparison with other approaches. Second, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with the S-ViLM framework. This analysis should include a breakdown of the number of parameters for each component of the model, the training time per epoch, and the inference time for a single video or text input. Furthermore, the authors should compare the computational cost of S-ViLM with other similar video-language pre-training models. This analysis should be presented in a table format, making it easy to compare the computational overhead of different models. This would provide a better understanding of the practical feasibility of the method and allow for a more informed comparison with other approaches. Third, the authors should conduct more thorough ablation studies to investigate the impact of various design choices. This should include exploring the sensitivity of the model to the number of groups in the intra-clip temporal grouping and the choice of learnable group tokens. The authors should also explore the impact of different video and text encoders on the performance of the model. For example, they could experiment with different pre-trained models for feature extraction and analyze their impact on the downstream tasks. This would provide a better understanding of the robustness of the proposed method and allow for a more informed selection of hyperparameters. Furthermore, the authors should provide a justification for the chosen values of the hyperparameters and explain how they impact the performance of the model. Fourth, the authors should include a dedicated "Limitations" section in the paper. This section should discuss the potential challenges and limitations of S-ViLM, such as its sensitivity to hyperparameter settings, its performance on specific types of videos or languages, and its potential failure cases. This discussion should be based on the results of the ablation studies and the overall experimental evaluation. By acknowledging the limitations of the framework, the authors can provide a more balanced and realistic assessment of their work. Finally, the authors should consider evaluating the performance of S-ViLM on a wider range of datasets, especially those that are more challenging or that have different characteristics. This would help to assess the generalizability and robustness of the framework. For example, evaluating on datasets with longer videos or videos with more complex temporal dynamics would provide a more thorough assessment of the framework's capabilities. Furthermore, comparing the performance of S-ViLM with other state-of-the-art methods on these additional datasets would help to establish its generalizability and robustness. These suggestions are aimed at addressing the identified weaknesses and further strengthening the contributions of the paper.

## Questions

Based on my analysis, I have several questions that I believe are crucial for a deeper understanding of the S-ViLM framework. First, I am curious about the specific implementation details of the intra-clip temporal grouping mechanism. While the paper describes the loss function, it does not provide a clear explanation of how the features are actually grouped. Specifically, how are the temporal boundaries between different groups determined? What is the mathematical formulation of the grouping process? Understanding this process in more detail would help to clarify the technical contributions of the paper. Second, I would like to know more about the role of the text modality during the temporal grouping process. The paper focuses on the video loss function for intra-clip temporal grouping, but it is not clear how the text modality influences this process. Does the text provide any guidance for the temporal grouping? If so, how is this guidance incorporated into the model? Understanding the interplay between the text and video modalities during temporal grouping is crucial for a comprehensive understanding of the framework. Third, I am interested in the impact of different pre-training datasets on the performance of S-ViLM. While the paper mentions pre-training on a large-scale video-text dataset, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how the choice of dataset affects the downstream task performance. How does the model perform when trained on different datasets? Are there any specific characteristics of a dataset that are particularly important for the success of S-ViLM? Exploring the impact of different pre-training datasets would provide valuable insights into the robustness and generalizability of the framework. Fourth, I would like to understand the sensitivity of S-ViLM to different hyperparameter settings. How does the performance vary with changes in the number of grouping blocks, the dimensionality of the group tokens, or the learning rate? A sensitivity analysis would provide valuable insights into the robustness of the framework and help to justify the chosen values for these hyperparameters. Finally, I am curious about the potential limitations of the inter-clip spatial grounding mechanism. How does the model perform when the visual regions in the video do not perfectly align with the object entities in the text? Are there any specific scenarios where this mechanism is likely to fail? Understanding the limitations of this mechanism is crucial for identifying areas for future improvement. These questions are aimed at clarifying key uncertainties and methodological choices in the paper, and I believe that addressing them would further strengthen the contributions of this work.
