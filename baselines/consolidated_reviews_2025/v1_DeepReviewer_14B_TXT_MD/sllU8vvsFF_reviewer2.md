### Summary

The paper proposes a large reconstruction model (LRM) for single image to 3D. The model uses a transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture to learn 3D representations of objects from a single image in a data-driven manner. The LRM takes an image as input and regresses a NeRF in the form of a triplane representation. The method adopts a large transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture for learning 3D representations of objects from a single image in a data-driven manner. The method takes an image as input and regresses a NeRF in the form of a triplane representation. The LRM utilizes the pre-trained visual transformer DINO as the image encoder to generate the image features, and learns an image-to-triplane transformer decoder to project the 2D image features onto the 3D triplane via cross-attention and model the relations among the spatially-structured triplane tokens via self-attention. The output tokens from the decoder are reshaped and upsampled to the final triplane feature maps. Afterwards, we can render the images at an arbitrary view by decoding the triplane feature of each point with an additional shared multi-layer perception (MLP) to get its color and density and performing volume rendering. The overall design of LRM maintains high scalability and efficiency. In addition to the use of a fully transformer-based pipeline, a triplane NeRF is a concise and scalable 3D representation since it is computationally friendly compared to other representations such as volumes and point clouds. It also has a better locality with respect to the image input compared to tokenizing the NeRF’s model weights as in Shap-E. Moreover, the LRM is trained by simply minimizing the difference between the rendered images and ground truth images at novel views, without excessive 3D-aware regularization or delicate hyper-parameter tuning, allowing the model to be very efficient in training and adaptable to a wide range of multi-view image datasets. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the LRM is the first large-scale 3D reconstruction model; it contains more than 500 million learnable parameters, and it is trained on approximately one million 3D shapes and video data across diverse categories; this is substantially larger than recent methods that apply relatively shallower networks and smaller datasets. Through experiments, the authors show that the LRM can reconstruct high-fidelity 3D shapes from a wide range of images captured in the real world, as well as images created by generative models. The LRM is also a highly practical solution for downstream applications since it can produce a 3D shape in just five seconds without post-optimization.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed Large Reconstruction Model (LRM). While the authors claim the model is robust to noisy camera poses, a more rigorous evaluation is needed. Specifically, the paper should include a quantitative analysis of the model's performance under varying degrees of camera pose noise, including both translation and rotation errors. This analysis should not only focus on the final reconstruction quality but also on the impact on the predicted camera parameters themselves. For instance, the authors could measure the error in the predicted camera parameters and correlate that with the degradation in reconstruction quality. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to explore the model's behavior when presented with out-of-distribution camera poses, such as those with significantly larger baselines or viewing angles than seen during training. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the model's robustness and generalization capabilities. The authors should also consider comparing the performance of LRM with other state-of-the-art methods under these challenging conditions to better contextualize its strengths and weaknesses.

Additionally, the paper should delve deeper into the computational cost and memory requirements of the LRM. While the authors mention that the model has over 500 million parameters, a more detailed breakdown of the computational resources required for training and inference would be valuable. This should include the number of GPUs used, the training time, and the memory footprint of the model. Furthermore, the authors should discuss the scalability of the model with respect to input image resolution and the size of the triplane representation. It would also be beneficial to analyze the trade-offs between model size, computational cost, and reconstruction quality. For example, the authors could explore the impact of reducing the number of parameters in the transformer networks or using a lower resolution triplane representation. This analysis would provide valuable insights into the practical applicability of the model and guide future research in this area. The authors should also consider the potential for model compression or quantization techniques to reduce the computational footprint of the LRM.

Finally, the paper should provide a more thorough discussion of the limitations of the triplane NeRF representation. While the authors mention that it is computationally friendly and has better locality compared to other representations, they should also acknowledge its potential drawbacks. For example, the triplane representation may struggle to capture fine details or complex geometries, especially for objects with significant self-occlusion or intricate surface structures. The authors should explore alternative 3D representations that could potentially address these limitations, such as hybrid representations that combine the strengths of different approaches. Furthermore, the paper should discuss the potential for artifacts or inconsistencies in the rendered images due to the discretization of the triplane representation. The authors could also consider techniques for improving the quality of the rendered images, such as using a higher resolution triplane representation or employing more advanced rendering techniques. This discussion would provide a more balanced view of the proposed approach and guide future research in this area.
