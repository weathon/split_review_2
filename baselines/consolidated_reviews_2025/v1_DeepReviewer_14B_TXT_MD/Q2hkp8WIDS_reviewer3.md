### Summary

This paper proposes an object-centric model-based reinforcement learning pipeline, OC-STORM, which integrates recent advances in computer vision to allow agents to focus on key decision-related elements. Specifically, the method first utilizes the foundation model Cutie to extract object features, and then incorporates these object features with the raw observations to predict environmental dynamics. The policy is trained with imagined trajectories generated by the world model. Experiments on Atari 100k and Hollow Knight show the effectiveness of the proposed method.

#### comment

1. The novelty of the proposed method is limited. The authors directly use the pretrained foundation model Cutie to extract object features, and the other modules are also from previous works. The integration of a foundation model with existing MBRL frameworks, while practically relevant, does not represent a significant theoretical advancement. The core idea of using object-centric representations is not new, and the method essentially combines existing components without introducing novel architectural or algorithmic contributions.

2. The authors should compare more MBRL baselines, such as TWM [1], STORM [2], and DreamerV3. The current evaluation lacks a comprehensive comparison against state-of-the-art model-based RL algorithms. The absence of these comparisons makes it difficult to assess the true performance gains of the proposed method relative to established benchmarks. Specifically, the method should be evaluated against both vanilla and more recent MBRL algorithms to understand its relative strengths and weaknesses.

3. The authors should discuss the limitations of the proposed method in detail. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of the potential failure modes and limitations of the approach. For example, the reliance on a pre-trained object segmentation model might introduce biases or limitations in scenarios where the segmented objects are not aligned with the task's reward structure. Furthermore, the computational cost of extracting object features using Cutie should be discussed.

4. Minor: The authors should provide the results of other baselines on Hollow Knight in Table 3. The absence of comparative results on Hollow Knight makes it difficult to assess the method's performance in this more complex environment. Including results from other baselines would provide a more complete picture of the method's capabilities.

### Suggestions

The paper would benefit from a more detailed analysis of the specific contributions of the object-centric representation. While the use of Cutie for object feature extraction is a practical choice, the paper should delve deeper into how these features are leveraged within the MBRL framework. For instance, it would be valuable to analyze the information content of the extracted features and how they compare to raw pixel inputs or other feature representations. A visualization of the learned object features and their impact on the predicted dynamics could also provide valuable insights. Furthermore, the paper should explore the sensitivity of the method to the choice of the object segmentation model. It would be beneficial to investigate how the performance changes when using different segmentation models or when the segmentation quality is varied. This would help to understand the robustness of the approach and its dependence on the specific object representation.

To strengthen the experimental evaluation, the authors should include a more comprehensive set of baselines, including both model-free and model-based methods. Specifically, the comparison should include state-of-the-art MBRL algorithms such as DreamerV3, TWM, and STORM, as well as model-free methods like PPO or DQN. This would provide a more complete picture of the method's performance relative to existing approaches. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method. This should include a breakdown of the time spent on object feature extraction, world model training, and policy optimization. A comparison of the computational cost with other MBRL methods would also be valuable. The authors should also discuss the potential limitations of the method in more detail, including the reliance on a pre-trained object segmentation model and the potential for biases or limitations in scenarios where the segmented objects are not aligned with the task's reward structure.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the results on Hollow Knight. This should include a comparison of the method's performance with other baselines, as well as an analysis of the method's strengths and weaknesses in this more complex environment. The authors should also discuss the potential limitations of the method in this environment, such as the difficulty of segmenting objects in complex scenes or the potential for the method to be sensitive to changes in the environment. Furthermore, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the failure cases of the proposed method. This should include a discussion of the types of scenarios where the method fails and the reasons for these failures. This would help to identify the limitations of the method and suggest potential directions for future research.
