## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to weight-only quantization for Large Language Models (LLMs), focusing on mitigating the impact of activation outliers. The authors begin by observing a correlation between activation outliers and sensitive weights, where large activations can amplify rounding errors during quantization. This observation motivates the introduction of per-input-channel (per-IC) quantization, which aims to isolate these outlier effects by selecting quantization ranges within each input channel. The core idea is that by grouping weights along the input channel dimension, the impact of outliers is contained within smaller groups, reducing their influence on the overall quantization process. Building upon this, the authors propose an adaptive per-channel quantization scheme, AdaDim, which dynamically selects between per-IC and per-output-channel (per-OC) quantization based on a reconstruction error objective. This adaptive approach allows the quantization process to tailor its strategy to the specific characteristics of each layer, potentially leading to better performance than a fixed quantization scheme. The experimental results demonstrate that AdaDim improves the performance of both round-to-nearest and GPTQ quantization methods across various LLMs and tasks. The authors also provide a hardware-efficient implementation of per-IC quantization using LUT-GEMM, showing latency speedups compared to cuBLAS. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of LLM quantization by highlighting the connection between activation outliers and weight sensitivity and proposing a practical method to address this issue. However, as I will discuss, the paper has some limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize its potential.

This observation is crucial for understanding the challenges of quantizing LLMs and provides a solid foundation for the proposed method. The authors' analysis of weight sensitivity patterns, as visualized in Figure 2, effectively illustrates this correlation and provides a compelling motivation for their approach. Second, the introduction of per-IC quantization is a novel contribution that addresses the outlier problem in a unique way. By grouping weights along the input channel dimension, the method effectively isolates the impact of outliers, which is a significant step forward in weight-only quantization. The authors' explanation of how per-IC quantization achieves this isolation, as described in Section 3.2, is clear and intuitive. Furthermore, the experimental results are thorough and well-presented, offering a clear understanding of the method's effectiveness. The authors demonstrate that AdaDim improves the performance of both round-to-nearest and GPTQ quantization methods across various LLMs and tasks, which is a strong indication of the method's robustness. The inclusion of a hardware-efficient implementation of per-IC quantization using LUT-GEMM, as described in Section 4.5, is also a significant strength. The latency speedups demonstrated in Figure 7 highlight the practical relevance of the proposed method and its potential for real-world applications. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly articulate their ideas and provide sufficient detail to understand the proposed method and its experimental evaluation.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper's motivation could be strengthened by providing more rigorous evidence that activation outliers are a primary cause of weight sensitivity. While the paper demonstrates a correlation between activation outliers and sensitive weights, it does not fully establish a causal relationship. As I observed, the paper lacks controlled experiments where outlier effects are selectively removed or amplified to isolate their impact on weight sensitivity. For instance, the authors could have applied techniques such as clipping or smoothing to the activations and then measured the resulting changes in weight sensitivity. This would help to isolate the specific contribution of outliers and provide a more robust justification for the proposed method. Furthermore, the paper does not explore other potential causes of weight sensitivity, such as the distribution of weights themselves or the specific operations performed in the network. This lack of exploration weakens the claim that outliers are the primary cause of sensitivity. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's motivation relies on the assumption that outliers are a primary cause, which is supported by correlation but lacks more rigorous experimental validation. Second, the novelty of the per-IC quantization approach is somewhat limited, as it bears a resemblance to the per-data-sample quantization method described in the GPTQ paper. While the authors introduce per-IC quantization as a novel approach, they do not adequately address the similarities with existing methods. A more detailed comparison with per-data-sample quantization is needed to clarify the specific differences and advantages of the proposed approach. Additionally, the paper lacks a clear explanation of why per-IC quantization is more hardware-efficient than per-OC. While the paper claims that per-IC quantization is more hardware-efficient, it does not provide a detailed theoretical justification for this claim. The potential for increased memory access overhead due to the finer granularity of quantization ranges is not adequately addressed. The paper only provides experimental results with a specific LUT-GEMM implementation, but a more general analysis of the hardware implications is needed. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper does not explicitly differentiate per-IC from per-data-sample quantization and lacks a detailed theoretical justification for general hardware efficiency claims. Third, the paper's main contribution, AdaDim, is presented as a method that dynamically selects the optimal quantization dimension. However, this selection process introduces additional computational overhead, which the paper does not adequately address. The paper describes the selection process based on minimizing reconstruction error, but it lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost of this search. The paper claims that AdaDim requires a "very small number of forward passes" to determine the optimal dimension, but it does not quantify this overhead or discuss its potential as a bottleneck, especially for very large models. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical viability of AdaDim. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper describes the selection process but lacks quantitative analysis of its computational cost and potential as a bottleneck. Finally, the paper lacks a theoretical analysis of the proposed method. While the empirical results are promising, the absence of a theoretical framework makes it difficult to fully understand the mechanisms behind per-IC quantization and its adaptive selection between per-IC and per-OC. A theoretical analysis should explore why quantizing along the input channel dimension effectively isolates outliers, perhaps by examining the statistical properties of activations and their impact on weight sensitivity. Furthermore, the adaptive selection process based on reconstruction error needs a more rigorous explanation. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper provides intuitive explanations and empirical validation but lacks mathematical proofs or formal justifications for the core mechanisms of per-IC quantization and AdaDim. While the paper does compare against AWQ, it does not compare against SmoothQuant, which is a significant omission given that SmoothQuant is a state-of-the-art quantization method. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper omits SmoothQuant from its comparisons.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should conduct more rigorous experiments to establish a causal link between activation outliers and weight sensitivity. This could involve selectively removing or amplifying outlier effects using techniques like clipping or smoothing and then observing the impact on weight sensitivity. This would help to isolate the specific contribution of outliers and provide a more robust justification for the proposed method. Furthermore, the authors should explore other potential causes of weight sensitivity, such as the distribution of weights themselves or the specific operations performed in the network. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the phenomenon and strengthen the paper's claims. Second, the authors should provide a more detailed comparison of per-IC quantization with per-data-sample quantization from the GPTQ paper. This comparison should highlight the specific differences and advantages of the proposed approach. Additionally, the authors should provide a more thorough discussion of the hardware efficiency claims. This should include a quantitative analysis of the memory access patterns and computational costs associated with both per-IC and per-OC quantization, as well as a comparison with per-data-sample quantization. This analysis should include a discussion of the potential for parallelization and the impact of different group sizes on performance. Furthermore, the authors should clarify the specific hardware platforms for which per-IC quantization is most suitable and discuss the potential limitations of the approach on different architectures. Third, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the AdaDim search process. This should include a breakdown of the time required for each step of the search, as well as a discussion of the potential for this search to become a bottleneck in the quantization process. The authors should also explore alternative search strategies that could reduce the computational overhead of AdaDim. For example, they could consider using a heuristic approach to guide the search or using a simplified model to estimate the reconstruction error. A more thorough analysis of the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost would be beneficial in assessing the practical viability of AdaDim. Fourth, the authors should provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed method. This analysis should explore why quantizing along the input channel dimension effectively isolates outliers, perhaps by examining the statistical properties of activations and their impact on weight sensitivity. Furthermore, the adaptive selection process based on reconstruction error needs a more rigorous explanation. For instance, under what conditions does per-IC quantization outperform per-OC, and can these conditions be formalized? A theoretical treatment could involve analyzing the Hessian of the loss function with respect to the weights, and how this relates to the chosen quantization axis. Finally, the authors should include a comparison with SmoothQuant, a state-of-the-art quantization method. This comparison should include not only accuracy but also computational efficiency and memory footprint. This would help to position the proposed method within the broader landscape of quantization methods for LLMs and provide a more comprehensive evaluation of its practical utility.
