### Summary

This paper introduces a novel dataset distillation method that leverages the conceptual understanding of large language models (LLMs) to enhance dataset quality. Traditional dataset distillation methods focus on matching the overall distribution of a dataset but often miss specific details. To address this, the authors propose using LLMs to retrieve relevant concepts for each category, which are then used to guide a diffusion model in refining the dataset. This approach improves the representational quality of images by ensuring that key attributes of each class are accurately depicted. The method demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on the ImageNet-1K benchmark, outperforming existing distillation techniques.

### Strengths

1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

2. The authors provide extensive experiments and ablation studies to support the effectiveness of the proposed method, including an evaluation of different prompts.

3. The approach of using LLMs to retrieve relevant concepts for each category is interesting. The authors also take into consideration concepts that may not be well-represented in the target dataset.

### Weaknesses

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

1. The novelty of this paper is limited to using LLMs to retrieve relevant concepts for each category. Apart from this, the paper is more or less the same as the Minimax paper (Gu et al., 2024). Therefore, the authors should move the comparison to the main paper and provide a more detailed analysis of the differences and innovations compared to the Minimax paper.

2. The proposed method is not efficient. It takes about 4 days to distill the ImageNet-1K dataset, which is about 10 times longer than the Minimax method. The authors should compare the efficiency with more methods, e.g., the DREAM method.

3. The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the cases where the proposed method performs worse than the Minimax method in the 1 IPC case in Tables 1 and 2.

4. The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the results in Table 3, particularly regarding the performance of the unCLIP baseline.

### Suggestions

The paper's core contribution, the use of LLMs for concept retrieval, needs to be more thoroughly contextualized within the existing literature on dataset distillation. While the authors highlight the limitations of traditional methods in capturing specific details, the technical novelty beyond concept retrieval is not sufficiently established. A more rigorous comparison with the Minimax method is needed, detailing the specific modifications and their individual impact on performance. The authors should provide a breakdown of the computational cost associated with each step of their pipeline, including concept retrieval, diffusion processing, and any other overhead. This would help to pinpoint the bottlenecks and allow for a more informed comparison with other methods like DREAM, which is known for its efficiency. Furthermore, the authors should explore potential optimizations to reduce the distillation time, such as using more efficient diffusion models or parallelizing the concept retrieval process.

To address the performance discrepancies observed in the 1 IPC case, the authors should conduct a more in-depth analysis of the generated images. This analysis should go beyond simple accuracy metrics and explore the quality and diversity of the generated samples. For example, are the generated images more prone to mode collapse or artifacts compared to Minimax? Are there specific categories where the proposed method struggles more than others? A detailed investigation into these aspects would provide a more nuanced understanding of the method's limitations. Additionally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the unCLIP baseline's performance in Table 3. The fact that it performs worse than random selection at 50 IPC is concerning and requires further investigation. The authors should analyze the quality of the images generated by unCLIP at different IPC values and provide insights into why its performance degrades so significantly. This analysis should include a discussion of the limitations of the unCLIP model and how the proposed method overcomes these limitations.

Finally, the authors should consider including additional experiments to further validate the effectiveness of their approach. For example, they could explore the impact of different LLMs on the quality of the retrieved concepts and the resulting distilled dataset. They could also investigate the sensitivity of their method to the number of concepts retrieved per category. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to evaluate the method on a wider range of datasets, including those with different characteristics and complexities. This would help to establish the generalizability of the proposed method and its applicability to different domains. The authors should also consider providing a more detailed analysis of the computational resources required for their method, including the memory footprint and the number of GPUs needed. This would help to assess the practicality of their approach for different research and development environments.
