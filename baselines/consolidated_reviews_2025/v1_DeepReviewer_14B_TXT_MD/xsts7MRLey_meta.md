## Summary

This paper introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) techniques in the context of time series classification. The authors address a critical gap in the literature by providing a standardized framework for assessing the performance of deep learning-based UDA methods on time series data, a domain where such evaluations have been relatively underexplored. The core contribution of this work lies in the creation of seven new benchmark datasets, which encompass diverse domain shifts and temporal dynamics, thereby offering a more robust and realistic evaluation environment compared to existing benchmarks. These datasets, along with nine state-of-the-art UDA algorithms, are rigorously evaluated using a consistent methodology, including three different hyperparameter tuning methods: Source Risk, Target Risk, and Importance Weighted Cross-Validation (IWCV). The paper's methodological approach involves adapting existing UDA techniques, such as Domain Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN) and Conditional DANN (CDAN), by incorporating the InceptionTime neural network backbone, which is a state-of-the-art architecture for time series classification. The empirical findings reveal that the InceptionRain model, which combines the Inception backbone with a frequency-based adaptation technique, generally achieves the highest average rank across the benchmark datasets. This suggests that frequency-based adaptation is a promising direction for UDA in time series. The paper also explores the impact of different hyperparameter tuning methods, finding that the Source Risk method often performs well, while the Target Risk method, which leverages labels from the target domain, is not always the most effective. The overall significance of this work is in providing a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners working on domain adaptation for time series data. The benchmark datasets and the systematic evaluation of UDA algorithms offer a solid foundation for future research and development in this field. By highlighting the strengths and limitations of various UDA approaches, this paper contributes to a better understanding of the challenges and opportunities in adapting time series models to new, unlabeled domains. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the results, including statistical comparisons and pairwise accuracy plots, which further enhances the paper's value as a reference for the community. The paper's focus on deep learning methods, while acknowledging the existence of other approaches, provides a targeted and in-depth analysis of a critical area in time series UDA.

## Strengths

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall value as a benchmark for unsupervised domain adaptation in time series classification. First and foremost, the introduction of seven new benchmark datasets is a significant contribution. These datasets, which cover a range of domain shifts and temporal dynamics, address a critical need for more diverse and realistic evaluation environments in the field. The authors have clearly put effort into curating these datasets, and their descriptions, while perhaps not exhaustive, provide sufficient detail to understand the nature of the domain shifts involved. This is a clear improvement over existing benchmarks, which often lack the diversity necessary to thoroughly assess the robustness of UDA methods. Secondly, the paper's systematic evaluation of nine UDA algorithms is commendable. By adapting existing methods with the InceptionTime backbone, the authors have created a consistent framework for comparing the performance of different approaches. This is crucial for ensuring a fair and objective evaluation. The inclusion of three different hyperparameter tuning methods further enhances the rigor of the evaluation, providing insights into the sensitivity of UDA methods to different tuning strategies. The paper's focus on deep learning methods is also a strength. While acknowledging the existence of other approaches, the authors have chosen to focus on deep learning, which is a dominant paradigm in time series analysis. This focus allows for a more in-depth analysis of the challenges and opportunities in adapting deep learning models to new, unlabeled domains. The empirical results, particularly the consistent top performance of the InceptionRain model, provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of frequency-based adaptation techniques. The detailed analysis of the results, including statistical comparisons and pairwise accuracy plots, further enhances the paper's value. The authors have clearly put effort into presenting the results in a clear and accessible manner, making it easier for readers to understand the strengths and limitations of different UDA approaches. Finally, the paper's overall contribution to the field of domain adaptation for time series data is significant. By providing a comprehensive benchmark and a detailed analysis of various UDA methods, this work serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners. It not only advances the state-of-the-art in time series UDA but also fosters innovation in this critical field. The paper's clear articulation of its goals, its rigorous methodology, and its insightful analysis make it a valuable contribution to the literature.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. A primary concern is the paper's exclusive focus on deep learning-based UDA methods. While the authors acknowledge the existence of other approaches, the absence of any discussion or evaluation of traditional machine learning methods for time series domain adaptation is a significant limitation. This narrow focus restricts the scope of the benchmark and fails to provide a comprehensive view of the field. For instance, methods like Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) or Correlation Alignment (CORAL), which are often used in traditional machine learning settings, are not considered. This omission limits the paper's ability to offer a complete picture of the landscape of time series domain adaptation and leaves a gap in understanding how deep learning methods compare to more established techniques. My analysis confirms this limitation, as the paper explicitly states its focus on deep learning approaches in the introduction and main idea sections. The lack of a detailed analysis of the computational cost and efficiency of the evaluated UDA algorithms is another significant weakness. While the paper mentions a fixed hyperparameter tuning budget and a maximum training budget, it does not provide a breakdown of the computational cost for each individual algorithm. This includes details such as training time per epoch, inference time, and memory usage beyond the GPU memory. This lack of information is problematic for practitioners who need to consider the trade-off between performance and computational resources when selecting a UDA method. For example, some deep learning models may achieve high accuracy but require significant computational resources, making them impractical for real-time applications or deployment on resource-constrained devices. My verification confirms this weakness, as the paper lacks a detailed computational cost analysis for individual algorithms beyond the overall budget. Furthermore, the paper lacks a systematic sensitivity analysis of the impact of different hyperparameter settings on the performance of UDA algorithms. While the paper evaluates different hyperparameter tuning methods, it does not explore how the performance of each algorithm varies as specific hyperparameters are changed. This is a critical omission, as UDA algorithms are often sensitive to hyperparameter settings, and a lack of sensitivity analysis limits the robustness and practical applicability of the findings. For instance, the paper does not explore how the performance of a specific algorithm changes as the learning rate is varied or how the choice of batch size affects the convergence of the model. My analysis confirms this weakness, as the paper does not include a systematic sensitivity analysis for individual hyperparameters across algorithms and datasets. Finally, while the paper introduces new datasets, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the specific challenges these datasets pose for UDA. For example, a detailed analysis of the feature distributions across different domains within each dataset could highlight the specific difficulties in achieving effective domain adaptation. This lack of in-depth analysis limits the paper's ability to provide insights into the underlying reasons for the observed results. My analysis confirms this weakness, as the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the feature distributions across different domains within each dataset. These weaknesses, taken together, limit the paper's ability to provide a comprehensive and practical guide to UDA for time series data. The lack of a broader scope, detailed computational analysis, hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, and in-depth dataset analysis restricts the paper's overall impact and usefulness for the wider research community.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the paper should broaden its scope to include a discussion and evaluation of traditional machine learning methods for time series domain adaptation. This would provide a more comprehensive view of the field and allow for a more thorough comparison of deep learning methods with established techniques. Specifically, the authors could include methods like MMD, CORAL, or kernel-based methods, and discuss how they can be applied to time series data. This would not only broaden the scope of the paper but also provide valuable insights for practitioners who may be working with limited computational resources or who prefer simpler, more interpretable models. Second, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with each UDA algorithm. This should go beyond simply reporting the training time and should include an analysis of the memory requirements, the number of parameters, and the inference time. This information is critical for practitioners who need to choose an algorithm that is not only accurate but also computationally feasible for their specific application. The authors could also discuss the scalability of each algorithm with respect to the size of the dataset and the length of the time series. This would allow practitioners to make informed decisions about which algorithm is most suitable for their specific needs. Third, the paper should conduct a more thorough investigation into the impact of hyperparameter settings on the performance of UDA algorithms. This should include a sensitivity analysis of the key hyperparameters for each UDA algorithm, such as learning rate, batch size, and regularization parameters. This analysis should explore how the performance of each algorithm varies as these hyperparameters are changed. This would provide valuable insights into the robustness of each method and help practitioners to tune their models effectively. For example, the authors could show how the performance of a specific algorithm changes as the learning rate is varied or how the choice of batch size affects the convergence of the model. This would not only improve the reproducibility of the results but also provide practical guidance for practitioners who are looking to apply these methods to their own data. Fourth, the paper should provide a more in-depth analysis of the specific challenges posed by the new datasets. This could include a detailed analysis of the feature distributions across different domains within each dataset, highlighting the specific difficulties in achieving effective domain adaptation. This would provide a better understanding of why certain methods perform well on some datasets but not on others. Finally, the paper should provide more concrete examples of how practitioners can use the benchmark to evaluate new UDA methods. This should include a discussion of the evaluation metrics, the hyperparameter tuning process, and the computational resources required. The paper should also address the limitations of the proposed benchmark, such as the number of datasets included and the types of domain shifts considered. A discussion of the potential biases in the datasets and the limitations of the evaluation metrics would also be beneficial. These improvements would significantly enhance the paper's value as a practical and comprehensive resource for researchers and practitioners working on domain adaptation for time series data.

## Questions

Several questions arise from my analysis of this paper, focusing on key methodological choices and uncertainties. First, given the paper's focus on deep learning methods, I am curious about the rationale behind not including any traditional machine learning approaches for time series domain adaptation. What specific challenges or limitations of traditional methods led to this decision, and how might the inclusion of such methods have altered the findings? Second, regarding the computational cost analysis, I would like to understand why the paper did not provide a more detailed breakdown of the computational resources required for each individual UDA algorithm. What were the practical constraints that prevented a more thorough analysis of training time per epoch, inference time, and memory usage? Third, concerning the hyperparameter tuning process, I am interested in a more detailed explanation of why the Source Risk method often performs as well or better than the Target Risk method, despite the latter leveraging labels from the target domain. What are the underlying mechanisms that explain this counterintuitive result, and how might this inform the selection of hyperparameter tuning methods in practice? Fourth, regarding the new datasets, I would like to understand more about the specific domain shifts and temporal dynamics that were considered during their creation. What were the key design principles that guided the selection of these shifts and dynamics, and how do these choices reflect the challenges encountered in real-world applications? Finally, concerning the performance of the InceptionRain model, I am curious about the specific mechanisms that contribute to its consistent top performance across the benchmark datasets. What are the key architectural features or adaptation techniques that make this model particularly effective for time series UDA, and how might these insights inform the development of future methods? These questions aim to clarify the underlying assumptions and choices made in the paper, and to explore the potential implications of these choices for future research and practical applications.
