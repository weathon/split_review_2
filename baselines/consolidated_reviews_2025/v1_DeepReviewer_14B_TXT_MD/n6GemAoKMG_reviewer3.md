### Summary

This paper presents a novel evolutionary attack method, EvA, which directly addresses the discrete optimization challenges inherent in adversarial attacks on graph neural networks (GNNs). Unlike traditional gradient-based methods, EvA employs a genetic algorithm that operates without gradient information, making it suitable for black-box models and non-differentiable objectives. The paper introduces two new attack objectives: reducing the effectiveness of robustness certificates and breaking conformal prediction sets. The proposed method demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art attacks, achieving an additional ~11% reduction in accuracy on average.

### Strengths

1. EvA directly tackles the discrete nature of graph perturbations, avoiding the relaxation of the adjacency matrix used in gradient-based methods.
2. The method is model-agnostic and can be applied to any black-box model, broadening its applicability.
3. EvA introduces a sparse encoding that reduces memory complexity to be linear in the attack budget, making it scalable.
4. The paper proposes two novel attack objectives that target robustness certificates and conformal prediction sets, expanding the scope of adversarial attacks on graphs.
5. The experimental results show that EvA outperforms existing state-of-the-art attacks, demonstrating its effectiveness.

1. The paper assumes that the adversary has full knowledge of the graph structure and node labels. This assumption may not always hold in real-world scenarios, potentially limiting the practical applicability of the attack. Specifically, the assumption of perfect knowledge of the adjacency matrix and node features is a strong one, as in many real-world graphs, this information might be partially obscured or noisy. The attack's effectiveness could be significantly reduced if the adversary only has access to a perturbed or incomplete version of the graph.
2. The method relies on multiple forward passes through the model, which can be unrealistic in some attack scenarios due to query limitations or computational costs. The number of forward passes required by the genetic algorithm could be prohibitively high, especially for large graphs or complex models. This computational overhead makes the attack less practical in scenarios where the model is not easily accessible or where each query incurs a significant cost. Furthermore, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the query efficiency of the proposed method compared to other black-box attack strategies.
3. While the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of EvA, it does not extensively explore potential defenses against these evolutionary attacks. The lack of discussion on potential defenses is a significant weakness. The paper should at least discuss the potential of adversarial training or other robustness techniques against the proposed attack. Without such analysis, it is difficult to assess the practical implications of the attack and the potential for developing effective countermeasures.

### Suggestions

The paper should investigate the robustness of the proposed attack under more realistic threat models where the adversary has only partial or noisy information about the graph structure and node features. This could involve evaluating the attack's performance when the adversary has access to a perturbed version of the graph, where some edges are randomly added or removed, or when the node features are noisy. Such experiments would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the attack's practical limitations and its applicability in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the authors should explore techniques to reduce the number of forward passes required by the genetic algorithm. This could involve using more efficient genetic operators or employing techniques such as batch evaluation to reduce the computational overhead. A detailed analysis of the query efficiency of the proposed method compared to other black-box attack strategies would also be beneficial. This analysis should include a comparison of the number of queries required to achieve a certain level of attack success, as well as the computational time required for each query.

Additionally, the paper should include a discussion of potential defenses against the proposed evolutionary attack. This could involve exploring the effectiveness of adversarial training, where the model is trained on adversarially perturbed graphs generated by EvA. The authors could also investigate other robustness techniques, such as graph smoothing or spectral filtering, to mitigate the impact of the attack. A thorough analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of different defense strategies would provide valuable insights into the practical implications of the proposed attack and the potential for developing effective countermeasures. The paper should also consider the potential for adaptive attacks, where the adversary iteratively refines the attack based on the model's defenses. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the attack's effectiveness in a dynamic environment.

Finally, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and space complexity of each step of the algorithm, as well as a comparison with the complexity of other black-box attack methods. The authors should also discuss the scalability of the proposed method to large graphs and provide guidelines for choosing the appropriate parameters for different graph sizes. This analysis would help to clarify the practical limitations of the proposed method and provide guidance for its application in real-world scenarios.
