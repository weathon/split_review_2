## Summary

This paper introduces the Information Bazaar, a novel simulated digital marketplace designed to address the buyer’s inspection paradox in information markets. The core idea is to create a platform where intelligent agents, powered by large language models (LLMs), act as buyers and sellers of information. The agents are equipped with two key capabilities: the ability to assess the quality of privileged information and the ability to forget, which is intended to mitigate the risk of unauthorized information retention. The marketplace operates through a series of tenders, where buyer agents post queries and vendor agents respond with quotes, allowing the buyer agents to strategically explore the information landscape. The authors conduct a series of experiments to investigate the microeconomic behavior of LLMs, focusing on their susceptibility to biases, their price sensitivity, and the impact of inspection and budget on the quality of outcomes. The experiments reveal that LLMs can effectively function as economic actors, but their performance is influenced by factors such as prompting techniques and the ability to inspect information before purchase. The paper also explores the use of debate prompting, a technique where the LLM simulates a debate between two characters to enhance decision-making. The findings suggest that debate prompting can improve the rationality of LLM agents. Overall, the paper presents a creative approach to addressing a long-standing economic problem by leveraging the capabilities of LLMs, and it provides valuable insights into the potential of AI in economic systems. However, the paper also has several limitations, particularly in the scope of its experimental validation and the depth of its analysis of the proposed system's limitations.
