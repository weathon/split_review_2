### Summary

This paper proposes a speech codec that can be controlled by the timbre of the target speaker, enabling the codec to perform voice conversion in addition to speech compression. The authors propose a causal projection network that can modify the codec tokens according to the timbre of the target speaker. The authors evaluate the proposed method in terms of both speech compression and voice conversion, showing that the proposed method can achieve high performance in both tasks.

### Strengths

The idea of integrating voice conversion and speech compression into a single model is interesting. The authors demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve high performance in both speech compression and voice conversion.

### Weaknesses

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

1. The authors claim that the proposed method can be applied to any encoder-quantizer-decoder codec. However, the proposed causal projection network is trained using a codec with scalar quantization. It is unclear whether the causal projection network can be applied to codec tokens with other quantization methods, such as vector quantization. Specifically, the network's architecture and training objective are tailored to the discrete nature of scalar-quantized tokens, and it is not clear how it would adapt to the continuous-valued codes produced by autoencoders or the discrete codebooks of VQ-VAEs. The projection network might require significant modifications or retraining to be compatible with these different token representations.
2. The authors claim that the proposed method can switch between the original voice mode and the voice change mode seamlessly. However, the causal projection network is trained with paired data generated by an open-source voice conversion toolkit. It is unclear whether the causal projection network can perform timbre adaptation on unseen speakers. The training data for the projection network is limited to a specific set of speakers, and the network's ability to generalize to unseen speakers is not demonstrated. This raises concerns about the robustness of the voice conversion performance when applied to speakers with significantly different timbral characteristics than those used in training.

### Suggestions

To address the limitation regarding the applicability of the causal projection network to different quantization methods, the authors should conduct experiments using codec tokens generated by autoencoders and vector quantization. This would involve adapting the projection network's architecture and training procedure to accommodate the different characteristics of these tokens. For example, when using autoencoder tokens, the network might need to be modified to handle continuous-valued inputs, potentially by incorporating a regression-based approach rather than a classification-based one. Similarly, for vector quantized tokens, the network would need to be adapted to operate on discrete codebook indices, possibly by using a cross-entropy loss or a similar approach. These experiments would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the network's generalizability and limitations.

To address the concern about the generalization of the causal projection network to unseen speakers, the authors should evaluate the performance of the voice conversion on a diverse set of speakers not included in the training data. This evaluation should include both objective metrics, such as Mel-Cepstral Distortion (MCD) and speaker identification accuracy, and subjective listening tests to assess the naturalness and similarity of the converted speech. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the impact of the speaker embedding quality on the voice conversion performance. This could involve analyzing the relationship between the speaker embedding similarity and the quality of the converted speech. Such analysis would provide insights into the robustness of the proposed method and its ability to handle a wide range of speaker variations.

Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the training process for the causal projection network, including the specific loss functions used, the optimization algorithms, and the hyperparameter settings. This would allow other researchers to reproduce the results and build upon the proposed method. Additionally, the authors should discuss the computational complexity of the proposed method and its suitability for real-time applications. This discussion should include an analysis of the latency and memory requirements of the different components of the system, particularly the causal projection network.

### Questions

1. How does the proposed method perform when applied to codec tokens generated by autoencoders or vector quantization?
2. How does the proposed method perform when the target speaker is unseen during training?
