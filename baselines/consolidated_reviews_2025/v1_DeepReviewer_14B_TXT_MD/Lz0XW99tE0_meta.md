## Summary

This paper introduces CrysBFN, a novel approach to crystal structure generation that leverages Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) adapted for the periodic nature of crystal data. The core contribution lies in the development of a periodic Bayesian flow on a hyper-torus, which allows the model to capture the inherent symmetries of crystal structures, a challenge that traditional Euclidean-based generative models struggle with. The authors address the limitations of existing diffusion-based methods by proposing a framework that operates directly on the periodic space of fractional coordinates, using the von Mises distribution as the base distribution for the Bayesian flow. This choice is crucial for modeling the circular nature of these coordinates. The method also incorporates an entropy conditioning mechanism to improve the training process. The empirical evaluation of CrysBFN is conducted on several benchmark datasets for both ab initio crystal generation and crystal structure prediction tasks. The results demonstrate that CrysBFN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of both generation quality and sampling efficiency. Notably, the paper reports a 100x speedup in sampling compared to diffusion-based methods like DiffCSP, which is a significant practical advantage. The authors also provide ablation studies to validate the importance of their design choices, such as the use of an equivariant function and the proposed accuracy schedule. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of crystal generation by introducing a novel and efficient approach that is well-suited to the unique challenges of this domain. The adaptation of BFNs to handle periodic data and the introduction of a periodic Bayesian flow are key innovations that have the potential to impact future research in materials science and related fields. The paper's findings suggest that CrysBFN is a promising tool for generating novel crystal structures and predicting stable structures, which could have significant implications for materials discovery and design.

## Strengths

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The most significant is the novel adaptation of Bayesian Flow Networks to handle the periodic nature of crystal structures. The introduction of a periodic Bayesian flow on a hyper-torus, using the von Mises distribution, is a clever and effective way to model the fractional coordinates of atoms. This approach directly addresses a key limitation of traditional Euclidean-based generative models, which struggle with the inherent symmetries of crystal data. The authors' decision to work directly in the fractional coordinate space, rather than transforming to Cartesian coordinates, is also a strength, as it allows the model to explicitly capture the periodicity of the system. Furthermore, the paper provides a solid theoretical foundation for the proposed method, including the derivation of the periodic Bayesian flow and the introduction of a new entropy conditioning mechanism. The reformulation of BFN for efficient sampling is also a notable technical contribution. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling, with CrysBFN achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks. The reported 100x speedup in sampling efficiency compared to diffusion-based methods is a major practical advantage, making the method more accessible for real-world applications. The ablation studies, while limited in scope, do provide evidence for the importance of the proposed design choices, such as the use of an equivariant function and the specific accuracy schedule. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, which is a significant strength. The authors clearly articulate the problem they are addressing, the proposed solution, and the main results. The figures and tables are also well-presented and help to convey the key ideas of the paper. Overall, the combination of theoretical innovation, empirical validation, and clear presentation makes this a strong and impactful contribution to the field of crystal generation.
