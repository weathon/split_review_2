## Summary

This paper tackles the significant challenge of architecture overfitting in dataset distillation, a phenomenon where distilled datasets, created to train neural networks with minimal data, exhibit poor performance when used with architectures different from the one used for distillation. The authors propose a multi-pronged approach to mitigate this issue, combining several existing techniques in a novel way. Their methodology includes a modified DropPath technique with a three-phase keep rate, which stochastically drops out layers during training to encourage robustness; knowledge distillation from a smaller teacher network, which transfers knowledge to larger test networks; a periodical learning rate scheduler, which adjusts the learning rate to escape local minima; and a stronger data augmentation scheme, which enhances the diversity of the training data. The core idea is that by addressing the architecture overfitting problem, the distilled datasets can be made more versatile and useful across a range of network architectures. The authors evaluate their approach on two dataset distillation algorithms, FRePo and MTT, and demonstrate its effectiveness across various network architectures, including ResNet18, AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet50. They show that their method significantly improves the performance of these architectures when trained on distilled data, especially for deeper networks like ResNet50. The empirical results, presented in detailed tables and figures, highlight the substantial performance gains achieved by their proposed methods compared to baseline approaches. The paper also demonstrates that their techniques improve the performance of training on limited real data, suggesting a broader applicability beyond just dataset distillation. Overall, this work makes a valuable contribution to the field by addressing a critical limitation of dataset distillation and providing a practical solution that enhances the generalization capabilities of distilled datasets. The authors present a well-structured and comprehensive study, supported by extensive experiments and clear results, which underscores the importance of addressing architecture overfitting for the broader adoption of dataset distillation techniques.
