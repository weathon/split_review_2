## Summary

This paper introduces Control-GIC, a novel generative image compression framework designed for fine-grained bitrate adaptation. The core innovation lies in its ability to dynamically adjust the granularity of image patch representations based on their information density, enabling a wide spectrum of bitrate control while maintaining high-fidelity compression. The method leverages a VQGAN architecture, where an image is encoded into a sequence of variable-length codes, which are then losslessly compressed. The authors propose a granularity-informed encoder that correlates the information density of local image patches with their granular representations, allowing for dynamic adjustment of VQ-indices to achieve desired compression rates. This is further enhanced by a probabilistic conditional decoder that reconstructs hierarchical granular features in a conditional probability manner, improving the realism of the reconstructed images. The method employs a statistical entropy coding strategy that captures the frequency distribution of indices usage across a natural dataset during training, leading to more efficient compression. The experimental results demonstrate that Control-GIC outperforms recent state-of-the-art methods in terms of flexibility, perceptual quality, and compression efficiency. The authors present a comprehensive evaluation, including comparisons with existing compression techniques and ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of their proposed components. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a controllable generative compression model that allows for fine-grained bitrate adaptation, addressing a significant limitation of existing methods that either require separate models for different compression ratios or support only limited ranges of bitrates. The use of a VQGAN framework, combined with the proposed granularity-informed encoder and probabilistic conditional decoder, provides a novel approach to image compression, offering both high perceptual quality and flexible bitrate control. The paper also introduces a statistical entropy coding strategy that further enhances compression efficiency by leveraging the frequency distribution of indices. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of image compression, offering a promising approach for practical applications requiring flexible and efficient compression.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and efficiency of the proposed method. While the experimental results demonstrate superior performance, it is crucial to understand the computational cost associated with the approach. Specifically, the paper does not provide a breakdown of the computational cost associated with each component of the proposed method, including the encoder, decoder, and entropy coding modules. This analysis should include metrics such as the number of parameters, FLOPs, and memory requirements. Furthermore, a comparison of the computational cost with existing methods would be valuable to understand the trade-offs between performance and efficiency. This is a significant omission, as it makes it difficult to assess the practicality of the proposed method for real-world applications, especially in resource-constrained environments. For example, the paper does not compare the number of parameters or FLOPs of their method against traditional codecs like JPEG2000 and H.265, or recent learned compression methods. This lack of analysis limits the reader's ability to fully evaluate the method's potential for deployment. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly lacks this information in the experimental section. Second, while the paper presents a comprehensive evaluation, the analysis of the results, particularly in the ablation study, is not sufficiently in-depth. The ablation study demonstrates the impact of different components on the performance, but the reasons behind these impacts are not discussed in detail. For example, the paper states that the probabilistic conditional decoder significantly improves performance, but it does not delve into the specific mechanisms or feature representations that are enhanced by the conditional probabilities. Similarly, the paper shows that the statistical entropy coding strategy leads to bit savings, but it does not analyze the specific characteristics of the learned frequency distribution that contribute to this improvement. This lack of detailed analysis limits the reader's understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's analysis of the ablation study results is primarily descriptive, lacking a deeper explanation of the underlying mechanisms. Finally, while the paper evaluates the method on multiple datasets, including Kodak, DIV2K, and CLIC2020, the evaluation lacks a granular analysis of performance across different image characteristics. The paper does not analyze how the method performs on images with varying textures, lighting conditions, and object types. This is a significant limitation, as it makes it difficult to assess the generalization ability of the proposed method and its robustness to different types of images. My confidence in this weakness is medium, as the paper does use multiple datasets, but lacks a detailed analysis of performance across different image characteristics within these datasets and the qualitative evaluation is limited.
