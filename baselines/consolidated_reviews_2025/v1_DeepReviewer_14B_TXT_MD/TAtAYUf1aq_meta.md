## Summary

This paper introduces Memoria, a novel memory architecture for neural networks inspired by Hebbian theory, designed to enhance the processing of long-term dependencies. The core idea is to implement a multi-level memory system, comprising working memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory, which interact through a Hebbian-based mechanism. The architecture stores and retrieves information in the form of 'engrams,' with connection weights between engrams adjusted according to a principle inspired by Hebb's rule, often summarized as 'fire together, wire together.' The method involves a 'remind' process, where relevant engrams are retrieved based on correlation, and a 'memorize & forget' process, where connection weights are updated based on co-activation, and engrams with zero lifespan are removed. The authors integrate Memoria with popular Transformer-based models, such as BERT and GPT, and evaluate its performance across various tasks, including sorting, language modeling, and text classification. The experimental results demonstrate that Memoria improves the ability of these models to handle long-term dependencies, showing better performance compared to baseline models. Specifically, the paper shows that Memoria outperforms standard Transformers and other long-sequence models like Transformer-XL and Compressive Transformer in language modeling tasks, and it also shows improvements in text classification tasks. The authors also provide an analysis of the average age of reminded engrams, which indicates that Memoria effectively utilizes long-term memory. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to memory management in neural networks, offering a biologically inspired alternative to traditional methods. However, as I will discuss, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened to fully realize its potential.

## Strengths

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly compelling. The core concept of integrating Hebbian theory into a memory architecture for neural networks is both novel and intriguing. The idea of using a multi-level memory system, with distinct working, short-term, and long-term components, is a logical approach to managing information over different time scales. The use of 'engrams' as the fundamental unit of memory, and the implementation of a Hebbian-inspired learning rule to strengthen connections between co-activated engrams, provides a plausible mechanism for learning and retrieving long-term dependencies. The experimental results, while not without limitations, do demonstrate that Memoria improves the performance of Transformer-based models on tasks that require long-term memory. Specifically, the improvements observed in language modeling and text classification tasks, compared to baseline models, provide empirical support for the effectiveness of the proposed architecture. The analysis of the average age of reminded engrams further supports the claim that Memoria effectively utilizes long-term memory. The paper also provides a clear description of the core mechanisms of Memoria, including the remind and memorize & forget processes, which makes the method relatively easy to understand. The integration of Memoria with popular Transformer-based models like BERT and GPT is also a strength, as it demonstrates the applicability of the proposed architecture to existing models. The paper also provides a mathematical formulation for updating the connection weights between engrams, which adds to the rigor of the proposed method. The inclusion of a statistical significance test in the classification task further strengthens the validity of the results.
