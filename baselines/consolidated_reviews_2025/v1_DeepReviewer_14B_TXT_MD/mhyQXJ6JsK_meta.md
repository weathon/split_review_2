## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to accelerate the computation of tensor products of irreducible representations (irreps) for E(3)-equivariant neural networks, a critical operation in modeling 3D data. The core innovation lies in leveraging the connection between Clebsch-Gordan and Gaunt coefficients, which allows the authors to reformulate the tensor product operation as a multiplication between spherical functions represented by spherical harmonics. By transforming these spherical harmonics into a 2D Fourier basis, the authors can then utilize Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) to efficiently compute the tensor products. This transformation significantly reduces the computational complexity from O(L^6) to O(L^3), where L is the maximum degree of the irreps. The proposed method, termed the Gaunt Tensor Product, is presented as a versatile technique applicable to various model architectures. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through experiments on the Open Catalyst Project (OC20) and 3BPA datasets, showcasing both increased efficiency and improved performance compared to existing methods. Specifically, they apply the Gaunt Tensor Product to three major operation classes: Equivariant Feature Interactions, Equivariant Convolutions, and Equivariant Many-body Interactions, demonstrating its broad applicability. The paper provides a thorough mathematical foundation for the proposed method, connecting Clebsch-Gordan coefficients to Gaunt coefficients and establishing the equivalence between tensor products of irreps and multiplication of spherical functions. The experimental results indicate that the Gaunt Tensor Product not only reduces computational cost but also leads to improved model performance on the chosen tasks. This work addresses a critical bottleneck in equivariant neural networks for 3D data, offering a promising avenue for scaling these models to more complex problems. The authors' approach of changing the basis to a 2D Fourier basis and using FFT is a clever way to circumvent the computational challenges associated with high-degree irreps. The paper's contribution is significant in that it provides a practical and efficient method for handling tensor products of irreps, which are fundamental to E(3)-equivariant neural networks. The potential impact of this work is substantial, as it could enable the development of more powerful and scalable equivariant models for a wide range of 3D modeling tasks. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are some limitations in the scope of the experimental evaluation and the level of detail provided on the practical implementation, which need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this method.
