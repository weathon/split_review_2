## Summary

This paper investigates the presence of racial and gender biases in the Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) text-to-image generative model, a widely used tool with significant societal impact. The authors conduct a comprehensive analysis of SDXL's outputs across six racial groups, two genders, 32 professions, and eight attributes, revealing substantial biases in the model's representations. They demonstrate that SDXL tends to underrepresent certain demographics and homogenize individuals within the same race, leading to stereotypical portrayals. To address these issues, the authors propose a debiasing method, SDXL-Inc, which involves fine-tuning SDXL using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with a curated dataset designed to promote more inclusive representations. They further introduce SDXL-Div to address racial homogenization by increasing the diversity of generated faces within each race. The paper's core methodological approach involves generating large numbers of images using specific prompts and then analyzing the resulting distributions of race and gender. The authors also develop a classifier to quantify these attributes in the generated images. The empirical findings demonstrate that SDXL exhibits significant biases, with certain races and genders being underrepresented in specific professions and attributes. The proposed SDXL-Inc model is shown to reduce these biases, leading to more balanced representations. Furthermore, the authors conduct a preregistered user study with 135 participants, which provides compelling evidence that exposure to inclusive AI-generated faces, produced by SDXL-Inc, can reduce people's own racial and gender biases, while exposure to non-inclusive images can increase such biases. This study highlights the potential for AI-generated content to either perpetuate or mitigate societal stereotypes. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by quantifying biases in a widely used generative model, proposing effective debiasing techniques, and demonstrating the impact of inclusive AI-generated content on human perception. The work underscores the importance of addressing biases in AI systems to prevent the reinforcement of harmful stereotypes and promote more equitable representations in AI-generated content. The paper's findings are particularly relevant given the increasing use of generative models in various applications, from art and advertising to education and entertainment, and the potential for these models to shape societal perceptions.

## Strengths

This paper makes several notable contributions to the field of AI and bias mitigation. First, it addresses a critical and timely issue by investigating the extent of racial and gender biases in the widely used Stable Diffusion XL model. Given the increasing prevalence of such generative models, understanding and addressing their inherent biases is crucial for ensuring fairness and preventing the perpetuation of harmful stereotypes. The authors' comprehensive analysis of SDXL's outputs across multiple dimensions—race, gender, profession, and attributes—provides a detailed understanding of the types and extent of biases present in the model. This thorough approach allows for a nuanced view of the model's shortcomings, rather than a superficial assessment. The development and implementation of the SDXL-Inc model, which fine-tunes SDXL using LoRA to promote more inclusive representations, is another significant strength. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of this debiasing technique in reducing biases in AI-generated images. The empirical results, showing a more balanced distribution of race and gender in the outputs of SDXL-Inc compared to the original SDXL, provide strong evidence for the method's efficacy. Furthermore, the inclusion of a preregistered user study adds substantial weight to the paper's findings. The study's results, demonstrating that exposure to inclusive AI-generated faces can reduce people's own racial and gender biases, highlight the real-world impact of the proposed debiasing method. This connection between AI-generated content and human perception is a crucial contribution, emphasizing the societal implications of bias in AI systems. The authors' use of a classifier to quantify the attributes in the generated images is also a strength, as it allows for a systematic and objective analysis of the model's outputs. This approach provides a quantitative basis for their claims, rather than relying on subjective observations. Finally, the paper's focus on both the technical aspects of debiasing and the societal impact of biased AI systems makes it a well-rounded and impactful contribution to the field. The combination of technical innovation with a strong awareness of the ethical implications of AI bias is particularly commendable.

## Weaknesses

While this paper makes significant contributions, several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, the authors claim that "none of the studies proposed debiasing solutions," which is an overstatement. As I verified, there are several relevant works, such as those by Friedrich et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023), that have proposed debiasing methods for generative models. This claim undermines the paper's positioning and potentially overlooks relevant prior work. The paper's debiasing method, SDXL-Inc, involves fine-tuning SDXL using LoRA, a technique that is not novel in itself. While the application of LoRA to this specific problem is a contribution, the core method is not new, which impacts the perceived novelty of the work. The user study, while well-designed, has several limitations that affect the generalizability of its findings. The study includes only 135 participants, which is a relatively small sample size. While the authors justify this number based on a power analysis for detecting a medium effect size, a larger sample would provide more robust and generalizable results. Furthermore, the study is limited to American participants who are residents of the US and whose first language is English. This narrow demographic scope raises concerns about whether the findings can be applied to people from other countries, those who speak other languages, or those who are not US residents. The study also does not explicitly state the number of US states the participants are from, but the recruitment process likely limits the geographical diversity within the US. This lack of diversity further limits the generalizability of the findings. The user study also lacks a crucial control condition. The authors do not conduct user studies on SDXL-Inc without revealing that the images are generated using SDXL-Inc, and compare the results with the "labeled as AI-generated" results. This is a significant oversight, as it is unclear whether the labeling of the images as AI-generated influences the participants' perceptions of bias. Finally, the authors do not conduct user studies on other debiasing methods, such as ITI-GEN and Fair Diffusion, and compare the results with SDXL-Inc. This lack of comparison makes it difficult to assess whether the proposed method is more effective than existing debiasing techniques. These limitations, particularly the narrow scope of the user study and the lack of comparative analysis, significantly impact the generalizability and robustness of the paper's conclusions. The overstatement of novelty regarding debiasing solutions also weakens the paper's positioning within the existing literature. My confidence in these weaknesses is high, as they are directly supported by the paper's content and the absence of specific data or conditions.

## Suggestions

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should revise their claims regarding the novelty of their debiasing solution. Instead of stating that no prior work has proposed debiasing solutions, they should acknowledge existing methods and clearly articulate how their approach builds upon or differs from them. This would involve a more thorough literature review and a more nuanced discussion of their contributions. Second, the authors should significantly expand the scope of their user studies. This includes increasing the sample size to at least 500 participants to ensure statistical significance and generalizability. The participant pool should also be more diverse, including individuals from different countries, languages, and regions within those countries. For example, the authors could recruit participants from different continents, including Africa, Asia, and South America, to ensure that the results are not biased towards a Western perspective. Furthermore, they should include participants who speak languages other than English, such as Spanish, French, or Mandarin, to examine whether the observed biases are consistent across different linguistic groups. The authors should also aim for a more balanced representation of participants from all 50 states in the US. Third, the authors should refine their experimental design to include more control conditions. Specifically, they should conduct user studies on SDXL-Inc without revealing that the images are generated using SDXL-Inc, and compare the results with the "labeled as AI-generated" results. This will help to determine whether the labeling of the images as AI-generated influences the participants' perceptions of bias. They should also conduct user studies on other debiasing methods, such as ITI-GEN and Fair Diffusion, and compare the results with SDXL-Inc. This will help to determine whether the proposed method is more effective than existing debiasing techniques. Fourth, the authors should consider using a more diverse set of prompts to generate images, including prompts that are more complex and nuanced. This will help to ensure that the results are not biased towards a specific set of prompts. They should also consider using a more diverse set of attributes, including attributes that are not directly related to race and gender, such as age, socioeconomic status, and physical abilities. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the results, including a breakdown of the results by demographic group. This will help to identify any potential biases in the results and to ensure that the conclusions are supported by the data. They should also consider using statistical methods to control for potential confounding variables, such as age, education level, and political affiliation. The authors should also provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their study and the potential implications of their findings. These changes would significantly strengthen the paper's findings and increase the generalizability of its conclusions.

## Questions

Several key questions arise from my analysis of this paper. First, given the existence of other debiasing methods for generative models, what specific advantages does the proposed SDXL-Inc method offer compared to these existing techniques? A more detailed comparison of the proposed method with other approaches, such as ITI-GEN and Fair Diffusion, would be beneficial. Second, how would the results of the user study change if the participants were not informed that the images were generated by AI? The lack of a control condition where SDXL-Inc images are presented without revealing their AI origin raises questions about the influence of the "AI-generated" label on participants' perceptions. Third, what is the impact of the specific prompts used to generate images on the observed biases? Exploring the sensitivity of the results to different prompts, including more complex and nuanced prompts, would be valuable. A more detailed analysis of the results by demographic group, including age, education level, and political affiliation, would help to identify potential biases in the results and to ensure that the conclusions are supported by the data. Fifth, what are the ethical implications of the work, particularly regarding the potential for the proposed method to be used to perpetuate biases and stereotypes or to create misleading or harmful content? A more detailed discussion of the ethical considerations would be beneficial. Finally, how can the proposed debiasing method be scaled to other generative models and other types of biases? Exploring the generalizability of the method to other contexts would be valuable. These questions target core methodological choices and seek clarification of critical assumptions, aiming to further refine the understanding of the paper's contributions and limitations.
