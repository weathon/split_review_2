## Summary

This paper introduces ZeroP, a novel zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) framework that leverages publicly available proxy data (PD) to enhance the performance of quantized neural networks, particularly when original training data is inaccessible. The core idea is to use PD as a substitute for original data (OD) during the quantization process, addressing the limitations of existing ZSQ methods that rely solely on synthetic data (SD). The authors propose a method to select optimal PD based on batch-normalization statistics (BNS) from a set of candidate datasets. This BNS-based selection method aims to identify proxy data that closely matches the distribution of the original data, thereby improving the effectiveness of the quantization process. The ZeroP framework directly combines the selected PD with SD as input for the ZSQ task, which is a departure from previous approaches that use PD to guide SD generation. The authors evaluate their method on several computer vision datasets, including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and ImageNet-1K, using a variety of convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures such as ResNet, MobileNet, and RegNet. The experimental results demonstrate that ZeroP achieves significant improvements in accuracy compared to state-of-the-art pure-SD ZSQ methods. For instance, on MobileNetV1, ZeroP achieves a 7% to 16% improvement in accuracy on ImageNet-1K in a 4-bit setting. Furthermore, the paper shows that ZeroP outperforms existing OD methods in terms of accuracy. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of their proposed framework, including the BNS-based PD selection method. The findings suggest that the combination of PD and SD, along with the BNS-based selection, contributes to the overall performance gains. The paper's main contribution lies in the introduction of a practical and effective approach to leverage publicly available data for zero-shot quantization, which has significant implications for deploying quantized models in real-world scenarios where original training data is often unavailable. By demonstrating the effectiveness of ZeroP across various datasets and network architectures, the authors highlight the potential of their method to improve the performance of quantized models in resource-constrained environments. The paper also provides valuable insights into the role of proxy data in zero-shot quantization and the importance of selecting appropriate proxy data based on batch-normalization statistics. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and empirically validated approach to address a critical challenge in the field of model compression and deployment.
