### Summary

This paper proposes a new decoding method called Permute-and-Flip (PF) decoder, which has stability properties similar to standard sampling but is provably up to 2x better in its quality-stability tradeoff. Additionally, the authors design a cryptographic watermarking scheme tailored for the PF decoder, similar to Aaronson's Gumbel watermark. This scheme allows for an arbitrarily low false positive rate and high recall when the generated text has high entropy. Experimental results show that the PF decoder and its watermarked version outperform naive sampling in terms of perplexity while maintaining the same stability and detectability.

### Weaknesses

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

1. The paper lacks experimental evaluation of the proposed PF decoder and watermarking scheme on multiple large language models (LLMs) of varying sizes. It would be beneficial to see how the PF decoder performs on different LLMs, such as LLaMA3 or other models, and whether the performance gap observed in the current experiments holds across different models. Additionally, it is unclear whether the PF decoder and watermarking scheme can be applied to closed-source models like ChatGPT, and if so, how to ensure that the watermarked text generated by these models comes from the same distribution as the original model.
2. The paper lacks evaluation of the robustness of the proposed watermarking scheme against attacks, such as paraphrasing. It is important to assess how well the watermarking scheme can withstand attempts to remove or alter the watermark through paraphrasing or other modifications to the text.

### Suggestions

The paper would significantly benefit from a more extensive experimental evaluation of the proposed Permute-and-Flip (PF) decoder and its associated watermarking scheme. Specifically, the current evaluation is limited to a single model, and it is crucial to assess the performance of the PF decoder across a range of large language models (LLMs) with varying architectures and sizes. This should include models such as LLaMA3, or other widely used models, to determine if the observed improvements in perplexity and stability are consistent across different model families. Furthermore, the experiments should explore the impact of model size on the effectiveness of the PF decoder. It is important to understand whether the performance gains are maintained, or if they diminish, as the model size increases. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the practical applicability of the proposed method. Additionally, the paper should investigate the feasibility of applying the PF decoder and watermarking scheme to closed-source models, such as ChatGPT. This would involve exploring methods to ensure that the watermarked text generated by these models maintains a distribution similar to the original model, which is a non-trivial task given the lack of access to the model's internal parameters. The evaluation should also include a comparison of the computational overhead of the PF decoder compared to standard sampling methods, especially when applied to larger models.

To address the lack of robustness evaluation, the paper should include a thorough analysis of the proposed watermarking scheme's resilience against various attacks. Paraphrasing is a critical attack vector that needs to be explored in detail. The evaluation should include both syntactic and semantic paraphrasing techniques to determine how well the watermark persists under different levels of text modification. This could involve using paraphrasing models or techniques to generate multiple paraphrased versions of the watermarked text and then assessing the detection rate of the watermark in these modified texts. The paper should also investigate the impact of different paraphrasing strategies on the watermark's detectability. Furthermore, the evaluation should consider other potential attacks, such as text editing, compression, or translation, to provide a comprehensive assessment of the watermark's robustness. The results of these experiments should be presented with clear metrics, such as the false positive rate and true positive rate, to quantify the effectiveness of the watermarking scheme under different attack scenarios. This would provide a more complete picture of the practical security of the proposed watermarking method.

Finally, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the theoretical underpinnings of the PF decoder and its watermarking scheme. While the paper presents theoretical proofs, it would be beneficial to include a more in-depth discussion of the assumptions and limitations of these proofs. For example, the paper could explore the conditions under which the PF decoder is guaranteed to achieve a better quality-stability tradeoff compared to standard sampling methods. Similarly, the paper could discuss the theoretical limits of the watermarking scheme's robustness against different types of attacks. This would provide a more rigorous foundation for the proposed methods and help to identify potential areas for future research. The paper should also include a discussion of the computational complexity of the PF decoder and its watermarking scheme, especially in comparison to standard sampling methods. This would help to assess the practical feasibility of the proposed methods for large-scale applications.
