### Summary

The paper presents a novel approach for scene decomposition using convex primitives, incorporating negative primitives and an ensembling method to improve accuracy. The authors demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods on the NYUv2 dataset, particularly in depth and normal prediction, as well as scene segmentation. The method's ability to handle complex geometries with a small number of primitives and its robustness to local minima are highlighted as key advantages. The paper also provides a detailed analysis of the impact of negative primitives and the ensembling strategy, showing that these components contribute to the overall performance gains.

### Strengths

1. **Novelty and Innovation**: The introduction of negative primitives and the ensembling method represents a significant advancement in the field of scene decomposition. The use of CSG with set differencing operators to indoor scenes is a unique contribution.
2. **Empirical Validation**: The paper provides extensive experimental results on the NYUv2 dataset, demonstrating substantial improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods. The ablation studies effectively isolate the contributions of negative primitives and the ensembling strategy.
3. **Technical Depth**: The method is well-grounded in geometric reasoning and provides a detailed explanation of the fitting process, loss functions, and optimization techniques. The polish-then-choose strategy is particularly innovative and addresses a key challenge in primitive decomposition.
4. **Clarity and Presentation**: The paper is well-organized, with clear figures and tables that effectively communicate the results. The qualitative comparisons, especially in Figure 1, provide a compelling visual demonstration of the method's effectiveness.

1. **Computational Complexity**: The ensembling method, while effective, significantly increases the computational cost. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity, especially in terms of training and inference time. A comparison with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency would be beneficial. Specifically, the paper should provide a breakdown of the time spent on each stage of the pipeline (e.g., initial primitive prediction, refinement, and ensembling) to better understand the computational bottlenecks. Furthermore, the memory footprint of the ensemble approach should be discussed, as this could be a limiting factor for deployment on resource-constrained devices.
2. **Generalization to Other Datasets**: The experiments are primarily conducted on the NYUv2 dataset. While this is a standard benchmark, the paper does not explore the method's performance on other datasets or in more diverse environments. This raises concerns about the generalizability of the approach. It would be beneficial to see results on datasets with different characteristics, such as those with more varied lighting conditions, object types, or scene complexities. For example, testing on datasets with outdoor scenes or synthetic data could reveal potential limitations of the method.
3. **Ablation Studies on Ensemble Size**: The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of ensembling but does not thoroughly explore the impact of ensemble size on performance and computational cost. A more detailed ablation study varying the number of networks in the ensemble would provide valuable insights into the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency. It is unclear if the performance gains saturate beyond a certain ensemble size, and this should be investigated. The paper should also analyze the diversity of the primitives generated by different ensemble members and how this diversity contributes to the overall performance.
4. **Robustness to Noise and Outliers**: The paper does not explicitly address the robustness of the method to noise and outliers in the input data. While the method shows good performance on clean data, real-world scenarios often involve noisy depth measurements or incomplete data. An analysis of the method's sensitivity to such imperfections would be valuable. For example, the paper could evaluate the performance of the method on datasets with simulated noise or on real-world datasets with known depth errors.

### Suggestions

To address the computational complexity concerns, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the time spent on each stage of their pipeline, including the initial primitive prediction, the refinement process, and the ensembling step. This analysis should be performed on a standard hardware setup and should include both training and inference times. Furthermore, the memory footprint of the ensemble approach should be quantified, as this is a crucial factor for practical deployment. The authors could also explore techniques to reduce the computational cost, such as model compression or knowledge distillation, to make the method more efficient without sacrificing accuracy. A comparison with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency would also be beneficial to contextualize the performance of the proposed approach.

To improve the generalization capabilities of the method, the authors should evaluate their approach on a wider range of datasets, including those with different characteristics such as outdoor scenes, synthetic data, or datasets with more varied lighting conditions. This would help to identify potential limitations of the method and to ensure that it is robust to different types of input data. The authors could also explore techniques to improve the generalization of the method, such as data augmentation or domain adaptation. Additionally, the paper should include a discussion of the limitations of the method and the scenarios where it might not perform well. This would provide a more balanced and realistic assessment of the proposed approach.

Finally, the authors should conduct a more thorough ablation study on the ensemble size, exploring the impact of varying the number of networks on both performance and computational cost. This study should investigate whether the performance gains saturate beyond a certain ensemble size and should analyze the diversity of the primitives generated by different ensemble members. The authors should also explore the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency and provide guidelines for selecting the optimal ensemble size for different applications. Furthermore, the paper should include an analysis of the method's robustness to noise and outliers in the input data, evaluating its performance on datasets with simulated noise or on real-world datasets with known depth errors. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses.
