## Summary

This paper introduces BAP, a branch-aware parallel execution framework designed to accelerate deep neural network (DNN) inference on mobile CPUs, particularly for models with dynamic control flows such as those found in automatic speech recognition (ASR) and transformer-based architectures. The core idea behind BAP is to identify parallelizable branches within a model's computational graph at runtime and execute them concurrently using multithreading. This is achieved through a combination of a runtime graph analysis pipeline, a branch-aware memory allocation strategy, and optimized thread management. The runtime analysis identifies independent branches that can be executed in parallel, while the branch-aware memory allocation isolates memory arenas for each branch, reducing data conflicts and optimizing cache locality. Multithreading is then employed to execute these branches concurrently, with a fixed thread pool and task stealing to optimize CPU utilization. The authors evaluate BAP on a range of mobile devices, including smartphones and single-board computers, using ASR and transformer-based models. Their results demonstrate significant reductions in inference latency, memory allocation requirements, and energy consumption compared to the standard TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) runtime. Specifically, they report up to 38.5% reduction in latency and up to 20.2% reduction in energy consumption. The authors emphasize that BAP achieves these improvements without requiring model refactoring, making it practical for real-world deployment. The paper's contributions lie in its novel approach to optimizing DNN inference on mobile CPUs by focusing on branch-aware memory allocation and multithreading, addressing the challenges posed by dynamic models, and providing a practical solution that can be readily integrated into existing mobile inference frameworks. The paper's focus on CPU-based optimization is justified by the widespread availability of CPUs and their flexibility in handling dynamic workloads, making it a relevant contribution to the field of mobile machine learning.

## Strengths

I find several strengths in this paper, primarily in its novel approach and practical contributions. The core idea of branch-aware parallel execution for dynamic neural networks on mobile CPUs is a significant contribution. The paper effectively addresses the challenges posed by models with dynamic control flows, which are often difficult to optimize for traditional hardware accelerators. By focusing on CPU-based optimization, the authors have targeted a widely available and flexible resource, making their approach highly relevant for real-world deployment. The proposed branch-aware memory allocation strategy is another notable strength. By isolating memory management at the granularity of layers and branches, the authors have effectively reduced data conflicts and optimized cache locality, leading to improved performance. The use of a runtime graph analysis pipeline to identify parallelizable branches is also a key innovation, enabling dynamic optimization without requiring model refactoring. This makes BAP highly practical, as it can be easily integrated into existing mobile inference frameworks without modifying the underlying models. The experimental results presented in the paper are compelling, demonstrating significant reductions in inference latency, memory allocation requirements, and energy consumption across a range of mobile devices and models. The authors have provided a thorough evaluation, including comparisons with the standard and naive memory plans of TFLite, which serves as a strong baseline. The reported improvements in latency and energy consumption are substantial and highlight the practical benefits of the proposed approach. Furthermore, the paper is well-written and clearly explains the technical details of BAP, including the graph analysis pipeline, memory allocation strategy, and multithreading execution. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the key concepts and results, making the paper accessible to a broad audience. Given the prevalence of heterogeneous computing environments in mobile devices, this limitation restricts the potential impact of BAP.
