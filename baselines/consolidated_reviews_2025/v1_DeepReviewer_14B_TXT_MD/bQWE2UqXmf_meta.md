## Summary

This paper introduces RAIDAR, a novel method for detecting AI-generated text by leveraging the rewriting behavior of large language models (LLMs). The core idea is that LLMs, when prompted to rewrite text, tend to modify human-written content more extensively than AI-generated content, which they perceive as already being of high quality. The RAIDAR method operates by prompting an LLM to rewrite an input text and then calculating the edit distance between the original and the rewritten text. This edit distance serves as a feature for a classifier, which is trained to distinguish between human and AI-generated text. The authors demonstrate that RAIDAR achieves significant improvements in detection performance compared to existing methods across various domains, with reported gains of up to 29 percentage points in F1 score. The method's key advantage is its reliance on word-level symbols, making it compatible with black-box LLMs and robust to new content. The experimental evaluation includes diverse datasets such as news articles, creative writing, student essays, code, Yelp reviews, and arXiv abstracts. The authors also explore the method's robustness to different LLMs used for both text generation and rewriting, as well as its sensitivity to different prompting strategies. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed approach offers a promising direction for detecting AI-generated content, especially in scenarios where access to internal model representations is limited. Overall, the paper presents a compelling and practical approach to a critical problem in the age of increasingly sophisticated AI models, with a focus on leveraging the inherent behavior of these models for detection purposes. The method's simplicity and effectiveness make it a valuable contribution to the field, although, as I will discuss, there are several areas where further investigation and refinement are needed.
