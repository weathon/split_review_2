### Summary

This paper proposes a gradient regularization-based cross-prompt attack method for vision-language models (VLMs). The authors identify the issue of non-stationarity in adversarial attacks against VLMs and propose the GrCPA method to alleviate overfitting in the optimization process. The method enhances the transferability of adversarial examples across different prompts by reducing the variance of back-propagated gradients in the Attention and MLP components of the model. The authors conduct experiments on several state-of-the-art VLMs, including Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA, and InstructBLIP, to demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach.

### Strengths

1. The paper addresses a critical issue in the field of adversarial attacks on VLMs, which is the non-stationary behavior during adversarial example generation.

2. The authors provide extensive experimental results on multiple state-of-the-art VLMs, demonstrating the effectiveness of their proposed method in enhancing the transferability of adversarial examples across different prompts.

1. The proposed method, while effective, is an incremental improvement over existing techniques. The core idea of gradient regularization has been explored in other domains, and its application to VLMs, though novel, does not represent a significant theoretical advancement.

2. The paper lacks a comprehensive analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the proposed method. It is unclear how the method performs with increasing model size and data volume, which is crucial for practical applications.

3. The evaluation of the method is limited to a few state-of-the-art VLMs. A more extensive evaluation across a wider range of models and datasets would strengthen the claims of generalizability.

4. The paper does not adequately address the potential for the proposed method to be mitigated by adversarial training or other defense mechanisms. This is a critical aspect that needs to be explored to understand the robustness of the method in real-world scenarios.

### Suggestions

The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with their gradient regularization technique. Specifically, they should quantify the increase in training time and memory usage as the size of the VLM and the input data increase. This analysis should include a breakdown of the computational overhead introduced by the gradient regularization process, such as the time spent on calculating and applying the regularization terms. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the scalability of their method by conducting experiments on larger models and datasets. This would help to determine the practical limitations of the proposed approach and identify potential bottlenecks. For example, they could explore how the method performs with models that have significantly more parameters or with datasets that are orders of magnitude larger than those used in the current experiments. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's applicability in real-world scenarios.

To strengthen the claims of generalizability, the authors should expand their evaluation to include a more diverse set of VLMs and datasets. This should include models with different architectures, training objectives, and pre-training datasets. For example, they could evaluate their method on models that use different attention mechanisms or that are trained on different types of multimodal data. Additionally, they should consider using datasets that cover a wider range of visual and textual content. This would help to determine whether the method is effective across different domains and tasks. Furthermore, the authors should conduct a more thorough analysis of the method's performance under different attack scenarios, such as targeted and untargeted attacks, and different levels of perturbation. This would provide a more complete picture of the method's strengths and weaknesses.

Finally, the authors should investigate the potential for their method to be mitigated by adversarial training or other defense mechanisms. This could involve training VLMs using adversarial examples generated by their method and evaluating the performance of the resulting models. They should also explore other defense strategies, such as input sanitization or gradient masking, to determine whether these can effectively counter the proposed attack. This analysis should include a discussion of the limitations of these defense mechanisms and the potential for future attacks to circumvent them. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the robustness of the method and its implications for the security of VLMs.
