### Summary

This paper proposes AdvI2I, an adversarial attack targeting Image-to-Image (I2I) diffusion models. Unlike previous adversarial attacks that focus on text prompts, AdvI2I manipulates input images to induce the generation of NSFW content, bypassing existing defense mechanisms. The authors also introduce AdvI2I-Adaptive, an enhanced version that adapts to potential countermeasures and minimizes the resemblance between adversarial images and NSFW concept embeddings, making the attack more resilient. Through extensive experiments, the paper demonstrates that both AdvI2I and AdvI2I-Adaptive can effectively bypass current safeguards, highlighting the urgent need for stronger security measures to address the misuse of I2I diffusion models.

### Strengths

1. The paper introduces a novel approach by focusing on adversarial image attacks on I2I diffusion models, a relatively unexplored area compared to text-based adversarial attacks. 
2. The proposed AdvI2I framework and its adaptive version are technically sound, with clear mathematical formulations and a well-defined methodology.
3. The paper highlights the limitations of current defense mechanisms against adversarial image attacks, providing valuable insights for future research on robust defense strategies.

### Suggestions

The paper should broaden its exploration of adversarial image attacks beyond NSFW content generation. While the focus on NSFW content is relevant due to its societal impact, the technical framework of AdvI2I could be applied to other malicious scenarios. For example, the authors could investigate how adversarial images could be used to manipulate I2I models for creating misleading or deceptive content, such as altering the appearance of objects or scenes in a way that is not immediately obvious to human observers. This could include changing the identity of individuals in images, modifying the context of a scene, or introducing subtle distortions that lead to misinterpretations. Furthermore, the paper should explore the potential for using adversarial images to degrade the quality of generated images, which could be a useful tool for evaluating the robustness of I2I models. By demonstrating the versatility of AdvI2I across different types of attacks, the authors can highlight the broader security risks associated with I2I diffusion models.

To strengthen the paper's experimental validation, the authors should evaluate AdvI2I on a more diverse set of I2I diffusion models. The current evaluation is limited to two specific models, which may not be representative of the broader landscape of I2I diffusion models. Including evaluations on more recent and advanced models, such as those employing different architectures or training techniques, would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the attack's effectiveness and generalizability. Specifically, the authors should consider models that use different attention mechanisms, normalization techniques, or diffusion processes. This would help to determine whether the attack is robust across different model architectures or if it is highly tailored to the specific models used in the current evaluation. Furthermore, the authors should explore the impact of different training datasets on the attack's success rate. By evaluating the attack on models trained on diverse datasets, the authors can assess whether the attack is sensitive to the training data or if it can generalize to models trained on different types of images. This would provide a more robust assessment of the attack's practical implications.

Finally, the paper should include a more thorough discussion of potential defense mechanisms against AdvI2I-Adaptive. While the authors introduce an adaptive version of the attack, they do not provide a detailed analysis of how existing defense mechanisms might be adapted to counter this attack. The paper should explore potential countermeasures, such as adversarial training, input sanitization, or detection-based approaches. For example, the authors could investigate whether adversarial training with adversarial examples generated by AdvI2I-Adaptive can improve the robustness of I2I models. Additionally, the authors should explore the effectiveness of input sanitization techniques, such as adding noise or applying transformations to the input images, in mitigating the attack. Furthermore, the paper should discuss the limitations of the proposed attack and identify potential weaknesses that could be exploited by future defense mechanisms. By providing a more balanced perspective on the attack and its potential countermeasures, the authors can contribute to the development of more robust and secure I2I diffusion models.
