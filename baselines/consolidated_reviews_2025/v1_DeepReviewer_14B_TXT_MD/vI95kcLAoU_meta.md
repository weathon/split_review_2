## Summary

This paper introduces Skip-Attention, a novel method designed to enhance the computational efficiency of Vision Transformers (ViTs) by reusing self-attention computations from preceding layers. The core idea is based on the observation that the attention of the CLS token to spatial patches exhibits high correlation across different transformer blocks, suggesting redundancy in self-attention calculations. To exploit this, Skip-Attention approximates the self-attention operation in selected subsequent layers by reusing the output from a previous layer, thereby bypassing the computationally expensive self-attention calculation. To ensure that this approximation does not significantly degrade performance, the authors introduce a simple yet effective parametric function. This function, consisting of two linear layers, a depth-wise convolution (DwC), and an efficient channel attention module (ECA), is used to refine the reused attention output. The method is presented as a plug-in module, applicable to various ViT architectures and tasks. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Skip-Attention through extensive experiments across seven different tasks, including image classification, semantic segmentation, and image denoising. The results show that Skip-Attention achieves improved throughput at the same or higher accuracy levels compared to baseline ViT models and other state-of-the-art efficient ViT methods. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the correlation between attention maps, which serves as the motivation for the proposed method. The authors also conduct ablation studies to explore the impact of different configurations of skipping self-attention layers. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to reduce the computational cost of ViTs, making them more practical for resource-constrained environments. The method's simplicity and effectiveness, coupled with its broad applicability, make it a valuable contribution to the field of efficient deep learning. However, as I will detail in the weaknesses section, there are some limitations that need to be addressed to further strengthen the paper's claims and impact.

## Strengths

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the core idea of reusing self-attention computations based on the observed correlation across layers is both intuitive and effective. The authors have identified a key redundancy in ViT architectures and have proposed a method that directly addresses this issue. The use of a parametric function to approximate the self-attention output is a clever way to mitigate the potential performance degradation that could arise from simply skipping self-attention layers. The design of this parametric function, while relatively simple, is well-motivated and incorporates elements that are known to be effective in capturing cross-token relations, such as depth-wise convolution and channel attention. The paper's presentation is also commendable. The authors have clearly articulated the motivation behind their work, the details of their method, and the results of their experiments. The inclusion of a detailed correlation analysis between attention maps provides strong empirical support for their approach. Furthermore, the extensive experimental evaluation across a diverse set of tasks and datasets is a significant strength. The authors have not only compared their method against vanilla ViT but also against several state-of-the-art efficient ViT methods, demonstrating the competitiveness of Skip-Attention. The ablation studies, which explore different configurations of skipping self-attention layers, provide valuable insights into the behavior of the proposed method. Finally, the paper's claim that Skip-Attention is a plug-in module applicable to various ViT architectures is a significant advantage, making it a practical solution for a wide range of applications. The inclusion of experiments on a mobile device further highlights the practical relevance of the work. In summary, the paper presents a well-motivated, technically sound, and empirically validated method for improving the efficiency of ViTs, making it a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, while the paper acknowledges related work in NLP on attention map sharing, the novelty of the proposed method is somewhat limited. The core idea of reusing attention across layers has been explored in prior work, and the main difference in this paper lies in the specific parametric function used for approximation. While the authors introduce a parametric function consisting of linear layers, a depth-wise convolution, and a channel attention module, the design of this function is relatively straightforward. As the reviewers have pointed out, the novelty is primarily in the application of these components in this specific context, rather than in the components themselves. This does not diminish the value of the work, but it does suggest that the contribution is incremental rather than transformative. Second, while the paper claims that the parametric function captures cross-token relations, the initial description of the function might lead to a misunderstanding. Although the depth-wise convolution inherently captures spatial relationships between tokens, the paper could be more explicit in explaining this mechanism. The reviewers initially misunderstood the function as being independent for each token, which is incorrect, but this highlights the need for a clearer explanation of how the depth-wise convolution operates on the token embeddings. Third, although the paper includes comparisons with several state-of-the-art efficient ViT methods, the experimental evaluation could be further strengthened by including comparisons with a wider range of techniques. For example, the paper does not compare against methods that reduce computational cost by dropping redundant tokens, merging tokens, or using token sampling techniques. While the paper does compare against methods that achieve similar FLOPs reductions, a more comprehensive comparison would provide a more complete picture of the proposed method's strengths and weaknesses. Fourth, the paper could provide more details about the implementation of the proposed method. While the paper mentions that the parametric function is applied to layers 3 through 8 based on observed correlation, it does not provide a clear rationale for this specific choice beyond the correlation analysis. A more in-depth analysis of the impact of skipping different layers, or combinations of layers, on both performance and computational cost would be beneficial. For example, it would be useful to see results for skipping only one layer, skipping non-adjacent layers, or skipping layers based on some criteria such as the magnitude of attention weights or the similarity of attention maps. This analysis would help to understand the sensitivity of the method to the choice of skipped layers and provide guidance for applying the method to different architectures and tasks. Fifth, the paper could provide more comprehensive details about the experimental settings. While the paper refers to the settings of the baseline methods, explicitly stating the key hyperparameters used for each task would improve the reproducibility of the results. For example, the paper could provide more details about the learning rate, batch size, weight decay, and dropout rate used for each task. Furthermore, the paper could provide more information about the data augmentation techniques used during training. Sixth, the paper could provide more details about the computational resources used for the experiments. While the paper mentions that the experiments were conducted on GPUs, it does not specify the type or number of GPUs used. It is also important to specify the memory capacity of the GPUs and the time taken for training and inference. This information is crucial for understanding the computational cost of the proposed method and for comparing it with other methods. Seventh, while the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of Skip-Attention on several tasks, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of the method in tasks requiring high accuracy. The paper does not provide experimental evidence for tasks like object detection and instance segmentation, where precise localization and fine-grained feature extraction are crucial. The approximation introduced by Skip-Attention might lead to performance degradation in such tasks. Finally, while the paper includes an experiment on video denoising, it does not evaluate the method on more complex video understanding tasks that require learning long-range temporal dependencies. The paper should address the potential challenges of applying the method to such tasks. In summary, while the paper presents a promising approach, these weaknesses highlight areas where further investigation and clarification are needed.

## Suggestions

Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements to strengthen the paper. First, I suggest that the authors provide a more detailed discussion of the novelty of their method in the context of existing work on attention sharing. While the paper acknowledges related work in NLP, it should more clearly articulate the specific contributions of Skip-Attention, particularly in the vision domain. This could involve a more in-depth comparison of the proposed parametric function with similar techniques used in other methods. Second, I recommend that the authors provide a more explicit explanation of how the depth-wise convolution in the parametric function captures cross-token relations. The paper should clearly describe how the convolution operates on the token embeddings and how the kernel size affects the interactions between tokens. This would help to clarify any potential misunderstandings about the function's operation. Third, I suggest that the authors expand their experimental evaluation to include comparisons with a wider range of state-of-the-art efficient ViT methods. This should include methods that reduce computational cost by dropping redundant tokens, merging tokens, or using token sampling techniques. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the proposed method's strengths and weaknesses relative to existing techniques. Fourth, I recommend that the authors conduct a more detailed analysis of the impact of skipping different layers or combinations of layers. This should include experiments on skipping only one layer, skipping non-adjacent layers, or skipping layers based on some criteria such as the magnitude of attention weights or the similarity of attention maps. This analysis would help to understand the sensitivity of the method to the choice of skipped layers and provide guidance for applying the method to different architectures and tasks. Fifth, I suggest that the authors explore the possibility of dynamically determining which layers to skip based on the input data or the training progress. This could potentially lead to further improvements in efficiency without sacrificing performance. Sixth, I recommend that the authors provide more comprehensive details about the experimental settings, including the specific hyperparameter settings used for each task, the training procedure, the optimization algorithm, and the learning rate schedule. This would improve the reproducibility of the results and allow other researchers to build upon this work. Seventh, I suggest that the authors provide more details about the computational resources used for the experiments, including the type and number of GPUs used, the memory capacity of the GPUs, and the time taken for training and inference. This information is crucial for understanding the computational cost of the proposed method and for comparing it with other methods. Eighth, I recommend that the authors include experiments on tasks that require high accuracy, such as object detection and instance segmentation. This would help to quantify the potential performance degradation of Skip-Attention in such tasks. Finally, I suggest that the authors evaluate the method on more complex video understanding tasks that require learning long-range temporal dependencies. This would help to assess the applicability of Skip-Attention to such tasks and identify potential challenges. In summary, these suggestions aim to address the identified weaknesses and further strengthen the paper's claims and impact.

## Questions

Based on my analysis, I have several questions that I believe are crucial for a deeper understanding of the proposed method. First, I am curious about the specific criteria used to determine the optimal layers for applying the Skip-Attention mechanism. While the paper mentions correlation analysis, I would like to understand if there are other factors that influence this choice, and how these factors might vary across different ViT architectures or tasks. Second, I am interested in the sensitivity of the proposed method to the hyperparameters of the parametric function, such as the kernel size of the depth-wise convolution and the parameters of the channel attention module. I would like to know if there are specific settings that work best across different tasks and architectures, and if there are any guidelines for tuning these parameters. Third, I am curious about the potential limitations of the proposed method when applied to very deep transformer architectures. While the paper shows results on ViT-B/16, I would like to know if the performance gains saturate or degrade with increasing model depth, and if there are any modifications needed to apply Skip-Attention to extremely deep models. Fourth, I would like to understand the trade-offs between computational cost and performance when using Skip-Attention. While the paper shows improved throughput, I would like to know if there are specific scenarios where the performance gains are not worth the reduction in computational cost, and if there are any guidelines for determining the optimal balance between these two factors. Fifth, I am interested in the potential for further optimization of the parametric function. While the current design is effective, I would like to know if there are other architectures or techniques that could further improve its performance or reduce its computational cost. Finally, I am curious about the potential for applying the Skip-Attention mechanism to other types of attention mechanisms, such as multi-head attention or global attention. I would like to know if the core idea of reusing attention computations could be generalized to other attention mechanisms, and if there are any challenges in doing so. These questions aim to address key uncertainties and explore the potential for further development of the proposed method.
