## Summary

This paper introduces a novel regularization technique, termed Overflow-Aware Regularization (OAR), designed to mitigate numerical overflow issues when training Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for evaluation under Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). The core problem addressed is the inherent limitation of FHE schemes, where operations on encrypted data introduce noise that can accumulate and lead to inaccurate results, particularly in deep learning models like RNNs. The authors' approach is grounded in the observation that numerical overflow, a significant source of error in FHE computations, can be managed by strategically regularizing the activation values during training. OAR penalizes pre-activation values that fall into regions where overflow would cause significant deviations in the encrypted computation, effectively guiding the model to learn parameters that are more robust to the noise introduced by FHE. The method is implemented within a quantization-aware training (QAT) framework, where model parameters and activations are quantized to ternary and binary representations, respectively, to further reduce the computational overhead of FHE. The authors evaluate their approach using a multi-layer RNN on the MNIST dataset, demonstrating that OAR achieves high accuracy with a minimal decrease compared to unencrypted training, while also significantly reducing the latency of FHE-based evaluation. The experimental results show that the proposed method achieves a 274x decrease in latency compared to previous FHE-based RNN evaluation methods, while maintaining over 90% accuracy, with only an 8% drop from the 99% accuracy of full-precision plaintext MNIST evaluation. The paper leverages the Concrete library, which provides GPU support for FHE, to accelerate the evaluation process. The authors also demonstrate the effectiveness of OAR on larger RNNs and longer sequences, showcasing the potential of their approach for more complex applications. Overall, this work presents a significant contribution to the field of privacy-preserving machine learning by addressing a critical challenge in FHE-based RNN evaluation and demonstrating a practical approach for achieving high accuracy and efficiency.
