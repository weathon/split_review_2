## Summary

This paper introduces a novel quantization-aware training (QAT) method, termed RAOQ, specifically designed to mitigate the accuracy degradation caused by analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) quantization in In-Memory Computing (IMC) systems. The core contribution of this work lies in its multi-faceted approach to addressing ADC quantization, which is a critical challenge for the practical implementation of IMC. The authors propose a two-stage training process. In the first stage, they employ a weight reshaping (W-reshape) technique, which involves the application of a kurtosis loss to encourage a weight distribution with larger variance, and an activation shifting (A-shift) method, which aims to maximize the second moment of the activation distribution by shifting it away from zero. These techniques are designed to maximize the signal-to-quantization-noise ratio (SQNR) of the ADC input. In the second stage, the authors introduce a bit augmentation (BitAug) method, which involves training the model with different ADC bit precisions to improve the optimization process. The authors evaluate their proposed RAOQ method on a variety of tasks, including image classification, object detection, and natural language processing, using various neural network models and ADC bit precisions. The empirical findings demonstrate that RAOQ consistently achieves high accuracy across these tasks, even with low-precision ADCs, outperforming conventional QAT methods. The authors also present an analysis of the energy efficiency of IMC systems with and without RAOQ, demonstrating the potential for energy savings. Overall, this paper makes a significant contribution to the field of IMC by addressing a key challenge in its practical implementation and demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method through extensive experiments. However, as I will detail in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened to further enhance its impact and practical relevance.
