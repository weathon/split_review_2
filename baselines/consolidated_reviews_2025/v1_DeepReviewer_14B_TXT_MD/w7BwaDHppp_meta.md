## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to rendering unbounded scenes using neural radiance fields (NeRFs). The core contribution lies in a geometry-aware projective mapping function based on the p-norm distance, coupled with a new ray parameterization technique. The authors argue that existing mapping functions, such as the inverted sphere and contract mappings, are insufficient for handling the complexities of unbounded scenes, particularly when cameras are positioned far from the scene origin. To address this, they propose a p-norm-based mapping that adaptively transforms unbounded scene coordinates into a bounded space, aiming to allocate ray samples more effectively based on scene geometry. This mapping is combined with a new ray parameterization that uses the p-norm distance to determine sample locations along rays, which is intended to improve the distribution of samples in unbounded regions. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method by integrating it into several existing NeRF frameworks, including DVGO, TensoRF, iNGP, and NeRF. They present experimental results on challenging datasets like Tanks and Temples, mip-NeRF 360, and Free Dataset, showing that their approach achieves state-of-the-art view synthesis results, particularly in scenarios where the camera is positioned further from the scene origin. The paper's key methodological approach involves a detailed analysis of existing mapping functions using stereographic projection, which provides a theoretical basis for the proposed p-norm mapping. The authors also use a RANSAC-based approach to estimate the optimal 'p' value for the p-norm mapping, which is crucial for adapting the mapping to different scene geometries. The empirical findings generally support the claim that the proposed method improves the rendering quality of unbounded scenes, especially in challenging camera configurations. However, the paper also acknowledges that the performance gains are not always substantial and that there are instances where baseline methods perform slightly better. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of neural rendering by introducing a novel and effective approach for handling unbounded scenes, although some limitations and areas for improvement remain.
