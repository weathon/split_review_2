## Summary

This paper introduces 3DTrajMaster, a novel framework designed to enable precise control over multi-entity 3D motion in video generation. Unlike previous methods that primarily rely on 2D control signals, 3DTrajMaster utilizes 6DoF pose sequences to manipulate object motions in 3D space. The core of the approach is a plug-and-play 3D-motion grounded object injector that fuses multiple input entities with their respective 3D trajectories through a gated self-attention mechanism. This injector is designed to maintain the video diffusion prior, which is crucial for generalization ability. To address the lack of suitable training data, the authors constructed a 360-Motion Dataset using Unreal Engine, incorporating advanced rendering techniques and GPT-generated trajectories. This dataset includes diverse 3D human and animal assets, capturing their motion with 12 evenly-surrounded cameras on various 3D platforms. The method also employs a domain adaptor during training and an annealed sampling strategy during inference to mitigate video quality degradation and enhance overall performance. The domain adaptor is trained using LoRA modules to align the generated videos with the base text-to-video model. The annealed sampling strategy gradually reduces the influence of the input trajectories during the denoising process, allowing the model to generate more natural and realistic videos. The paper presents extensive experimental results, including both quantitative and qualitative evaluations, which demonstrate that 3DTrajMaster achieves state-of-the-art performance in both accuracy and generalization for controlling multi-entity 3D motions. The authors evaluate their method using metrics such as FVD, FID, and CLIP Similarity, and also conduct a user study to assess the visual quality of the generated videos. The results show that 3DTrajMaster outperforms existing methods in terms of motion accuracy and video quality. The authors also demonstrate the method's ability to handle complex scenes with multiple interacting entities, although they acknowledge limitations in handling more than three entities. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of video generation by introducing a method for precise 3D motion control, addressing a critical gap in existing techniques that primarily rely on 2D control signals. The construction of the 360-Motion Dataset and the introduction of the 3D-motion grounded object injector are key contributions that enable the method's effectiveness. The paper's findings suggest that 3DTrajMaster has the potential to enable a wide range of applications, including virtual cinematography, gaming, and robotics.
