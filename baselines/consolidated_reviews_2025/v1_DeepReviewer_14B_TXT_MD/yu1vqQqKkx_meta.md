## Summary

This paper introduces LICO (Large Language Models for In-Context Optimization), a novel approach that leverages the power of Large Language Models (LLMs) for black-box optimization, with a specific focus on molecular property optimization. The core idea behind LICO is to extend pretrained LLMs with additional embedding layers for molecular representations and prediction heads for estimating molecular properties. This allows the LLM to perform in-context learning directly on molecular data, bypassing the need for verbose textual descriptions. The model is trained using a semi-synthetic approach, combining intrinsic molecular properties with synthetically generated functions derived from Gaussian Processes (GPs) using a Tanimoto kernel. This training strategy aims to enable LICO to generalize to unseen molecular properties through in-context prompting. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of LICO on the Practical Molecular Optimization (PMO) benchmark, where it achieves state-of-the-art performance across 21 optimization objectives. The empirical results show that LICO outperforms several established baselines, including Gaussian Process-based Bayesian Optimization (GP BO), Graph GA, and REINVENT. The paper also includes ablation studies that highlight the importance of language instructions and the semi-synthetic training approach for LICO's performance. The authors further investigate the impact of different LLM sizes on performance, showing that larger models generally lead to better optimization results. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach for applying LLMs to scientific optimization tasks, demonstrating the potential of LLMs to go beyond natural language processing and contribute to areas like molecular design. The key innovation lies in the specific architecture and training methodology that allows the LLM to effectively handle non-textual data and perform in-context optimization. The use of semi-synthetic data for training is also a significant contribution, enabling the model to generalize to unseen molecular properties. The empirical results on the PMO benchmark provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach, showcasing its potential for advancing molecular optimization.

The paper should include a more in-depth analysis of how the LLM's pre-trained knowledge is leveraged in the context of molecular optimization.
