### Summary

This paper presents a benchmark for uncertainty quantification methods in molecular representation models. The authors evaluate various uncertainty quantification methods across different backbone models and molecular descriptors, providing insights into their effectiveness for property prediction and uncertainty estimation. The study highlights the importance of selecting appropriate uncertainty quantification methods for reliable predictions in materials science and drug discovery.

### Strengths

1. The paper addresses an important and practical problem in molecular representation learning, which is crucial for applications in materials science and drug discovery.
2. The authors evaluate a diverse set of uncertainty quantification methods, including deterministic prediction, Bayesian neural networks, post-hoc calibration, and ensembles.
3. The study provides valuable insights into the performance of different uncertainty quantification methods across various molecular property prediction tasks.

### Weaknesses

#### Some Related Works


#### comment

1. The authors use scaffold splitting to create training and test sets, which may not fully capture the challenges of real-world scenarios where the test data distribution can differ significantly from the training data. Specifically, scaffold splitting, while ensuring novelty, does not address the issue of covariate shift, where the underlying data distribution changes, leading to potential issues with model generalization. The authors should consider evaluating their models on datasets with more pronounced distribution shifts, such as those generated by chemical space exploration techniques or those derived from different experimental conditions.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost associated with each uncertainty quantification method. This information is crucial for practitioners who need to balance performance with computational resources. The analysis should include not only the total training time but also the inference time, memory usage, and the number of parameters for each method. This is particularly important for Bayesian methods and deep ensembles, which are known to be computationally expensive.
3. The study could benefit from a more in-depth discussion of the limitations of the evaluated uncertainty quantification methods and potential directions for future research. For example, the authors could discuss the sensitivity of each method to hyperparameter tuning, the potential for overconfidence in uncertainty estimates, and the challenges of applying these methods to very large molecular datasets. Furthermore, the authors should explore the potential for combining different uncertainty quantification methods to achieve more robust and reliable predictions.

### Suggestions

The authors should consider incorporating more rigorous evaluation protocols that explicitly address the issue of distribution shift. This could involve using datasets with known chemical space gaps or generating synthetic datasets with controlled distribution shifts. For example, they could use techniques like fragment-based drug design to create molecules with varying degrees of similarity to the training set, allowing for a more systematic evaluation of the model's ability to generalize to unseen regions of chemical space. Additionally, the authors should explore the use of domain adaptation techniques to improve the model's performance on out-of-distribution data. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the model's robustness and its applicability to real-world scenarios where the test data may not closely resemble the training data.

To address the lack of computational cost analysis, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the computational resources required for each uncertainty quantification method. This should include the training time, inference time, memory usage, and the number of parameters for each method. The authors should also consider the scalability of each method to larger datasets and more complex molecular representations. For example, they could evaluate the performance of each method on datasets of varying sizes and report the computational cost as a function of the dataset size. This would provide valuable insights into the practical applicability of each method and help practitioners make informed decisions about which method to use based on their available computational resources. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the potential for optimizing the computational cost of each method, such as using techniques like model compression or knowledge distillation.

Finally, the authors should provide a more in-depth discussion of the limitations of the evaluated uncertainty quantification methods and potential directions for future research. This should include a discussion of the sensitivity of each method to hyperparameter tuning, the potential for overconfidence in uncertainty estimates, and the challenges of applying these methods to very large molecular datasets. The authors should also explore the potential for combining different uncertainty quantification methods to achieve more robust and reliable predictions. For example, they could investigate the use of ensemble methods that combine the predictions of multiple uncertainty quantification methods or explore the use of meta-learning techniques to learn how to best combine different uncertainty estimates. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of each method and help guide future research in this area.

### Questions

1. How do the evaluated uncertainty quantification methods perform on out-of-distribution data, which is a common challenge in real-world applications?
2. What are the computational costs associated with each uncertainty quantification method, and how do they scale with the size of the molecular dataset?
3. How do the authors plan to address the limitations of the current study and extend their benchmark to include more recent advances in molecular representation learning and uncertainty quantification?
