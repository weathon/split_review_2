## Summary
This paper presents an empirical study of encoder and decoder architectural choices in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). The authors systematically vary dense (DNN) and convolutional (CNN) configurations for both encoder and decoder across four latent dimensions (25, 50, 100, 200), training models on the MNIST dataset. The central finding is an architectural asymmetry: small, shallow dense networks (DNN-1) perform best as encoders, while deeper convolutional networks (CNN-4/CNN-5) perform best as decoders. The paper also reports that non-zero KL divergence correlates with better reconstruction quality, and that moderate latent compression retains class separability while aggressive compression degrades representation quality.

The study addresses a relevant question — architecture selection for VAEs is often heuristic, and systematic evidence is indeed scarce. However, the work has substantial limitations that reduce its immediate impact: (a) experiments are conducted on MNIST only, making generalization to larger-scale natural image datasets unverified; (b) training details and architecture specifications are critically underspecified, preventing full reproducibility; (c) the analysis relies on qualitative visual evaluation and unquantified claims ("weak correlation," "negative trend," "nearly half collapsed") rather than statistical testing; (d) the "top 25%" selection approach introduces potential base-rate bias; (e) key claims in the Conclusion (MLP compression failure, non-interference of powerful encoders) lack direct experimental support in the Results section; and (f) a citation error (Luhman & Luhman, 2021, cited for posterior assumptions, is actually about sampling speed) undermines literature accuracy. The paper would benefit from a second dataset (e.g., Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10), rigorous statistical analysis, complete architecture/hyperparameter disclosure, and a more measured contribution framing.

## Strengths
1. **Timely and practical research question.** The impact of encoder/decoder architecture on VAE performance is genuinely underexplored relative to the volume of work on loss functions and priors. The paper identifies a real gap in the literature and addresses it directly.

2. **Systematic experimental design.** Varying both encoder and decoder architecture independently across multiple latent dimensions (25, 50, 100, 200) with 25+ architectural combinations is a reasonable coverage of the design space. The factorial-like design enables some separation of the effects of encoder vs. decoder choices.

3. **Clear, reproducible findings with practical value.** The central asymmetry result — simple dense encoders outperform deeper alternatives, while convolutional depth helps decoders — is actionable for practitioners. If validated on more datasets, this could influence default architecture choices for VAE-based systems.

4. **Transparent failure analysis.** The paper explicitly identifies "collapsed latent spaces" (near-zero KLD) as a common failure mode affecting nearly half the models, and shows that non-zero KLD is associated with better reconstruction. This negative-result reporting is more informative than only presenting top-performing configurations.

5. **Decoupled loss analysis.** Separately analyzing reconstruction loss and KL divergence provides clearer insight into the reconstruction-regularization tradeoff than a single aggregated ELBO metric would.

6. **Readable presentation.** The paper is clearly written at the paragraph level, with well-structured figures and consistent labeling conventions that make the main results easy to follow despite the large number of model configurations.

## Weaknesses
### W1. Single-dataset evaluation severely limits external validity [Severity: Major]
All experiments are conducted exclusively on MNIST (28×28 grayscale digits). While MNIST is a standard benchmark, VAE architectural conclusions may not transfer to higher-resolution RGB images (e.g., CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet) where spatial structure is more complex and convolutional inductive bias matters differently. The paper's title makes a general claim ("When Encoders Should Stay Simple") that implies broader applicability, but the evidence only supports MNIST-specific conclusions. **Fix:** Add at least one additional dataset (e.g., Fashion-MNIST for grayscale, or subsampled CIFAR-10 for color) with the same architecture sweep to test generalizability.

### W2. Results lack statistical rigor and quantitative thresholds [Severity: Major]
The paper repeatedly relies on unquantified language: "nearly half of the experiments result in collapsed latent spaces" (no collapse threshold defined), "weak correlation" (no correlation coefficient or p-value), "negative trend" (no regression or trend test), "top 25%" (post-hoc selection without base-rate correction), and "visual evaluation revealed." For an empirical paper whose core contribution is experimental evidence, this level of qualitative reporting is insufficient. **Fix:** (a) Define collapse as KLD < 0.01 nats, report exact proportions. (b) Report Spearman/Pearson correlations with confidence intervals. (c) Use a mixed-effects model (architecture type × latent size → reconstruction loss) instead of top-quartile selection. (d) Add statistical significance tests (e.g., ANOVA, Mann-Whitney U) for all comparative claims.

### W3. Method section critically underspecified — experiments not reproducible [Severity: Major]
The Method section omits: batch size, learning rate, optimizer type, number of epochs, convergence criteria, train/validation/test split, random seeds, and number of independent runs per configuration. Architecture definitions lack hidden dimensions for DNNs (what size are the layers in DNN-1, DNN-4, DNN-16?), filter counts and padding strategy for CNNs, and output-padding details for transposed convolutions. Without these details, no other researcher can reproduce the experiments, undermining the paper's value as an empirical reference. **Fix:** Add a comprehensive hyperparameter table and architecture specification table in the main text or appendix, and report all models with 3+ seeds.

### W4. Citation error and incorrect referencing [Severity: Major]
The VAE limitation paragraph ("Simplistic Posterior Assumptions") cites Luhman & Luhman (2021), which is about knowledge distillation for sampling speed, not about posterior expressivity. This is a factual citation mismatch that misleads readers about the literature. Additionally, the VAE paper is cited as Kingma & Welling (2022) — the arXiv resubmission date — whereas the original publication was at ICLR 2014. **Fix:** Replace the Luhman citation with appropriate references (e.g., Kingma et al., 2016 for normalizing flows; Rezende & Mohamed, 2015 for flow-based posteriors). Correct the VAE citation year to 2014.

### W5. Top-25% analysis suffers from base-rate selection bias [Severity: Major]
The paper analyzes "top 25% of models" by reconstruction loss, then counts how many of each architecture type appear in this set. If architecture types are trained at different rates (e.g., more CNN variants than DNN variants), raw counts are misleading. The paper does not report how many models of each type were trained, nor does it divide by the base rate. **Fix:** Report selection-rate-adjusted proportions (e.g., "DNN-1 encoders comprised 55% of the top quartile but only 30% of all trained encoders, giving a selection ratio of 1.83×") and report global main effects via regression, not only top-quartile membership.

### W6. Conclusion contains unsupported claims [Severity: Major]
Two claims in the Conclusion lack experimental support in the Results section: (a) "powerful CNNs did not negatively impact encoding performance, suggesting that the encoder's capacity does not interfere with the decoder's ability to reconstruct data" — this interaction was never directly tested (no experiment where encoder complexity is varied while decoder is fixed, and vice versa, with statistical interaction test). (b) "data compression proved challenging for MLPs, which struggled to effectively handle compact latent representations" — the Results section has no experiment or figure comparing MLP vs CNN on compression tasks. **Fix:** Either add supporting experiments or remove these claims from the Conclusion. If retained, rephrase as hypotheses requiring future verification.

### W7. Theoretical framing and prior work connections are weak [Severity: Minor]
The introduction correctly notes that VAE blurriness is often attributed to posterior mismatch, but then the paper does not connect its architectural findings back to this question. Does a simple encoder with a deep decoder reduce the posterior mismatch? This could be tested by measuring the Wasserstein distance between aggregated posterior and prior. The DGSN and NVAE discussions are informative but lack a clear theoretical prediction that the paper then tests. **Fix:** Add one targeted analysis that quantifies posterior quality (e.g., MMD, Wasserstein distance) across architectures, connecting back to the posterior-mismatch motivation.

### W8. Paper structure — excessive background, insufficient experimental detail [Severity: Minor]
Approximately 1.5 pages (Section 2 + part of Section 3) are dedicated to textbook-level VAE derivation and tangentially related methods (DGSN). For an 8-page empirical paper, this space could be better used for complete method specification, additional experiments, or a limitations section. The Background could be condensed to 3-4 sentences with a reference to Kingma & Welling (2014). **Fix:** Cut half of Section 2, expand Method with a reproducibility table, and add an explicit Limitations subsection before the Conclusion.

### W9. Missing quantitative latent-space evaluation [Severity: Minor]
The latent space analysis (Section 4.3, Figures 6-7) relies on 2D PCA scatter plots and visual inspection. No quantitative separability or quality metric (silhouette score, mutual information, classification accuracy on latent codes, MMD between aggregated posterior and prior) is reported. **Fix:** Add quantitative metrics for each model's latent space: (a) kNN classification accuracy on latent codes, (b) MMD to standard Gaussian prior, (c) ELBO decomposition over a validation set.

### W10. Figure 2 design is misleading [Severity: Minor]
Figure 2 uses a dual-y-axis plot with reconstruction loss (left axis, range ~0-0.0002) and KLD (right axis, log scale, range e^{-22} to e^{-18}). The shaded areas for KLD appear "flat" because of the log-scale compression, but the narrative claims "reconstructive loss increases as model complexity increases, while the generative inference loss remains relatively stable." The stability of KLD should be evaluated on a linear scale or by reporting actual values. The log scale visually attenuates variation. **Fix:** Replot Figure 2 with both losses on comparable scales or with two separate subplots; report mean±std for both losses across model groups.

### W11. No comparison to established VAE variants [Severity: Minor]
The paper motivates the study by noting that most VAE improvements come from loss/prior modifications (β-VAE, VampPrior, etc.), but then does not compare its best architecture against any of these baselines. It would strengthen the contribution to show that architectural optimization (simple encoder, deep CNN decoder) can match or complement these methods. **Fix:** Add a comparison experiment where the best architecture found (e.g., DNN1 encoder + CNN4 decoder) is combined with a standard baseline modification (e.g., β = 2 or VampPrior) to show additive benefit.

## Score
**Final Score: 4/10**

**Rationale:** The paper addresses a genuine and underexplored question — how encoder/decoder architecture affects VAE performance — and the central finding (asymmetric optimal architectures: simple encoder, deep convolutional decoder) is potentially valuable. However, the score is limited by several critical weaknesses that reduce the paper's immediate scientific contribution:

- **Limited external validity (single dataset, MNIST only):** The main claim is presented as general ("When Encoders Should Stay Simple"), but the evidence is entirely MNIST-bound. Until validated on additional datasets, the contribution is preliminary.
- **Lack of statistical rigor:** Core results rely on unquantified language ("weak correlation," "nearly half," "visual evaluation") rather than hypothesis tests, effect sizes, or confidence intervals. For an empirical paper, this is a significant gap that undermines the reliability of conclusions.
- **Irreproducible methodology:** The Method section omits critical training details (optimizer, learning rate, batch size, epochs, seeds, hidden dimensions, filter counts), making it impossible for other researchers to replicate the experiments.
- **Unsupported claims in Conclusion:** Two claims lack direct experimental support — the "MLP compression failure" appears only in the Conclusion, and the "non-interference of powerful encoders" claim was never tested via interaction analysis.
- **Citation error:** Luhman & Luhman (2021) is cited for posterior expressivity but is actually about sampling speed — a factual error that reduces confidence in literature coverage.

The paper has a clear structure, readable writing, and the right research direction. With substantial revision (additional datasets, statistical analysis, complete method specification, correction of unsupported claims, and addition of limitations), the score could improve to 6-7/10. In its current form, the evidence base is too narrow and too qualitatively reported to serve as a reliable reference for VAE architecture design.
