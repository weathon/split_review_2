## Summary

This paper presents InfoTok, an adaptive discrete video tokenization framework grounded in Shannon's information theory. The key idea is to replace fixed-rate compression (used by most existing tokenizers) with an adaptive mechanism that allocates tokens proportional to each video's information complexity. InfoTok consists of two novel components: (1) an ELBO-based router that estimates the optimal token length for a given video by using the evidence lower bound as a surrogate for the intractable log-likelihood, and (2) a transformer-based adaptive compressor that discards tokens with the lowest information content according to per-token ELBO values.

The paper makes three main contributions: (C1) a theoretical proof that fixed-rate and data-agnostic adaptive tokenizers are suboptimal in expected token length, grounded in the Shannon Source Coding Theorem; (C2) the InfoTok framework that achieves near-optimal compression by bridging token length selection to ELBO estimation; and (C3) empirical results on TokenBench and DAVIS showing that InfoTok saves 20% tokens at matching reconstruction quality versus Cosmos-DV, achieves 2.3× higher compression than ElasticTok, and requires only 1 forward pass for length selection versus ElasticTok's 11.

The paper is generally well-written, with strong theoretical motivation and comprehensive experiments. Key strengths include the principled information-theoretic framing, the clean ELBO-based router design, and consistent empirical gains. However, several issues require attention: a logical contradiction in the binary mask definition (Section 3.2), the theoretical optimality bound depends on unquantified slack (Theorem 3.1), the lossless compression framing does not fully address the lossy nature of video tokenization, and baseline comparability is weakened by dataset preprocessing choices. Novelty assessment is deferred due to external literature retrieval being unavailable in this review run.

## Strengths
**1. Principled Theoretical Foundation.** The paper grounds adaptive tokenization in Shannon's Source Coding Theorem, providing a rigorous motivation for why fixed-rate compression is suboptimal for video data with varying information density. Theorem 2.1's restatement of the source coding theorem and Theorem 2.2's proof that uniform routers can lead to arbitrarily suboptimal expected token lengths (k × H_C(D) for any k > 1) provide a strong theoretical basis for the work. The four-data example concretely illustrates this bias.

**2. Clean ELBO-Based Router Design.** The use of ELBO as a surrogate for the intractable log-likelihood is well-motivated and elegantly connects the theoretical optimality condition (N_x ∝ -log p(x)) to a practical, computable quantity. The normalization by expected ELBO and the β scaling factor provide a simple mechanism to control the average compression rate. The InfoTok-Flex variant that ensembles multiple β values during training is a practical contribution that simplifies deployment across different compression regimes.

**3. Strong and Consistent Empirical Results.** The experimental evaluation is thorough and the results are convincing. InfoTok consistently outperforms ElasticTok across two datasets and multiple compression levels, with FVD reductions of 40-60% and PSNR improvements of 1.0-2.0 dB at matched BPP_16. The 11× reduction in inference NFEs (1 pass vs ElasticTok's 11) is a substantial practical advantage. The ablation studies (Table 3) isolate the contributions of both the ELBO-based router and the likelihood-based compressor, showing that each component improves over heuristic alternatives.

**4. Architecture-Agnostic Framework.** By building InfoTok on top of existing fixed-length tokenizers (implemented with Cosmos-DV but also tested with ViT), the paper demonstrates that the adaptive mechanism generalizes across backbone architectures. This modularity is valuable for future work where improved fixed-rate tokenizers can directly benefit from the InfoTok adaptive wrapper.

**5. Honest Limitation Disclosure.** The paper candidly acknowledges that downstream video generation experiments are beyond scope due to computational constraints, and that the ELBO router adds computational overhead. This transparency improves the manuscript's credibility.

## Weaknesses
**W1 (Major). Logical Contradiction in Binary Mask Definition (Section 3.2).** The adaptive compressor description contains an internal contradiction. The text states: "the adaptive compressor computes a binary mask m ∈ {0,1}^N where N_x tokens with the lowest ELBO values are 1 and the remaining are 0." However, the preceding sentence says "we preserve the top N_x tokens according to their per-token log-likelihood." If the goal is to preserve the top N_x tokens (highest ELBO), the mask should be 1 for the highest ELBO values. As written, a reader cannot determine whether m=1 indicates "keep" or "discard," and whether the mask targets high-ELBO or low-ELBO tokens. This is a reproducibility-critical error that must be corrected. *Suggested fix:* Rewrite to "m_i = 1 for the N_x tokens with the highest per-token ELBO values (preserved), and m_i = 0 otherwise (discarded)."

**W2 (Major). Theoretical Optimality Bound Depends on Unquantified Quantities (Section 3.1).** Theorem 3.1 provides a bound on expected token length: E[N_x] ≤ H_C(D) + β - E[-log p(x)]. The slack term involves E[-log p(x)] (the true data entropy), which is unknown and never estimated in the paper. The bound is expressed relative to a quantity that cannot be computed or verified. While the paper argues that ELBO is a tight approximation for large neural tokenizers, this is asserted without empirical verification. The practical gap between the theoretical guarantee and the actual performance should be explicitly characterized. *Suggested fix:* (a) Add an empirical estimate of ELBO tightness on validation data; (b) Rephrase "optimal up to approximation error" to "optimal under the assumption that ELBO ≈ log p(x), which is approximately satisfied for well-trained tokenizers"; (c) Acknowledge that global loss minimization is never achieved in practice, making the bound a qualitative motivation rather than a quantitative guarantee.

**W3 (Major). Lossless Compression Framing Conflicts with Lossy Tokenization Practice (Section 2.2).** Theorem 2.1 assumes perfect (lossless) reconstruction. However, video tokenizers are inherently lossy — they minimize MSE between original and reconstruction, not achieve perfect reconstruction. The paper uses this theorem to claim fixed-length tokenizers are suboptimal, but does not discuss Shannon's rate-distortion theory, which provides the appropriate lossy compression framework. The gap between log_C(|D|) and H_C(D) for lossless coding may be very different from the gap under a distortion constraint. This weakens the theoretical foundation. *Suggested fix:* After Theorem 2.1, explicitly acknowledge the lossy nature of tokenization and discuss how rate-distortion theory modifies the bound. Argue qualitatively that content-adaptive coding still outperforms fixed-rate coding under distortion constraints, using the same information-theoretic reasoning.

**W4 (Major). Baseline Comparability Weakened by Dataset Preprocessing (Section 4.1).** The evaluation uses a processed subset of TokenBench and DAVIS (256px partition, random square crops) that differs from the original evaluation protocols of the baselines. The paper honestly acknowledges this creates results "not directly comparable with their paper," but the tension with the claim of "fair comparisons" is unresolved. If ElasticTok's architecture constraint forces a specific preprocessing pipeline, it is unclear whether other baselines (Open-MAGVIT2, OmniTokenizer, Cosmos-DV) are evaluated under the same preprocessed conditions or their original protocols. The potential for preprocessing choices to differentially affect methods (e.g., random cropping affecting spatial-structure-sensitive methods) is not discussed. *Suggested fix:* (a) Clarify whether all methods see exactly the same preprocessed video frames; (b) Report results on the original dataset format without cropping as a sensitivity check; (c) Add ablation with multiple random crop seeds to show performance ordering stability.

**W5 (Major). Missing Variance and Statistical Significance (Tables 1-3).** All experimental results are reported as point estimates without variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Given that InfoTok-Flex and InfoTok have very close performance at some settings (e.g., BPP_16=0.56 on TokenBench: 29.30 vs 29.27 PSNR), and improvements over ElasticTok are about 1-2 dB, it is impossible to assess whether these differences are statistically significant. Without variance estimates, the ranking between methods could be driven by random seed variation rather than genuine superiority. *Suggested fix:* Report mean and standard deviation over at least 3 random seeds for all table entries. Include a paired significance test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank) for the primary comparison (InfoTok vs ElasticTok).

**W6 (Moderate). Binary Mask Overhead and Double Inference Cost Understated (Section 6).** The Limitations section omits two practical constraints. First, the binary mask consumes approximately 1/16 ≈ 6.25% of the token budget regardless of video complexity, reducing the maximum effective compression ratio. Second, the ELBO estimation requires a full encode-decode pass through the base tokenizer, effectively doubling inference computation compared to standard fixed-rate tokenizers (though this is still far more efficient than ElasticTok's 11× overhead). *Suggested fix:* Add these two points to the limitations paragraph.

**W7 (Minor). Abstract Claim Inconsistency (Abstract vs Introduction).** The Abstract claims "saving 20% tokens without influence on performance" while the Introduction claims "save approximately 50% tokens without loss of reconstruction quality." These refer to different baselines (20% vs Cosmos-DV at equal quality; 50% possibly aggregated across comparisons), but the Abstract does not clarify this. Unify the claims with explicit scope qualifiers.

**W8 (Minor). The paper reports "state-of-the-art compression performance" without scoping to specific benchmarks. Since external literature verification is unavailable in this run, I recommend bounding SOTA claims to the evaluated datasets and baselines.**

**W9 (Minor). The claim that InfoTok "ensures each token represents a similar amount of information" (Page 2) is an appealing intuition but is not directly measured. The paper evaluates aggregate reconstruction quality, not per-token information uniformity. Consider qualifying as a design principle rather than a verified property.**

**W10 (Minor). The Related Work section on Adaptive Representation is organized as a chronological list rather than structured around comparison axes (router design, token type, inference procedure, masking strategy). Restructuring around these axes would strengthen the positioning of InfoTok's contributions.**

### Note on Novelty (deferred)
Due to external literature retrieval being unavailable in this review run (Retrieval-Disabled Mode), I cannot verify novelty claims against prior work beyond what is cited in the manuscript. The paper's claim that existing methods use "biased" objective functions (Theorem 2.2) appears theoretically sound based on the manuscript's own reasoning, but a comprehensive novelty assessment requires targeted literature verification against the most relevant adaptive tokenization methods (ElasticTok, ALIT, One-D-Piece, FlexTok, CAT). I recommend the authors explicitly clarify in the paper the boundary between their contributions and ElasticTok's, since ElasticTok is the most directly comparable prior method. Novelty conclusions in the final report are marked as deferred.

## Score
The paper presents a well-motivated adaptive video tokenization framework grounded in information theory, with consistent empirical gains over fixed-rate and prior adaptive methods. The ELBO-based router design is clean and the empirical evaluation is extensive. However, several issues temper the score: the theoretical optimality bound contains unquantified slack (W2), the lossless compression framing does not fully address the lossy nature of tokenization (W3), a logical contradiction in the mask definition must be resolved (W1), baseline comparability requires clarification (W4), and statistical significance is absent (W5). These issues are fixable and do not invalidate the core contribution, but they reduce the confidence in the paper's claims as currently written. Novelty assessment is deferred due to external literature retrieval being unavailable in this review run.

**Final Score: 7/10**

*Score rationale:* The paper combines theoretical motivation, clean methodology, and strong empirical results on reconstruction benchmarks. The primary scoring dimensions are research value (positive — the information-theoretic framing is valuable and the adaptive architecture is practically useful) and novelty (promising but deferred verification). The main deductions are due to the unresolved theoretical gap (W2, W3) and the reproducibility-blocking mask definition error (W1), both of which are fixable with targeted revisions. The score reflects a solid paper that requires moderate revisions before reaching full rigor.
