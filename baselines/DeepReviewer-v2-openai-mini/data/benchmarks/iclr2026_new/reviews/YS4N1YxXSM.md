# Final Review Report

## Summary

This paper presents QUOKA, a training-free sparse attention method designed for accelerating the prefill stage of LLM inference under chunked prefill. QUOKA operates by (1) selecting a small set of representative queries via cosine dissimilarity from the mean query, (2) scoring keys via cosine similarity with these selected queries, and (3) aggregating scores via max-over-queries and mean-over-GQA-heads to subselect the most relevant KV pairs before feeding them into a dense attention kernel. The method relies entirely on standard linear algebra operations, making it hardware-agnostic and compatible with existing optimized kernels like FlashAttention.

The paper provides empirical evaluation across four benchmarks (Needle-in-a-Haystack, RULER, LongBench, Math500) on multiple model families (Llama3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen3-4B, SmollM3, GPT-OSS-20B), reporting near-baseline accuracy (within 1-3% of dense attention) with up to 5x attention speedup and 3x time-to-first-token reduction on Nvidia A100 GPUs, and up to 7x speedup on CPUs.

**Key strengths:** the core geometric insight (low cosine-similarity queries interact with more keys) is intuitive and well-motivated; the method is genuinely hardware-agnostic and training-free; the experimental coverage across multiple models and benchmarks is thorough; and the latency speedups are substantial and demonstrated on multiple hardware platforms.

**Key weaknesses:** (1) the theoretical justification (Theorem 1) contains notation ambiguities that weaken its formal grounding; (2) all experimental results are reported without variance or significance measures, undermining reliability claims; (3) the selection overhead cost is not profiled, making it difficult to assess net efficiency; (4) the conclusion introduces an unsupported claim about TPS optimization; (5) the Related Work section lacks depth and a structured comparison positioning. Novelty assessment is deferred due to external literature unavailability in this run.

## Strengths
1. **Novel geometric insight for query selection.** The paper's core observation — that queries with low cosine similarity to the mean query attend to more keys and contribute more to attention logits — is a genuinely useful empirical finding. This insight is supported by PCA visualizations (Figure 2b) and the correlation analysis between S_q and max attention weights (Figure 2c, correlation 0.737). Using this geometric property to guide query subselection is a conceptually clean design choice that distinguishes QUOKA from prior query-dependent methods that treat all queries homogeneously.

2. **Hardware-agnostic and training-free design.** QUOKA's reliance on standard linear algebra operations (cosine similarity, mean, max, top-k) rather than custom CUDA kernels is a practical strength. This makes the method deployable on CPUs, consumer GPUs, and edge accelerators without kernel reimplementation. The paper demonstrates this advantage concretely by reporting speedups on Intel Xeon CPUs (up to 7x) and RTX 2080 GPUs (5-6x), which is uncommon in the sparse attention literature.

3. **Broad and systematic experimental evaluation.** The paper evaluates on four complementary benchmarks (synthetic retrieval: NIAH, multi-task reasoning: RULER, real-world long-context: LongBench, mathematical reasoning: Math500) across five model families spanning different architectures (dense, MoE, NoPE). The ablation study covers the key hyperparameters (B_SA, B_CP, N_Q), and the latency evaluation spans enterprise and consumer hardware. This level of breadth strengthens the empirical contribution.

4. **Strong empirical results.** QUOKA consistently achieves substantially higher accuracy than competing sparse attention baselines (SampleAttention, SparQ, Loki, LessIsMore) across nearly all configurations and models — often by 10-20% on RULER (Table 1) and with normalized LongBench scores above 0.94 even at B_SA=512 (Table 3). The method also maintains accuracy within 1-3% of the dense baseline while using 88% fewer KV pairs.

5. **Clear algorithmic presentation.** Algorithm 1 provides a concise and well-structured pseudocode of the KV selection procedure. The three-stage design (query subselection → cosine similarity scoring → aggregation) is easy to follow, and the aggregation choices (max over queries, mean over GQA heads) are justified with empirical evidence (Figure 3, Table 10). The pre-aggregation trick for GQA efficiency is a practical contribution that reduces overhead.

## Weaknesses
1. **Missing statistical evidence for all experimental results (Major).** The paper reports every accuracy and latency result as a single point estimate with no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests. This is a fundamental methodological weakness. In Table 1 (RULER), QUOKA's advantage over baselines is large in most configurations, but in Table 3 (LongBench), some margins are smaller — without variance, the reader cannot assess stability. The paper states each data point is "averaged over 100 trials" for latency (Section 4.6), but this is not done for accuracy experiments. Multi-seed evaluation (at least 3 seeds) with standard deviation reporting is standard practice in the sparse attention literature and should be added for at least the primary configurations. *Impact: Without this, all comparative claims are statistically ungrounded.* [Supported by annotation on Page 1 — Results/setup]

2. **Theorem 1 notation ambiguity and incomplete theoretical grounding (Major).** The theorem statement in Section 3.1 uses notation q* that is never explicitly defined (should it be q_0?). The relationship between the theorem's bound and the selection score S_q = -CosSim(M_Q, q) is not directly established — the theorem bounds CosSim(M_Q, q*) from above, but a bound on -CosSim would be more directly relevant. Additionally, the theorem assumes CosSim(k, q_0) > 0 and CosSim(M_Q, k) < 0, but does not justify when these conditions hold or how frequently they occur in practice. The proof in Appendix D could not be verified in this review. The geometric intuition (Section 3.1 opening) is compelling and likely sufficient without the theorem; the theorem in its current form adds limited formal rigor. *Impact: Weakened theoretical credibility; the theorem may be challenged or dismissed by reviewers.* [Supported by annotation: Page 1 — Section 3.1 query subselection]

3. **Selection overhead not profiled (Major).** QUOKA introduces non-trivial computational overhead for query subselection (sorting N_Q queries by cosine similarity), normalization, the O(N_Q * T) scoring matrix multiply, and top-k selection. The paper reports end-to-end attention speedups but never breaks down how much time is spent on selection vs. the reduced attention computation. This makes it impossible to assess whether the method's net speedup is driven by efficient selection or merely by reducing the attention matrix size. The paper's claim of "sub-quadratic complexity" is also imprecise — the cosine similarity scoring step still requires O(N_Q * T) operations, which is O(T) if N_Q is constant, but the paper does not analyze this scaling explicitly. *Impact: Reviewers cannot evaluate the practical efficiency trade-off, which is central to the paper's contribution.* [Supported by annotation: Page 1 — Ablation/Latency analysis]

4. **Conclusion introduces unsupported TPS claim (Major).** The conclusion states that QUOKA "suggest[s] that selective attention is a promising direction for TTFT and TPS optimization." TPS (tokens per second) is a decode-phase throughput metric that is not explicitly evaluated anywhere in the paper. The paper evaluates TTFT (prefill latency) and attention module latency, but not generation throughput. Adding a new performance dimension in the conclusion without supporting evidence is a writing discipline issue. *Impact: Could be flagged as overclaim by reviewers; reduces the conclusion's precision.* [Supported by annotation: Page 1 — Conclusion]

5. **Related Work lacks depth and structured positioning (Moderate).** The Related Work section (Section 5) groups prior methods into three broad categories but does not provide a structured comparison along axes relevant to the paper's contribution (e.g., prefill compatibility, hardware portability, selection mechanism, scoring function). The differentiation from each key baseline is stated at a high level without explaining the technical mechanism behind their limitations under chunked prefill. For instance, why does SampleAttention's uniform query sampling lose information specifically under chunked prefill? Why does SparQ's channel-wise subselection degrade with many queries? A comparison table would substantially strengthen this section. *Impact: The paper's scholarly depth appears limited; positioning feels assertive rather than evidence-driven.* [Supported by annotation: Page 1 — Related Work]

6. **NIAH heatmap comparison (Figure 4) is visually misleading (Minor).** Figure 4 compares QUOKA, SampleAttention, and Full attention on NIAH. The full attention heatmap (c) shows lower accuracy than QUOKA (a) at many settings. This is unexpected since full attention should be the upper bound. The paper attributes this to chunked prefill effects, but the comparison is between QUOKA+chunked prefill vs. Full+chunked prefill, not vs. standard full attention. The caption and text could clarify this to avoid reader confusion. Additionally, the choice of SampleAttention as the only baseline in the main figure is odd — other baselines (SparQ, Loki) are relegated to Figure 7, making the comparison seem selective. *Impact: Minor clarity/reproducibility concern.* [Supported by textual evidence in Section 4.1]

7. **Reproducibility statement claims comprehensiveness prematurely (Minor).** Section 8 states that "All hyperparameters and test configurations are documented in the main text and appendix," but key details are referenced only as Appendix tables (Tables 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12) that are not included in the provided manuscript excerpt. The paper also states it does not include source code, relying on the "simplicity of QUOKA's training-free design" for reproducibility. While the algorithm description is clear, the lack of code and inaccessible appendix details reduces practical reproducibility. *Impact: Reproducibility is plausible but not fully verifiable from the main text alone.* [Supported by textual evidence in Section 8]

8. **Novelty assessment is incomplete (Deferred).** Due to external literature search being unavailable in this run (Retrieval-Disabled Mode), the novelty of QUOKA relative to the broader sparse attention literature cannot be independently verified here. The paper compares against a reasonable set of baselines (SampleAttention, SparQ, Loki, LessIsMore, SnapKV, KeyDif) and shows clear improvements. However, whether the core insight about query geometry is genuinely novel, and whether all relevant competing methods (e.g., StreamingLLM, H2O, Quest, or recent learned sparse attention methods) have been considered, requires manual literature verification. *Impact: Deferred — novelty conclusions should be treated as provisional pending external verification.* [See runtime status]

9. **Missing analysis of failure cases and limitations (Minor).** The paper does not discuss settings where QUOKA might underperform — e.g., very short sequences, tasks requiring dense attention (like fine-grained reasoning over all tokens), or models with unusual attention patterns. The ablation study focuses on accuracy vs. sparsity but does not characterize specific failure modes. Explicitly stating limitations would strengthen the paper's scientific credibility. *Impact: Minor completeness issue.* [Supported by textual evidence throughout Section 4]

10. **Algorithm 1 dimension notation is ambiguous (Minor).** Line 8 of Algorithm 1 reshapes Q and takes mean over dim=2, described as `(b, n_KV, n_Q/n_KV, N_Q, d)`. The fraction n_Q/n_KV assumes exact divisibility, which may not hold for all model configurations. The paper does not discuss how non-divisible cases are handled (e.g., truncation, padding). *Impact: Minor implementation ambiguity.* [Supported by annotation on Page 1 — Algorithm 1]

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Scoring rationale:**

The paper presents a technically sound and empirically well-evaluated sparse attention method for LLM prefill. The core geometric insight is interesting, the algorithm is clean, and the experimental breadth across models and hardware platforms is commendable. QUOKA consistently outperforms the evaluated baselines by substantial margins, and the speedups are practically meaningful.

However, the score is constrained by three major methodological weaknesses:
- **Missing statistical evidence** (no variance/significance on any accuracy result) is a significant gap that undermines the reliability of all comparative claims.
- **The selection overhead is not profiled**, making the efficiency claims incomplete.
- **The theoretical justification (Theorem 1) has notation issues** and does not directly support the selection criterion as cleanly as claimed.

These issues are fixable with additional analysis and clarifications, but in their current form they reduce the paper's scientific rigor and reproducibility. Novelty assessment is deferred due to external literature unavailability in this run; if the query-geometric insight proves genuinely novel against the full literature, the score could increase to the 7-7.5 range after the methodological gaps are addressed.

**Strengths outweigh weaknesses** in the sense that the core method is well-designed and the empirical trends are consistently positive. The paper makes a useful practical contribution to efficient LLM inference. With revisions addressing the statistical rigor, overhead profiling, and theoretical clarity, this work would be suitable for a conference or workshop on efficient ML systems.
