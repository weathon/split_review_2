## Summary

This paper introduces TD-JEPA, a zero-shot unsupervised reinforcement learning method that uses a novel temporal-difference (TD) latent-predictive loss to learn state representations, task encoders, and policy-conditioned multi-step predictors entirely from offline, reward-free data. The key methodological contribution is to extend latent-predictive (JEPA-style) learning from one-step, single-policy, on-policy settings to a multi-step, multi-policy, off-policy setting using TD bootstrapping. The learned predictor approximates successor features in latent space, enabling zero-shot optimization of downstream reward functions via linear regression of the task embedding. Theoretically, the paper proves gradient matching between the proposed latent-predictive loss and a successor measure approximation loss under strong assumptions (orthonormal representations, uniform state distribution, symmetric transitions). Empirically, TD-JEPA is evaluated on 65 tasks across 13 datasets (ExoRL/DMC and OGBench), showing competitive performance with state-of-the-art zero-shot RL methods, with the largest gains in pixel-based domains, and demonstrating that pre-trained representations enable sample-efficient fine-tuning.

**Research value**: The paper addresses a timely problem — how to learn reusable, task-agnostic representations from unsupervised data that can support zero-shot reward optimization. The connection between TD latent-prediction and successor feature factorization is conceptually elegant and builds on a well-established body of work (FB, BYOL-γ, RLDP). The empirical results, particularly on pixel-based tasks where zero-shot RL has been challenging, represent a measurable advance.

**Primary limitations**: (1) The theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (uniform state distribution, symmetric transitions) that are rarely satisfied in practice, and the unquantified constant c in Theorem 4 weakens the bound's practical implications. (2) The core ablation study confounds prediction horizon and policy conditioning, making it difficult to attribute gains to the claimed mechanism. (3) Multiple comparisons across 65 tasks and 7 baselines are reported without statistical correction. (4) The method introduces a BC regularization term for OGBench that is not part of the core algorithm, complicating interpretation of those results.

## Strengths
**S1. Clean theoretical connection between latent-prediction and successor features.** The paper's core theoretical contribution — showing that the TD-JEPA loss is equivalent (up to gradient matching) to optimizing a bilinear factorization of the successor measure — is novel and clearly articulated. The gradient matching argument unifying multiple prior analyses (Tang et al., 2023; Khetarpal et al., 2025; Voelcker et al., 2024; Lawson et al., 2025) is an independent contribution that could benefit the broader latent-predictive representation learning community. The connection to forward-backward TD losses (Th. 3) provides a principled bridge between the JEPA paradigm and established successor-feature methods.

**S2. Strong pixel-based results.** Table 1 shows TD-JEPA achieving 628.8 ± 5.5 on DMC_RGB (vs. next-best BYOL-γ* at 582.4 ± 9.8), a clear and substantial improvement. This is noteworthy because pixel-based zero-shot RL has been a challenging regime where most prior methods struggle. The consistency of improvement across all four DMC_RGB domains (walker, cheetah, quadruped, pointmass) suggests a genuine methodological advantage rather than task-specific luck.

**S3. Practical demonstration of reusable representations.** Figure 4 provides compelling evidence that pre-trained state encoders from TD-JEPA enable fast downstream adaptation. The frozen-representation experiments (dashed lines in Figure 4) show that the learned encoder captures task-relevant features without any fine-tuning, often matching or exceeding scratch-trained TD3 within 100K steps. This is a practically valuable property for real-world deployment where fine-tuning may be expensive or impossible.

**S4. Comprehensive evaluation across diverse domains.** The benchmark covers 13 datasets, 65 tasks, both proprioceptive and pixel inputs, and locomotion/navigation/manipulation domains. This breadth is rare in the zero-shot RL literature and strengthens the external validity of the findings. The inclusion of both high-coverage (DMC) and low-coverage (OGBench) datasets tests the method under different data regimes.

**S5. Clear exposition of the method.** The algorithm description (Section 3, Algorithm 1) is well-structured, with clear motivation for each design choice (MC→TD transition, asymmetric encoders, orthonormality regularization, actor loss). The transition from a simple one-step loss (Eq. 3) to the full TD-JEPA objective (Eq. 9) via intermediate steps is pedagogically effective.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Theoretical analysis relies on strong, rarely-satisfied assumptions (major).** Theorems 1 and 3 require (A1) orthonormal representations $\phi^\top\phi = \psi^\top\psi = I$, (A2) uniform state distribution, and (A3) symmetric transition matrices $P^{\pi_z}$. While A1 is approximately enforced via regularization, A2 and A3 are almost never satisfied in practice: directed MDPs have non-symmetric transitions, and state visitation is non-uniform under any policy. The paper acknowledges these can be "relaxed" (citing Appendix C), but the main text's theoretical claims are presented without qualification. The absence of explicit bounds on how violations affect the gradient-matching result limits the practical utility of the theory. **Fix**: Add a paragraph quantifying the gradient mismatch under non-uniform / non-symmetric conditions, or state explicit error bounds in terms of measurable quantities (e.g., state distribution ratio, degree of asymmetry).

**W2. Unquantified constant c in Theorem 4 weakens the bound's practical value (major).** Theorem 4 states $\mathcal{L}_{SM} \leq c \,\mathcal{L}_{fw}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{SM} \leq c \,\mathcal{L}_{bw}$ "for some c" without characterizing c. If c depends on $1/(1-\gamma)$ or $S$ (state space size), the bound could be very loose, making the claim that TD-JEPA "indirectly optimizes" the policy evaluation error through TD losses much weaker than it appears. **Fix**: Report the explicit value or bound of c, or acknowledge the limitation and refer readers to the gradient-matching results (Th. 1, 3) as the primary theoretical justification instead.

**W3. Core ablation conflates prediction horizon and policy conditioning (major).** The ablation study comparing BYOL* (one-step behavioral), BYOL-γ* (multi-step behavioral), and TD-JEPA (multi-step policy-conditional) simultaneously changes two variables: prediction horizon and whether dynamics are conditioned on the target policy. The conclusion "directly modeling policy-conditional successor measures is on average beneficial" is not supported by the evidence because the benefit could be due to longer-horizon prediction alone. **Fix**: Add a controlled comparison: a TD-JEPA variant without policy conditioning (predicting behavioral multi-step dynamics) and/or a BYOL-γ* variant conditioned on z. This cleanly isolates the effect of each design choice.

**W4. No multiple-testing correction across 65 tasks × 8 methods (major).** The evaluation compares 8 methods across 65 tasks with both aggregated and per-domain metrics. The paper uses "confidence intervals overlap" as a significance criterion, but this is not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Figure 2's pairwise improvement probabilities are reported without correction, meaning some of the claimed advantages may be false positives. **Fix**: Apply a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction or use an omnibus test (Friedman + Nemenyi) to control Type I error. Report which comparisons survive correction.

**W5. BC regularization on OGBench introduces a hidden confound (moderate).** Footnote 4 mentions "BC regularization in OGBench based on Park et al. (2025b)" that is detailed only in Appendix E.6. This means the method evaluated on OGBench includes a behavioral-cloning auxiliary loss not present in the core TD-JEPA algorithm. Since OGBench provides expert-like demonstrations, BC regularization could independently improve performance without any contribution from the TD latent-prediction objective. The paper does not ablate this. **Fix**: (a) Report OGBench results without BC regularization, (b) add BC regularization to the core algorithm description in Section 3.3, and (c) clarify which results depend on BC.

**W6. No direct empirical validation of the claimed factorization mechanism (moderate).** The theoretical story says TD-JEPA learns a low-rank factorization $M^{\pi_z} \approx \phi T_z \psi^\top$, but the experiments only measure downstream zero-shot performance — an indirect proxy. There is no attempt to measure the reconstruction error of the successor measure, the effective rank of learned representations, or how these correlate with downstream performance. **Fix**: Add an analysis measuring $\|\phi T_z \psi^\top - \hat{M}^{\pi_z}\|_F$ on a subset of states and policies, and trace how it evolves during training.

**W7. Missing characterization of the constant offset in Proposition 1 (moderate).** Proposition 1 shows $\mathcal{L}_{MC-JEPA}$ equals the successor feature prediction error plus a constant, but the paper does not characterize this constant. If it depends on the variance of $\phi(s^+)$ along trajectories, then the actor loss $\mathcal{L}_{actor} = -T_\phi^\top z$ may optimize a biased objective. **Fix**: State explicitly whether the constant affects the TD bootstrap target or the actor gradient, and provide the fixed-point characterization formally.

**W8. Conclusion introduces unsupported claims (minor).** The conclusion states "exceeds them [best zero-shot methods] when learning from pixels" without acknowledging that on OGBench_Proprio and several individual OGBench tasks, TD-JEPA underperforms FB, HILP, or BYOL-γ* (Table 1). **Fix**: Bound the claim: "exceeds prior methods on average in pixel-based DMC domains, while being competitive on other benchmarks."

**W9. Abstract overstates consistency of results (minor).** The abstract says "matches or outperforms state-of-the-art baselines" without specifying the domain-dependent nature of the comparison. On OGBench (proprioception), FB (39.04) and HILP (37.98) match or slightly exceed TD-JEPA (37.98). **Fix**: Qualify: "TD-JEPA matches or outperforms baselines on average across 13 datasets, with the largest gains in pixel-based settings."

## Score
**Final Score: 7/10**

**Scoring rationale**: The paper makes a solid methodological contribution (TD-based latent-prediction for multi-policy successor feature learning) and provides strong empirical evidence on pixel-based domains. The theoretical analysis is elegant but relies on assumptions that limit its direct applicability. The research value is moderate: the method improves over prior art in a practically important regime (pixels) but the ablation study does not cleanly isolate the source of improvement. The experimentation is broad (65 tasks) but lacks multiple-testing correction and the BC regularization confound on OGBench requires clarification. Reproducibility is supported by clear algorithm description and provided code, though key hyperparameters ($\lambda$, BC weight) are only partially specified. The paper is well-written and the narrative is coherent. The identified weaknesses (strong theory assumptions, unquantified constant $c$, confounded ablation, missing multiple-testing correction, hidden BC regularization) are fixable and do not invalidate the core contribution, but they reduce confidence in the strength of the claims. A revision addressing these points could raise the score to 8/10.
