## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of **spurious unlearning** in LLM unlearning — the phenomenon where gradient ascent (GA) and its variants (NPO, WGA) suppress target responses but redistribute probability mass into semantically related rephrasing regions (the "squeezing effect"), resulting in superficial forgetting that standard metrics fail to detect. The authors propose a **bootstrapping framework** that augments the unlearning objective with the model's own high-confidence predictions ("beliefs") as additional forgetting targets. Two instantiations are provided: **BS-T** (token-level) interpolates the one-hot target label with the model's top-k token predictions to form a soft target; **BS-S** (sequence-level) samples entire high-confidence generations and adds them as auxiliary forget data. Theoretical analysis using the AKG learning dynamics framework shows how BS-T reshapes gradient residuals to distribute forgetting pressure across belief neighborhoods. Experiments on TOFU, WMDP, and MUSE across Llama 3.2 1B/3B and Llama 3.1 8B show that BS-S achieves the best aggregate forgetting-retention trade-off (e.g., Agg. 0.64 on TOFU 10% 8B vs NPO 0.63), while BS-T offers a computationally lighter alternative.

The paper makes a useful conceptual contribution by identifying the squeezing effect and connecting it to model beliefs. The bootstrapping idea is intuitive and well-grounded in the identified failure mode. However, several weaknesses reduce confidence: (1) the gains over baselines are modest (0.01–0.03 on aggregate metrics) and not universal across all settings (utility sometimes lags behind SimNPO); (2) no statistical significance or variance is reported; (3) critical hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, hardware, $k$ selection, temperature) are not specified, harming reproducibility; (4) the lazy eNTK assumption underlying the theoretical analysis is not validated for the unlearning setting; (5) the paper lacks a limitations section and does not discuss potential negative side effects of suppressing model beliefs; (6) novelty/comparison claims cannot be verified due to the absence of external retrieval (Retrieval-Disabled Mode).

## Strengths
**S1. Clear problem identification and mechanistic explanation.** The paper makes a genuine conceptual contribution by identifying the squeezing effect — the redistribution of probability mass into semantically related high-likelihood regions during GA-based unlearning. This explanation goes beyond prior work's surface-level observations about metric unreliability and provides a concrete, testable mechanism. The two case studies (GA syntactic collapse and NPO semantic rephrasing) clearly illustrate the practical failure mode.

**S2. Principled method design rooted in the identified mechanism.** The bootstrapping framework follows directly from the mechanistic analysis: if the squeezing effect shifts probability mass into model beliefs (high-confidence tokens/sequences), then suppressing those beliefs is a natural remedy. The two-level design (BS-T for local token-level suppression, BS-S for global sequence-level removal) is well-motivated and covers different granularities of the squeezing effect. The theoretical analysis using the AKG learning dynamics framework (Theorems 5.2 and 5.3) provides a formal justification for why BS-T reshapes gradients differently from GA.

**S3. Comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple benchmarks.** Experiments span three distinct unlearning benchmarks (TOFU for privacy forgetting, WMDP for hazardous knowledge removal, MUSE for verbatim/factual forgetting) and multiple model scales (Llama 3.2 1B/3B, Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 2 7B, Zephyr 7B). The comparison covers six recent baselines (GradDiff, NPO, RMU, SimNPO, WGA) within a unified OpenUnlearning framework, ensuring consistent experimental conditions. The LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (Naturalness and Similarity) provides a more faithful assessment than surface metrics alone.

**S4. Practical compatibility with existing methods.** BS-T and BS-S are designed as plug-in components that can be combined with existing unlearning losses (NPO, WGA) and regularization (GradDiff). This backward compatibility lowers the barrier for adoption and makes the contribution additive rather than requiring a complete reimplementation of the unlearning pipeline.

## Weaknesses
**W1. Incremental empirical gains without statistical verification [Evidence: Page 1 — Section 6.2, Tables 1-2].** Across all experimental settings, BS-S's gains over prior methods are modest (typically 0.01–0.03 on aggregate score), and BS-T's gains are even smaller. On several configurations (e.g., TOFU 10% Llama 3.1 8B Utility), SimNPO achieves better retention (0.72) than both BS-T (0.70) and BS-S (0.71). No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests are reported, making it impossible to determine whether the reported advantages are reliable or within noise. **Required action (Must):** Report mean ± std over at least 3 random seeds for all main results. Include a paired significance test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank) comparing BS-S against the strongest baseline for each benchmark.

**W2. Missing implementation details and hyperparameter disclosure [Evidence: Page 1 — Section 6.1, lines 186-188].** The experimental setup section does not report learning rate, batch size, optimizer, number of epochs, warmup schedule, or hardware for any experiment. For the proposed methods, critical hyperparameters — $k$ (top-k set size), $\lambda_{BST}$, $\lambda_{BSS}$, $N$ (number of sampled sequences), temperature $T$, and decoding parameters — are not specified. This makes the experiments impossible to reproduce independently. **Required action (Must):** Add a comprehensive hyperparameter table covering all methods and models. Release a configuration file with the code repository. At minimum, specify all hyperparameters used in the main experiments.

**W3. Theoretical analysis rests on an unvalidated assumption [Evidence: Page 1 — Section 5.1, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2].** The AKG learning dynamics framework relies on the lazy eNTK (empirical Neural Tangent Kernel) assumption, which requires that model parameters change negligibly during training. Unlearning with gradient ascent can cause rapid parameter changes (especially for the forget set), potentially violating this assumption. The paper does not empirically verify that the kernel distance remains small during unlearning, nor does it provide bounds on when the approximation holds. **Required action (Must):** Either (a) empirically verify the lazy regime by measuring kernel distance before/after training, or (b) provide a theoretical analysis that relaxes the lazy eNTK assumption, or (c) explicitly acknowledge this limitation and discuss how violations might affect the conclusions.

**W4. Absence of limitations and negative side-effect analysis [Evidence: Page 1 — Section 7, conclusion].** The paper contains no limitations section. The bootstrapping framework suppresses the model's high-confidence predictions, which could inadvertently suppress benign factual knowledge if it falls in the same high-likelihood region as forget targets. The method's sensitivity to $k$ and $\lambda$ is not analyzed. BS-S's computational overhead (additional forward passes for each forget prompt) is mentioned qualitatively but not quantified. **Required action (Must):** Add a dedicated limitations paragraph (or subsection) covering: (a) potential collateral damage to retained knowledge, (b) sensitivity to hyperparameters, (c) compute overhead of BS-S, (d) settings where the method may underperform compared to simpler baselines, and (e) boundary conditions for the squeezing effect (e.g., model scale, forget ratio).

**W5. Overclaiming and imprecise language in contribution statements [Evidence: Page 1 — Abstract, Contributions list lines 44-49, Section 6.2].** The paper repeatedly uses "consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines" and "superior performance" without qualifying the modest effect sizes and incomplete coverage. The first contribution bullet characterizes an observation (that NPO suffers from spurious unlearning) as a contribution separate from the method itself, inflating the contribution count. The SOTA claim is unqualified — it does not specify which baselines, under which settings, or on which metrics. **Required action (Must):** Replace unqualified SOTA claims with bounded statements of the form "BS-S achieves higher aggregate scores than NPO, GradDiff, and WGA on TOFU under the evaluated settings, with modest margins requiring further statistical validation." Merge the first contribution bullet into the problem statement.

**W6. Novelty/comparison claims deferred due to retrieval unavailability.** This run operates under Retrieval-Disabled Mode (paper_search unavailable). Therefore, external literature verification cannot be performed. Claims about the squeezing effect being "underexplored," the bootstrapping framework being "novel," and the comparison with methods like "RMU," "SimNPO," and "WGA" could not be independently verified against the broader literature. **Required action:** The authors should ensure that their related-work section (Appendix B) comprehensively covers all prior methods that use model predictions as training signals in unlearning contexts, particularly any prior work on self-distillation for forgetting or adversarial training with model-generated samples.

**Additional Minor Issues:**
- The abstract lacks quantitative anchor points (no concrete numbers).
- The "bootstrapping" term is used in a nonstandard sense compared to its established ML meaning (Yarowsky, 1995) — this should be explicitly clarified.
- The Naturalness/Similarity LaaJ evaluation uses Gemini 2.5 Flash as judge — the potential bias of using one specific LLM judge should be discussed (different judges may yield different rankings).
- The AKG residual analysis (Theorems 5.2-5.3) would benefit from empirical validation (e.g., measuring residual norms during training to confirm the predicted structure).
- The MUSE results are relegated to the appendix despite being part of the claimed evaluation scope.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

The paper makes a meaningful conceptual contribution by identifying and naming the squeezing effect in LLM unlearning, and proposing a well-motivated bootstrapping framework that incorporates model beliefs into the unlearning objective. The two-level instantiation (BS-T and BS-S) is technically sound, the theoretical analysis using AKG learning dynamics is a valuable addition, and the empirical evaluation covers multiple benchmarks and model scales.

However, the score is tempered by several significant concerns:

- **Research value/novelty (primary dimension):** The core insight — that suppressing only target responses causes probability mass to shift to semantically related regions — is clearly articulated and conceptually valuable. However, similar redistribution effects have been studied in the context of LLM fine-tuning (Ren & Sutherland, 2025; Razin et al., 2025), and the adaptation to unlearning, while useful, is an incremental extension. The bootstrapping solution is intuitive following from the identified mechanism, but its similarity to self-distillation and adversarial training paradigms (using model outputs as training targets) is acknowledged by the authors. Without external literature verification, the novelty claim cannot be fully assessed. **(Score weight: high)**

- **Validity/soundness:** The theoretical analysis rest on the lazy eNTK assumption which is not validated for the unlearning setting. The empirical results lack statistical significance testing, and gains are modest. Several hyperparameters are undisclosed, preventing full reproducibility assessment. **(Score weight: high)**

- **Presentation and reproducibility:** The paper is generally well-written with clear motivation and logical flow. However, missing implementation details, the absence of a limitations section, and occasional overclaiming reduce the overall quality.

The paper presents solid work that could become a useful contribution after addressing the reproducibility, statistical validation, and limitations concerns. In its current form, the empirical evidence supports the method's potential but is not yet fully convincing.

**Post-Revision Target: 7/10** — achievable with addition of multi-seed statistics, hyperparameter disclosure, limitations section, and more measured claims.
