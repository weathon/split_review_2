## Summary

This paper investigates whether the "counterintuitive phenomenon" — where deep generative models assign higher likelihoods to out-of-distribution (anomalous) data than to in-distribution (normal) data — occurs in tabular anomaly detection. The authors propose a domain-agnostic definition of the phenomenon based on relative AUROC performance (Definition 3.3), then conduct extensive experiments on 47 tabular datasets and 10 CV/NLP embedding datasets from ADBench, comparing NF-SLT (Normalizing Flow with Simple Likelihood Test) against 12 baseline models. The main finding is that, under the proposed definition, the counterintuitive phenomenon is rarely observed in tabular data: NF-SLT achieves the highest average AUROC (0.8575) and lowest fail ratio (0.02) among all methods. Theoretical analysis links this result to lower dimensionality and weaker feature correlation in tabular data compared to images, using Theorem 5.4 (dimensionality's impact on likelihood gap) and intrinsic dimension (ID) estimation to quantify feature correlation (d-Ratio). The work identifies a meaningful empirical regularity — that likelihood-based detection with normalizing flows is more reliable for tabular data than for images — and provides both experimental evidence and theoretical grounding.

The paper's strengths include its comprehensive benchmark coverage, the attempt to formalize a previously vague concept, and the novel use of intrinsic dimension to characterize domain differences. However, several methodological concerns reduce the strength of the conclusions: (1) Definition 3.3 depends on unspecified thresholds β and γ, making the central claim unfalsifiable as presented; (2) the hyperparameter selection protocol (global best average AUROC) may inflate NF-SLT's reported advantage; (3) NeuTraLAD received different preprocessing, creating an asymmetric comparison; (4) Theorem 5.4 assumes feature independence, which limits its relevance to the correlation-based explanation; and (5) several claims are worded more strongly than the evidence supports. The paper would benefit from tighter operationalization of its core definition, fairer evaluation protocols, and more bounded claims.

## Strengths
**1. Comprehensive benchmark evaluation.** The paper evaluates NF-SLT on all 47 tabular datasets and 10 CV/NLP embedding datasets from ADBench without selection bias, addressing a valid concern raised by Shwartz-Ziv & Armon [2022]. The inclusion of 12 baseline models spanning both shallow (PCA, LOF, IF, OCSVM, COPOD, ECOD) and deep (DAGMM, DeepSVDD, GOAD, NeuTraLAD, ICL, MCM) anomaly detection methods provides a thorough empirical foundation. The 10-run repeated experiments with average reporting add basic statistical grounding.

**2. Novel formalization of a vague concept.** The counterintuitive likelihood phenomenon, while widely discussed in the image domain (Nalisnick et al., 2019a), lacked an operational definition suitable for cross-domain comparison. Definition 3.3, which operationalizes the phenomenon through relative AUROC comparisons against a portfolio of baselines, represents a genuine attempt to move beyond informal discussions. The two-component threshold (proportion of outperforming baselines + minimum performance gap) captures both conditions that practitioners would intuitively associate with "something going wrong" with likelihood-based detection.

**3. Creative use of intrinsic dimension for domain characterization.** The d-Ratio (intrinsic dimension / ambient dimension) provides a novel lens for comparing data domains. The Gaussian toy example (Equation 5 with autoregressive covariance) convincingly demonstrates that stronger correlation reduces estimated intrinsic dimension. The empirical comparison showing that image datasets have d-Ratio ≈ 0.2–1.9% while tabular datasets have d-Ratio ≈ 39–81% is striking and provides a measurable, conceptually clean explanation for why the counterintuitive phenomenon manifests differently across domains.

**4. Two-factor theoretical framework.** The paper's decomposition of the explanation into dimensionality (Theorem 5.4) and feature correlation (Section 5.2) provides a structured framework that future work can build upon. The ICA dimensionality reduction experiment (Table 2) showing AUROC increasing as dimensionality decreases under the entropy condition H(P) > H(Q) provides direct empirical support for the dimensionality hypothesis.

**5. Honest exception reporting.** The paper explicitly identifies datasets where NF-SLT underperforms (yeast, imdb, and the 25 datasets with rank ≥ 3) and links the latter group to low d-Ratio. This transparency about failure modes strengthens the credibility of the empirical analysis.

## Weaknesses
### W1 (Major): Definition 3.3 is incompletely specified — β and γ thresholds are not stated, making the central claim non-falsifiable

The counterintuitive phenomenon is formalized via two conditions: (a) the proportion of baselines outperforming the generative model exceeds β, and (b) the minimum AUROC gap among outperforming baselines exceeds γ. However, β and γ are never given concrete values in the main text. The paper evaluates datasets as "not exhibiting the phenomenon" using this definition without disclosing the thresholds used. This is a fundamental scientific completeness issue: without stated thresholds, the reader cannot independently verify the conclusion, and the authors could adjust β and γ post hoc to produce any desired classification. Appendix B is referenced for the "fully rigorous formulation" but is not available in the provided text. If thresholds are stated only in an appendix, the main text must still report them for independent assessment.

**Required action (Must):** State the exact β and γ values used in the main body, with explicit justification. Include a sensitivity analysis showing how the conclusions change over a range of β ∈ [0.3, 0.8] and γ ∈ [0.01, 0.10]. If no fixed thresholds were used, the definition is insufficient for scientific use and must be revised.

### W2 (Major): Hyperparameter selection via global-best average AUROC risks overfitting and may inflate NF-SLT's reported advantage

The evaluation protocol selects "the hyperparameter combination with the highest average AUROC for all datasets" as the representative configuration. This means the reported NF-SLT performance (AUROC 0.8575) is the maximum achievable under a single global configuration. Three issues arise: (1) This protocol implicitly optimizes for the benchmark aggregate, potentially overfitting to the 47-dataset collection. (2) Per-dataset variance is hidden — NF-SLT's advantage may be small or negative on many individual datasets. (3) If NF-SLT has higher hyperparameter sensitivity than baselines, the global-best selection could create an uneven playing field. Additionally, no standard deviations or confidence intervals are reported for the aggregate AUROC values in Table 1, despite the claim of 10 repeated experiments.

**Required action (Must):** Report per-dataset mean and standard deviation across the 10 runs. Show that the ranking in Table 1 is stable under per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (or quantify the shift). At minimum, provide the range of AUROC values for NF-SLT across different hyperparameter configurations to bound the selection bias.

### W3 (Major): Asymmetric preprocessing for NeuTraLAD undermines comparison fairness

NeuTraLAD is explicitly excluded from RobustScaler normalization "because a significant performance decrease was observed when scaling." This creates an asymmetric evaluation: 12 models use scaled data, while NeuTraLAD (the second-best deep model with AUROC 0.8081) uses unscaled data. NF-SLT benefits from scaling. The paper does not control for this asymmetry by testing NF-SLT without scaling or NeuTraLAD with scaling (even at lower performance). Without this control, the reported ranking (NF-SLT 0.8575 vs NeuTraLAD 0.8081) may partly reflect preprocessing choice rather than methodological superiority.

**Required action (Must):** Add a control experiment with NF-SLT on unscaled data and NeuTraLAD on RobustScaler-scaled data. Report both outcomes to bound the impact of the asymmetric preprocessing. If the ranking changes, recalibrate the conclusions accordingly.

### W4 (Major): Theorem 5.4 assumes feature independence (P = ∏ p_i, Q = ∏ q_i), which limits its relevance to the correlation-based explanation

The paper's central explanatory narrative is that tabular data avoids the counterintuitive phenomenon due to weaker feature correlation. However, Theorem 5.4 — the primary theoretical result — assumes that P and Q are products of independent univariate distributions. This independence assumption eliminates the very factor (correlation) that the paper argues is critical. Under independence, all dimensions contribute independently to the likelihood gap, which is the most favorable case for the theorem's bound. For correlated data (images), the effective degrees of freedom are lower, so the bound may not apply straightforwardly. The theorem shows that higher ambient dimension d reduces the likelihood gap bound under independence, but the connection to the correlation-based story (Section 5.2) is left as an informal argument rather than a formal extension.

**Required action (Must):** Clarify the logical bridge between Theorem 5.4 and Section 5.2. Either extend the theorem to correlated settings (using intrinsic dimension in place of d), or explicitly state that the theorem applies to the independence case and the correlation argument is a separate, complementary explanation. Provide a unified theoretical statement that combines both factors.

### W5 (Moderate): Several claims are worded more strongly than the evidence supports

The abstract concludes that "likelihood-only detection with normalizing flows offers a practical and reliable approach for anomaly detection in tabular domains." The conclusion similarly states "outperforming traditional models without facing image domain challenges." These claims overstate the evidence because: (a) NF-SLT underperforms on the 'imdb' dataset (0.5013 vs best 0.5398), (b) the authors' own analysis shows 25 out of 47 tabular datasets have NF-SLT rank ≥ 3, (c) the hyperparameter and preprocessing caveats (W2, W3) mean the performance advantage may be partially artifactual, and (d) Definition 3.3's threshold dependence (W1) means "rare" is an operational conclusion, not an unconditional one.

**Required action (Must):** Replace "reliable" and "without facing challenges" with bounded language that explicitly acknowledges the method's limitations: "NF-SLT achieves competitive performance on the majority of evaluated tabular benchmarks, with key exceptions on datasets with stronger feature correlation (low d-Ratio). Its reliability for high-dimensional or strongly correlated tabular data requires further validation."

### W6 (Moderate): The relationship between d-Ratio and the counterintuitive phenomenon is correlational, not causal

The paper shows that datasets where NF-SLT underperforms (rank ≥ 3) tend to have lower d-Ratio. However, the causal mechanism is not established. Low d-Ratio could indicate many properties beyond feature correlation (manifold nonlinearity, redundant features, discrete structure), and the paper does not control for these confounds. The Gaussian toy example (Equation 5) shows that correlation reduces d-Ratio in a synthetic setting, but real tabular data may have low d-Ratio for entirely different reasons that independently affect anomaly detection difficulty. The paper acknowledges this implicitly but does not provide controlled experiments that isolate correlation from other ID-reducing factors.

**Required action (Nice-to-have):** Include a controlled experiment using synthetic tabular data with independently varied correlation strength (e.g., using copula models with controlled dependence) to directly measure how d-Ratio and NF-SLT performance co-vary when other factors are held constant. This would substantially strengthen the causal interpretation.

### W7 (Minor): Related Work sections are organized as citation lists rather than structured comparisons

Section 2.1 reads as a textbook summary of normalizing flows (invertibility, coupling layers, Jacobian constraints), spending space on well-known material without connecting to the paper's specific architectural choices. Section 2.2 lists prior work on the counterintuitive phenomenon in a dense, sentence-by-sentence citation chain. Neither section provides a thematic organization that helps the reader understand the landscape of approaches, their relative strengths, and the specific gap addressed by this paper.

**Required action (Nice-to-have):** Reorganize Section 2.2 into thematic groups (detection-improvement methods, theoretical explanations, and remaining gaps) as suggested in the annotation. Condense Section 2.1 to 2-3 sentences and add focused discussion of prior flow-based AD work on tabular data.

### W8 (Minor): No statistical significance testing on the primary comparison

Despite 10 repeated experiments, Table 1 reports only point estimates (mean AUROC) without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests. Given that performance differences between NF-SLT (0.8575) and the next best ICL (0.8208) or NeuTraLAD (0.8081) are moderate (3.7-4.9 percentage points), and the hyperparameter selection protocol (W2) could favor NF-SLT, the lack of variance reporting makes it impossible to assess whether these differences are statistically reliable.

**Required action (Nice-to-have):** Add standard deviations to Table 1. Include a paired significance test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank) comparing NF-SLT against each baseline across the 47 datasets to establish statistical reliability of the ranking.

## Score
**Final Score: 6/10**

**Scoring rationale:** The score is grounded in the following evidence-based assessment across the four primary dimensions (research value, validity/soundness, novelty, reproducibility).

**Research Value (weight: high):** The paper addresses a practically relevant question — whether a known failure mode of likelihood-based anomaly detection in images transfers to tabular data. The comprehensive benchmark (47 datasets, 12 baselines) provides useful empirical reference points for practitioners. However, the research value is partially diminished because the core definition (the main claimed contribution) is incompletely specified, making the headline conclusion ("the phenomenon is rare") operationally ambiguous. The theoretical framework (dimensionality + correlation) is intuitive but the formal results do not fully support the narrative.

**Validity/Soundness (weight: high):** The evaluation has several threats to validity that reduce confidence in the reported rankings: (W1) unfalsifiable definition thresholds, (W2) hyperparameter selection protocol that may inflate NF-SLT's advantage, (W3) asymmetric preprocessing that advantages NF-SLT over NeuTraLAD, (W4) theoretical gap between the independence assumption and the correlation claim, and (W5) overclaimed wording that extends beyond the evidence. While these issues are individually addressable, their combination means the reader cannot fully trust the reported performance advantage at face value.

**Novelty (weight: medium; deferred due to Retrieval-Disabled Mode):** The paper's claims include (C1) a domain-agnostic definition, (C2) comprehensive benchmark results, and (C3) theoretical analysis. C1 is partially novel but incompletely specified. C2 is a useful empirical contribution (large-scale comparison) rather than a conceptual advance. C3 builds on prior work (Caterini & Loaiza-Ganem, 2022) with added dimensionality analysis but has the independence limitation noted in W4. External literature verification was not available in this run; novelty conclusions are deferred for manual verification.

**Reproducibility (weight: medium):** The paper provides code and Appendix references for hyperparameters. However, the missing threshold specification (W1) and the global-best hyperparameter selection (W2) mean that exact reproduction may yield different rankings depending on choices. Standard deviations are not reported despite 10-run experiments.

**Score interpretation:** A score of 6/10 indicates a paper with a solid empirical contribution and an interesting research question, but with several methodological concerns that must be addressed before the core claims can be fully accepted. The paper's strengths (comprehensive benchmarking, d-Ratio analysis) are genuine, but the weaknesses in definition specification and evaluation fairness prevent a higher score at this stage. With careful revision addressing W1-W5, the paper could move to 7-8/10.
