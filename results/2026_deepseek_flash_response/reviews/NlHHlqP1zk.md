## Summary

The paper proposes the Fast and Slow Effect (FSE) framework — a fully automated protocol for evaluating the sufficiency of LLM/VLM-generated concept annotations for XAI without human supervision. The framework iteratively collects concepts through a 5-stage refinement chain, measures a Class Representation Index (CRI) at each step comparing "fast mode" (direct visual classification) to "slow mode" (text-only concept-based classification), and introduces the CRI-Gap metric. Experiments across 6 models and 5 datasets show that slow mode systematically underperforms fast mode on fine-grained datasets (CRI gap of -25% to -27% on average) while matching or exceeding it on coarse-grained datasets. The paper also demonstrates that the widely-used "utility-as-proxy" assumption can be misleading: fused fast+slow mode achieves ~90% CRI while slow mode alone scores only ~50-60%.

## Strengths

- **Fully automated evaluation of annotation sufficiency without human supervision.** The framework defines a principled notion of sufficiency (Definition 3.1) and operationalizes it through CRI (Eq. 2), directly addressing a gap where prior work relied on expensive human evaluation or the unreliable utility-as-proxy assumption. The framework can be applied to any LLM/VLM without additional annotation cost.

- **Systematic empirical demonstration across 6 models, 5 datasets, and two annotation paradigms.** Table 2 and Figure 3 show consistent negative CRI-Gap across all models and fine-grained datasets (-25% to -27% on average), establishing a clear and reproducible pattern. The contrast between fine-grained and coarse-grained datasets (Table 3) provides an informative sanity check: on CIFAR-100 and Caltech-101, slow mode achieves 94-95% CRI, suggesting the model *can* reason from text when concepts are sufficiently informative — making the fine-grained failure genuinely diagnostic of concept insufficiency.

- **Empirical refutation of the utility-as-proxy assumption (Table 4).** The fused mode (fast+slow) achieves 83-96% CRI while slow mode alone scores only 42-69% under identical conditions, directly showing that strong downstream task performance can coexist with conceptually insufficient annotations. This is a concrete, practically important finding for the XAI community.

- **Careful experimental design in distractor selection (Section 5.3, Table 1).** The semantic similarity-based distractor construction produces significantly higher contradiction rates (34-45%) than random selection (14-20%), ensuring the evaluation is genuinely challenging and not trivialized by easy candidate sets.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The CRI conflates concept quality with the LLM's ability to reason from text — a confound that the paper does not adequately disentangle.** The CRI measures whether the *same model* that generated the concepts can correctly classify when fed only those concepts as text. Low CRI could reflect the model's limited textual reasoning ability rather than concept insufficiency per se. Definition 3.1 says concepts should be "expressive, clear, and precise enough to enable accurate inference" but does not specify who performs the inference — implicitly, it is the generating model itself. The paper's headline claim ("current annotation methods fail to provide sufficient semantic coverage") treats the finding as solely about annotation quality, but the evidence does not uniquely support this interpretation over the alternative that LLMs struggle to reason from their own textual outputs. **Crucially, the results on coarse-grained datasets (Table 3) partially mitigate this concern** — slow mode succeeds on CIFAR-100 and Caltech-101 (94-95% CRI), showing that the models *can* reason from text when concepts are informative. This pattern suggests the CRI is indeed capturing something about concept discriminativity rather than just general reasoning failure. However, the paper never explicitly discusses this distinction, nor does it acknowledge that the confound tempers its central claims. The main claims in the abstract and conclusion would benefit from more precise language (e.g., "the same model cannot reliably use its own generated concepts for classification" rather than "annotation methods fail to provide sufficient semantic coverage").

### Minor

- **No cross-model or human validation.** The framework is entirely self-contained within the generating model. A concept-transfer experiment (concepts generated by model A, evaluated by model B using CRI) would substantially strengthen the claim that CRI measures concept quality rather than model-specific reasoning quirks. Human validation on even a small subset (50-100 examples) would establish whether low CRI correlates with human-perceived concept insufficiency.

- **No ablation of the 5-stage refinement chain.** The paper extends prior 3-stage concept extraction (Oikarinen et al., 2023) to 5 stages but provides no analysis of which stages contribute most, whether the ordering matters, or whether more stages reliably improve CRI (Section 4.1). This makes it difficult to assess whether the 5-stage design is well-justified or could be simplified.

- **No analysis of generated concepts.** The paper treats concepts as a black box and measures only whether they suffice for classification. An analysis of what the LLM actually generates (e.g., are concepts too generic? do they miss key discriminative features for certain classes?) would strengthen the claim about insufficiency. Error analysis across classes is also absent.

- **Sample size for main experiments not reported.** While the preliminary experiment (Table 1) reports "100 images from each dataset," the main CRI results (Figure 3, Tables 2-3) do not state the number of test cases used. This is important for assessing statistical confidence.

### Trivial

- The CRI formula (Eq. 2) uses *t* as both the annotation step index and the (apparent) number of test cases in the summation — the notation conflates two different quantities.
- The paper refers to multimodal VLMs as "LLMs" throughout, which could confuse readers about what capabilities are being tested.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A cross-model concept-transfer experiment (concepts from model A, evaluated by model B) would directly address the self-referential confound.
- Human validation of CRI on a small subset (50-100 examples) would establish ecological validity.
- Ablation analysis isolating which of the 5 annotation stages contribute most to CRI improvement.
- Error analysis showing whether CRI failures concentrate in particular classes or concept types.

## Removed Points

These points were raised by the reviewers but are excluded from the main review for the following reasons:

- **"Slow Mode Superiority hypothesis is theoretically unmotivated"** — The paper hypothesizes slow mode superiority based on dual-process theory (Kahneman, 2011), then empirically tests and contradicts this hypothesis. This is standard scientific practice, not a weakness. The critic's claim that "it is entirely expected that the full signal contains more information" ignores the fact that slow mode involves structured multi-step reasoning that could in principle outperform quick visual inference. The paper's own coarse-grained results (where slow mode matches fast mode) validate this possibility.

- **"Contribution 2 conflates semantic sufficiency and freedom from superficial correlations"** — The CRI measures whether concepts let the model distinguish the correct class from semantic distractors. A concept capturing a superficial correlation would not help in this task. This is a reasonable operationalization.

- **"Use of ResNet-18 for semantic similarity introduces an external reference standard that might not align with LLM similarity"** — Without evidence that LLMs organize similarity differently in a way that would change results, this is a hypothetical concern.

- **"Prompt details being in Appendix B" / missing appendix content** — The appendix is stripped by the PDF parser, not excluded by the authors.

- **Various formatting/style nitpicks** (typos, grammar, whitespace, broken characters) — These are parser artifacts, not author errors.

- **Strength Finder claims that were generic, superficial, or conflict with verified weaknesses** — Removed claims about the problem being "important" or "addressing a gap" when not backed by specific evidence unique to this paper.

## Novel Insights

The most interesting pattern in the paper is the sharp asymmetry between fine-grained and coarse-grained datasets (Table 3). The fact that slow mode achieves 94-95% CRI on CIFAR-100 and Caltech-101 but only 50-62% on fine-grained datasets reveals that the bottleneck is not general textual reasoning ability but rather the precision of the concepts generated. This asymmetry is itself a meaningful finding: LLMs can generate sufficiently discriminative concepts for coarse categories but produce concepts that lack the specificity needed for subordinate-level distinctions. The utility-as-proxy finding (Table 4) further sharpens this: even when concepts are insufficient, they don't hurt performance when combined with visual input, meaning end-to-end accuracy actively masks annotation quality problems. These two observations together constitute a genuine methodological contribution to the XAI evaluation toolkit.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the central claims** to acknowledge the self-referential nature of CRI. The paper would be stronger by stating: "CRI measures whether a model's own textual concepts support classification. When CRI is low on fine-grained datasets but high on coarse-grained datasets, this suggests the concepts lack the specificity needed for subordinate-level distinctions — though a confound with textual reasoning ability remains." This is more honest and harder to attack than the current framing.

2. **Add a cross-model experiment** using at minimum one held-out model (e.g., GPT-4o concepts evaluated by Llama-3.2, and vice versa) to disentangle concept quality from model-specific reasoning. Even a limited version would substantially strengthen the paper.

3. **Report sample sizes** for the main experiments and add the 5-stage ablation analysis.
