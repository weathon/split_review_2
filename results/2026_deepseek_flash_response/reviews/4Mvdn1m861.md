## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework built on SAM with two components: probabilistic prompt generation (iteratively sampling prompts from a similarity-derived probability distribution, with verified regions suppressed) and output token-based verification (using SAM's decoder output tokens rather than image embeddings, with a TS-SS geometric metric). The core idea — repurposing SAM's output tokens for counting verification — is novel and the paper provides systematic ablations on iteration/prompt trade-offs. However, a significant issue with how the TS-SS verification metric is characterized, combined with overstated claims and thin evidence for the central contribution, limits the paper's overall impact.

## Strengths

- **Clean ablation isolating output-token benefit (Table 5):** The paper directly compares output tokens (MAE 16.25) vs. image embeddings (MAE 16.94) with all other components held fixed. This provides clear causal evidence that the output-token approach, not better prompt sampling or post-processing, drives the improvement over prior image-embedding methods like TFOC.

- **Strong CARPK results without training:** On CARPK, the method achieves MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13, outperforming all listed training-based methods (BMNet+: 5.76/7.83, SAFECOUNT: 5.33/7.04, FamNet: 18.19/33.66) without any category-specific training.

- **Systematic evaluation of accuracy-efficiency trade-offs (Table 4):** The ablation across 8/16/32 iterations and 32/64/128 prompts per iteration gives practitioners a quantitative basis for configuring the method to their latency budget — something prior training-free SAM counting works do not provide.

- **Inference speed reported with explicit comparison:** 1.69 seconds per image on FSC-147, noted as "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC, supporting the claim that the single-model approach has a practical efficiency advantage.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **TS-SS metric characterization is inconsistent and the motivation is unsubstantiated.** TS-SS (Eq. 2–3) is defined as TS·SS. For identical vectors, sin(θ)=0 ⇒ TS=0, and ED=MD=0 ⇒ SS=0, so TS-SS=0. For different vectors, TS-SS>0. This makes TS-SS mathematically a *dissimilarity* (distance) measure, yet the paper calls it "TS-SS Sim" (Table 3) and treats it as a similarity metric without clarifying the threshold direction (whether the 300 threshold is an upper bound — accept TS-SS < 300 as positive, treating it as distance — or a lower bound). L2 distance (also a distance measure, MAE 16.53) provides a fairer comparison baseline, but the paper frames TS-SS against cosine similarity (a similarity measure, MAE 16.48) as if all metrics were in the same direction. More importantly, the paper's justification for TS-SS rests on the claim that output tokens "jointly encode semantic (angular) and positional (magnitudinous) information," but no evidence is provided for this geometric decomposition. Without probing experiments or analysis linking specific token dimensions to semantics vs. position, the motivation for adopting a hybrid geometric metric is asserted rather than demonstrated. The net improvement over L2 distance is 0.28 MAE (16.53 → 16.25), which is negligible without statistical significance testing.

### Minor

- **Headline performance claims are imprecise.** The abstract states the method "outperforms existing training-based and training-free counting methods." However, TFCAC (a training-free method using SAM+DINO) achieves MAE 12.26 on FSC-147 vs. the paper's 16.25 — a 32% relative improvement. The body of the paper later categorizes TFCAC under "SAM+α" and acknowledges the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, but the abstract's blanket claim is not supported by the data.

- **High RMSE on FSC-147 is not discussed.** The method achieves RMSE 133.11 on FSC-147, compared to TFCAC's 56.33 (2.4× better). While this is in line with other SAM-only methods (TFOC: 132.16, C-Anything: 131.24), the paper provides no analysis of the variance, per-image error distributions, or failure modes that would explain this high RMSE. The single MAE number in the abstract hides this issue.

- **Evidence for the core contribution is thin.** The headline result in Table 5 (output tokens MAE 16.25 vs. image embeddings MAE 16.94) is a 0.69 MAE difference — about 4% relative improvement. No confidence intervals, statistical significance tests, or per-category breakdowns are provided. The qualitative PCA visualization (Figure 5) is illustrative but not strong standalone evidence. Given that the entire verification contribution rests on this comparison, stronger evidence is needed.

- **Key implementation details are underspecified.** The decay factor for negative prompts (described only as "the value... is decreased") is never quantified. The exemplar prompt type (click, bounding box, or mask) and how it is processed into the mask *M* in Eq. 1 is not fully specified. These affect reproducibility.

- **No variance analysis across random choices.** No standard deviations are reported for any experiment. The CARPK exemplar selection ("randomly selected 12 objects from the training set") introduces randomness that is not analyzed across seeds.

- **No direct prompt efficiency comparison with baselines.** The paper claims probabilistic prompting is more efficient than grid-based methods but does not report prompt counts for competing methods under comparable accuracy.

### Trivial

- Figure descriptions in extracted text contain parser artifacts.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify the TS-SS characterization:** State explicitly whether TS-SS is used as a similarity (accept > threshold) or a distance (accept < threshold). If it is a distance, rename it from "TS-SS Sim" to "TS-SS distance" and re-frame the comparison in Table 3 accordingly (e.g., compare against inverted cosine similarity 1−cos, or explain why the raw comparison is fair). Provide the empirical distribution of TS-SS values for positive vs. negative pairs to justify the 300 threshold.

2. **Provide evidence for the angular=semantic, magnitude=positional claim.** Without this, the motivation for adopting TS-SS over simpler metrics is not well-supported. Consider probing experiments (e.g., manipulating token magnitude vs. direction and measuring effects on counting accuracy).

3. **Add variance information.** Report standard deviations or confidence intervals for key comparisons, especially Table 5 (output tokens vs. image embeddings). A bootstrap analysis or per-category breakdown would significantly strengthen the evidence.

4. **Discuss the high RMSE.** Analyze per-image error distributions, identify failure modes, and be transparent about when the method fails relative to when it succeeds.

5. **Adjust the abstract's claims** to accurately reflect the benchmark position relative to TFCAC and other training-free methods.
