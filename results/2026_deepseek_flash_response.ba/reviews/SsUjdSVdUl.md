## Summary

This paper proposes Critique-RL, a two-stage reinforcement learning approach for training language models to critique and provide feedback on LLM outputs, targeting math reasoning tasks. The key insight is that prior RL-based methods using only indirect reward signals (based on actor refinement correctness) optimize helpfulness at the expense of discriminability (the ability to judge whether a response is correct). Critique-RL addresses this by first optimizing discriminability directly (Stage I), then optimizing helpfulness while regularizing to maintain discriminability (Stage II). Experiments on Qwen2.5-3B/7B across MATH, GSM8K, AQuA (in-domain) and SVAMP, TheoremQA (OOD) show consistent improvements over SFT, STaR, Retroformer, and CTRL baselines.

## Strengths

- **Empirical diagnosis of why indirect reward signals fail (Section 4.1, Figure 3):** The paper provides a concrete, data-driven analysis showing that RL with indirect rewards (r_refine, r_correction, r_Δ) produces critics that are either overly conservative or overly aggressive, with discriminability (Acc@Dis) flatlining or degrading throughout training. This diagnosis directly motivates the two-stage design and is a genuine empirical contribution not present in prior work.

- **Clean two-stage decomposition with targeted reward functions and strong ablation evidence:** Stage I uses a direct discriminability reward r_dis = 𝟙(f(x,y,c) = r_oracle(x,y)) (Eq. 7), and Stage II combines r_refine + β₁·r_dis with KL regularization toward the Stage-I model (Eq. 9). The ablation in Table 3 convincingly quantifies each component's contribution: removing Stage I drops MATH Acc@Refine from 48.6 to 47.6, removing Stage II drops it to 45.9, and removing discrimination regularization in Stage II drops it to 47.3 and Acc@Dis to 77.7. This provides clear evidence that both stages and the regularization matter.

- **Substantial and consistent gains on the primary metrics, without requiring stronger supervision:** On MATH (7B), Critique-RL achieves 58.40 vs CTRL's 53.86 (+4.54 points); on GSM8K (7B), 87.72 vs 81.35 (+6.37 points). The SFT critique data is generated by Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (not GPT-4o), supporting the "no stronger labeling" claim. Acc@Dis improvements are particularly large (e.g., 82.8% vs. 69.3% for CTRL on MATH-3B), showing Stage I's discrimination reward is highly effective.

- **Out-of-domain generalization and iterative improvement:** Table 4 shows Critique-RL transfers to SVAMP and TheoremQA (e.g., 89.7 Acc vs. 85.1 CTRL on SVAMP-7B). Table 2 demonstrates that a second iteration of two-stage training continues to improve accuracy (48.6 → 51.0 on MATH-3B).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
None. The paper's core claims are supported by the evidence.

### Minor

- **Absence of variance or statistical significance estimates:** Every result in Tables 1–4 is a single point. Given the stochasticity of RL training and evaluation sampling, the reliability of smaller-margin improvements is unclear. For example, on AQuA (7B), Critique-RL leads CTRL by only 0.79 points (65.75 vs. 64.96); on TheoremQA (7B), the gap is 0.3 points (21.4 vs. 21.1). These margins fall well within typical LLM evaluation noise, and without multiple seeds or significance tests the reader cannot distinguish signal from noise.

- **The RL algorithm differs between Critique-RL (RLOO) and the main RL baselines (Retroformer uses PPO, CTRL uses GRPO):** The paper does not fully disentangle whether the gains come from the two-stage reward design or from RLOO being better suited to this setting. **The ablation study in Table 3 partially mitigates this concern** — it keeps RLOO constant and shows that both stages contribute within that framework (removing Stage I drops 1.0 points, removing Stage II drops 2.7 points). This demonstrates the two-stage design matters *within* RLOO. However, a complete factorial design (e.g., running the two-stage rewards with PPO/GRPO or running the baselines' rewards with RLOO) would strengthen the attribution.

- **Method requires an oracle verifier during training:** The discrimination reward (Eq. 7) directly depends on r_oracle(x,y), i.e., ground-truth correctness of every training response. This constrains the method to tasks with automatically checkable answers. While the paper mentions a summarization experiment in Appendix G, the core evidence is limited to math word problems. The abstract's claim of "without stronger labeling" is technically true (no stronger model used for annotation), but the requirement for an oracle verifier is a distinct and important limitation that should be explicitly bounded in the main paper.

- **Diagnostic analysis (Figure 3) only shown for GSM8K with the 3B model:** The paper's key motivating finding — that indirect rewards cause discriminability collapse — is demonstrated for a single dataset and model size. Showing the same failure modes on MATH or with the 7B model would strengthen the generality of this insight.

- **β₂ hyperparameter unreported:** Equation 9 includes β₂ as a scaling factor for the KL regularization toward the Stage-I model, but unlike β₁ (=0.2), β₂ is never stated. Sensitivity analysis for both β₁ and β₂ is absent.

### Trivial

- The "9.02% gain" cited in the abstract is the average improvement over the "No Critic" baseline rather than over the best existing method. The paper's Table 1 contains all the relevant comparisons, so this is a presentation choice, not a substantive flaw — but it is somewhat selective framing.
- The filtering rate (what fraction of SFT critique data was kept) is not reported. This matters for understanding data quality and the difficulty of the task.
- The right panel of Figure 1 has garbled labels ("w/o Critique-RL" appears twice, with different parenthetical suffixes whose meaning is unclear).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Running the diagnostic analysis (Figure 3) on a second dataset and model size (e.g., MATH with 7B) would strengthen the generality of the motivating findings.
- A brief discussion of how the critique model could adapt if the actor model were also updated during training (currently the actor is fixed). This is relevant to the scalable oversight framing.
- Sensitivity analysis for the KL coefficients β₁ and β₂.

## Removed Points

- **RL algorithm confound as a Fatal/Major issue:** The harsh critic framed this as a critical issue, but the paper's ablation study (Table 3) controls for the RL algorithm by keeping RLOO constant and varying only the reward/regularization design, showing the two-stage approach drives the gains. The comparison to Retroformer/CTRL is a standard system-level comparison. Demoted from Major to Minor.
- **"9.02% gain over No Critic is a weak baseline" as a major weakness:** This is a framing choice in the abstract, not an experimental flaw. The paper's Table 1 includes all relevant comparisons against actual baselines. Demoted to Trivial.
- **Missing related work:** Removed per policy — I cannot independently verify existence of uncited works.
- **Reproducibility concerns about undisclosed hyperparameters (beyond β₂):** The paper reports the key hyperparameters (KL coefficient 0.01, β₁=0.2, learning rates, training steps) at a level consistent with community norms.
- **Formatting/style nitpicks from the harsh critic:** Removed per policy (parser artifacts).

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The key insight — that indirect reward signals from actor refinement fail to optimize discriminability, and that a two-stage RL approach can address this — is the paper's own contribution, not a novel cross-paper synthesis.

## Suggestions

1. **Add variance estimates:** Report means and standard deviations/ranges across 2–3 seeds for the main experimental results (Table 1). This is especially important for the smaller-margin comparisons (AQuA, TheoremQA).
2. **Disentangle the RL algorithm confound:** Ideally, run the two-stage rewards with PPO or GRPO, or run the baselines' reward designs within RLOO. At minimum, acknowledge the confound more explicitly.
3. **Report β₂ and hyperparameter sensitivity:** State β₂ and show how performance varies with β₁ and β₂ over a reasonable range.
4. **Extend the diagnostic analysis:** Show Figure 3-style training dynamics for at least one additional setting (e.g., MATH with 7B).
5. **Explicitly bound the method's scope:** Add a limitations paragraph noting the oracle verifier requirement and that the primary evidence is on math tasks with verifiable answers.
