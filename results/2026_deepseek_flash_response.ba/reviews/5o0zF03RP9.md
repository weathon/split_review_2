## Summary

IncentRL augments standard RL with a KL-divergence penalty that aligns predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) with preferred outcome distributions q(o|s), weighted by a scalar β. The paper proposes Bayesian adaptation of β as its central novelty, and evaluates on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid DoorKey. While the conceptual framing is clear, the paper suffers from critical gaps: the Bayesian adaptation mechanism — the claimed main contribution — is never actually described, and the KL(p||q) formulation is mathematically undefined for the paper's own examples.

## Strengths

- **Clean conceptual framework.** The idea of aligning predicted outcomes with preferred outcomes via KL divergence is clearly communicated, and the separation from Free Energy Principle / Active Inference (fixed vs. adaptive preferences) is well-motivated (lines 17–18, 81).

- **MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8 results (Figure 2).** With β=0.01, the method achieves 98% success rate vs. 90.5% for β=0, and reduces mean episode length from 75 to 29 steps — practically meaningful gains on a standard sparse-reward benchmark.

- **Proposition 1.** The result that small β preserves the optimal policy (Section 4.1) is technically correct and provides a formal guarantee absent from many intrinsic motivation approaches, even if it follows from basic continuity arguments.

- **Honest failure reporting.** The paper transparently reports that higher β values (0.3, 1.0) on MountainCar degrade performance (Table 2), rather than cherry-picking only successes. This strengthens credibility.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

1. **Bayesian β adaptation — the claimed central novelty — is never specified as a method.** The abstract states that the "central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable, updated online to balance exploration and exploitation without manual tuning" (line 9). The introduction lists "Bayesian adaptation (novelty)" as the first contribution (line 21). Yet the paper contains: no prior distribution over β, no likelihood function, no inference procedure (MCMC, VI, conjugate update, particle filtering, or any other), no description of how the posterior is maintained or updated online, and no algorithm pseudocode. The entire specification is: "we tracked the posterior evolution of β using our Bayesian adaptation scheme" (Section 5.4). This is not a missing implementation detail — it is the absence of the contribution itself. A reader cannot evaluate whether the adaptation is principled, cannot reproduce it, and cannot assess whether it is genuinely Bayesian or a heuristic presented as Bayesian. For a paper whose central thesis is this adaptation mechanism, this omission is fatal.

2. **KL(p||q) formulation is mathematically undefined for the paper's own illustrative example.** In Section 5.2, the toy MDP specifies p(o|s₀,a₁) = {s₁: 0.3, s₀: 0.7} and q(o|s₀) = {s₁: 1.0, s₀: 0.0}. KL(p||q) = 0.3·log(0.3/1.0) + 0.7·log(0.7/0.0). The second term is undefined/infinite because q assigns zero probability to s₀ while p assigns positive probability (0.7). KL(p||q) conventionally requires the support of p to be a subset of the support of q. This means the penalty for taking action a₁ — the very action the method is designed to incentivize — is infinite under the described formulation. The same issue extends to MountainCar (where q "assigns all probability to the goal" but p is a distribution over a continuous state space) and likely MiniGrid. The reported smooth, plausible results are incompatible with the described formulation, meaning either a different divergence (e.g., KL(q||p)), a smoothed q, or some other modification was used without disclosure. This is not a minor typo — it is a mathematical error in the paper's core equation.

### Major

3. **Internal inconsistency in the Bayesian β results.** The text claims the posterior converges "near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)" (line 230), while Figure 3's caption and Table 3 show the posterior mean converging to approximately 0.02 by Round 4 (mean 0.0173, std 0.0182). Figures 3–4 label a dashed line at "target effective β = 0.1," yet the posterior has drifted to a value 5× smaller than this target. For the MiniGrid setting where these results are presented, the best fixed β was 0.01 (Section 5.4), meaning β ≈ 0.02 is actually near the effective region for MiniGrid — but the text and figure labels incorrectly reference β ≈ 0.1 (which was the best value for MountainCar, not MiniGrid). This confusion between environments and mismatch between text, figures, and data undermines the reported evidence for Bayesian adaptation.

4. **No comparison to any intrinsic motivation baseline.** The paper positions IncentRL against curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al., Burda et al.), empowerment (Mohamed & Rezende), and information gain (Houthooft et al.) in Section 2, yet never runs any of these methods. The only baseline throughout is β=0 (standard RL without the KL term). For a paper claiming to address limitations of intrinsic motivation methods, this makes it impossible to assess whether IncentRL offers any improvement over existing approaches.

5. **No description of how p(o|s,a) is obtained in non-toy environments.** For MountainCar (continuous state space: position and velocity) and MiniGrid, the paper never specifies the architecture, training procedure, loss function, or accuracy of the forward model that produces the predicted outcome distribution. In continuous or partially-observed environments, learning a reliable predictive distribution is a substantial undertaking that is entirely unaddressed.

### Minor

6. **Bayesian adaptation results for MiniGrid are relegated to "not shown"** (line 228: "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value"). The central claim's primary evidence is qualitative (Figures 3–4 showing posterior concentration) with no direct comparison of adapted-β performance to fixed-β sweeps on the same domain.

7. **Only 3 seeds for MountainCar and MiniGrid experiments.** With high variance in MountainCar (β=0.1 has std 535 vs mean 4136, CV ≈ 13%), statistical reliability is unclear. The toy MDP uses 7 seeds, which is adequate.

8. **Propositions 1 and 2 are mathematically correct but basic.** Proposition 1 is a standard continuity argument (small bounded perturbations to the reward function preserve optimality in finite MDPs with positive reward gaps). Proposition 2 follows directly from the definition of the objective. Neither offers insight about learning dynamics, sample efficiency, or exploration behavior.

## Suggestions

1. **Fully specify the Bayesian adaptation mechanism.** Without a prior, likelihood, and inference procedure, the paper's central contribution does not exist. Provide pseudocode and justify why the approach is Bayesian rather than a heuristic scheduling rule.
2. **Fix the KL divergence formulation.** Replace KL(p||q) with a divergence that handles support mismatch (e.g., KL(q||p), Jensen-Shannon divergence, or explicitly specify ε-smoothing of q). Clarify what was actually implemented.
3. **Resolve the β inconsistency.** Correct the text (currently states β≈0.1) and figure labels (currently show "target effective β=0.1") to match the actual posterior values (β≈0.02) and the relevant environment's best fixed β.
4. **Add intrinsic motivation baselines.** Compare against at least one established method (ICM, RND, or simple count-based exploration) to contextualize the results.
5. **Specify the forward model.** Describe how p(o|s,a) is obtained in each environment, including architecture, training, and accuracy.
