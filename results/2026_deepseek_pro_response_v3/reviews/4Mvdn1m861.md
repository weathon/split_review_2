## Summary
TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework built on SAM with two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation using image-embedding similarity with iterative probability distribution updates, and (2) output token-based verification using a TS-SS hybrid metric. Evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK, the method achieves strong results on CARPK (MAE 4.68, outperforming all training-based methods) but mixed results on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25 but RMSE 133.11, barely better than the naive SAM baseline at 137.50).

## Strengths
- **The idea of using SAM's decoder output tokens — rather than image embeddings — as a verification signal is novel and well-supported.** Table 5 shows output-token-space verification yields MAE 16.25 vs 16.94 in image-embedding space, and Figure 5 provides PCA evidence that positive/negative prompts form clearly separable clusters in output-token space but overlap in image-embedding space.
- **On CARPK, the method achieves MAE 4.68, outperforming all training-based methods** (FamNet: 18.19, BMNet+: 5.76, SAFECOUNT: 5.33, LOCA: 9.97) by substantial margins. This demonstrates that properly exploiting SAM's internal representations can surpass supervised approaches on structured counting tasks.
- **The iterative probabilistic prompt generation with distribution updates is validated by Table 4:** performance degrades sharply from MAE 16.25 (16 iterations × 128 prompts) to 29.10 (8 iterations × 32 prompts), confirming that adaptive prompting matters.
- **The one-shot counting results (Table 2, MAE 18.57)** provide additional robustness evidence, outperforming training-based CFOCNet (28.60) and FamNet (26.76).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **RMSE on FSC-147 indicates severe failure modes that are not acknowledged or analyzed.** The method's RMSE of 133.11 is barely better than the naive SAM baseline (137.50) and actually worse than TFOC (132.16), despite a substantially better MAE (16.25 vs 19.95). The RMSE-to-MAE ratio of ~8:1 far exceeds TFCAC's ~4.6:1 or LOCA's ~5.3:1, indicating the method produces wildly incorrect counts on a subset of images. For a counting method proposed for practical deployment, catastrophic failures are arguably more damaging than a slightly elevated average error. The paper never discusses this pattern.
- **The runtime comparison against TFCAC is unsubstantiated.** The paper claims its method is "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC (line 230) and attributes TFCAC's performance to "lower computational efficiency," but reports only its own runtime (1.69 s/image) without any TFCAC runtime measurement. The paper's abstract claim of "comparable performance to training-free approaches that require additional models" is also questionable given the 4-point MAE gap with TFCAC (16.25 vs 12.26).
- **The TS-SS metric's contribution over cosine similarity is not convincingly demonstrated.** Table 3 shows TS-SS (MAE 16.25) vs cosine similarity (16.48) — a difference of 0.23 MAE. No confidence intervals or statistical tests are reported. The paper's claim that "non-hybrid metrics have reached a performance plateau" and TS-SS is needed to "break through this barrier" overstates this evidence. The geometric machinery (triangle areas, sector areas, originally from Heidarian & Dinneen 2016 for document clustering) adds complexity without a commensurate demonstrated benefit.

### Minor
- **Key implementation details are unspecified.** The number of exemplars used for FSC-147 (the primary benchmark) is never stated; only CARPK's 12 exemplars are reported (line 175). The temperature parameter τ in Eq. (1) is never assigned a value. The validation set used to tune the t-scale factor (0.066) and verification threshold (300) is not specified (line 180).
- **The "SAM only" vs "SAM+α" taxonomy** (Table 1) is an unconventional categorization that separates the strongest training-free competitor (TFCAC, MAE 12.26) into a different category. While the distinction between methods that use additional models and those that don't has some merit, it also serves to obscure a significant performance gap.
- **The similarity computation in Eq. (1)** (cosine similarity between exemplar mask embedding and image embedding with ReLU + temperature scaling + softmax) closely follows the approach used in TFOC and other prior work. The novel contribution is the iterative probability update mechanism, not the base similarity computation.

### Trivial
- The discussion section (line 305) has a referent ambiguity: "Our method... demonstrates superior computational efficiency... This limitation affects scalability" — it's unclear whether "This limitation" refers to the previously mentioned dense-scene degradation or to computational efficiency.
- The paper does not specify which decoder layer's output tokens are used for verification, nor how many tokens are extracted per prompt.
- The metric is labeled "TS-SS Sim" in Table 3 while the formulas (Eq. 2-3) produce a dissimilarity score (zero for identical vectors, large for dissimilar ones), creating ambiguity about the verification threshold direction.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Analyze the RMSE outliers with a histogram or case study of worst-performing images to understand and potentially mitigate failure modes.
- Report TFCAC's actual runtime for a genuine efficiency comparison, or remove the unsubstantiated "more than twice as fast" claim.
- Include an ablation isolating the probability update mechanism (e.g., against uniform grid prompting with the same output token verification) to separately evaluate the probabilistic prompting contribution.
- Report variance estimates or confidence intervals for the small differences in Table 3.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:
- **Harsh Critic's claim that "the core of probabilistic prompt generation is not novel"** — While the base similarity computation (cosine similarity on image embeddings) is standard, the iterative update with zeroing out positives and decreasing negatives is a genuine addition. Removed as a standalone criticism; merged into Minor point about delineating borrowed vs. new components.
- **Strength Finder's claim that "TS-SS meaningfully improves over standard similarity measures"** — The 0.23 MAE improvement over cosine similarity is too small to support "meaningful" without statistical evidence. Removed as a standalone strength; the output-token-space contribution (Table 5) is the more solid contribution.
- **Harsh Critic's "Section-by-Section Notes" about missing § references and figure caption repetition** — These are parser artifacts, not paper problems. Removed entirely.
- **Strength Finder's claim that the method "achieves competitiveness without auxiliary foundation models" as a separate strength** — This is already covered by the CARPK results and the output-token verification strengths. Merged rather than listed separately.
- **Harsh Critic's note about the RMSE confidence interval for TFOC** — The reviewer claimed TFOC's RMSE ratio is ~6.6, but from Table 1 TFOC's RMSE is 132.16 and MAE is 19.95, giving ~6.6. This is a correct observation but the point is already captured in the Major RMSE weakness.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most interesting insight is that SAM's decoder output tokens — which jointly encode semantic (angular) and positional (magnitude) information through bidirectional attention with image embeddings — provide a more discriminative verification space than the commonly used image embeddings. Table 5 quantitatively demonstrates this (16.25 vs 16.94 MAE), and Figure 5 provides qualitative PCA evidence of better separability. This insight is potentially applicable beyond counting to other tasks that use SAM for instance-level reasoning, and represents a genuinely underexplored direction in the literature.

## Suggestions
- The output-token-space verification is the paper's strongest contribution. Consider simplifying the verification metric to cosine similarity on output tokens (which already achieves 16.48 MAE) and claiming the output-token space as the primary contribution, de-emphasizing TS-SS unless stronger statistical evidence can be provided.
- Provide a thorough analysis of high-error images to understand the RMSE problem — this would substantially strengthen the paper and could reveal whether the failures are systematic (e.g., always on dense small-object scenes, consistent with the limitations paragraph) or random.
- Either measure and report TFCAC's runtime or remove the unsupported speed comparison claim.
