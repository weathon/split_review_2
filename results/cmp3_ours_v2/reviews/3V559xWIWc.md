## Summary

This paper identifies a genuine training-inference mismatch in tree-based speculative decoding: existing draft model training methods optimize for predicting the single most likely token sequence, while inference-time tree-based SpD requires good probability estimates across multiple branches. The authors propose TALF (tree-aware loss function), which trains the draft model on tree-structured targets generated by the target model, and SALF (stopping at low further gains), a dynamic tree construction method with a provably monotonic stopping criterion. Combined, the methods deliver 15.6–39.4% and 6.5–24.4% end-to-end speedups over EAGLE-2 and HASS respectively across three target models and five tasks.

## Strengths

1. **Well-motivated problem with concrete evidence (Section 3.1, Figure 2).** The paper identifies a real and previously overlooked mismatch, backed by specific data: HASS's gains concentrate on 1st-ranked tokens while degrading or stagnating on lower-ranked tokens that constitute ~45% of tree nodes. This empirical grounding is stronger than a purely conceptual motivation.

2. **Clean, principled solution (TALF, Algorithm 1).** The tree-aware loss is a natural fix: train the draft model on the same tree structure it will use at inference. The approach of using the target model to pre-generate tree structures and aggregating per-node cross-entropy losses is conceptually simple and directly aligns training with the inference objective.

3. **Thorough ablation isolating both contributions (Table 2).** Testing all 9 combinations of (beam search / optimal tree search / SALF) × (EAGLE-2 loss / HASS loss / TALF loss) cleanly separates the benefits of the new loss function from those of the new tree construction method. TALF improves mean generation length (τ) consistently across all three tree construction methods (7–13% over EAGLE-2, 4–7% over HASS). This level of disentanglement is rare and valuable in SpD papers.

4. **Consistent speedups across diverse settings.** Improvements hold across 3 target models (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B), 5 tasks, and 2 temperatures. The pattern of larger gains for stronger target models (DeepSeek) is internally coherent with the paper's thesis.

5. **No architecture change required.** The method works within the existing EAGLE draft model architecture, making integration into serving frameworks (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, SGLang) straightforward.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Unequal training budgets partially confound the headline EAGLE-2 comparisons.** For Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B, EAGLE-2 is evaluated after 10 training epochs, while HASS and TALF receive 10+3=13 epochs (lines 196–198). The 15.6–39.4% improvement claims over EAGLE-2 are therefore partially confounded — part of the gain may come from longer training rather than from TALF's tree-aware objective. **However**, this does not invalidate the paper: (a) the HASS comparisons (6.5–24.4%) are clean since both methods receive 13 epochs and already show meaningful improvements; (b) the DeepSeek setup uses equal wall-clock time (24 hours); (c) Table 2's ablation shows TALF consistently outperforms regardless of tree construction method. The authors should retrain EAGLE-2 for matched epochs to cleanly resolve this.

### Minor

- **TALF drops the regression loss (used by both EAGLE and HASS for feature alignment) without an ablation study.** Line 114 states it was "sufficient" without empirical support. An ablation comparing TALF with and without ℒ_reg would clarify whether this design choice is optimal or if the regression loss could further improve performance.

- **No confidence intervals or variance reported.** With 5 benchmarks per condition and single-run evaluations, it is difficult to assess whether the reported differences between methods are statistically significant. While this is common practice in the SpD literature, the paper would be strengthened by reporting variance across runs or bootstrapped intervals.

- **No explicit verification that the output distribution matches the target model's distribution.** The paper claims "without any generation quality degradation" but tree-based verification can deviate from exact guarantees depending on the acceptance scheme. A brief empirical check (e.g., perplexity on a held-out set, or output agreement rate with the target model) would substantiate this claim.

- **Preprocessing cost for TALF training not quantified.** Having the target model generate trees over 68k dialogues (line 110) has a non-trivial computational cost. Reporting this overhead would help practitioners evaluate adoption.

### Trivial

- **SALF threshold is model-dependent.** Table 4 shows th=0.5 yields the best speedup for DeepSeek (2.62×), but the paper defaults to th=0.6 for "more consistent" results across models. The authors acknowledge this limitation (lines 264–265) and note tuning as future work, so this is not a substantive weakness.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct measurement of drafting overhead (number of draft model forward passes per iteration, or average drafting latency) for SALF vs. optimal search vs. beam search would make the SALF contribution more transparent to practitioners.
- A multi-step analysis for the Figure 2 training-inference misalignment study (currently limited to single-step conditioning) would strengthen the motivation, though the single-step evidence is sufficient to establish the problem.

## Removed Points

- **"Optimal tree search not clearly identified as SpecExec"** — The paper explicitly states (lines 72–73) that the optimal tree construction approach is from SpecExec and the Table 2 row label "Optimal tree search" follows naturally from this description. The call for SpecExec as a full-method baseline in Table 1 overreaches the paper's scope, as Table 1 compares methods within the same EAGLE architecture family.
- **"Single-step analysis limitation for Figure 2"** — The harsh critic acknowledged this was "sufficient to establish the problem."
- **"SALF threshold model-dependent"** — Already acknowledged by the authors and listed above as Trivial.

## Novel Insights

A key observation from the review is that the training budget confound primarily affects the EAGLE-2 comparison. The HASS comparisons (both methods at 13 epochs) are clean and already show meaningful improvements (6.5–24.4%), which means the core TALF contribution is valid even without fully resolving the EAGLE-2 confound. Additionally, Table 2's decomposition shows TALF improves τ regardless of tree construction method, further supporting the claim independently of the inference-side SALF contribution.

## Suggestions

1. **Retrain EAGLE-2 for the same total number of epochs as TALF** (13), or equivalently train all methods from scratch for a matched number of steps. This single fix would substantially strengthen the headline claims.
2. **Add a regression loss ablation for TALF** — compare TALF with and without ℒ_reg to validate the design choice stated in line 114.
3. **Report confidence intervals or per-trial statistics** for the main speedup measurements.
4. **Quantify the preprocessing cost** for TALF's tree generation over the 68k-dialogue training set.
