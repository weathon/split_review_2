## Summary

RedSage presents a data-centric pipeline for building an open-source, 8B-parameter cybersecurity LLM. The pipeline includes (1) 11.7B tokens of cybersecurity-filtered web data (CyberFineWeb) for continual pretraining, (2) a curated seed of 28.6K high-quality cybersecurity documents (RedSage-Seed), (3) an agentic framework that produces 266K multi-turn SFT conversations, and (4) a new benchmark (RedSage-Bench: 30K MCQs + 240 open-ended items) spanning knowledge, skills, and tools. The model is evaluated on RedSage-Bench, external cybersecurity benchmarks, and general LLM benchmarks, showing consistent improvements over Qwen3-8B.

## Strengths

1. **Large-scale open data release.** The paper curates 11.7B tokens of filtered cybersecurity pretraining data (CyberFineWeb) plus ~0.85B tokens of curated seed and dump data, and commits to releasing all data, model weights, and code. This is substantially larger than prior open efforts (PRIMUS: 2.57B; Foundation-Sec: 5.10B) and contrasts with works that keep data closed.

2. **Agentic augmentation pipeline (Section 3.2, Figure 4).** The Planner-Agent + Augmenter-Agent framework that produces 266K grounded, multi-turn, role-based dialogues is a more principled approach to SFT data creation than simple Q&A scraping used by prior work. The explicit design of augmentation plans per skill set goes beyond fixed-templated approaches.

3. **Comprehensive benchmark taxonomy (Table 1, Figure 2).** RedSage-Bench covers knowledge, skills, and tool proficiency — a dimension missing from all prior cybersecurity benchmarks. The 240 human-verified open-ended items with an LLM-as-Judge rubric add qualitative assessment beyond MCQ-only benchmarks.

4. **Consistent gains on independently constructed external benchmarks.** On CTI-Bench, CyberMetric, SECURE, SecBench, and other external benchmarks (Table 5), RedSage variants improve over Qwen3-8B by +3.75 (base) to +5.59 (instruct) points — gains that are not subject to the seed-sharing confound discussed below.

5. **Decontamination step (Section 3.3).** The semantic-similarity decontamination between generated training data and benchmark items (threshold >0.9) shows awareness of data leakage concerns, a step many domain-specialized LLM papers omit.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Circularity between RedSage-Bench and the training data.** Both RedSage-Conv (the SFT training conversations) and RedSage-Bench (the primary evaluation benchmark) are derived from the same source — RedSage-Seed, a corpus of 28,637 curated cybersecurity documents. MCQs are generated by prompting an LLM on each seed document (Section 3.3); the augmented SFT conversations are generated by prompting an LLM on the same seed documents (Section 3.2). The decontamination step removes only 2.96% of data relative to the benchmark — it filters surface-level textual overlap but cannot address the deeper problem that both training and evaluation draw from a shared knowledge pool. A model's performance on RedSage-Bench partly reflects its ability to absorb RedSage-Seed rather than generalized cybersecurity competence. The paper partially mitigates this by also evaluating on external benchmarks (CTI-Bench, CyberMetric, etc.) where gains hold, but RedSage-Bench is presented as a primary evaluation and conclusions draw on it without caveating this confound.

2. **No ablation isolating the agentic augmentation pipeline.** The agentic augmentation pipeline is presented as a core methodological contribution (Section 3.2, abstract), but no experiment isolates its effect. The instruction-tuned variants are trained on the *combination* of RedSage-Conv (augmented data) and SmollLM3 general SFT data. There is no variant trained on (a) only raw seed data formatted as SFT without augmentation, or (b) an equivalent volume of non-augmented cybersecurity Q&A. Without these controls, the reader cannot distinguish whether gains come from the agentic framework, simply having 266K domain-specific SFT samples, or the general SFT data. The claim that "domain-aware agentic augmentation...enhance[s] cybersecurity-specific expertise" (Abstract) is not directly testable from the reported experiments.

3. **General-benchmark improvements confounded by addition of general SFT/DPO data.** Section 4.3 reports RedSage-8B-DPO achieves 74.33 on the Open LLM Leaderboard vs. Qwen3-8B's 65.92 — a +8.41 gain. But RedSage uses SmollLM3 SFT data and Tulu3 DPO data that the Qwen3-8B baseline does not receive. The simplest interpretation is that general SFT and DPO data — not the cybersecurity augmentation — drive these general-benchmark improvements. The claim that cybersecurity training improves general reasoning (Abstract) requires a control: a Qwen3-8B model fine-tuned on SmollLM3 + Tulu3 alone.

### Minor

4. **LLM-as-Judge evaluation on open-ended Q&A has a potential consistency bias.** The open-ended evaluation (Section 4.1, Figure 6) uses an LLM judge to score model answers against reference answers derived from RedSage-Seed. Since RedSage is trained on conversations from that same seed, the judge may systematically favor RedSage's outputs because they match the reference distribution more closely in style and factual coverage. The paper should report agreement between the LLM judge and human judges on a held-out sample.

5. **No confidence intervals or significance tests.** No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported for any experiment. Given that some improvements are small (e.g., +0.97 macro-accuracy from CPT on RedSage-Bench), it is unclear whether these differences are reliable.

6. **Missing instruct-model ablations on data sources.** The paper reports base-model variants (CFW-only, Seed-only, both) but does not report instruct variants of CFW-only and Seed-only. The contribution of each pretraining data source to the final instructed model cannot be assessed.

### Trivial

7. **Limitations section (Section 5) is brief.** The limitations section is only three sentences and does not acknowledge the seed-sharing confound, the lack of augmentation ablation, or the general-benchmark confound. Given the paper's strong claims, a more substantive discussion would strengthen the paper.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Construct a small independent evaluation set (500–1000 items) from sources not in RedSage-Seed (e.g., recent CVEs, new OWASP entries) to demonstrate generalization to unseen knowledge.
- Report GPU-hours or estimated training cost for reproducibility.
- Report human evaluation of a sample of open-ended responses to validate the LLM-as-Judge scores.
- Train a Qwen3-8B instruct model on SmollLM3 + Tulu3 alone (no cybersecurity data) and report its performance.

## Removed Points
- *Criticism that "+5.59 points" combines RedSage-Bench with external benchmarks.* This is factually inaccurate. Section 4.2 states "+5.59" specifically in the context of external cybersecurity benchmarks (Table 5), which are not derived from RedSage-Seed. This figure is not affected by the seed-sharing confound.
- *Missing statistical significance / variance listed as a "critical issue."* Demoted to Minor. While the lack of CIs is a real limitation, it is a common weakness in LLM papers of this type and does not threaten the core claims.
- *Formatting/style nitpicks.* These are parser artifacts, not author errors.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The primary novel observation from the reviews is the structural circularity between RedSage-Bench and the training seed — a concern the paper itself does not adequately address and that the authors should prioritize resolving.

## Suggestions
1. **Add a control for the agentic augmentation.** Train an instruct variant that replaces RedSage-Conv with the same volume of raw seed-document chunks formatted as simple Q&A. If the agentic pipeline adds value, the augmented variant should clearly outperform this simpler alternative.
2. **Add a control for the general SFT confound.** Fine-tune Qwen3-8B on SmollLM3 + Tulu3 alone (no cybersecurity data) and report its performance on both general and cybersecurity benchmarks.
3. **Caveat RedSage-Bench results explicitly** regarding the shared seed origin, or construct a held-out evaluation from sources not in the seed.
4. **Report confidence intervals or standard deviations** for key comparisons, especially where deltas are ~1 point.
5. **Report LLM-judge vs. human-judge agreement** on a held-out sample of open-ended responses.

## Score and Decision

**Calibration protocol.** Calibration was performed in two rounds.

**Round 1 (bracketing):** 24 anchor papers were retrieved across six score bands (strong reject through strong accept) using topically similar queries ("domain-specific LLM for cybersecurity training data curation benchmark dataset"). The most informative anchors were:
- AttackQA (avg 4.25, reject) — cybersecurity Q&A dataset with the same training/evaluation circularity weakness, narrower scope
- "Unearthing Large Scale Domain-Specific Knowledge" (avg 5.0, reject) — domain data curation, similar scope to part of our paper
- Catastrophic Cyber Capabilities Benchmark (avg 5.33, reject) — cybersecurity LLM benchmark paper
- 3DS: Medical Domain Adaptation (avg 5.75, reject) — domain adaptation with evaluation gaps
- CURIE (avg 6.4, accept) — scientific benchmark with cleaner evaluation design
- TiC-LM (avg 6.25, reject) — continual pretraining benchmark

**Round 2 (narrowing):** Additional anchors in the 5.0–6.0 range confirmed the calibration: CLDyB (5.67, accept), FinMTEB (5.25, reject), Structure-aware Knowledge Injection (5.25, reject), NIRANTAR (5.80, reject).

**Bracket:** 3.5–7.5 (Round 1) → narrowed to 5.0–6.0 (Round 2).

**Final score:** 5.5. The paper's contributions (scale of open data, comprehensive benchmark taxonomy, agentic augmentation pipeline, consistent external benchmark gains) are more substantial than AttackQA (4.25) or "Unearthing" (5.0), and comparable to 3DS (5.75). However, like AttackQA, it suffers from a training-evaluation circularity on its own benchmark — a weakness that contributed to AttackQA's rejection. The external benchmark results and open-data commitment push it above AttackQA, but the missing ablations and confounded general-benchmark claims prevent it from reaching the CURIE/TiC-LM tier (~6.25–6.4).
