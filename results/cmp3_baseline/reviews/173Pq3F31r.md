## Summary

This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models expressive piano performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content (pitch, rhythm) from global performance style. A diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module is introduced to generate style embeddings conditioned solely on the score, enabling automatic style-appropriate rendering without manual control. The framework uses sequence-level supervision (no note-level alignment required) and training on both paired and unpaired data. Experimental results on the ASAP and ATEPP datasets show competitive performance on both tasks, effective content-style disentanglement verified through classification and style transfer, and stylistically appropriate PSR-generated outputs.

## Strengths

- **Novel and well-motivated problem formulation**: The joint modeling of EPR and APT as inverse tasks is a natural but underexplored direction; the paper convincingly draws parallels to ASR/TTS joint modeling in speech and demonstrates mutual benefits between the two tasks.
- **Disentangled representation learning with clear architectural choices**: Content is encoded as note-level sequences while style is a single global vector, with KL regularization on the style space. The disentanglement is validated through performer/composer classification experiments (Table 4) and style transfer listening tests (Figure 5), providing strong evidence that the representations capture distinct factors.
- **Alignment-free Seq2Seq EPR formulation**: Removing the need for note-level alignment is a practical advance that increases scalability and robustness to musically complex passages (trills, ornaments). The competitive EPR results (Tables 2-3) demonstrate that this relaxed supervision does not sacrifice quality.
- **PSR module for automatic style recommendation**: Generating style directly from score content mimics how skilled pianists interpret a piece and makes style-aware rendering accessible to non-experts. Evaluation on era classification (Figure 4) and subjective ratings (Figure 2) confirm that PSR produces appropriate and diverse styles.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Both objective metrics (MUSTER, ScoreSimilarity, variance/KL/MAE, alignment rates) and subjective listening tests (11 participants, multiple dimensions and composers) are used, along with representation visualizations and style transfer experiments.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Unclear use of unpaired data**: The paper claims to support training on both paired and unpaired data and curates large unpaired datasets (75,913 MuseScore scores, YouTube performances). However, Section 4.2 (Training Setup) only describes training on the ASAP dataset; it never explicitly states whether the unpaired datasets were actually used during training or how they were incorporated. The loss function (Eq. 6) includes unpaired reconstruction terms, but it is ambiguous whether these are computed on the curated unpaired data or just on masked versions of paired data in the same batch. A clear description of the training data composition and an ablation study quantifying the contribution of unpaired data are needed to substantiate the claim.

- **No note-level alignment is claimed, but evaluation still requires alignment**: While the model operates at the sequence level, the EPR evaluation in Table 3 (alignment/insertion/missing rates) presumably requires aligning predicted and reference notes post-hoc, and the MAE metrics in Table 2 require note-level correspondence. The paper does not explain how this post-hoc alignment is performed or whether the evaluation is fair to the alignment-free approach. The gap between "no alignment during training" and "alignment needed for evaluation" should be explicitly addressed.

- **Comparison scope for EPR is limited**: The paper compares only against VirtuosoNet and DExter. Other recent transformer-based EPR models (e.g., Tang et al. 2023, Borovik & Viro 2023, Renault et al. 2023) are discussed in related work but not included as baselines, making it hard to judge where the proposed approach stands relative to the current state of the art.

### Minor
- **KL divergence regularization is applied only to the style space, not to content**: While this architectural choice is reasonable (content has no direct prior), there is no explicit mutual information minimization or orthogonality constraint between content and style. The empirical evidence supports disentanglement, but the lack of stronger regularization is a concern for generality.
- **Subjective evaluation (N=11) is relatively small**: While the results are consistent across composers and attributes, a larger participant pool would increase statistical confidence.
- **Diffusion-based PSR model details are sparse**: The architecture of the denoising network and the conditioning mechanism are described only at a high level, making reproduction harder.

### Trivial
- None significant.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation study isolating the contribution of the unpaired data would strengthen the paper considerably.
- Visualizing the content space (e.g., via pitch/rhythm prediction) alongside the style space would further validate disentanglement.
- Including a comparison with the EPR-Only variant in Table 3 (alignment/insertion/missing rates) would help isolate the effect of joint training.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the observation that composer identification accuracy is higher using style embeddings (77.46%) than content embeddings (29.99%) is insightful: it suggests that pianists' expressive choices encode composer-specific conventions, and that a model can learn to infer these conventions from score content alone. This connects to the broader question of how much "stylistic knowledge" is implicitly present in symbolic scores and supports the feasibility of score-conditioned style generation. The era-wise clustering of PSR-generated styles (Figure 4) further shows that historical stylistic trends are learnable from symbolic data without explicit era labels.

## Suggestions
- Provide a clear specification of how the unpaired data (MuseScore scores, YouTube performances) is used during training, including whether it is mixed with paired ASAP data, the sampling ratio, and any preprocessing. Report an ablation comparing training with only paired data vs. with added unpaired data.
- Explain the post-hoc alignment procedure used to compute alignment/insertion/missing rates in Table 3 and discuss whether it biases results toward or against different methods.
- Consider adding a simple content-style orthogonality regularization (e.g., cosine loss between pooled content and style) to strengthen disentanglement.

## Score and Decision

This paper presents a well-motivated, technically sound framework with clear contributions to joint EPR/APT modeling, disentangled representation learning for music, and automatic style recommendation. The core weaknesses are the ambiguous use of unpaired data and the limited EPR baseline comparison, which should be addressed. The paper is within the top tier of ICLR submissions in terms of novelty and thoroughness.
