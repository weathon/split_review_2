## Summary

This paper introduces VisualPRM400K, a dataset of approximately 400K multimodal process supervision samples with step-wise correctness annotations, and VisualProcessBench, a human-annotated benchmark for evaluating step-level error detection in multimodal reasoning. Building on these resources, the authors train VisualPRM, an 8B multimodal Process Reward Model (PRM) that serves as a critic for Best-of-N (BoN) test-time scaling. Experiments across seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks and multiple policy model families (MiniCPM-V2.6, QwenVL2.5, InternVL2.5 at various scales) show consistent improvements of 3.7–8.4 points, with PRM outperforming Outcome Reward Models and Self-Consistency.

## Strengths

- **Timely and important problem**: The paper addresses the underexplored area of test-time scaling for multimodal reasoning via process reward models, which is of high current interest to the community.
- **Substantial new resources**: VisualPRM400K (≈400K samples) and VisualProcessBench (2,866 samples with 26,950 human-annotated step labels) are significant contributions that will enable future research on multimodal PRMs.
- **Comprehensive and convincing experiments**: The evaluation covers seven diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, four policy model families at multiple scales, and includes comparisons with ORM and Self-Consistency. The results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed PRM.
- **Clear and well-structured presentation**: The paper is easy to follow, with well-motivated design choices and thorough ablation studies (value vs. advantage PRM, aggregation methods, early stopping, BoN size).

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Potential noise in training data labels**: The automatic Monte Carlo pipeline for generating step correctness may introduce label noise, especially since the binary threshold (mc_i > 0) is used without a confidence margin. The paper notes that setting a threshold hurts performance, suggesting the raw mc_i values are noisy. The impact of this noise on PRM training is not deeply analyzed.
- **Limited exploration of PRM training strategies**: The paper acknowledges this limitation. Only value-based and advantage-based formulations are compared, and the base model is fixed to InternVL2.5-8B. Alternative training objectives, loss functions, or base architectures are not explored, leaving room for potentially stronger PRMs.

### Minor

- **Inter-annotator agreement not reported**: For VisualProcessBench, the paper describes a careful annotation process but does not report inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen’s kappa), which would help assess label reliability.
- **Potential distribution bias**: The training data solutions are generated by InternVL2.5 series models. While the paper tests on other policy models, the PRM may be biased toward reasoning patterns of that family. The paper does not discuss this or attempt to mitigate it.
- **Modest absolute gains on strong baselines**: For InternVL2.5-78B, the improvement on MMMU is only +0.7 points. While the overall average gain is 5.9 points, the gains are uneven across benchmarks.

### Trivial

- None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Compare with PRMs trained on different base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) to assess generality of the training pipeline.
- Report inter-annotator agreement for VisualProcessBench and analyze annotation difficulty per benchmark.
- Include an analysis of the computational cost (FLOPs or wall-clock time) of BoN evaluation with VisualPRM vs. using an MLLM-as-judge.
- Explore multi-task training that jointly predicts step correctness and final answer correctness.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper’s own contributions, the key insight is that existing open-source MLLMs are poor critics for multimodal reasoning because they tend to label most steps as correct, yielding near-random F1 scores on VisualProcessBench. This observation underscores the need for dedicated PRM training and validates the paper’s approach of constructing a process supervision dataset. The finding that value-based PRMs outperform advantage-based PRMs when training data is noisy is also practically useful.

## Suggestions

- Provide an analysis of the noise in the training data (e.g., distribution of mc_i values, agreement with human judgments on a small subset) to help users understand label quality.
- Release the dataset and benchmark with clear documentation, including the annotation guidelines and inter-annotator agreement statistics.
- Consider releasing the PRM trained on different base models to facilitate broader adoption.
