## Summary

This paper proposes a pipeline to enhance 3T BOLD fMRI signals to approximate 7T quality for visual retinotopic decoding. The method uses conformal mapping to project cortical surface ROIs onto a 2D disk (Brain Disk), then applies an unpaired Schrödinger Bridge diffusion model (BDSB) to translate low-quality 3T brain disks into high-quality 7T-like disks. Evaluation on synthetic, cross-dataset, and small paired real data shows improvements in standard similarity metrics and downstream pRF analysis over several baselines.

## Strengths

- **Novel problem and approach**: Enhancing 3T fMRI for retinotopic decoding with unpaired learning is a practical and underexplored application. The use of conformal mapping to establish a shared parametric domain across subjects and datasets is a clever geometric strategy that addresses a key alignment challenge.
- **Comprehensive experimental design**: The authors construct three evaluation settings (synthetic with ground truth, cross-dataset real without ground truth, and small paired real data) to partially compensate for the scarcity of paired 3T/7T fMRI. This multi-pronged evaluation strengthens the credibility of the results.
- **Clear downstream validation**: The paper goes beyond image quality metrics and evaluates the impact on pRF decoding (\(R^2\), receptive field stability), which directly demonstrates the practical value of the enhancement for visual neuroscience.
- **Ablation study**: The ablation (Table 3) isolates the contributions of conformal mapping versus harmonic mapping and the two regularization terms, providing insight into which components are most important.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
1. **Limited real paired validation**: The only real paired dataset (TDM) has only 2 subjects and one session each. The improvement over baselines on TDM is marginal (SSIM slightly below OTT-GAN, PSNR comparable). This weakens the claim that the method works on real 3T→7T translation under truly paired conditions.  
2. **Synthetic data may not reflect real 3T characteristics**: The synthetic data is generated by down-sampling 7T data and adding Gaussian noise. Real 3T fMRI has different noise structure, physiology, and acquisition artifacts that are not captured. Without stronger evidence on real 3T data (beyond TDM’s small sample), the generalizability is unclear.  
3. **Cross-dataset evaluation lacks ground truth for per-subject validation**: On the cross-dataset real experiment, there is no ground truth 7T for test subjects. The evaluation relies solely on FID (which is designed for natural images, not brain signal distributions) and \(R^2\) of the enhanced data itself, which can be inflated if the model simply smooths or overfits the pRF model. This makes the real-world improvement hard to quantify.

### Minor
1. **FID on brain disks**: Fréchet Inception Distance is intended for natural images in RGB space. Its use on 2D parameterizations of fMRI activation maps (which have different statistics) is not standard and its interpretability is questionable. The paper would benefit from a justification or an alternative metric.  
2. **Insufficient analysis of failure cases**: Figure 5 notes that inert (low \(R^2\)) vertices show weaker alignment, but the paper does not discuss how often this occurs or whether the enhancement could introduce artifacts in silent regions that mislead downstream analysis.  
3. **Dependence on ROI selection**: The pipeline focuses on a specific occipital ROI. The impact on other brain regions or on whole-brain analyses is unexplored, limiting the scope of the claimed “improving 3T fMRI quality” more generally.

### Trivial
- Some figure captions are verbose and repeat text from the main body (e.g., Figure 1 caption almost duplicates the pipeline description). This does not affect technical correctness.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A quantitative comparison of computational cost (training/inference time, GPU hours) with baseline methods.
- An analysis of how the choice of conformal mapping parameters (e.g., \(\epsilon_\mu\)) affects downstream performance.
- A sensitivity study on the noise level used in synthetic data generation to test robustness.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper’s own contributions. The combination of conformal brain disk parameterization with unpaired Schrödinger Bridge for fMRI enhancement is methodologically interesting, but the core idea of using Schrödinger Bridge for unpaired translation and the brain disk representation were each introduced in prior work (referenced in the paper).

## Suggestions
- Conduct a controlled experiment where the same subject has both 3T and 7T scans (even if single session) and measure voxel-wise correlation or residual maps to supplement TDM results.
- Replace or supplement FID with a more domain-appropriate metric, such as distributional distance between vertex-wise time series or a permutation test on \(R^2\) improvements.
- Clearly report the fraction of vertices where enhanced fMRI produces lower \(R^2\) than the original 3T to flag potential harm.
- Release the pre-trained model and conformal mapping templates to facilitate reproducibility and broader adoption.
