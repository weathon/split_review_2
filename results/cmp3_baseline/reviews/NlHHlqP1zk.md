## Summary

The paper proposes the *Fast and Slow Effect* (FSE) framework to automatically evaluate the sufficiency of concept-class annotations generated by LLMs/VLMs for Explainable AI (XAI). The framework progressively collects concept annotations across five stages (Background → Auxiliary Features) and measures how well the accumulated concepts alone support accurate class discrimination via the *Class Representation Index* (CRI). Experiments on fine-grained datasets reveal that the "slow mode" (concept-based prediction) underperforms the "fast mode" (direct visual prediction) by over 25% on average, indicating that current LLMs struggle to externalize their knowledge into sufficient textual concepts. The paper further questions the *utility-as-proxy* assumption, showing that strong performance in multimodal fusion does not guarantee annotation sufficiency.

## Strengths

- **Important and timely problem**: The paper tackles a critical gap in XAI—how to validate automated concept annotations without human supervision, which is essential for scaling concept-based interpretability methods.
- **Novel evaluation framework**: The proposed FSE is fully autonomous, requires no human annotation, and provides a principled definition of annotation sufficiency (Definition 3.1). The five-stage concept refinement is grounded in prior hierarchical extraction methods.
- **Clear and thought-provoking results**: The finding that slow (concept-based) mode underperforms fast (visual) mode on fine-grained datasets is counter-intuitive and challenges optimistic assumptions about LLM annotation quality. The contrast with general datasets (where slow mode wins) strengthens the diagnostic value of the framework.
- **Critique of standard practice**: The fusion experiment (Table 4) convincingly demonstrates that good downstream accuracy does not imply sufficient concept-level supervision, which is a useful caution for the community.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Definition of sufficiency vs. actual usage**: The paper requires that concepts alone enable accurate class inference. However, concept-based models in practice use concepts *alongside* visual features (often via fusion or bottleneck layers). The standalone sufficiency test may be overly strict—concepts could still be informative for interpretability even if not standalone-sufficient. The paper acknowledges this discrepancy but does not fully argue why standalone sufficiency is necessary for trustworthy XAI, beyond the motivating example.

2. **Fairness of fast vs. slow comparison**: The fast mode uses the full visual input; the slow mode uses only textual concepts generated *by the same model*. The observed performance drop may partly reflect information loss in the text-generation step (e.g., inability to verbalize fine details) rather than a genuine limitation of the conceptual knowledge itself. The paper attributes this to "opaque expertise," but alternative explanations (e.g., text bottleneck) are not ruled out.

3. **Distractor selection methodology**: The semantically related distractors are chosen using predictions from a pretrained ResNet-18. This introduces a potential bias: the distractors are only those that a specific visual classifier confuses, which may not capture all semantically similar alternatives. Furthermore, the ResNet was trained on ImageNet, which has different class granularities than the fine-grained datasets used. The impact of this choice on the observed results is not analyzed.

### Minor

1. **Lack of ablation on concept stages**: The five-stage refinement process is motivated by prior work, but there is no experiment varying the number of stages, their order, or their content. It is unclear whether the specific five-stage design is optimal or whether simpler/hierarchical prompts would yield different conclusions.

2. **Limited model coverage**: Only three model families are tested, and reasoning-enhanced models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) are only briefly examined in the appendix. Including such models in the main results would strengthen the claim that even advanced reasoning models suffer from the slow-mode deficit.

3. **CRI relies on a forced-choice task**: The CRI is essentially accuracy on a 5-way multiple-choice question. This may not capture nuanced aspects of annotation sufficiency, such as whether concepts are redundant or whether they cover multiple discriminative features. Additional metrics (e.g., concept diversity, coverage) could enrich the evaluation.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison with human-annotated concepts as a gold-standard sufficiency baseline.
- An ablation showing that the slow-mode deficit is not simply due to the LLM interpreting its own handwriting ("same model annotating and predicting") but persists when concepts from one model are evaluated by another.
- Discussion of how the FSE framework could be used *during annotation* to iteratively improve concept sets.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the key insight is that LLMs exhibit a *conceptualization gap*: they can correctly classify visually but fail to produce textual concepts that support the same classification. This points to a dissociation between the model's internal representations and its verbalizable outputs, which has implications not only for XAI but also for understanding the nature of reasoning in large models. The finding that this gap virtually disappears on general-domain datasets suggests that fine-grained expertise is particularly hard to externalize, perhaps because the relevant distinctions depend on non-linguistic features or highly specific domain knowledge.

## Suggestions

- Discuss more explicitly how the proposed sufficiency definition relates to the intended use of concepts in XAI. If concepts are meant to be complementary to visual features, consider a variant of CRI that evaluates concept-conditional visual predictions.
- Perform an ablation study where the text-generation step is controlled (e.g., by providing the model with a fixed set of high-quality human-written concepts and measuring the slow-mode CRI). This would help disentangle whether the deficit is due to insufficient concept generation or insufficient concept utilization.
- Include reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4 with Chain-of-Thought) in the main results to show whether explicit reasoning steps alleviate the slow-mode deficit.
