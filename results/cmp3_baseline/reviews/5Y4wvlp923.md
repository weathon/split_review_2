## Summary

This paper identifies a semantic imbalance problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). The authors propose the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric to quantify component recoverability, and integrate it into an adaptive weighting strategy for view-specific contrastive objectives within the CoST framework. Experiments on forecasting benchmarks show that the proposed asymmetric perceptual weighting yields improvements over TS2Vec and vanilla CoST, particularly under semantic skew conditions.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: The paper identifies a genuine and under-explored issue in contrastive time-series learning—semantic imbalance where dominant temporal components suppress weaker but important ones. This is a relevant problem for the community.
- **Novel diagnostic metric**: SDE provides a principled, quantitative way to measure whether individual semantic components are linearly recoverable from composite embeddings, drawing an interesting connection to vector arithmetic in word embeddings.
- **Clean integration**: The asymmetric weighting mechanism is simple and can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes, which is a practical advantage.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Inconsistent and contradictory results**: The paper's core claim is that the proposed CoST+APW method improves forecasting. However, Table 3 shows that CoST+APW is often *worse* than vanilla CoST or even TS2Vec/TNC. For example, on ETT1 horizon 48, CoST (0.06 MSE) outperforms CoST+APW (0.07). On ETT1 horizon 720, CoST (0.148) beats CoST+APW (0.152). On Electricity horizon 168, 336, and 720, CoST+APW is substantially worse than all baselines. On Weather, TS2Vec and TNC frequently outperform CoST+APW. The claimed "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy" is not supported by the presented data.
- **Missing critical experimental details**: The paper does not report SDE values for the main experiments (Table 3 only shows MSE/MAE). Without SDE values on real datasets, the central claim that the method reduces semantic imbalance is unsubstantiated. The SDE analysis is only shown on synthetic data (Table 1) and for the failed TS2Vec+SDE regularization attempt (Table 2).
- **The "failed" SDE regularization is not properly analyzed**: Section 4.3 shows that direct SDE regularization on TS2Vec does not improve performance, but the paper does not investigate *why*. This is a missed opportunity to understand the limitations of SDE as an optimization target versus a diagnostic tool. The conclusion that "SDE... fails to provide constructive optimization gradients" is asserted without gradient analysis or ablation.
- **Unclear contribution relative to CoST**: The paper builds heavily on CoST, which already uses frequency-aware contrastive learning and separate trend/seasonal encoders. The main addition is the asymmetric weighting based on SDE. However, the results do not convincingly show that this addition provides consistent gains over CoST. The paper's framing as addressing TS2Vec's limitations is somewhat misleading, as the final method is essentially a modification of CoST.

### Minor
- **Terminology inconsistency**: The paper uses both "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title, abstract) and "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2) and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3) to refer to the same metric. This is confusing.
- **Limited baseline comparison**: The paper only compares against TS2Vec, TNC, and CoST. More recent time-series representation learning methods (e.g., PatchTST, TimesNet) are not included, which limits the assessment of relative performance.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces hyperparameters γ and γ', but there is no analysis of their sensitivity or how they were chosen.

### Trivial
- The paper states "we propose three complementary strategies" but the first (SDE regularization) is shown to fail, and the second (multi-view contrastive learning) is adopted from CoST. Only the third (asymmetric weighting) is novel.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An analysis of why direct SDE regularization fails (e.g., gradient norm analysis, loss landscape visualization) would strengthen the paper.
- Reporting SDE values on real datasets for all methods would directly support the claim of reduced semantic imbalance.
- Ablation on the MLP fusion layer: is it necessary, or does simple addition of embeddings work?

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The idea of using a directional metric to reweight contrastive losses is interesting but not deeply analyzed. The key insight—that semantic imbalance can be quantified and mitigated—is promising but insufficiently validated.

## Suggestions

1. **Address the contradictory results**: Explain why CoST+APW underperforms on several settings (e.g., Electricity long horizons, Weather). If the method is only beneficial under specific semantic skew conditions, characterize those conditions clearly.
2. **Report SDE values on real datasets**: Without this, the central claim of reduced semantic imbalance is unverified.
3. **Clarify the contribution**: The paper should be more honest about building on CoST and clearly delineate what is novel versus inherited.
4. **Add more recent baselines**: Include methods like PatchTST or TimesNet to contextualize performance.
5. **Analyze the failed SDE regularization**: Understanding why it fails could lead to a better method or provide useful insights for the community.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a relevant problem and proposes a reasonable diagnostic metric (SDE). However, the experimental results do not support the claimed improvements—the proposed method is often worse than the baseline CoST and other methods. The central claim of reduced semantic imbalance is not validated on real data. Given these issues, the paper does not meet the bar for acceptance.
