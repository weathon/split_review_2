## Summary

This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework built on SAM. It contributes two core components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that samples prompts from a multinomial distribution over image-embedding similarity scores, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with the TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method requires no architectural modifications to SAM, no additional models, and no fine-tuning. Evaluated on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25) and CARPK (MAE 4.68), it achieves competitive results within the training-free SAM-only family.

## Strengths

- **Core idea is novel and well-motivated.** The paper correctly identifies that SAM's output tokens — generated through bidirectional attention in the decoder — integrate semantic and positional information, making them a natural and cheap source of discriminative signal for verification. This insight genuinely departs from prior training-free methods (TFOC, C-Anything) that operate in the shared image-embedding space. [impact=+9.86]

- **Architecturally clean.** The method runs SAM off-the-shelf without modifications or additional models. Both stages sit on top of SAM's existing outputs. The ablation (Table 5) shows output-token space yields better separation than image-embedding space (16.25 vs. 16.94 MAE), with PCA visualization (Figure 5) providing qualitative support. [impact=+9.47]

- **CARPK results are genuinely competitive.** MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13 beats all listed training-based methods and is close to TFCAC (4.39) despite using no additional models. This is the paper's strongest empirical result. [impact=+9.96]

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **No statistical evaluation for a fundamentally stochastic method.** The method involves multinomial sampling of prompts from a probability distribution, iterative verification with random ordering, and early stopping. Yet the paper reports a single MAE/RMSE per setting with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or information about number of runs or seeds. Given that the headline improvements are small — TS-SS improves over cosine similarity by 0.23 MAE (Table 3); output-token space improves over image-embedding space by 0.69 MAE (Table 5) — it is entirely possible these differences lie within the noise of the sampling procedure. Without multiple runs, the reader cannot assess whether these gaps are real effects or random fluctuations. [impact=-9.99]

- **The abstract makes claims contradicted by the paper's own data.** The abstract states: *"outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods."* On FSC-147 (Table 1), training-based methods BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), and LOCA (10.79) all outperform the reported MAE of 16.25. The body text more accurately limits the comparison to SAM-only methods and to Counting-DETR, but the abstract overstates the results. On CARPK the claim holds, but the abstract's phrasing creates a misleading impression of general superiority across both datasets. [impact=-6.81]

### Minor

- **TS-SS contribution is overclaimed relative to its evidence.** The improvement over cosine similarity (16.48 to 16.25, 1.4% relative) is very small. The paper claims non-hybrid metrics have *"reached a performance plateau"* and TS-SS *"breaks through this barrier,"* which dramatically overstates a 0.23 MAE improvement. Without statistical significance testing, the claimed benefit is not properly established. [impact=-10.00]

- **High RMSE (133.11 on FSC-147) suggests outlier failures that are not discussed.** The RMSE/MAE ratio is ~8.2, compared to ~4.6 for TFCAC and ~5.3 for LOCA, indicating the method produces some very large individual errors. The paper acknowledges struggles with dense/small instances but does not connect this to the RMSE evidence or characterize the failure modes. [impact=-0.00]

- **Token verification threshold (300) is unexplained.** The implementation details state *"we set the token verification threshold to 300"* without specifying what quantity this threshold is applied to, how it was determined, or how sensitive results are to its value. No ablation on this threshold is provided. [impact=-0.01]

### Trivial

- **Internal coherence:** The probabilistic prompt generation uses cosine similarity in the image embedding space (Eq. 1) — the same approach the paper criticizes in prior work for introducing *"significant uncertainty."* This is a valid design choice (exploration vs. verification are separate stages), but the paper should acknowledge that it inherits some of the same similarity-based uncertainty for prompt placement. [impact=-0.00]

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report mean ± std over multiple runs (e.g., 10 seeds) for all key results to establish whether claimed improvements are statistically significant.
- Clarify what the token verification threshold (300) is applied to and add a sensitivity analysis.
- Discuss the gap between one-shot (MAE 18.57) and few-shot (MAE 16.25) performance on FSC-147.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The core observation (SAM output tokens encode integrated semantic+positional information suitable for verification) is already articulated by the authors.

## Suggestions

1. **Run multiple seeds and report variance.** The single highest-leverage improvement. Without it, the 0.23 MAE TS-SS advantage and the 0.69 MAE output-token advantage cannot be distinguished from noise.
2. **Revise the abstract** to accurately reflect comparative results, e.g., *"state-of-the-art among training-free SAM-only methods on FSC-147"* rather than *"outperforming existing training-based methods."*
3. **Add a failure-case analysis** linked to the high RMSE. A per-category breakdown or exemplar of worst-case images would help readers understand when the method breaks.
4. **Provide an ablation on the token verification threshold** to establish sensitivity.
