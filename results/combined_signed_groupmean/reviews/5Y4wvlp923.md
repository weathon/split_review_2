## Summary

This paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement/Separability/Decomposition Error (SDE), a metric to diagnose semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, and integrates it into an asymmetric reweighting scheme (APW) on top of the CoST framework. The diagnostic insight — that dominant trend components suppress seasonal components in embeddings — is illustrated convincingly on synthetic data (Table 1). However, the paper's central claims that the proposed method yields "consistent gains" and "consistently lower SDE values" are not supported by the evidence presented. The main results show CoST+APW substantially underperforming vanilla CoST on Electricity and Weather (especially at long horizons), SDE values are never reported for the main experiments despite being claimed, and the evaluation protocol has an unexplained horizon discrepancy. These evidential gaps undermine the paper's headline contribution.

## Strengths

- **The diagnostic observation in Table 1 (Section 4.2) is clean and informative.** Using synthetic signals with controlled amplitude ratios, the paper demonstrates that TS2Vec's embeddings exhibit substantial asymmetry in component recoverability (e.g., at r=10, SDE_{trend,period}=0.0011 vs SDE_{period,trend}=1.1083), providing clear quantitative evidence of the semantic imbalance problem. This is the most convincing evidence in the paper.

- **The paper honestly reports a failed attempt.** Section 4.3 and Table 2 show that direct SDE regularization does not improve TS2Vec performance. Reporting this negative result is good scientific practice.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The paper's central empirical claim — that the proposed method yields "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" (abstract) and "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy" (line 300) — is not supported by the evidence presented, and in several cases is directly contradicted.**

  (a) **Forecasting results are mixed, with substantial degradations on key benchmarks.** Table 3 shows CoST+APW is substantially worse than vanilla CoST on Electricity long horizons (h=168: 0.566 vs 0.425, +33% MSE; h=336: 0.677 vs 0.576, +18%; h=720: 1.010 vs 0.911, +11%) and loses or ties on most Weather horizons. Where CoST+APW wins (primarily on ETT datasets), margins are tiny (0.001–0.022 MSE). Calling this pattern "consistent gains" is inaccurate.

  (b) **SDE values for the main experiments are never reported.** The paper states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" (line 263), but Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE. No SDE values for any main experiment appear anywhere in the paper. The repeatedly made claim of "consistently lower SDE values" is thus unverifiable.

- **The evaluation protocol stated in Section 4.1 (line 152) specifies forecasting horizons {24, 48, 96, 192}, but Table 3 primarily reports horizons 24, 48, 168, 336, 720.** Horizon 192 never appears; horizon 96 appears only for the ETTm1 block. Horizons 168 and 336 appear across multiple datasets but are not in the stated set. This discrepancy undermines confidence in experimental control and reporting.

### Minor

- **The acronym SDE is expanded inconsistently throughout the paper:** the title and abstract use "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (lines 5/10), Section 3.2 uses "Semantic Separability Error" (line 71), and Sections 4.1/4.3 use "Semantic Decomposition Error" (lines 150, 178). For a metric that is the paper's core conceptual contribution, this inconsistency is confusing and suggests hasty assembly.

- **The abstract claims the approach can be plugged into CoST "without architectural changes" (line 10), but Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP *g_φ* as a new learned component** that creates a composite embedding from concatenated trend/seasonal features. Adding a learned module is an architectural change, contradicting the abstract's claim.

- **The conclusion (line 304) lists "multi-view contrastive learning" as one of three strategies "we proposed,"** but multi-view contrastive learning with separate trend/seasonal contrastive losses is the central contribution of CoST (Woo et al., 2022), the framework this paper builds upon. This inflates the paper's contribution at the expense of prior work.
