## Summary

This paper proposes Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a diagnostic metric to quantify semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, and Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW), a mechanism that reweights CoST's contrastive losses based on this metric. The goal is to prevent dominant temporal components (e.g., trend) from suppressing weaker ones (e.g., seasonality) during representation learning.

## Strengths

- **Demonstrates the existence of semantic imbalance via synthetic experiments (Table 1).** The paper constructs mixtures of trend and periodic components with varying amplitude ratios and shows that TS2Vec's SDE values systematically track which component dominates. When trend dominates (r=10), SDE_period,trend = 1.108, confirming that the seasonal component is poorly recoverable. This cleanly illustrates that the problem is real.
- **Honestly reports a negative result (Table 2).** Directly using SDE as a regularization term in TS2Vec does not improve performance — MSE is flat or slightly worse across all settings (e.g., ETTm1 horizon 288: 0.101 vs. 0.126). Reporting this failure mode adds credibility to the paper's narrative.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None. The paper's core idea (a diagnostic metric + adaptive reweighting) is not invalidated by any single fatal flaw, though several major issues weigh heavily against its current form.

### Major

- **The empirical results contradict the paper's central claims.** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and the conclusion claims "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy." Table 3 directly contradicts these statements on multiple benchmarks:
  - **Electricity** at horizons 168/336/720: CoST+APW (MSE 0.566/0.677/1.010) is decisively the *worst* method, far behind CoST (0.425/0.576/0.911), TS2Vec, and TNC. This dataset is explicitly cited for "strong calendar-driven periodicities" (line 142) — precisely where the method should excel.
  - **Weather** at horizons 336/720: CoST+APW (0.266/0.299) underperforms TNC (0.215/0.219) by 24% and 37%, respectively.
  - **ETT1 (first block)** at horizon 720: CoST+APW (0.152) is *worse* than vanilla CoST (0.148).
  - Across 22 forecasting settings, CoST+APW is the best method in roughly 6–8 settings (by small margins), tied with CoST in several, and decisively worse in at least 6. This is not "consistent gains"; it is a mixed and inconsistent pattern with significant degradation on key benchmarks.

- **The SDE metric rests on an unexamined additivity assumption for nonlinear encoders.** The metric computes SDE = 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)), which assumes that the encoder is approximately additive: v(a+b) ≈ v(a) + v(b) in the latent space. Neural network encoders are highly nonlinear, and there is no justification for this assumption. A large SDE could reflect genuine semantic suppression, or it could simply be an artifact of nonlinear interactions in the encoder. The analogy to word embeddings (line 77) does not carry over, because word embeddings exhibit linear structure due to the skip-gram objective, which has no parallel in time-series contrastive learning.

- **The method is severely under-specified, preventing reproduction.**
  - Hyperparameters γ and γ' (lines 103, 259) control the entire reweighting mechanism but are never reported, ablated, or analyzed.
  - The MLP (Section 4.4.2) is described only as "a small MLP" — no layer count, hidden dimension, or activation function.
  - The low-pass filter for trend extraction (Section 4.3) is described as "e.g., moving average or Butterworth filter" with no cutoff frequency or filter order.
  - No error bars, confidence intervals, or standard deviations are reported for any result, making it impossible to assess whether the claimed improvements are statistically meaningful or within noise range.

### Minor

- **Strained narrative and unverified "pluggable" claim.** The paper diagnoses semantic imbalance in TS2Vec (Section 4.2), attempts a fix on TS2Vec that fails (Section 4.3), then applies a different fix to CoST (Section 4.4). The title claims the approach is "pluggable," but it is never demonstrated on TS2Vec or any model other than CoST. The reader is left without evidence that the mechanism generalizes.

- **Inflated framing of contributions.** The introduction (lines 18–22) identifies three limitations of "current contrastive approaches": (1) no inductive bias for decomposition, (2) time-domain-only objectives, (3) isotropic embedding collapse. The paper then presents itself as addressing all three. In reality, limitations (1) and (2) are already addressed by CoST (separate trend/seasonal encoders, frequency-aware contrastive objectives), which the paper adopts as its base architecture. Only limitation (3) is newly addressed. The paper also counts "multi-view contrastive learning" as one of its three proposed strategies (line 304), but multi-view contrastive learning is CoST's contribution, not the paper's.

- **Inconsistent and confusing presentation in Table 3.** The dataset "ETT1" is used for two different subsets (ETTh1 with horizons 24/48/168/336/720 and ETTm1 with horizons 24/48/96/288/672), violating standard naming that distinguishes them. The bolding convention is uninterpretable: on line 274 (ETT1, horizon 24), every single entry is bolded, which cannot indicate the best result as intended.

### Trivial

- **Three different names for the same metric:** "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title, abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2 heading), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3).
- **γ/γ' inconsistency:** The loss formulation (line 101) uses both γ and γ', but the figure caption (line 128) uses only γ ("weighted by 1+γΔ and the trend loss by 1−γΔ"), suggesting γ = γ' without stating it.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Validate the SDE metric's additivity assumption.** A controlled experiment on linear encoders or linear mixtures with known ground truth would establish whether SDE measures what it claims to measure, or primarily reflects nonlinear encoder interactions.
- **Apply the method to TS2Vec.** Testing APW on the model that motivated the investigation would substantiate the "pluggable" claim.
- **Report γ, γ' values and include an ablation study.** Without understanding how sensitive performance is to these hyperparameters, the method's robustness and reproducibility are unknown.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's observation that the Electricity and Weather failures are not marginal weaknesses but direct counterexamples to the paper's central claim is the key insight. The critic correctly notes that Electricity, with its strong calendar-driven periodicities, is precisely the kind of data where the method should perform best if its premise is correct — yet it catastrophically underperforms there. This suggests either a fundamental flaw in the SDE-APW approach (e.g., the metric confounds nonlinearity with suppression) or a systematic issue (e.g., the weighting mechanism harms long-horizon forecasting on datasets with certain statistical properties). The paper's failure to address or even acknowledge this pattern is its most serious weakness. Additionally, the observation that the SDE metric's additivity assumption is unexamined for nonlinear encoders is a novel and important critique that goes beyond surface-level evaluation.

## Suggestions

1. **Temper the claims to match the evidence.** The abstract and conclusion should honestly reflect that CoST+APW improves results on some datasets/horizons but degrades them on others (particularly Electricity and Weather at longer horizons). A bounded claim — e.g., "improves on short-to-medium horizons but can degrade on very long horizons, especially on datasets with X property" — would be far more credible.
2. **Report γ, γ' values, include an ablation, and fully specify the MLP architecture and low-pass filter parameters.** Without these, the method is not reproducible.
3. **Report standard deviations or confidence intervals** over multiple random seeds.
4. **Justify or replace the SDE metric's additive assumption.** At minimum, discuss when this assumption holds and when it might break under nonlinear encoders.
5. **Apply the APW mechanism to TS2Vec** to substantiate the "pluggable" claim, or remove the claim.
6. **Use standard dataset names** (ETTh1, ETTm1, ETTh2) and clarify the bolding convention in Table 3.

## Score and Decision

The paper identifies a real problem and proposes a reasonable intuitive fix, but the experimental evidence directly contradicts the central claim of "consistent gains." The method fails badly on two of five benchmarks, the SDE metric rests on an unjustified theoretical assumption, key implementation details are missing, and no statistical significance is reported. These issues are addressable in principle, but in its current form the paper does not provide credible evidence for its strongest claims.
