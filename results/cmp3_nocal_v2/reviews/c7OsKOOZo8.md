## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy grading that generates internal "lesion proposals" via a Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module (which uses auxiliary classifiers and CAM-based Top-K selection) and fuses them across views through a Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion (LGRF) module (which uses gated MoE for context-dependent fusion). On two multi-view datasets (MFIDDR, DRTiD), the method achieves competitive accuracy without external lesion annotations.

## Strengths

1. **Well-motivated architectural design.** The paper targets a genuine practical problem — reducing reliance on costly external lesion/vessel annotations in DR grading — and proposes a coherent two-module solution. GALP's use of stage-wise auxiliary classifiers with CAM-based Top-K selection and LGRF's MoE-based gating (where the current view's features route which experts process the adjacent view's proposals) are internally consistent design choices. **(Sections 3.2, 3.3)**

2. **Competitive headline results.** On MFIDDR, the lesion-free variant achieves 83.9% accuracy, outperforming all end-to-end baselines (best end-to-end: ETMC at 81.5%) and matching/exceeding several externally informed methods. The full lesion-informed variant reaches 84.6%, establishing the top-line on this benchmark. **(Table 1)**

3. **Ablation confirms module-level contributions.** Removing GALP, the expert pool, or the entire LGRF module each produces clear accuracy drops (82.7, 82.6, and 82.3 vs. 83.9 for the full model). These gaps (1.2–1.6 points) exceed many between-method differences in the main table, supporting the claim that both modules contribute meaningfully. **(Table 4)**

4. **Hyperparameter analysis shows limited brittleness.** Varying the retention ratio (α=0.2–1.0), number of activated experts (K₂=1–6), and total experts (M=2–8) yields variation of roughly 1–2%, suggesting the method is not critically sensitive to these choices. **(Fig. 3)**

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The paper never verifies that GALP's proposals correspond to actual lesions.** The central narrative — and the headline claim about "self-derived lesion proposals" — hinges on the assertion that the Top-K selected CAM regions from auxiliary classifiers are lesion-specific. However, the paper provides: no qualitative examples of proposals overlaid on fundus images; no comparison against the available MFIDDR lesion segmentation masks (mentioned at line 185: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"); no proposal-to-lesion overlap statistics; and no analysis of what non-lesion structures (vessels, optic disc, image artifacts) the CAMs might be capturing. GALP's CAMs reflect features that are *discriminative for the grade*, not necessarily lesional. Without validation, "lesion proposals" is an asserted label, not a demonstrated fact. The method could succeed for a different reason (e.g., the auxiliary losses strengthen intermediate representations, and the Top-K selection acts as a general spatial pruning mechanism), which would still be useful but does not support the lesion-awareness claim. **(Section 3.2, particularly lines 91–99 and Eq. 3–4)**

2. **No variance or statistical significance is reported for any result.** All numbers in Tables 1–4 are single-point estimates with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. This matters because cross-method differences are small: Ours (w/o lesion) at 83.9% vs. WGLIN at 84.2% (0.3 pp), Ours (with lesion) at 84.6% vs. SMVDR-M at 84.0% (0.6 pp), and Ours at 76.0% vs. CrossFIT at 75.6% on DRTiD (0.4 pp). Without variance, it is impossible to assess whether these differences are meaningful. The MFIDDR test set is ~2,584 eyes across 5 classes; even the ablation gaps (1.2–1.6 pp) would benefit from variance estimates. **(Tables 1, 2, 3, 4)**

### Minor

3. **Grade 4 (proliferative DR) performance is notably weak and undiscussed.** On the most clinically important grade, Ours (w/o lesion) achieves F1=36.0, far below CVSA's 64.1 and even below MVCINN's 44.8. Ours (with lesion) improves to 51.6, still well below CVSA. This suggests the CAM-based approach may systematically miss the complex pathology of proliferative DR (neovascularization, vitreous hemorrhage). The paper does not acknowledge or discuss this pattern. **(Table 2)**

4. **The ablation does not isolate the proposal selection mechanism from the auxiliary loss.** The "w/o GALP" ablation removes both the auxiliary loss and the Top-K proposal selection (using all tokens instead). Since the auxiliary loss itself could strengthen intermediate representations independently of any proposal mechanism, the source of GALP's 1.2-point gain is ambiguous. Adding a variant that keeps the auxiliary loss but uses all tokens (instead of Top-K) would disambiguate. **(Table 4, line 286)**

5. **Cross-method comparisons are not fully controlled for architecture and pretraining differences.** The paper adopts Swin-B as its backbone and uses dataset-specific pretraining (ImageNet for MFIDDR, EyePACS for DRTiD). However, the baselines' backbones and pretraining regimens are not systematically reported or controlled. The paper states consistency with CVSA's pretraining on MFIDDR and CrossFIT's on DRTiD, but the other externally informed methods (WGLIN, SMVDR, LFMVDR) may use smaller backbones (e.g., ResNet-50), meaning some of the reported gains could be architectural rather than attributable to the proposal-and-fusion mechanism. This is a common limitation of reported-results comparisons but should be acknowledged. **(Section 4.1, Implementation details)**

### Trivial

6. **Notation redundancy in Eq. 3.** The weight vector is written as $\mathbf{w}_{s_n, c}^{(s_n)}$, where the stage index appears both in the subscript and superscript.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Validate proposals against the available MFIDDR lesion masks.** This is the single highest-leverage improvement (see Major Weakness 1). Qualitative overlay examples and quantitative overlap statistics (precision/recall w.r.t. lesion masks) would directly substantiate or refute the core claim.
- **Report computational cost.** The method uses Swin-B + auxiliary classifiers at 3 stages + MoE with 6 experts per stage per view + cross-attention. Parameter count, FLOPs, and inference time would clarify practical feasibility for the clinical deployment mentioned in the conclusion.
- **Discuss why adjacent-only (cyclic) fusion was chosen over all-pairs fusion** for the 4-view setting (Section 3.3, line 123). For N=4, each view exchanges information with only one other view, and non-adjacent signals must propagate through intermediates.
- **Add a failure analysis for Grade 4.** Understanding why the method underperforms on proliferative DR (e.g., are the CAM proposals missing neovascularization because it is not a focal lesion pattern?) would strengthen the paper.

## Removed Points

- **"Unfalsifiable narrative about w/o vs. with lesion comparison":** Removed because the paper reports two straightforward empirical results (w/o lesion and with lesion), and both findings are independently falsifiable. The framing is logically consistent, not unfalsifiable.
- **"Striding/tokenization claim asserted without evidence":** Removed because this is a general motivation statement referencing a known property of CNNs/Transformers, not a specific empirical claim requiring evidence within this paper.
- **"GPT-generated content concern":** Removed as speculative and not a substantive criticism of the research.
- **"Different resizing confounds":** Removed because the paper explains that resizing follows prior SOTA works on each dataset, which is standard practice.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviews correctly identify the key evidential gap (unvalidated lesion proposals) but do not surface a fundamental methodological flaw or contradiction beyond what can be read from the paper.

## Suggestions

1. **(Highest priority)** Validate GALP proposals using the available MFIDDR lesion masks — show qualitative overlays, compute proposal-to-lesion overlap, and analyze how proposal quality varies across grades. If proposals do not align well with lesions, reframe the method as a general self-guided spatial attention mechanism rather than lesion-aware grading.
2. Report results from 3–5 random seeds with mean ± std across all tables to enable meaningful comparison of small accuracy margins.
3. Add an ablation that keeps the auxiliary loss but uses all tokens (no Top-K selection) to isolate the contribution of the proposal mechanism from the auxiliary loss.
4. Discuss the Grade 4 performance gap — this is the clinically most important class and the method's weakest result.
5. Report the model's parameter count and inference throughput to support the clinical deployment claim.
