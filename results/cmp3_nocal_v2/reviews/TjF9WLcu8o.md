## Summary

The paper proposes COM, a framework that combines contrastive pre-training with an online update mechanism for instruction-tuned CodeLLMs, aiming to address catastrophic forgetting during deployment. The core idea is to learn task-invariant representations via contrastive learning while performing lightweight online updates through a "meta-learner" and a FIFO memory buffer.

## Strengths

None. The paper's claimed architectural decomposition is reasonable at a conceptual level, but with zero experimental results and a mischaracterized core technical claim, no strength is both concrete and evidence-grounded.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **No experimental results are reported despite precise quantitative claims.** Section 5 (Experimental Setup and Evaluation) describes datasets, baselines, metrics, and implementation details but transitions directly into Section 6 (Discussion) without presenting any results. There are no tables, no figures with numerical data, and no reported performance numbers for any method on any metric. The abstract claims "outperforming instruction-tuned baselines by 12-18% on unseen programming languages" and "3-5x fewer updates than conventional meta-learning approaches." The introduction and conclusion repeat these numbers. But zero experimental evidence appears anywhere in the manuscript. A paper that makes specific numerical performance claims and presents itself as an empirical contribution cannot be accepted without any experimental results.

### Major

- **The "online meta-learning" formulation is not meta-learning.** Equation (5) defines the meta-learner update as:
  $$\phi_{t+1} = \phi_t - \alpha \nabla_\phi (\|g_\phi(f_\theta(x_t)) - y_t\|^2 + \lambda \|\phi_t - \phi_{t-1}\|^2)$$
  This is online gradient descent on a regression objective with a temporal smoothing regularizer. There are no meta-tasks, no inner-loop/outer-loop structure, no distribution over tasks, and no optimization of a learning procedure — none of the defining characteristics of meta-learning (Finn et al., 2017, which the paper cites). The paper's central claimed contribution of unifying contrastive learning with "online meta-learning" rests on a terminological overclaim; the actual mechanism is standard online fine-tuning with weight decay toward the previous timestep.

### Minor

- **Notation inconsistency in the method description.** The instruction encoder is labeled $f_\theta$ in Section 4.1 (Equation 4) but becomes $f_\phi$ beginning in Section 4.2 (Equations 6, 8). In Equation (8), both the encoder $f_\phi$ and the meta-learner $g_\phi$ use the same parameter symbol $\phi$, making it unclear which parameters are updated by the contrastive loss versus the meta-update, or whether they share parameters at all. The paper never clarifies which components are trainable or how the parameter counts relate to the claimed "5% of the base model's parameters."

- **Equation (4) deviates from the stated contrastive loss formulation without explanation.** The standard InfoNCE loss in Equation (3) includes the positive term in the denominator. Equation (4), presented as the paper's contrastive loss, drops the positive term from the denominator. This is either a typo or an intentional modification, but it is not discussed or justified.

- **Citation inaccuracy for the base model.** CodeGen-16B is attributed to "Liu et al., 2023a," but the cited paper (Liu et al., 2023a, "Is your code generated by ChatGPT really correct?") is about evaluating code generated by ChatGPT, not the CodeGen model. This misattributes a standard baseline.
