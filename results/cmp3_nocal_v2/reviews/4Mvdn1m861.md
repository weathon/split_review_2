## Summary

This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that repurposes SAM for counting without architectural modifications or auxiliary models. It combines (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that samples prompts from a softmax-normalized similarity map and iteratively updates probability at discovered locations, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS geometric similarity metric to classify prompts as positive or negative. The method is evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK, achieving best-in-class results among SAM-only methods on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25) and surpassing all training-based methods on CARPK (MAE 4.68).

## Strengths

1. **Output token verification is a genuinely novel and well-motivated idea.** Most prior training-free SAM-based counting operates in the image embedding space, which the paper correctly identifies as ambiguous for class-agnostic counting. Leveraging SAM's decoder output tokens — which integrate both semantic and positional information through cross-attention — is a sensible direction. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) provides supporting evidence that output token space yields more separable positive/negative clusters than image embedding space. (Section 3.2, lines 152–156; Table 5, lines 283–290.)

2. **The method requires no additional models.** It uses only SAM in its off-the-shelf form, unlike TFCAC (the best-performing training-free method on FSC-147) which requires an additional DINO encoder. Being SAM-only is a genuine operational advantage. On FSC-147, TokenCount (MAE 16.25) outperforms all other SAM-only methods (TFOC: 19.95, C-Anything: 27.97, vanilla SAM: 42.48). (Table 1, lines 194–202.)

3. **Performance on CARPK is genuinely strong and practically meaningful.** With MAE 4.68, the method outperforms all training-based methods (FamNet: 18.19, BMNet+: 5.76, SAFECOUNT: 5.33, LOCA: 9.97) and all SAM-only training-free methods. This is a nontrivial result given that training-based methods have category-specific supervision. (Table 1, line 202.)

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **The abstract makes a factually incorrect performance claim.** The abstract states: *"Experimental results show our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods."* This is false on FSC-147, where the method is beaten by TFCAC (training-free, MAE 12.26), LOCA (training-based, 10.79), BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), and PseCo (13.05) — all verifiable from Table 1. The method's actual position is: best among SAM-only methods on FSC-147, and best among all methods on CARPK against training-based approaches, but behind TFCAC on both datasets. The abstract overclaim must be corrected to match the data. (Lines 9, 194–202.)

2. **The efficiency argument is asserted but not empirically supported against competitors.** The paper repeatedly frames the probabilistic prompt generation as *"enhancing computational efficiency"* (line 34) and *"efficiently exploring the entire image while minimizing redundant prompt generation"* (line 30). However, the paper never reports how many prompts per image the competing methods (TFOC, OmniCount, TFCAC) use, nor does it report their inference times. The claim at line 230 that the method is *"more than twice as fast"* as TFCAC is given without any TFCAC timing data. Without prompt-budget-controlled comparisons or inference time baselines, the efficiency advantage is asserted rather than demonstrated. This does not invalidate the method, but it means a core stated advantage is unsubstantiated. (Lines 30, 34, 228–230.)

### Minor

1. **The TS-SS metric shows only marginal improvement over cosine similarity.** Table 3 reports TS-SS MAE 16.25 vs. cosine similarity MAE 16.48 — a difference of 0.23 (1.4% relative). The paper claims this breakthrough from a "performance plateau" (line 275), but a 0.23 MAE gap on a scale where values range from ~10 to ~42 is weak evidence that the specialized geometric metric is necessary. Cosine similarity, which discards magnitude/positional information, achieves near-identical results. A statistical significance test (e.g., paired bootstrap) would be needed to establish that this difference is meaningful. (Table 3, lines 248–256; line 275.)

2. **The RMSE-to-MAE ratio is notably higher than comparable methods, suggesting undiagnosed severe outliers.** On FSC-147, the ratio is 8.2× (RMSE 133.11 / MAE 16.25), compared to TFCAC at 4.6×, LOCA at 5.3×, OmniCount at 6.1×, and TFOC at 6.6×. This indicates the method produces catastrophically large errors on some images. The paper acknowledges performance degradation on *"dense distributions of very small instances"* (line 305) but does not quantify or characterize these failure cases. Given the severity of the RMSE signal, this deserves dedicated analysis rather than a single sentence in the discussion. (Table 1; lines 303–305.)

3. **Several implementation details are underspecified, affecting reproducibility.** 
   - How the temperature parameter τ=0.066 was chosen is not explained (line 180).
   - The verification threshold of 300 (line 180) is opaque — TS-SS is an unnormalized product of geometric terms, and no sensitivity analysis is provided.
   - When the exemplar list grows (line 146), it is not specified whether TS-SS is computed as max similarity or average similarity against the exemplar set.
   - For CARPK, 12 exemplars are "randomly selected" from the training set (line 174), but it is not stated whether results are averaged over multiple random draws.
   - The early stopping criterion (IoU > 0.95, patience 5) is introduced only in the ablation section (line 277) rather than in the method description (Section 3.1).

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A prompt-budget-controlled experiment where all SAM-only methods are evaluated at matched prompt counts (e.g., 250, 500, 1000) would directly isolate whether the output-token verification is genuinely superior to image-embedding verification at equal compute.
- Reporting inference times for baselines would substantiate the efficiency claims.
- An analysis of the threshold parameter (300) showing performance over a range of values would clarify robustness.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- **"The method's accuracy is achieved through brute-force prompting, not through intelligent design"** — This framing is overly harsh. The probabilistic mechanism demonstrably outperforms grid prompting (SAM baseline: 42.48 MAE vs. ours: 16.25). The method does use intelligent sampling; the legitimate question is whether it is efficient relative to competitors, not whether it is intelligent at all.
- **"If TFCAC or OmniCount were given 1,400+ prompts, would their performance improve?"** — Pure speculation. No evidence in the paper supports or refutes this hypothetical. Remove.
- **"The probabilistic component is not doing anything more intelligent than density-based sampling from a similarity heatmap"** — This is essentially what the method is (softmax-normalized similarity sampling), and the paper describes it as such. There is no deception. The criticism mischaracterizes a design choice as a failure.
- **Strengths removed as generic:** "the paper addressed an important problem" / "the problem is well-motivated" — These are applicable to most papers and lack specific grounding in this paper's evidence. The three retained strengths are concrete and data-anchored.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviews surface the tension between the method's genuine empirical contributions (best SAM-only on FSC-147, best overall on CARPK vs. training-based methods) and its inflated framing, but this is a calibration observation about presentation, not a novel insight about the content.

## Suggestions

1. **Correct the abstract and introduction** to state accurately: best among SAM-only methods on FSC-147, competitive with but behind TFCAC and the top training-based methods on FSC-147, and superior to training-based methods on CARPK.
2. **Provide prompt-budget-controlled comparisons** with at least one other SAM-only method (e.g., TFOC) at matched prompt counts to substantiate the efficiency and verification quality claims.
3. **Report inference times for baselines** (especially TFCAC) or retract the unsupported "more than twice as fast" claim.
4. **Diagnose the high-RMSE images**: characterize the subset where errors are largest, show whether these are systematic failures (e.g., dense small objects, occlusion), and report the proportion of images where error exceeds a threshold.
5. **Add a statistical test** (paired bootstrap or Wilcoxon signed-rank) for the TS-SS vs. cosine similarity comparison to establish whether the 0.23 MAE difference is reliable.
6. **Specify the undersized implementation details**: how τ was chosen, how TS-SS is aggregated over multiple exemplars, whether CARPK results are averaged over random draws, and the verification threshold sensitivity analysis.

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a genuine contribution: the output-token verification idea is novel, the results on CARPK are strong, and the method is the best SAM-only approach on FSC-147. However, the abstract contains a factual overclaim, the efficiency argument is unsupported, and the marginal TS-SS improvement plus high RMSE ratio weaken the evidentiary strength. These are fixable issues — the core contribution is real — but they are nontrivial in the current version.
