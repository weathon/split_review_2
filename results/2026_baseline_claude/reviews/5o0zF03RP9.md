## Summary

IncentRL is a reinforcement learning framework that augments external rewards with a KL divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s). The paper's claimed central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable updated online, thereby eliminating manual trade-off tuning. Experiments are conducted on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar-v0 with DQN, and MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8.

---

## Strengths

- **Cognitively motivated framing:** The connections drawn to the Free Energy Principle and dopamine-based RPE are reasonable and provide an interpretable motivation for using KL divergence as a shaping term. The distinction from active inference (fixed vs. adaptive preferences) is clearly articulated.
- **KL-based shaping on sparse-reward tasks:** The observation that a small β can provide dense gradient signal in sparse-reward settings is empirically supported in MiniGrid (98% vs. 90.5% success rate, shorter episode lengths), and the intuition is sound.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

1. **The central claimed novelty—Bayesian β adaptation—is never actually specified.** The paper repeatedly states that β is "treated as a Bayesian random variable, updated online," but no update rule, likelihood model, prior, or inference procedure is ever given, anywhere in the paper. There is no algorithm box for the Bayesian update. The reader cannot reproduce, verify, or understand the key contribution.

2. **The empirical results for Bayesian β adaptation are internally contradictory.** The paper claims the posterior "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)." However, Figure 3 and Table 3 both show the posterior mean *decreasing from 0.11 to 0.017 across rounds*, with Round 4 statistics: mean=0.0173, min=0.0000, max=0.0587—none near 0.1. The dashed red "target effective β = 0.1" line is shown, but the posterior is actively diverging from it. The paper's narrative and its own figures are inconsistent, which raises serious questions about the validity of the claimed Bayesian adaptation results.

### Major

3. **Critical implementation details are absent.** In the continuous-state experiments (MountainCar, MiniGrid), the paper never explains: (a) how p(o|s,a) is estimated (neural network? forward model? bootstrap?), (b) how q(o|s) is represented as a distribution for continuous state spaces (MountainCar says "all probability to the goal x ≥ 0.5" but this is a set, not a distribution), and (c) how KL(p||q) is computed in practice. Section 4.3 mentions Gaussian/VAE representations as possibilities but does not say which was used in experiments. Without these details, results cannot be reproduced.

4. **No comparison to existing intrinsic motivation baselines.** The paper positions itself against curiosity-driven exploration (ICM, RND), empowerment, and VIME in the related work section, yet experiments compare only against a no-shaping baseline (β=0). Demonstrating that IncentRL outperforms or is complementary to established intrinsic motivation baselines on the same benchmarks is essential for the paper's claims of being a better alternative to "fixed trade-off" approaches.

5. **Extreme sensitivity to β contradicts the motivation for Bayesian adaptation.** In MountainCar, β=0.1 improves by +18%, but β=0.3 degrades by −53% and β=1.0 by −75%. This steep sensitivity is precisely the problem the paper claims to solve, yet the Bayesian scheme is not validated on MountainCar. The experimental evidence is incomplete.

6. **Very limited experimental scale.** Three seeds per configuration (2 for Figure 4) on benchmarks from 2017–2019 (MountainCar, MiniGrid 8×8) is insufficient to support claims about a general-purpose intrinsic motivation framework. Only a single MiniGrid variant is tested.

### Minor

7. **Theoretical propositions are trivial.** Proposition 1 (for small enough β, optimal policy is unchanged) follows immediately from the fact that finite MDPs have finitely many policies separated by a positive reward gap. Proposition 2 (β→∞ makes the agent ignore external reward) is definitional. Neither provides non-obvious insight beyond what is apparent from the objective function.

8. **The "Bayesian adaptation" results from the appendix (Table 3, Figures 3–4) appear to be run on a different setting than the main MiniGrid experiment.** The best fixed β for MiniGrid (Figure 2) is 0.01, but the Bayesian scheme targets β ≈ 0.1 (from MountainCar). The paper does not clarify which environment generated Figures 3–4, making these results ambiguous.

### Trivial
- The note "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" is qualitative and not quantified.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- A complete algorithmic specification of the Bayesian β update (prior, likelihood, inference step) is needed before any evaluation of the novelty is possible.
- Comparison to at least one well-known intrinsic motivation baseline (e.g., RND or ICM) on MiniGrid.
- Ablation on the Bayesian scheme vs. simple annealing or grid search across more environments.

---

## Novel Insights

The conceptual framing of treating the gap between predicted and preferred outcome distributions as a distributional analog of scalar RPE (connecting distributional RL with FEP-style goal-directedness) is an interesting research direction. However, the paper does not deliver a complete, validated instantiation of this idea. The Bayesian weighting scheme, if properly specified and validated, could be a useful contribution, but as presented it is not substantiated.

---

## Suggestions

- Write out the full Bayesian β update rule: define the prior p(β), the likelihood L(data|β), and the posterior update explicitly.
- Reconcile Figures 3–4 and Table 3 with the main experimental text: clarify which environment produces those β traces and why the posterior moves toward 0.02 rather than 0.1.
- Add comparison to ICM or RND on MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8 to demonstrate that IncentRL offers competitive or complementary performance.
- Specify the forward model architecture and KL computation method for the continuous-state experiments.
- Increase seeds to at least 5–10 per condition and include confidence intervals computed via bootstrap or t-test.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper is built around a claimed novelty—Bayesian online adaptation of the incentive weight β—that is never algorithmically specified. The empirical evidence for this novelty is internally inconsistent (posterior converges to the wrong value according to the paper's own figures). The supporting experiments lack key baselines, are limited in scale, and omit critical implementation details. The theoretical results are trivially weak. These are not presentation issues; they constitute a missing core contribution. The paper's research direction is interesting but the current submission is not ready for acceptance.
