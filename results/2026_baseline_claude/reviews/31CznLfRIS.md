## Summary
VideoJudge introduces a bootstrapping framework for training small MLLM-based judge models (3B and 7B parameters) to evaluate video understanding model outputs. The core pipeline uses a generator-evaluator loop to synthesize over 100K training examples without human annotation, iteratively refining responses until their predicted rating aligns with the target. The resulting judge models are fine-tuned to assign ratings, optionally generate instance-specific evaluation rubrics, and output pairwise preferences — achieving performance competitive with models up to 10× larger on multiple benchmarks.

---

## Strengths

- **Addresses a genuine gap with principled methodology.** Evaluation of open-ended video understanding outputs is under-resourced; BLEU/ROUGE are inadequate and human annotation is expensive. The generator-evaluator bootstrapping loop is a principled, scalable solution. Starting from 25K seed triplets and growing to 103K+ training examples with verified rating alignment (MAD acceptance criterion) is both practical and replicable.

- **Rubric generation is a novel and effective capability.** Training VideoJudgeR-3B to produce instance-specific rubrics at inference time is a meaningful contribution beyond simple scoring. The model achieves MAE 0.59 and Pearson 74.0, comparable to Qwen2.5-VL-32B/72B, while producing rubrics preferred by human annotators over much larger models (63.9% vs. Qwen-72B, 74.2% vs. Qwen-32B). This interpretability-efficiency trade-off is genuinely useful for practitioners.

- **Robust, multi-dimensional empirical analysis.** The paper goes beyond accuracy tables: temperature robustness analysis (Figure 4), frame-count scaling analysis, human validation of bootstrapped data quality (Cohen's κ = 89.5, >92% correctness vs. gold), LLM-as-judge and human rubric quality comparisons, and detailed error analysis with calibration issues all contribute to a comprehensive picture.

- **Important negative finding with practical implications.** The paper shows that text-only LLM judges (Qwen3 family, even with detailed video descriptions from a 72B model) consistently underperform direct MLLM video judges, and that long chain-of-thought reasoning does not help. This result is nontrivial given that description-based evaluation is widely used, and informs practitioners about the hidden cost of the LLM-judge approach.

- **Independent benchmark validation exists.** Performance gains on genuinely external benchmarks — VATEX (VideoJudge-3B RMSE 1.33 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-3B 2.27), LongVideoBench (VideoJudge-7B Δ(C-D) 1.16 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-72B 1.06), and VideoAutoArena (VideoJudge-7B 85.49 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-7B 75.29) — corroborate that the training provides genuine capability rather than purely distributional fitting.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

**Closed-loop evaluation on primary benchmarks.** VideoJudgeLLaVA-MetaEval and VideoJudgeVCG-MetaEval, which constitute two of the four main evaluation benchmarks, are constructed using the identical generator-evaluator pipeline used to produce the training data. Although the video-instruction pairs come from different source datasets, the candidate responses and their gold labels are generated and scored by the same models. A judge fine-tuned to mimic this evaluator's rating behavior will naturally correlate strongly with evaluator-labeled test data, inflating the apparent advantage over baselines. The paper acknowledges this ("closed-loop effects") but does not quantify how much of the gain on these two benchmarks is attributable to distributional alignment vs. genuine judgment capability. The most credible evidence for real generalization resides in VATEX, LongVideoBench, VideoAutoArena, and VideoJudge-Human — and on VideoAutoArena (the only large-scale external human-annotated pairwise benchmark), VideoJudge-7B (85.49) still lags Qwen2.5-VL-72B (89.80).

**Severe calibration failure in the mid-to-high quality range.** The error analysis reveals that 81.3% of gold-4 responses are incorrectly rated as 5, and 46.6% of gold-3 responses are inflated to 5. This is not a minor calibration issue — it means the judge nearly collapses the upper half of the rating scale, making fine-grained discrimination impossible precisely where most modern video understanding models operate. While the paper correctly diagnoses the problem (insufficient hard negatives near the top of the scale), the severity calls into question the practical utility of VideoJudge for differentiating competitive systems.

### Minor

**Gold response quality is unverified.** The bootstrapping pipeline treats responses from VideoInstruct-100K, VCG-Plus-112K, and VideoChat2-IT as rating-5 gold standards. These datasets are generated by instruction-tuned models and are not human-verified. If gold responses are themselves mediocre, the entire rating scale is anchored to a noisy reference, potentially explaining both the overestimation bias and poor upper-range calibration.

**Performance improvements are not consistently monotonic with model scale.** On VideoJudgeVCG, VideoJudge-7B (MAE 0.72, S=0.74) is only marginally better than Qwen2.5-VL-7B (MAE 0.76, S=0.65), and VideoJudge-3B (MAE 1.06, S=0.59) actually underperforms Qwen2.5-VL-3B (MAE 1.12, S=0.51) on MAE while barely improving Spearman. The paper's headline claim that bootstrapping enables smaller models to "match or surpass" much larger ones is strongest on the bootstrapped benchmarks, where the closed-loop concern applies.

**Rubric quality evaluation has limited statistical power for marginal comparisons.** The win rate of VideoJudgeR-3B vs. GPT-4o-mini in the LLM-as-judge setting is 92.7%, but in human evaluation it drops to 53.4% — a statistically marginal result with 300 pairs. The discrepancy between LLM-judge and human-judge preferences for rubrics (92.7% vs. 53.4%) suggests the LLM-judge evaluation for rubrics may be measuring stylistic similarity rather than quality, and the human result barely crosses the 50% line.

### Trivial
Figure 1 is reproduced three times in the main text with slightly different captions, which is likely a PDF parser artifact but adds redundancy.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Ablation on the quality of seed data: training on curated vs. raw VideoInstruct/VCG responses would clarify how sensitive the framework is to gold-response quality.
- Analysis of whether bootstrapped models can reliably rank outputs from modern frontier video models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini), which were likely not represented in the 2023–2024 training datasets.
- Quantification of how often the iterative refinement loop actually succeeds (acceptance rate per iteration), to inform practitioners about the pipeline's computational overhead.

---

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel insight is the demonstration that providing raw video input to a judge MLLM is not merely convenient but necessary: text-based descriptions generated by a 72B model are insufficient substitutes for direct visual processing, even with chain-of-thought reasoning. This finding is non-obvious given the high quality of modern video captioning systems, and directly contradicts common cost-cutting practices of using LLM judges with video summaries. The complementary finding that extended chain-of-thought reasoning provides no benefit for video judging (unlike in other domains) suggests that the bottleneck in video evaluation is perceptual grounding rather than inferential capacity — a result that could inform training priorities for future video MLLM judges.

---

## Suggestions

- Construct a held-out evaluation set where the gold ratings come exclusively from human annotators across all 1–5 bins, particularly with sufficient examples in the 3–5 range, to directly diagnose and track the overestimation bias.
- Experiment with weighted sampling or contrastive objectives that over-represent rating-4 vs. rating-5 pairs in training, to directly address the upper-range calibration failure without requiring new data collection.
- Report confidence intervals or statistical significance tests for the VideoAutoArena and VATEx results, given that the margins between VideoJudge-7B and the Qwen2.5-VL-72B baseline on external benchmarks are narrow.

---

## Score and Decision

VideoJudge tackles a real and important problem with a principled framework, thorough analysis, and tangible improvements on independent benchmarks. The rubric generation capability is a genuine contribution. However, the primary evaluation benchmarks suffer from a structural closed-loop circularity that limits the interpretability of the headline claims, and the severe overestimation bias in the mid-to-high quality range is a significant practical limitation for real-world deployment. The paper is transparent about both issues. The independent benchmark results are more modest but still support the core thesis that bootstrapped supervision provides meaningful capability gains. On balance, the contribution is sufficient for acceptance at the borderline.
