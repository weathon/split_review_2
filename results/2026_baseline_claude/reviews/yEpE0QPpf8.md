## Summary

This paper proposes **Grounding-IQA**, a new image quality assessment paradigm that integrates multimodal referring and grounding into IQA to enable fine-grained spatial quality perception. The work introduces two sub-tasks — GIQA-DES (quality description with bounding boxes) and GIQA-VQA (quality VQA about local regions) — along with a 167K-sample dataset (GIQA-160K) built via a multi-stage automated annotation pipeline, and a benchmark (GIQA-Bench) for evaluation from three angles: description quality, VQA accuracy, and grounding precision.

---

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated task paradigm.** Combining bounding-box grounding with IQA addresses a real limitation of existing MLLM-based IQA methods, which can describe quality verbally but cannot spatially localize quality-affecting regions. The problem is illustrated concretely with examples in Fig. 2 and in the motivation for local referring/grounding tasks.

- **Careful automated annotation pipeline.** The four-stage GIQA-DES pipeline (object-tag extraction → Grounding DINO detection → IQA-Filter & Box-Merge refinement → coordinate discretization) is thoughtfully designed. The description-phrase trick for grounding (`T_r` vs. object name) and the IQA-Filter algorithm that uses Q-Instruct to remove incorrect/irrelevant boxes are practically motivated and validated in Fig. 4 and Fig. 6.

- **Dataset scale and diversity.** GIQA-160K spans 167K instruction-tuning samples, 42,960 images, and multiple domains (in-the-wild, AI-generated, artificially degraded). The source heterogeneity (KonIQ, SPAQ, LIVE-FB, LIVE-itw, AGIQA-3K, ImageRewardDB, KADIS-700K) is a strength.

- **Multi-baseline ablation and cross-model compatibility.** The ablation studies (Tab. 2, 3) justify each design choice (refinement vs. raw boxes, discrete vs. normalized coordinates, multi-task vs. single-task training), and Tab. 4 demonstrates that GIQA-160K improves all four tested backbone architectures, suggesting genuine dataset quality rather than backbone-specific tuning.

- **Comprehensive benchmark evaluation metrics.** Assessing three orthogonal aspects—description quality (BLEU@4 + LLM-Score), VQA accuracy (Acc-Y, Acc-W), and grounding precision (mIoU, Tag-Recall)—provides a more complete picture than prior IQA benchmarks that use only a single score.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **GIQA-Bench is very small (100 images / 250 samples).** For a benchmark paper, this scale raises serious concerns about statistical reliability. Small sample sizes amplify variance: the differences in mIoU or Acc between methods could easily be within noise, yet no confidence intervals or significance tests are reported. A benchmark with 100 test images is insufficient to support strong conclusions about model ranking, especially when differences are sometimes < 1–2%.

- **LLM-Score circularity risk.** The LLM-Score metric uses Llama3 to score description quality, and Llama3 is also used to generate training data (GIQA-DES descriptions and GIQA-VQA pairs). Even though the benchmark descriptions are derived from Q-Pathway (with manual adjustments), the LLM judge and the data generator share the same model family and prior. This creates a potential circularity where models fine-tuned on Llama3-generated text happen to align more closely with Llama3's scoring preferences, inflating their apparent advantage.

- **Coordinate discretization formula appears inconsistent.** Eq. (1) states `id_l = y_1 · m · n + x_1 · n`. For normalized coordinates (x₁, y₁ ∈ [0,1]) and an n×m grid, this formula is dimensionally incorrect — it would yield fractional, non-integer grid IDs and values far outside [0, nm−1]. The correct discrete formula should use floor operations: `id_l = floor(y_1 · m) · n + floor(x_1 · n)`. This may be a notation artifact from PDF parsing, but if it reflects the actual implementation it would corrupt all ground-truth box coordinates in the dataset.

### Minor

- **Backbone models are dated.** LLaVA-v1.5, LLaVA-v1.6, and mPLUG-Owl2 (all from 2023–early 2024) are no longer representative of MLLM capability. Results on stronger and more recent backbones would better contextualize the contribution's ceiling.

- **Benchmark ground-truth boxes are manually determined only for GIQA-DES.** For GIQA-VQA, questions are generated by the pipeline then "refined and answered by humans." The degree of human oversight applied (whether annotators verify or merely adjust pipeline outputs) is not described precisely, leaving uncertainty about annotation quality.

- **Comparison baselines are not designed for the task.** The comparison is inherently asymmetric: "Ours" models are fine-tuned specifically for grounding-IQA, while all baselines are zero-shot on this task. Reporting baseline scores after a lightweight adaptation or prompting strategy tuned for the new task format would make the comparison fairer.

### Trivial
- Minor formatting inconsistencies between "IQA" and "IQG" in abbreviations across the paper body.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- A user study or human evaluation of the generated bounding-box quality would complement the automated metrics and strengthen claims about annotation pipeline fidelity.
- Reporting inter-annotator agreement for the GIQA-Bench human annotations would strengthen trust in the benchmark's reliability.

---

## Novel Insights

The paper's most interesting observation is that multi-task training (GIQA-DES + GIQA-VQA jointly) outperforms single-task training on both sub-tasks, particularly boosting grounding performance in VQA (Tab. 3: Tag-Recall 0.4872 → 0.7372). This suggests that the richer contextual descriptions in GIQA-DES act as a grounding "scaffold" that VQA training alone cannot replicate — a transferable finding for multimodal instruction tuning more broadly. Additionally, the IQA-Filter algorithm, which uses an IQA model to verify detected bounding boxes by quality attributes rather than semantic class, is a conceptually clean way to inject domain-specific priors into an otherwise general detection pipeline.

---

## Suggestions

- Expand GIQA-Bench to at least 500–1,000 images/samples with stratified quality levels and distortion types to enable robust quantitative conclusions. The current 100-image benchmark is too narrow.
- Validate or correct the coordinate discretization formula in Eq. (1) and report grid-localization precision (e.g., mean L1 error between discretized and original coordinates) to confirm no accuracy loss is introduced.
- Add an ablation on the LLM judge in LLM-Score (e.g., GPT-4 vs. Llama3) to assess metric robustness and rule out circularity.
- Include at least one modern MLLM backbone (e.g., LLaVA-1.6-34B, InternVL2, or Qwen-VL) in the compatibility study.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale dataset and benchmark that combines spatial grounding with image quality assessment. The automated annotation pipeline is well-designed and the ablation evidence is solid. However, the benchmark's small size and the LLM-Score circularity risk are significant weaknesses for a contribution whose primary value is in the benchmark. The coordinate formula issue, if not a parser artifact, would be a serious data quality problem. With these concerns in mind, the work is above the average ICLR submission for its niche and provides a useful community resource, but does not fully meet the bar for a robust benchmark paper.
