## Summary

This paper identifies a "tokenization dilemma" in Scientific LLMs (Sci-LLMs) for biomolecular understanding: sequence-as-language models destroy functional motifs through granular tokenization, while sequence-as-modality models face a semantic misalignment between biological and linguistic embedding spaces. The authors propose a context-driven paradigm that bypasses raw sequences entirely, instead providing LLMs with structured annotations produced by bioinformatics pipelines (InterProScan/Pfam, BLASTp against Swiss-Prot, ProTrek fallback). Through a systematic comparison across three specialized Sci-LLMs and four general-purpose LLMs on a protein QA benchmark, the paper argues that (i) context-only dramatically outperforms sequence-only, and (ii) adding raw sequence alongside context consistently degrades performance—sequences act as "informational noise."

---

## Strengths

- **Practical value and clear framing.** The idea of grounding LLM-based biomolecular reasoning in established bioinformatics tool outputs rather than raw sequences is immediately actionable. The pipeline using InterProScan + BLASTp + ProTrek is well-motivated and reproducible.
- **Multi-model, multi-task coverage.** Evaluating 7 models (Evolla, Intern-S1, NatureLM, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Qwen3-235B) across three task types (function, pathway, subcellular localization) with a common evaluation protocol is thorough and allows fair cross-model comparison within the paper's setup.
- **Layer-wise degradation analysis (Section 5.3).** Tracing ARI through SaProt encoder → Q-Former → decoder (0.945 → 0.916 → 0.809 for Evolla) provides concrete mechanistic evidence that the alignment module, not the biological encoder, is where functional information is lost. This is a genuinely informative diagnostic.
- **Temporal analysis (Section 5.4).** Plotting performance versus a protein's first publication year to reveal different degradation rates across paradigms is a creative evaluation dimension and a useful contribution for the field.
- **Efficiency advantage (Table 2).** The cost-per-query analysis (23× cheaper, 154× faster in batch vs. Evolla) is concrete and positions the approach as practically superior, not just academically interesting.
- **Wet-lab validation on unpublished sequences.** Including a real validation on sequences absent from major databases at submission time (Section 5.6) adds external credibility beyond benchmark overfitting.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

*None that fully invalidate the work, but the following major issue significantly undermines the core narrative.*

### Major

1. **The "consistent degradation" claim is directly contradicted by the authors' own Table 1.** The paper repeatedly asserts that adding raw sequence to context "consistently degrades performance" across all models. This is **not supported by the data**. For three out of four general-purpose LLMs, Seq+Context *outperforms* Context-Only:
   - DeepSeek-V3: Seq+Context 86.03 (underlined as that model's best) vs. Context-Only 84.99
   - GPT-5: Seq+Context 76.45 (underlined) vs. Context-Only 75.76
   - Qwen3-235B: Seq+Context 85.90 (underlined) vs. Context-Only 84.99
   - Gemini 2.5 Pro: Context-Only 87.19 vs. Seq+Context 86.98 (difference < 0.3 points)

   The "noise" phenomenon holds for specialized Sci-LLMs (Evolla, Intern-S1, NatureLM) but not for general-purpose LLMs. The correct conclusion is more nuanced: specialized models fine-tuned on sequences are confused when sequence and context are co-presented, perhaps because the combined input shifts the input distribution away from their training regime. General-purpose models are unaffected or marginally helped. Generalizing this to a universal "sequences act as noise" claim misrepresents the evidence.

2. **Potential information leakage undermines the most compelling result.** The test set proteins are drawn from Swiss-Prot (answers "directly excerpted from the source database entry"). The BLASTp context is generated by querying Swiss-Prot. For any non-orphan query protein, BLASTp will return homologs at high sequence identity, whose Swiss-Prot GO annotations are functionally near-identical to the query's own annotations. The paper acknowledges it avoids "reading from the query protein's own record," but if a 95% identity homolog's annotations are retrieved, the context trivially encodes the ground truth. This confound means it is impossible to determine from the current evaluation how much of the gain stems from genuine reasoning versus near-trivial retrieval. The experimental design does not report or control for the sequence identity of BLAST hits used, nor does it construct a sub-analysis restricted to low-identity or orphan proteins in the main benchmark.

3. **The ARI comparison between paradigms (Sections 5.2) is not apples-to-apples.** The context-driven ARI (0.958) is computed by embedding the *textual functional annotations* of proteins (using Qwen-embedding), while the competing ARI values (0.492–0.809) are computed by embedding *sequence model outputs*. The ground-truth clusters are defined by 50% sequence identity (MMseqs2), and sequence identity strongly predicts functional annotation similarity. It is expected that embedding text that *directly states* the function will cluster better by function than embedding latent representations of raw sequences. This comparison does not provide evidence that the context-driven approach yields better *biological understanding*; it shows that text about function groups by function, which is circular.

### Minor

1. **Wet-lab validation uses well-characterized protein families.** Rhodopsin and PETase are both heavily studied; even novel sequence variants would have close homologs in public databases. This makes BLASTp highly effective and does not approximate the "truly novel orphan protein" scenario that the paper identifies as the primary limitation. Reporting what BLAST hit identity/coverage was achieved for these wet-lab sequences would clarify how representative this test is.

2. **Evolla Rhodopsin result in Figure 6 (5.00% accuracy) is anomalously low.** A 5% accuracy on a binary classification task (below random chance) suggests a systematic response inversion—Evolla may have inverted its predictions or misunderstood the prompt—rather than a fundamental biological understanding failure. This warrants an explanation rather than being cited at face value as evidence of catastrophic failure.

3. **Temporal confounding in Section 5.4.** All three approaches (context-driven, Evolla, Intern-S1) share a common confound: evaluation proteins' ground-truth annotations are richer for older proteins in Swiss-Prot, meaning the reference-based LLM-Score is naturally harder for recent proteins regardless of the method. Disentangling "sparse BLAST context" from "sparse/ambiguous ground truth" would strengthen this analysis.

4. **The LLM-Score evaluation metric is not validated.** Using an LLM as judge without reporting inter-rater agreement with human experts or calibration experiments introduces unquantified evaluation noise. The judge LLM is not named in the main text.

### Trivial

- The scope of the paper is framed around "biomolecular understanding" but the main results are proteins-only; DNA/RNA/small-molecule coverage is preliminary and relegated to an appendix.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- A sub-analysis conditioning on BLAST hit sequence identity bands (e.g., <30%, 30–50%, 50–90%, >90%) would directly address the leakage concern and clarify when the context-driven approach generalizes versus merely retrieves.
- Reporting standard deviations or confidence intervals for LLM-Score would allow readers to assess whether the Seq+Context vs. Context-Only differences are statistically significant, especially given that many differences are <2 points.
- A direct ablation removing BLASTp and using only InterProScan/Pfam (no Swiss-Prot lookup) would isolate the "intrinsic analysis" component and reduce leakage risk.

---

## Novel Insights

The layer-wise ARI analysis through Evolla's encoder → alignment → decoder stages is a genuinely novel diagnostic that localizes the "modality gap" problem to the Q-Former alignment module rather than the biological encoder itself. This insight is actionable: improving the alignment module is more targeted than redesigning the encoder. The temporal performance analysis by protein publication year is also a creative lens that reveals how structural dependence on evolutionary databases creates temporal brittleness in both the sequence-as-modality paradigm and the context-driven approach—though the context-driven method degrades more gracefully.

---

## Suggestions

- Correct the central claim: restate that sequences act as noise specifically for specialized Sci-LLMs, and that general-purpose LLMs are unaffected. This is more accurate and still a meaningful finding.
- Report BLAST hit sequence identities for both the benchmark test set and the wet-lab sequences; design a subset-analysis for low-identity hits (<50%) to separate reasoning from retrieval.
- Replace the ARI comparison in Section 5.2 with a pairwise test: for pairs of proteins from the same vs. different MMseqs2 clusters, compare the cosine similarity of context embeddings, sequence embeddings, and model output embeddings. This controls for the text-vs-sequence asymmetry.
- Add ablation: context from InterProScan-only (no BLASTp) to measure how much of the gain is intrinsic domain analysis versus homolog retrieval.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a genuinely important question—whether current Sci-LLMs actually understand sequences or merely associate text—and produces empirically grounded results that are useful to the community. The context-driven pipeline is practical, well-described, and the efficiency advantage is convincing. However, the central narrative claim ("sequences consistently act as noise") is contradicted by the authors' own data for general-purpose LLMs, and the primary performance comparison is potentially confounded by near-trivial retrieval via high-identity BLASTp hits. The representation analysis (Section 5.2) compares inherently incomparable quantities. These are substantive methodological issues, not peripheral details, as they bear directly on the two main conclusions of the paper.
