## Summary

The paper presents three coordinated contributions for multimodal test-time scaling: (1) **VisualPRM400K**, a ~400K-sample dataset of multimodal process supervision data constructed via Monte Carlo sampling from InternVL2.5 model outputs; (2) **VisualPRM**, an 8B multimodal Process Reward Model trained on this dataset and formulated as a multi-turn chat task; and (3) **VisualProcessBench**, a 2,866-sample benchmark with 26,950 human-annotated step-wise correctness labels for evaluating PRM and MLLM performance on detecting erroneous reasoning steps. Empirically, VisualPRM improves Best-of-8 reasoning performance across 3 model families, 4 model scales, and 7 benchmarks, consistently outperforming outcome reward models and self-consistency.

---

## Strengths

- **Clear gap, principled extension**: While PRM research is mature for text (PRM800K, MathShepherd, OmegaPRM), no comparable multimodal process supervision dataset existed. The paper directly addresses this gap by extending Monte Carlo–based automatic annotation to image-conditioned reasoning, producing the first large-scale multimodal PRM dataset. Resulting gains on a wholly different policy family (MiniCPM-V2.6: +8.0 points) corroborate that the dataset's value is not model-specific.

- **Credible human-annotated benchmark**: VisualProcessBench is a genuine contribution. The annotation procedure—13 university-level annotators, 39 person-days, 10% per-split author review with re-annotation for faulty splits—is methodologically sound. The design decision to require annotation of *all* erroneous steps (rather than only the first) is well-motivated by the rise of reflection-capable models and reduces false-negative rate.

- **Thorough empirical validation**: Experiments span 3 model families (MiniCPM, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5), 4 model scales (7B–78B), 7 multimodal benchmarks, 3 critic baselines (SC, ORM, PRM), text-only reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond), and a scaling study from N=1 to N=128. Consistent gains across this breadth are convincing.

- **Informative ablation studies**: The comparison between value-based and advantage-based PRM formulations, three score aggregation strategies, and supervised-vs-early-stop training yields actionable engineering insights. The finding that "max" aggregation degrades due to high-scoring early steps is specifically useful for practitioners.

- **Efficient inference design**: VisualPRM scores all steps in a single forward pass using a "+" placeholder, contrasting with autoregressive MLLM-as-judge which is slow. This practical advantage is clearly demonstrated.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Absence of oracle (Pass@N) baselines in scaling curves**: Figure 4 shows SC, ORM, and PRM scaling curves but omits Pass@N oracle selection. Without the oracle bound, it is impossible to judge how efficiently VisualPRM approaches the upper limit at each N. For instance, if oracle Pass@128 is 47% and VisualPRM reaches 46%, the model is near-optimal; if oracle is 70%, there is substantial headroom obscured by the plot. This analysis is standard in BoN evaluation (it is implicit in the MathShepherd and OmegaPRM papers) and its absence weakens the evaluation narrative.

2. **In-distribution confound between training and evaluation**: VisualPRM400K solutions are sampled from the InternVL2.5 series; VisualPRM is then applied to InternVL2.5 as the primary policy. The largest BoN improvements occur precisely for InternVL2.5 models (InternVL2.5-8B +8.4 vs Qwen2.5-VL-7B +3.7). The paper does not discuss whether the PRM may partly exploit stylistic or distributional idiosyncrasies of InternVL2.5-generated text rather than learning genuinely transferable step-quality signals. A direct ablation—training on InternVL2.5-generated steps, evaluating on Qwen2.5-VL solutions only, and vice versa—would isolate this effect. As written, the in-distribution advantage cannot be ruled out.

### Minor

1. **Threshold for VisualProcessBench evaluation unspecified**: Section 4.2 states "a step is considered correct if the probability of outputting '+' exceeds that of outputting '−' by a certain threshold," but the threshold value is never stated. Since VisualProcessBench is proposed as an evaluation resource for the community, this choice should be documented and ideally ablated.

2. **Class imbalance in training (10% incorrect steps) lacks mitigation detail**: The paper acknowledges the ~10:1 imbalance but does not describe whether any mitigation was applied (e.g., weighted loss, oversampling). For readers who wish to reproduce or extend the method, this omission is consequential.

3. **VisualProcessBench excludes solutions generated by Gemini-2.0-Flash**: Table 1 lists GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, QvQ-72B-Preview, and InternVL2.5-78B as solution sources, but Gemini-2.0-Flash—one of the strongest proprietary models evaluated on the benchmark (F1=62.3)—generated none of the solutions. This may inflate Gemini's F1 slightly since its outputs do not appear in the benchmark's solution pool; mentioning this asymmetry would improve transparency.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation is the *advantage-based versus value-based* PRM comparison in the multimodal setting. The paper finds that advantage-based labeling (measuring marginal change in expected accuracy) underperforms value-based labeling, attributing this to noise in MC-estimated accuracy differences at the step level—differences that are too small relative to the noise floor at 16 continuations per step. This is a concrete, testable hypothesis and differs from prior text-domain findings; it suggests that the multimodal reasoning domain, with its more heterogeneous step structure, may have higher MC-estimation variance than purely textual math chains. Additionally, the finding that ORMs plateau and can *decline* at large N while PRMs continue to improve is a clean empirical result that speaks to a known theoretical difference between the two reward-model classes.

## Suggestions

- Include a table or figure showing oracle Pass@N alongside BoN results for at least two policy models.
- Conduct a cross-dataset training experiment (train on InternVL2.5-generated steps, evaluate BoN on Qwen2.5-VL only) to estimate the in-distribution advantage quantitatively.
- State the VisualProcessBench decision threshold explicitly and report F1 as a function of threshold for VisualPRM to make the benchmark setup fully reproducible.
- Clarify Figure 1's axis labels (which appear corrupted by the parser) and ensure the numeric values therein are consistent with Table 2 in the final manuscript.

## Score and Decision

The paper makes three concrete, well-executed contributions to a timely problem—multimodal test-time scaling—where prior work genuinely did not exist at this scale. The empirical evidence is broad and the human-annotated benchmark adds durable value beyond a single paper's experiments. The primary weaknesses—missing oracle curves and the unaddressed in-distribution confound—are meaningful but do not invalidate the contributions. This is a solid resource/empirical paper appropriate for the venue.
