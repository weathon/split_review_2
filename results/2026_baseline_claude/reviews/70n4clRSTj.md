## Summary
SpookyBench is a diagnostic benchmark that evaluates purely temporal understanding in video-language models by encoding information exclusively through temporal motion sequences — individual frames appear as random noise, but temporal integration reveals text, object shapes, and dynamic scenes. Human participants achieve 98% accuracy on these stimuli, while 27 evaluated VLMs (open and closed source, 2B–78B parameters) all achieve 0% accuracy across three task categories, two prompting strategies, multiple frame rates, and after targeted fine-tuning.

## Strengths

- **Compelling and robust null result**: The 0% vs. 98% accuracy gap is extraordinarily clean and consistent. Across 27 diverse architectures, model scales (2B–78B), closed-source commercial systems (GPT-4o, Gemini), specialized temporal models (TimeChat), multiple prompting strategies, and fine-tuned variants, the result holds without exception. This consistency is itself notable evidence.

- **Principled benchmark design with formal encoding algorithms**: The temporal encoding mechanisms (opposing foreground/background noise motion for text/images; threshold-based motion for depth maps) are precisely specified with formal algorithms, enabling reproducibility and providing a theoretically clean isolation of temporal from spatial cues.

- **Multi-axis ablation that rules out alternative explanations**: The paper systematically eliminates confounds: (a) frame-rate experiments show VLMs fail at all sampling rates (1–30 FPS) while humans degrade predictably, ruling out subsampling as the sole issue; (b) CoT prompting fails as well as direct prompting, ruling out prompt engineering; (c) fine-tuning on 400 in-distribution SpookyBench videos still yields 0%, ruling out domain shift as the primary explanation. Together these ablations strongly support the architectural interpretation.

- **Quantitative signal characterization**: The SNR analysis (basic, perceptual, temporal coherence, motion contrast) provides a concrete quantification of why individual-frame analysis is insufficient and characterizes why the task is hard for current architectures even in principle.

- **Practically important finding with safety implications**: The binary threshold phenomenon in SNR for text detection (abrupt rather than gradual degradation below ~2.5 dB) has direct implications for safety-critical applications (road signs, medical labels) and adversarial robustness.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Underpowered human baseline (6 participants)**: The human evaluation — which provides the upper bound the paper is built around — involved only 6 participants. While the reported 98% accuracy is internally consistent across participants, a benchmark paper at ICLR typically requires a more rigorous human study (30+ participants, with power analysis, inter-rater reliability, and statistical testing beyond a single weighted average). A finding of 98% vs. 0% with such a small N leaves open the question of whether results generalize across demographics, viewing conditions, and monitor calibrations.

2. **Ambiguous number of frames actually fed to VLMs**: The paper states average video duration is 7.11 s with 333.5 frames per video (≈46.8 FPS average). At 30 FPS this is ~213 frames per video. The paper says only "We input sequences of multiple video frames simultaneously for models that do not directly support video input," without specifying the number of frames given to each model. This is a critical methodological detail: if models received only 8–16 frames (common in VLM inference), the effective sampling rate would be <2.5 FPS even for the "30 FPS" condition, which humans cannot handle either (human performance at 1 FPS is 0%). The fine-tuning experiment provides some insurance here, but the primary evaluation's frame count needs to be clearly documented per model.

3. **Fine-tuning experiment is limited in scale and does not explore efficient signal compression**: Training on 400 videos for 10 epochs is a modest intervention for models that contain billions of parameters with spatially pre-wired visual encoders. A negative result here is compelling but not conclusive — it cannot easily distinguish "architecturally incapable" from "insufficient training compute/data." Probing whether any gradient flows reach motion-sensitive pathways during fine-tuning would strengthen the architectural claim.

### Minor

1. **Dataset scale (451 videos) is modest**: While the dataset can be extended indefinitely via the generator, the actual evaluated benchmark is small, particularly the Dynamic Scenes category (57 videos). This limits statistical power for per-category conclusions.

2. **CoT analysis is shallow**: The paper reports that CoT prompts yield no improvement (0% → 0%), but does not analyze the intermediate reasoning text generated by the models. Understanding whether models describe any temporal change in their chain-of-thought (even if incorrect) would give richer insight into *where* the processing fails.

3. **The SNR–performance relationship for VLMs is asserted but not demonstrated**: Section 3.3.1 identifies SNR patterns and states these "explain" the observed performance gap, but no analysis is presented relating SNR values to per-video or per-model outcomes (which are all 0%). The SNR analysis is well-described but its explanatory connection to VLM failure is assumed rather than evidenced.

### Trivial
None worth noting beyond parser artifacts.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A table documenting the exact number of frames provided to each evaluated model during inference would resolve the major ambiguity and substantially strengthen the paper.
- Even a brief analysis of what the models output when they fail (do they describe noise? do they confabulate? do they acknowledge uncertainty?) would enrich the failure-mode characterization.
- Testing a simple optical-flow preprocessing baseline (compute optical flow and feed the flow field to a VLM) would provide a useful lower bound on whether the limitation is in temporal integration vs. pre-processing.

## Novel Insights

SpookyBench delivers a genuinely novel empirical insight: the failure of current VLMs on purely temporal stimuli is not merely a matter of benchmark difficulty, temporal-reasoning data scarcity, or prompting strategy — it survives fine-tuning on in-distribution data, suggesting that the spatial-first processing paradigm (ViT frame encoders → temporal integration → LLM) structurally prevents information present only in relative inter-frame motion from being recovered. This is qualitatively different from prior temporal benchmarks (TemporalBench, TVBench), which still allow spatial shortcuts; SpookyBench is the first benchmark to surgically eliminate them. The binary SNR threshold phenomenon — where temporal detectability behaves like a step function rather than a gradient — is also an original empirical finding with underappreciated implications for adversarial robustness in safety-critical perception systems.

## Suggestions

1. Document the exact number of frames fed to each model in the main paper, and verify that at least some models receive enough frames (≥20 FPS effective rate) to exceed the human operating threshold before concluding all models fail architecturally.
2. Expand the human study to ≥30 participants with proper statistical testing and inter-rater reliability reporting.
3. Add a per-video model output analysis (qualitative failure modes) to the results section, showing representative model responses for each category.
4. Consider including an optical-flow preprocessing baseline to separate "architectural temporal integration failure" from "pre-processing failure."

## Score and Decision

SpookyBench presents a clean, well-executed, and important diagnostic result that is directly relevant to the video understanding community. The 0% vs. 98% gap, replicated across 27 models, multiple prompting strategies, frame rates, and after fine-tuning, is a compelling and striking contribution. The methodological concern about frame count per model and the small human study are real gaps that ideally would be resolved, but the convergent evidence from ablations (particularly fine-tuning) provides meaningful support for the core claim. As a benchmark paper that reveals a fundamental blind spot in current architectures and provides a public, extendable dataset, this is a genuine community contribution.
