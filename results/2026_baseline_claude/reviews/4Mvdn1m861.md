## Summary

The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM) without any fine-tuning or auxiliary models. The two core contributions are: (1) a **probabilistic prompt generation** stage that computes a cosine-similarity-based probability distribution over image embeddings and uses multinomial sampling with iterative refinement to place prompts efficiently, and (2) an **output token-based verification** stage that exploits SAM's decoder output tokens—which jointly encode semantic and positional information—via the TS-SS (Triangle-Sector Similarity) metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. On FSC-147, the method achieves MAE 16.25 (best among SAM-only training-free methods), and on CARPK achieves MAE 4.68 (surpassing all training-based methods).

---

## Strengths

- **Novel and principled use of SAM's output tokens.** Using the decoder output tokens rather than image-encoder embeddings for verification is a genuinely new insight. The bidirectional attention mechanism in SAM's decoder enriches these tokens with position-conditioned semantics, making them more discriminative than the raw image embedding space. The PCA visualizations in Figure 5 concisely substantiate this claim: image embeddings of positive and negative instances overlap significantly, while output tokens form cleanly separated clusters.

- **No auxiliary models required.** Unlike competitive training-free methods (e.g., TFCAC which adds DINO), the proposed method uses SAM alone. This is a meaningful practical advantage—simpler deployment, lower memory footprint—and not merely a performance trade-off: on CARPK it outperforms all supervised baselines and most training-free ones even with this constraint.

- **Well-structured ablation study.** Tables 3–5 collectively validate the key design choices. Table 3 establishes that neither dot product, cosine similarity, nor L2 distance alone is sufficient and motivates TS-SS as a hybrid. Table 4 provides a clear compute-accuracy trade-off characterization. Table 5 directly compares output-token space to image-embedding space under identical sampling, confirming the core claim.

- **Iterative probabilistic prompt strategy.** The adaptive update rules (zeroing out confirmed positives, down-weighting confirmed negatives) are simple yet well-motivated: they prevent redundant sampling and enable the search to progressively cover spatially distant objects—directly addressing the locality limitation of output tokens noted by the authors.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Persistently high RMSE on FSC-147 despite good MAE.** The method achieves MAE 16.25 but RMSE 133.11—one of the highest in Table 1. For comparison, LOCA (supervised) reaches RMSE 56.97 and TFCAC (training-free, SAM+α) reaches RMSE 56.33. An RMSE more than twice these baselines indicates catastrophic failures on a non-trivial subset of images (likely dense or small-object scenes). The paper briefly acknowledges "dense distributions of very small instances" as a limitation but does not quantify how many images are affected, what counts they exhibit, or whether any mitigation is feasible. Given that RMSE penalizes large errors quadratically, this gap represents a real reliability problem for applications involving high-density scenes and should be discussed more substantively.

2. **Marginal empirical support for TS-SS over cosine similarity.** Table 3 shows cosine similarity at 16.48 MAE and TS-SS at 16.25 MAE—a difference of 0.23, or about 1.4% relative improvement. While the geometric motivation for combining angle and magnitude is conceptually sound, the actual performance delta is well within noise for benchmark-scale datasets. Given that TS-SS is presented as a core contribution, the evidence for its necessity is weak. An experiment on a harder or more varied subset, or on CARPK, would substantially strengthen this claim.

3. **Threshold hyperparameter (300) lacks sensitivity analysis.** The token verification threshold of 300 is stated without a sweep over candidate values. This threshold gates which instances are accepted as positive, so its setting directly controls precision-recall trade-off. Even a coarse grid search (e.g., 100, 200, 300, 500, 1000) with corresponding FSC-147 metrics would be necessary to understand how sensitive the method is and whether the reported number is meaningfully optimized or incidentally chosen.

### Minor

1. **CARPK evaluation protocol differs from FSC-147.** On FSC-147 the standard 3-shot protocol is used; on CARPK, 12 exemplars are randomly drawn from the training set. This makes direct comparison of difficulty levels ambiguous and should be stated more prominently.

2. **Efficiency claim is qualitative only.** The paper asserts the method is "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC on CARPK but provides no wall-clock numbers for TFCAC or other training-free competitors. The 1.69 s/image figure for FSC-147 is given only for the proposed method; a table or even rough comparison would meaningfully support the efficiency narrative.

3. **One-shot FSC-147 (Table 2) is underdiscussed.** MAE 18.57 vs LOCA's 12.53 is a 48% gap; RMSE 135.86 far exceeds all other methods. Given that the table is included, it deserves more than a single sentence of commentary.

### Trivial

- The discussion section contains a self-contradictory sentence: "Our method…demonstrates superior computational efficiency compared to state of the art. This limitation affects scalability for real-time applications" — the referents of "this limitation" and "state of the art" are unclear and could be rephrased for precision.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A per-category MAE/RMSE breakdown on FSC-147 would pinpoint which object types cause the high RMSE and support the dense-scene failure hypothesis.
- A failure-case figure complementing Figure 4 would give reviewers and future readers a clearer picture of where the method degrades.

## Novel Insights

The key novel insight is the repurposing of SAM's decoder output tokens as a category-aware discriminator for counting. While SAM's image encoder embeddings have been used extensively for similarity in prior work (TFOC, OmniCount, TFCounter), the output tokens—formed through bidirectional attention between prompt embeddings and image features—integrate prompt-specific positional context in a way that raw encoder features do not. The PCA visualization provides concrete evidence that this joint encoding produces a more linearly separable feature space for positive/negative instance discrimination. This observation may have broader implications: any encoder-decoder vision foundation model whose decoder produces prompt-conditioned tokens could potentially be repurposed for few-shot recognition or localization tasks without accessing decoder heads or class embeddings.

---

## Suggestions

- Conduct a threshold sweep for the TS-SS verification threshold (e.g., {100, 200, 300, 500, 1000}) and report how MAE/RMSE vary—this would validate robustness and is a straightforward experiment.
- Add per-bucket analysis (e.g., low/medium/high count scenes) to explain the RMSE gap versus TFCAC; this would reveal whether failure is concentrated in a specific regime.
- Provide inference time comparisons for all competing training-free methods in a single table to make the efficiency claim verifiable.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a genuine and well-motivated insight—using SAM's decoder output tokens rather than image encoder embeddings for few-shot object counting—and supports it with PCA visualizations, ablation studies, and competitive benchmarks. The training-free, single-model design is practically valuable. However, the RMSE gap (more than 2× vs. TFCAC on FSC-147) points to systematic failure in hard scenes that is not adequately characterized, and the headline contribution of TS-SS shows only a 0.23 MAE gain over cosine similarity. These weaknesses are real but do not invalidate the core observation. The paper sits above the average quality bar for this venue and merits a borderline accept, contingent on the authors better characterizing the high-RMSE failure mode.
