## Summary

This paper identifies semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representation learning—specifically that dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality)—and proposes a diagnostic metric (variously named throughout the paper) together with an asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) mechanism that dynamically re-weights seasonal and trend contrastive losses in the CoST framework based on component imbalance.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: The observation that dominant temporal components suppress weaker ones in contrastive learning is practically meaningful, and the SDE formulation (cosine similarity of residual embeddings) is a reasonable probe of this phenomenon. Table 1 clearly validates that TS2Vec exhibits the described imbalance across amplitude ratios.
- **Honest negative result reporting**: The paper explicitly reports that direct SDE regularization (Table 2) does not help, and uses this to motivate the multi-view approach—this kind of scientific transparency is valuable.
- **Plug-and-play design**: The APW mechanism requires no architectural changes to CoST, making it practically accessible.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None that completely invalidate every result.

### Major

1. **Core metric has three different names throughout the paper.** The title and abstract call it "Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE)," Section 3.2 calls it "Semantic Separability Error (SDE)," and Section 4.3 explicitly re-introduces it under yet another name "Semantic Decomposition Error (SDE)." This is not a typo—the three labels appear in different headings and describe the same quantity. This suggests the paper was not carefully unified and raises questions about the rigor of the contribution.

2. **Experimental results contradict the paper's central claim of "consistent gains."** In Table 3:
   - On Electricity at horizons 168, 336, and 720, CoST+APW is the *worst* method, with MSEs of 0.566, 0.677, 1.010 vs. TNC's 0.412, 0.548, 0.859.
   - On Weather at horizons 336 and 720, TNC and TS2Vec outperform CoST+APW by wide margins (e.g., Weather-720: CoST+APW MSE=0.299 vs. TS2Vec's 0.233 and TNC's 0.219).
   - On ETT1 at horizon 24, TS2Vec (0.039) edges out CoST+APW (0.04); at horizon 48, CoST (0.06) beats CoST+APW (0.07).
   These are not minor perturbations—in some cases the proposed method significantly underperforms. The abstract's claim of "consistent gains" is not supported.

3. **No ablation separating APW from the MLP-based composite embedding.** The paper introduces two intertwined components in Section 4.4: an MLP that fuses trend and seasonal embeddings, and the asymmetric weighting. Without an ablation comparing (a) CoST alone, (b) CoST + MLP only, (c) CoST + APW only, and (d) CoST + MLP + APW, the individual contribution of APW cannot be attributed.

4. **Theoretical basis for SDE's compositionality assumption is unaddressed.** SDE is defined as `1 - cos(v(a+b) - v(b), v(a))`, implicitly assuming that `v(a+b) - v(b) ≈ v(a)` when the component is well-represented. This is a strong linearity assumption for a nonlinear neural encoder, and the paper provides no justification for why this approximation should hold or when it breaks down.

### Minor

1. **γ and γ' are unexplained.** These hyperparameters control the sensitivity of the loss reweighting to Δ, but there is no ablation over their values, no guidance for practitioners, and no analysis of how stable they are across datasets.

2. **Δ computation during training is unspecified.** Whether Δ is computed per-batch, per-epoch, or on a held-out set, and how noisy these estimates are early in training, is never discussed. This is necessary for reproducibility.

3. **Table 3 has a labeling error**: Two separate groups of rows are both labeled "ETT1" (rows 1–5 and rows 11–15), likely representing ETTh1 and ETTm1. The duplicated label makes the table hard to interpret.

### Trivial

- The figure caption is repeated three times in the text in slightly different forms.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A direct comparison table where CoST+APW is applied to Electricity and Weather without the MLP would clarify whether the degradation at long horizons stems from the fusion layer or the weighting scheme.
- Analysis of how Δ evolves during training would build intuition about whether the adaptive weighting is doing useful work or is effectively constant after warmup.

## Novel Insights

The paper surfaces a genuine and underappreciated issue: contrastive objectives trained on composite time-series signals can systematically underrepresent weaker frequency components, and this is quantifiable via cosine residual similarity in embedding space. The diagnostic framing (Table 1) and the failed regularization experiment are instructive. However, the subsequent solution—reweighting existing losses by a scalar derived from this diagnostic—is straightforward and its empirical payoff is not demonstrated convincingly.

## Suggestions

- Rerun experiments on Electricity and Weather long horizons with careful debugging; the large degradation there suggests a possible bug or inappropriate hyperparameter choice rather than a principled failure mode.
- Unify the metric name throughout the paper before any resubmission.
- Add a 2×2 ablation table: CoST vs. CoST+MLP vs. CoST+APW vs. CoST+MLP+APW on at least one dataset to isolate contributions.
- Provide a sensitivity analysis of γ and γ' across at least three values per dataset.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a meaningful problem and includes some honest diagnostic work. However, the core empirical claim is directly contradicted by the results in Table 3 (CoST+APW fails badly on Electricity and Weather at long horizons), the single most important identifier of the contribution changes names three times, and the ablation structure needed to validate APW's specific contribution is absent. These are not minor presentation flaws—they undermine confidence in both the method and its evaluation.
