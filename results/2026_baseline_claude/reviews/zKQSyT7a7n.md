## Summary

The paper introduces multi-task Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM), which augment vision-only latent-space world models with fingertip tactile sensing (Digit 360 sensors) to better capture contact physics in robot manipulation. The architecture uses Cosmos and Sparsh-X pretrained encoders, fused via a 12-layer transformer with factorized spatio-temporal self-attention and action cross-attention. Key claims: (1) VT-WM reduces normalized Fréchet distance of imagined object trajectories by ~33% (object permanence) and ~29% (causal compliance) compared to a vision-only baseline; (2) CEM-based zero-shot planning with VT-WM yields up to 35% higher real-robot success rates on contact-rich tasks; (3) VT-WM is more data-efficient than behavioral cloning (BC) when adapting to a new task with only 20 demonstrations.

---

## Strengths

- **Clear motivation tied to a genuine failure mode.** Vision-only world models are known to hallucinate under occlusion. Tactile sensing, which directly captures local contact, is the natural complement. The paper demonstrates this qualitatively (fig. 5, fig. 7) and quantitatively, making the case convincingly rather than by assertion alone.

- **Principled evaluation of world model quality.** Using CoTracker keypoint tracking to compute normalized Fréchet distance between imagined and real object trajectories is a well-grounded methodology. Separating *object permanence* (tracked objects that should move) from *causal compliance* (static objects that should remain still) decomposes a complex concept into testable criteria. Paired t-tests with p-value reporting further strengthen the analysis.

- **Real-robot experiments with a multi-step task battery.** Five tasks of increasing difficulty—from reaching to cube stacking—are evaluated zero-shot with CEM. The 35% gain in *reach & push* and 31% in *wipe cloth* are large and practically significant. The data-efficiency comparison (7/9 vs. 2/9 on plate-insertion with 20 demos) strengthens the case for pre-training reuse.

- **Multi-task generalization is explicitly demonstrated.** Unlike prior tactile dynamics work (task-specific), VT-WM is trained on all tasks jointly and the encoders are frozen pretrained networks. This design choice is justified and makes the contribution more broadly applicable.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Surprisingly small real-robot trial counts with inconsistent percentages.** The paper states "five trials per task from distinct initial conditions," yet the reported success rates—69%, 92%, 93%, 83%—are not representable as exact fractions of five (k/5 ∈ {0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100}%). Either more trials were run, the counts per task differ, or the bar-chart OCR introduced rounding. This ambiguity undermines the precision of the comparisons; no statistical test is reported for the planning results, so marginal differences (e.g., 100% vs. 100% for *reach button*; 11% gain on *stack cubes*) cannot be distinguished from noise. The paper should explicitly state the number of trials per task and, if n ≥ 10, report confidence intervals.

2. **Comparison with BC in the data-efficiency experiment is structurally asymmetric, but the implications are not fully unpacked.** VT-WM fine-tuned on 20 demos already benefits from multi-task pretraining across all contact-rich tasks, whereas ACT (BC) is trained from scratch on the 20 demos alone. The paper acknowledges this correctly in its framing, but the conclusion ("3× data efficiency") mixes architectural advantages with world-model-vs-policy advantages. A stronger experiment would compare a BC policy pre-trained on the same multi-task dataset and fine-tuned on the 20 new demos, to isolate how much gain comes from the world-model planning component vs. the pre-training setup.

3. **Open-loop execution limits practical scope.** Plans generated by CEM are executed open-loop. For contact-rich tasks, accumulated execution errors propagate uncorrected. The BC policy (ACT) used for comparison is closed-loop (re-planning at each timestep). This means VT-WM's lower success rate in some sub-tasks could partly reflect open-loop limitations rather than world model quality. The paper does not control for this asymmetry; at minimum, a brief discussion of open-loop failure modes and how they interact with planning quality is warranted.

### Minor

1. **Degradation in causal compliance on *scribble with marker*.** VT-WM achieves a *higher* Fréchet distance than V-WM on this task (approximately 0.50 vs. 0.35), violating the paper's central claim of uniform improvement. The paper notes this briefly but provides no analysis. Understanding why tactile sensing hurts in this case (stylus tip mechanics? sensor geometry? task ambiguity?) is important for scoping the contribution's generality.

2. **Binary hand action space.** The Allegro Hand has 16 degrees of freedom, yet the planning action space encodes hand state as a single binary (open/close). This forces all contact-requiring manipulation to pre-defined grasp postures, potentially confounding conclusions about fine-grained contact grounding—any nuanced finger interaction during, e.g., cube stacking is invisible to the planner.

3. **No analysis of planning computational cost.** CEM with a transformer predictor iterating over populations of candidate action sequences on a real-robot closed loop is potentially expensive. The paper does not report inference time, population size, number of CEM iterations, or planning frequency. For practitioners considering adoption, this is relevant information.

### Trivial

- The figure 8 table shows "1250.0%" as a relative improvement annotation, which is a formatting artifact that is misleading at first glance (it appears to be a second success-rate column).

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation of tactile encoder choice (e.g., Sparsh-X vs. raw tactile images) would clarify how much the contribution depends on the specific foundation model vs. any form of touch.
- A closed-loop MPC version with rolling replanning would significantly improve practical relevance and allow a fair comparison with closed-loop BC.
- Reporting how many CEM function evaluations and wall-clock time are required per task would help practitioners.

---

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel contribution is the systematic decomposition of world-model failure modes into *object permanence* (active object hallucinations under occlusion) and *causal compliance* (spurious motion of passive objects), operationalized via CoTracker Fréchet distance and paired significance testing. This evaluation framework—independent of any specific model—is transferable to benchmarking future world models for manipulation. The demonstration that fixing causal compliance (objects that should remain still) is as important as object permanence is a non-obvious result: even when no contact is intended, V-WM hallucinates object motion that leaks into cost evaluation during CEM planning, explaining planning failures on wipe/push tasks.

---

## Suggestions

- Clarify the exact number of real-robot trials per task and verify that stated percentages match integer counts; if the current percentages are approximate bar-chart readings, provide precise numbers in the paper.
- Add a multi-task-pretrained BC baseline (same dataset, fine-tuned on 20 demos) to cleanly isolate the world-model planning advantage from the pre-training advantage.
- Analyze the *scribble with marker* causal compliance degradation; understanding when tactile sensing hurts (not just when it helps) is important for framing the contribution accurately.
- Consider reporting planning frequency / latency for at least one task to enable reproducibility assessment.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a meaningful, concrete contribution to visuo-tactile world modeling: the first multi-task system demonstrating that touch-augmented world models produce more physically grounded imagined futures and better zero-shot plans on a real dexterous robot platform. The evaluation is principled and uses sound statistical methodology for the imagination quality metrics. The primary weaknesses are experimental scale (very few real-robot trials, no significance reporting for planning results) and an incomplete isolation of what drives the data-efficiency advantage. These are important gaps but do not invalidate the core claims.
