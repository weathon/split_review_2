## Summary

The paper proposes PromptArmor, a defense against prompt injection that uses an off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) to detect and remove injected prompts from data samples before they reach the backend LLM. The authors show that with modern LLMs possessing strong reasoning capabilities, this simple prompting-based approach achieves very low false positive and false negative rates on multiple benchmarks (AgentDojo, Open Prompt Injection, TensorTrust), outperforming existing specialized defenses. The paper argues that this method should be reconsidered as a strong baseline for evaluating future prompt injection defenses.

## Strengths

- **Comprehensive and rigorous evaluation.** The paper evaluates PromptArmor across three diverse benchmarks (agent and non-agent settings), multiple guardrail LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Qwen3 variants), and compares against seven representative baseline defenses. The ablation studies on model size, reasoning mode, and prompting strategies are thorough and informative.

- **Clear demonstration of a previously dismissed approach becoming effective.** Prior work (2023) found prompting-based defenses ineffective, but this paper convincingly shows that modern LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1) change that picture, achieving FPR and FNR below 1% on AgentDojo. This is a practically important finding for the community.

- **Practical and modular design.** PromptArmor requires no model retraining or architectural changes, operates as a drop-in guardrail, and can be deployed with the same LLM used as the backend. The removal step (rather than discarding inputs) preserves utility, which is a thoughtful design choice.

- **Robustness analysis.** The paper includes a memorization test to rule out data contamination, and evaluates against adaptive attacks generated by an automated red-teaming method, showing the defense remains effective.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Limited novelty.** The core idea—prompting an LLM to detect prompt injection—is not new; the paper's main contribution is an empirical re-evaluation with stronger models. While this is valuable, the methodological contribution is minimal. The paper would benefit from clearer articulation of what new knowledge it adds beyond "stronger models make a simple baseline work."

- **Dependence on strong proprietary LLMs.** The best results rely on GPT-4o and GPT-4.1, which are expensive, closed-source, and may not be accessible to all practitioners. The open-source Qwen3-32B achieves comparable performance, but smaller models (0.6B, 8B) perform poorly. This limits the general applicability of the approach, especially for resource-constrained settings.

- **Adaptive attack evaluation is narrow.** The paper tests only one automated red-teaming method (AgentVigil). More sophisticated adaptive attacks—especially human-crafted ones or those that exploit the guardrail LLM's own vulnerabilities—could potentially bypass PromptArmor. The claim of robustness is not fully supported without broader adversarial evaluation.

### Minor

- **The fuzzy matching removal step is not deeply analyzed.** The paper mentions using a regular expression to handle inexact matches, but does not discuss failure cases (e.g., when the injected prompt overlaps with benign content, or when removal corrupts the data). The impact of imperfect removal on downstream task performance is not quantified.

- **The paper does not discuss computational overhead.** While the method is described as efficient, there is no measurement of latency or cost compared to other defenses, which would be useful for practitioners deciding whether to deploy it.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A discussion of scenarios where PromptArmor might fail (e.g., injected prompts that are semantically indistinguishable from benign instructions, or attacks that target the guardrail LLM's own reasoning process).
- An analysis of how the guardrail LLM's output format (e.g., "Yes/No" with extraction) could be exploited by adversarial prompts designed to confuse the guardrail.

## Novel Insights

The paper's key insight is that the reasoning capabilities of modern LLMs have crossed a threshold where a simple prompting-based defense becomes highly effective, overturning prior negative results. This suggests that the community should not dismiss straightforward baselines too quickly, as they may become viable as underlying models improve. The finding that model capacity (size) is the primary driver of performance, with reasoning providing secondary benefits, is also useful for guiding deployment choices.

## Suggestions

- Broaden the adaptive attack evaluation to include human-crafted attacks or attacks that specifically target the guardrail LLM (e.g., by embedding instructions that tell the guardrail to ignore its detection prompt).
- Provide quantitative measurements of the computational cost (latency, token usage) of PromptArmor relative to other defenses.
- Discuss potential failure modes of the fuzzy matching removal and how they might affect utility in practice.
