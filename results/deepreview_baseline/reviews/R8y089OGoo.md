## Summary

This paper proposes DIPOLE, a novel RL algorithm for stable and controllable diffusion policy optimization. The key idea is to reformulate the KL-regularized RL objective with a greedified reference policy, which leads to a decomposition of the optimal policy into two dichotomous policies (positive for reward maximization, negative for reward minimization) that can be trained stably with sigmoid-weighted losses. During inference, actions are generated by linearly combining the score estimates of these policies (analogous to classifier-free guidance), enabling flexible control over greediness. Experiments on offline and offline-to-online RL benchmarks (ExORL, OGBench) and a large-scale autonomous driving task (NAVSIM with a 1-billion parameter VLA model) demonstrate improved performance over existing diffusion-based RL approaches.

## Strengths

- **Novel and theoretically motivated approach:** The derivation from a greedified KL-regularized objective to a dichotomous policy decomposition is elegant. The connection to classifier-free guidance provides a principled, controllable inference mechanism.
- **Addresses a key limitation:** The method resolves the instability–optimality trade-off of exponential-weighted regression in diffusion RL by using bounded sigmoid weights, while also avoiding the biased likelihood approximations of policy gradient methods.
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation:** The paper tests on 39 tasks across two RL benchmarks and a real-world autonomous driving benchmark with a large VLA model, covering offline, offline-to-online, and pixel-based settings. The results are generally strong, often outperforming prior SOTA methods.
- **Clear writing and exposition:** The paper is well-structured, with a clear motivation, precise mathematical notation, and helpful figures that illustrate the core weighting scheme.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Lack of discussion on computational/memory cost:** The method requires training two separate diffusion models (positive and negative) for each task, doubling the parameter count and inference cost compared to single-model baselines. The paper does not address this overhead or compare with methods that use a single model with alternative stabilization techniques.
- **Rejection sampling is essential for best performance:** The "DIPOLE w/o rs" variant often underperforms strong baselines like IFQL on ExORL (e.g., Walker tasks), and the gains from the core method without rejection sampling are modest. Since rejection sampling can be applied to any diffusion policy, the contribution of the dichotomous decomposition per se is somewhat diluted. An ablation isolating the effect of the learning objective from the inference-time sampling strategy would strengthen the paper.

### Minor

- **Incremental gains on OGBench:** On several task categories (e.g., humanoidmaze-large-navigate, antsoccer-arena), DIPOLE’s performance is within error bars of IFQL or FQL. The improvement over prior work is not consistently large, which suggests the method may be most beneficial in specific regimes.
- **Limited comparison in autonomous driving:** The RL fine-tuning baseline is only DPPO; comparision with other diffusion RL methods (e.g., direct reward gradient) or with a simple weighted regression baseline is missing. Given the extremely high baseline performance of DP-VLA (88.3 PDMS), the 1.4-point gain on navtrain is modest and may not be statistically significant.
- **No analysis of hyperparameter sensitivity:** The parameters \(\beta\) (temperature) and \(\omega\) (greediness factor) are critical, but the paper provides no ablation or guidance on how to set them across tasks. The choice of advantage function vs. Q-function for \(G(s,a)\) is also not empirically justified.

### Trivial

- The paper states “DIPOLE achieves the best performance” in Table 1, yet DIPOLE w/o rs is sometimes substantially worse than IFQL. This overclaim is minor given the full method with RS does perform best.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison with a single diffusion model trained using the exponential-weighted loss but with clipped/normalized weights to stabilize training (a natural baseline not included).
- An analysis of computational cost: training time, memory footprint, and inference latency compared to single-model baselines.
- Learning curves and more detailed variance information (e.g., individual seed results) for the OGBench tasks.

## Novel Insights

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework connecting KL-regularized RL with classifier-free guidance. The key insight is that the unstable exponential weighting can be decomposed into a ratio of stable sigmoid-weighted distributions by introducing a greedified reference policy. This reformulation not only stabilizes training but also yields a controllable inference mechanism where the greediness parameter \(\omega\) directly controls the trade-off between the positive and negative policies. The observation that the resulting score combination is exactly CFG is elegant and opens a new perspective on diffusion-based RL.

## Suggestions

- Add a computational cost analysis (e.g., parameters, training time) and discuss potential remedies (e.g., parameter sharing between the two LoRA modules as done in the NAVSIM experiment, but not in the main RL benchmarks).
- Include an ablation comparing the full method to a variant that replaces the negative policy with a uniform/unconditional model (as in standard CFG) to isolate the benefit of the learned negative policy.
- Report statistical significance (e.g., paired t-tests) for the main results to clarify when differences are meaningful.
