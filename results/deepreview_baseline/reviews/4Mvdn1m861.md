## Summary

This paper proposes a training-free object counting framework that leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM) without architectural modifications or auxiliary models. The method consists of two key components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that efficiently samples prompts based on probability distributions derived from SAM's image embedding, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method achieves competitive results on FSC-147 and CARPK benchmarks, outperforming several training-free and some training-based methods.

## Strengths

- **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for counting**: The paper introduces a clever approach of repurposing SAM's internal output tokens (which encode both semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention) as verification criteria for object counting, bypassing the need for costly similarity calculations in image embedding space.

- **Well-designed probabilistic prompt generation**: The iterative probability distribution update mechanism that reduces probability for verified positive regions (setting to 0) and decreases probability for negative regions is a principled approach to avoid redundant prompting and efficiently explore the image space.

- **Strong empirical results on CARPK**: The method achieves MAE of 4.68 on CARPK, outperforming all supervised methods listed in Table 1, which is a notable achievement for a training-free approach.

- **Comprehensive ablation studies**: The paper systematically ablates the verification metric (Table 3), iterative sampling parameters (Table 4), and verification criteria space (Table 5), providing clear evidence for design choices.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Incomplete comparison with state-of-the-art training-free methods**: The paper claims superiority over training-free methods but does not compare against several recent strong baselines. Notably, TFCAC (which uses DINO as an additional encoder) outperforms the proposed method on both FSC-147 (MAE 12.26 vs 16.25) and CARPK (MAE 4.39 vs 4.68). The paper's framing of "comparable performance" to methods requiring additional models is misleading when TFCAC clearly outperforms the proposed method on both benchmarks.

- **Limited novelty of the core idea**: Using SAM for counting by generating prompts and verifying outputs has been explored in prior work (TFOC, Count-Anything, C-Anything). The main claimed novelty—using output tokens instead of image embeddings for verification—is incremental. The TS-SS metric is borrowed from document clustering (Heidarian & Dinneen, 2016) and applied without theoretical justification for why it is particularly suited to SAM's output token space.

- **Missing critical implementation details**: The paper does not specify how the exemplar is provided (e.g., bounding box, point, or mask), how the exemplar output token is obtained, or the exact procedure for generating the initial exemplar mask. The description of the probability distribution update (decreasing vs. setting to 0) lacks precise mathematical formulation.

- **No statistical significance or error analysis**: Results are reported as single numbers without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or statistical significance tests. Given the stochastic nature of the probabilistic prompt generation, reporting variance across multiple runs is essential.

### Minor

- **The RMSE on FSC-147 is notably high (133.11) compared to training-based methods like LOCA (56.97) and BMNet+ (91.83)**, suggesting the method may have high variance or struggle with certain difficult cases. This is not adequately discussed.

- **The one-shot counting results (Table 2) show MAE of 18.57**, which is worse than several training-based methods and TFCAC (15.15), and the paper's claim of "competitive performance" is overstated.

- **The qualitative results (Figure 4) show only two examples**, which is insufficient to demonstrate robustness across the 147 categories in FSC-147.

### Trivial

- The paper states "our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" but this is contradicted by the results in Table 1 where several training-based methods (BMNet+, SAFECOUNT, PseCo, LOCA) and TFCAC achieve lower MAE.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison of inference time/memory usage across all methods would strengthen the computational efficiency claims.
- Analysis of failure cases, particularly for dense small-object scenarios mentioned in the Discussion section.
- An open-source implementation would significantly increase the paper's impact.

## Novel Insights

The key insight is that SAM's output tokens, which integrate both semantic and positional information through the decoder's bidirectional attention mechanism, provide a more discriminative representation for object verification than the raw image embeddings. The paper demonstrates that the entangled nature of these tokens (encoding both "what" and "where") requires a hybrid similarity metric like TS-SS that considers both magnitude and angular information, rather than standard metrics like cosine similarity or Euclidean distance. This observation about the geometric structure of SAM's output token space is the paper's most novel contribution.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify the comparison framing**: Acknowledge that TFCAC outperforms the proposed method on both benchmarks and reframe the contribution as achieving competitive performance without additional encoders rather than claiming superiority.

2. **Add statistical significance**: Report results over multiple runs with different random seeds to account for the stochastic prompt generation.

3. **Provide more implementation details**: Include the exact procedure for obtaining the exemplar output token, the initial exemplar mask generation, and the mathematical formulation of the probability distribution update rules.

4. **Expand qualitative analysis**: Show more examples, including failure cases, to give a balanced view of the method's capabilities and limitations.

5. **Consider adding a comparison with methods that use SAM's mask decoder outputs directly** (e.g., using mask confidence scores) to better motivate the need for output token-based verification.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a reasonable training-free approach to object counting using SAM, with a novel use of output tokens and a well-designed probabilistic prompt generation strategy. However, the claimed superiority over existing methods is not fully supported by the results (TFCAC outperforms on both benchmarks), the novelty is incremental, and several important implementation details are missing. The paper would benefit from more rigorous evaluation and clearer positioning of its contributions.
