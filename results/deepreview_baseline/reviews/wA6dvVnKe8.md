## Summary

This paper investigates adversarial robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. It proposes the Embedding Disruption Patch Attack (EDPA), a model-agnostic adversarial patch that operates by maximizing discrepancy between clean and adversarial visual embeddings and disrupting vision-language alignment. EDPA requires only access to the visual encoder parameters, making it less restrictive than prior attacks. The paper also proposes an adversarial fine-tuning defense that trains the visual encoder to produce similar embeddings for clean and adversarially perturbed inputs. Experiments on the LIBERO simulation benchmark with OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and π₀ show that EDPA substantially increases failure rates, and the defense partially mitigates these effects.

## Strengths

- **Timely and important problem**: Adversarial robustness of VLA models is critically under-explored, and the paper addresses a practical security concern for embodied agents.
- **Practical attack design**: EDPA requires only encoder parameters (no knowledge of model architecture, action space, or robot hardware), which is a meaningful relaxation compared to prior work (UADA/UPA). Table 1 clearly summarizes the reduced requirements.
- **Comprehensive evaluation across models**: The attack is tested on three different VLA architectures (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π₀) and achieves substantial failure rate increases on all of them.
- **Defense method with measurable improvement**: The adversarial fine-tuning scheme reduces failure rates under EDPA, UADA, and UPA with only a minor (1.6%) clean performance drop, demonstrating the feasibility of countermeasures.
- **Clear methodology**: The two loss components (patch contrastive loss and image-instruction alignment loss) are well-motivated, and Algorithm 1 provides a reproducible description of the defense training procedure.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Limited evaluation of defense generalizability**: The defense is only applied to OpenVLA. While the authors note that OpenVLA was the weakest model, testing the defense on OpenVLA-OFT or π₀ would strengthen claims about its general applicability. The defense might be overfitted to the specific encoder architecture of OpenVLA.
- **Simulation-only results limit practical conclusions**: The paper argues that EDPA is more practical for real-world deployment, yet all experiments are conducted entirely in simulation with patches applied directly to image pixels. Real-world factors such as lighting variations, viewing angles, physical placement of printed patches, and camera calibration are not considered. The claim of practicality is not backed by physical experiments.
- **Threat model ambiguity**: EDPA's "model-agnostic" property relies on access to the vision encoder parameters. While this is less restrictive than full model access (as claimed), it still assumes the adversary can query or obtain the encoder. The paper does not discuss scenarios where the encoder is a black box or the patch must be placed physically without knowing the exact camera pose.

### Minor

- **Defense effectiveness remains incomplete**: After adversarial fine-tuning, failure rates under EDPA are still high in several task suites (e.g., Goal: 73.9%, Long: 91.2%). The defense reduces but does not eliminate the vulnerability, which limits its practical impact.
- **Baseline comparison to random noise is weak**: The random noise patch is a trivial baseline; prior work (Wang et al., 2024) also used it. A stronger baseline such as a generic adversarial patch designed for image classifiers (e.g., Brown et al., 2017) transferred to VLAs would contextualize EDPA's effectiveness more clearly.
- **Visualization analysis is speculative**: The observation that generated patches resemble robotic arms is interesting, but the paper offers only a hypothesis about overfitting due to limited training data. No experiments (e.g., controlled studies with different dataset distributions) are provided to support this claim.
- **Adaptive attacks not considered**: The defense is trained using patches generated by EDPA during training. An adversary aware of the defense could in principle design patches that are effective despite the fine-tuned encoder. The paper does not discuss or evaluate such adaptive attacks.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Physical-world evaluation with printed patches placed in or near the camera's field of view (e.g., on the robot base or a nearby surface).
- Applying the adversarial fine-tuning defense to multiple VLA models to demonstrate broader robustness.
- Analysis of how patch location within the image affects attack success, since real-world placement cannot be perfectly controlled.
- Evaluation against adaptive attacks that are aware of the defense mechanism.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the observation that adversarial patches learned for different VLA models consistently exhibit patterns resembling robotic arms suggests a systematic overfitting in current VLA visual encoders due to limited and viewpoint-constrained robotic training data. This hypothesis—that the encoder becomes biased toward the presence of the robot arm—offers a plausible explanation for why patch-based attacks are so effective and points to dataset diversity as a potential avenue for robustness.

## Suggestions

- Include a real-world validation experiment, even a simple one with a static image capture and a printed patch, to support the claim of practical applicability.
- Evaluate the defense on OpenVLA-OFT or π₀ to confirm that the fine-tuning scheme transfers across model architectures.
- Discuss and ideally evaluate the case where the adversary knows the defense exists and designs patches to work against the fine-tuned encoder (i.e., an adaptive attack). This would strengthen the security claims.
