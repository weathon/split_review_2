## Summary

The paper identifies a "semantic imbalance" problem in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality). To address this, the authors propose the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric quantifying component recoverability, and integrate it into an adaptive weighting strategy for view-specific contrastive losses within the CoST framework. Experiments on benchmark datasets show that their asymmetric perceptual weighting mechanism yields improved forecasting accuracy and representational robustness compared to TS2Vec and baseline CoST.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: The observation that semantic imbalance (dominant components suppressing weaker ones) degrades downstream performance is a legitimate and practical concern in time-series representation learning. The paper provides clear synthetic and empirical evidence of this phenomenon in TS2Vec.
- **Novel diagnostic tool**: SDE provides a principled, quantitative way to measure component-level recoverability, going beyond simple aggregate metrics. The asymmetry analysis (Δ) is a natural extension that turns a diagnostic into an actionable signal.
- **Clean, pluggable integration**: The proposed asymmetric weighting mechanism is lightweight—it only requires computing SDE on a batch and adjusting loss weights. It builds on top of an existing framework (CoST) without architectural changes, increasing practical applicability.
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation**: Experiments span multiple forecasting horizons (24 to 720) across three diverse datasets (ETT, Electricity, Weather), comparing against strong baselines (TS2Vec, TNC, CoST). The results show that the proposed method often achieves the best or competitive MSE/MAE, particularly at challenging long horizons.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1.  **Lack of clarity and potential inconsistency in the method definition**. The paper introduces SDE in Section 3.2 as "Semantic Separability Error" but later in Sections 4.3 and captions refers to it as "Semantic Decomposition Error" or just "SDE". The core SDE equation is defined as a vector arithmetic in embedding space using `v(a+b) - v(b)`. However, for this to be meaningful, the encoder must be approximately linear (additive) in the latent space such that `v(a+b) ≈ v(a) + v(b)`. The paper does not verify this linearity assumption, nor does it discuss cases where it fails. If the encoder is highly nonlinear, SDE becomes an arbitrary and potentially misleading number.

2.  **Direct SDE regularization fails (Table 2), yet the paper still uses SDE in the loss weighting**. In Section 4.3 (Table 2), the authors show that adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec *hurts* performance. They hypothesize that "SDE, defined as a diagnostic metric, fails to provide constructive optimization gradients." Yet, their main proposed method (Section 4.4.3) uses SDE to compute Δ, which is then used to *dynamically weight* the contrastive losses. The paper does not explain why Δ—computed from the same SDE values that were "unconstructive" for optimization—is now effective as a weight signal. This is a critical gap in the explanation.

3.  **The paper overclaims the importance of the SDE-based weighting mechanism**. The forecasting results in Table 3 show that CoST+APW is often statistically comparable to vanilla CoST (e.g., many entries have nearly identical MSE). On the Weather dataset, CoST+APW is often *worse* than TS2Vec and TNC. The Electricity dataset also shows that the advantage of CoST+APW is inconsistent across horizons. The paper argues that APW helps, but the empirical evidence is marginal—not a breakthrough. The contribution seems more about diagnosing imbalance than achieving large performance gains.

4.  **Lack of ablation separating the effects of the MLP fusion layer vs. asymmetric weighting**. The method introduces a small MLP to learn the composite embedding `v(a+b)` (Section 4.4.2). This MLP is a non-trivial addition since it could be learning cross-component interactions that help forecasting independently of the weighting mechanism. The paper does not ablate whether the MLP alone (without APW) provides gains, nor whether APW without the MLP (just reweighting CoST losses) is effective. Without this ablation, it is unclear whether the MLP or the APW is driving the improvements.

5.  **Missing statistical significance or ablation across multiple seeds**. The results are reported without error bars, standard deviations, or multiple seed runs. Given that many improvements are small (e.g., 0.04 vs 0.04; 0.147 vs 0.152), it is impossible to know whether these differences are meaningful or noise. This is a standard expectation for forecasting benchmarks.

### Minor

- The paper states three contributions (Section 1) but the first two (diagnosing the problem, systematic ablations) are largely descriptive and not novel in a technical sense. The primary contribution is the SDE+APW mechanism.
- The synthetic SDE analysis in Table 1 is interesting but only uses TS2Vec. It would be stronger to show that the same imbalance exists in CoST (the method they are actually modifying).
- The paper mentions "isotropic embedding collapse" and "asymmetric perceptual weighting" but neither term is clearly defined or formally linked to the methodology. The connection between embedding collapse and the proposed weighting mechanism is not elaborated.
- Section 3.4 describes γ and γ' as separate hyperparameters, but Figure 1 and the later text (Section 4.4.3) use only a single γ for both weights (or apparently set γ = γ' or use 1+γΔ and 1-γΔ as shown in the figure). This inconsistency suggests the method is not fully finalized.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Verify the linearity assumption for SDE by testing whether `v(a+b) ≈ v(a) + v(b)` on real data (e.g., compute cosine similarity or MSE between the two expressions).
- Provide a clear, formal definition of "isotropic embedding collapse" in the time-series context, and explain exactly how APW counteracts it.
- Add an ablation removing the MLP fusion layer (i.e., using standard CoST embeddings) to isolate the effect of APW.
- Report all results with standard deviations over 3-5 seeds, and include a statistical test (e.g., paired t-test) for key comparisons (CoST vs CoST+APW on longest horizons).

## Novel Insights

The paper's key insight is that contrastive time-series representations can be dominated by a single semantic component, and that a simple directional metric (SDE) computed on the final embeddings can capture this imbalance. Furthermore, the asymmetry factor Δ—computed as the difference between two SDEs—can be used as an online signal to reweight contrastive losses during training. This turns a diagnostic into an optimization signal, which is a neat idea. However, the empirical support for this reweighting is weak, and the paper fails to resolve why SDE works as a weight but fails as a direct regularizer. The truly novel insight—that component recoverability can be measured and exploited during training—is valuable, but the current presentation does not fully demonstrate its effectiveness.

## Suggestions

1.  **Clarify the SDE notation and its application to CoST**. Explicitly state that SDE is computed on the pooled CoST embeddings for trend and seasonality using the composite MLP output `v(a+b)`. Avoid calling it both "Separability" and "Decomposition" Error; pick one name and stick with it.

2.  **Explain why SDE works for weighting but fails as a direct regularizer**. Provide an intuitive or theoretical reason. For instance, maybe SDE itself is noisy as a loss but its mean/aggregate across batches produces a stable Δ that is useful for weighting. If no clear reason exists, this is a limitation that should be acknowledged.

3.  **Add a critical ablation**: report CoST + MLP (without APW) vs CoST + APW (without MLP) vs CoST + MLP + APW. This will clarify which component contributes to the observed gains.

4.  **Acknowledge the marginal nature of the improvements**. In the conclusion, rather than claiming "consistently lower SDE values and improved forecasting accuracy", discuss cases where the improvement is small or absent, and speculate on why.

5.  **Report results with standard deviations or confidence intervals**. Without them, the numerical improvements cannot be interpreted reliably.

## Score and Decision

Score: 4.0

The problem is well-motivated and the diagnostic tool (SDE) is a reasonable contribution. However, the core methodological contribution (asymmetric weighting) is not convincingly demonstrated: the empirical improvements are marginal and inconsistent, the ablation is incomplete (missing MLP vs APW separation), and there is an unresolved tension regarding why SDE fails as a regularizer but succeeds as a weight. The paper reads more like a workshop paper or a preliminary study than a complete, impactful ICLR contribution.
