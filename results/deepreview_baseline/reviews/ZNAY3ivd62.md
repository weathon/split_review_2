## Summary

This paper introduces GUI-Spotlight, a visual grounding model for GUI screens that performs iterative focus refinement by coordinating three specialized tools (crop, extract, find_color) through reinforcement learning. Using a three-stage training pipeline (SFT warm-up followed by modified GSPO with a stabilizing cross-entropy term), the model achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro and 23.4% on UI-Vision using only 18.5K training samples, surpassing several 7B-level baselines that use orders of magnitude more data. The paper also provides a detailed ablation study on RL algorithm choices and reward designs, including negative results.

## Strengths

- **Data efficiency**: GUI-Spotlight obtains competitive accuracy (52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro) with only 18.5K training samples, two to three orders of magnitude fewer than many comparable 7B models (e.g., UGround-V1-7B uses 10M samples). This is a practically meaningful achievement.
- **Thorough empirical analysis**: The paper presents a systematic ablation of RL algorithms (including 7 variants) and reward designs (sparse vs. dense answer rewards, crop/extract weighting), and reports negative results (e.g., why certain modifications are discarded). This documentation is valuable for practitioners building agentic grounding systems.
- **Strong generalization across benchmarks**: The method improves over both UI-specialized (UI-TARS-1.5-7B) and non-UI-specific (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) backbones, and shows gains on ScreenSpot-Pro, UI-Vision, and OSWorld-G, indicating robustness to different environments and resolutions.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Modest improvement margin over strong baselines**: The final accuracy of GUI-Spotlight (52.8%) is only 2.0 points above UI-Venus-7B (50.8%) and 2.2 points above V2P-7B (50.6%). Given that no error bars or statistical significance tests are reported, it is unclear whether this gap is reproducible or consistent. The claim of "substantially improving visual grounding accuracy" is overstated for this margin.
2. **No discussion of inference cost**: The iterative spotlighting pipeline requires multiple model calls per prediction (up to T_max steps). Compared to single-step grounding models, GUI-Spotlight incurs substantially higher latency and computational cost. The paper evaluates only accuracy and does not report inference time, number of tool invocations per sample, or cost-benefit trade-offs, which are critical for practical deployment.

### Minor

- The "think-with-image" concept is presented as an inspiration from attention mechanisms, but the connection is not formally developed. The iterative tool-use framework is a concrete engineering design rather than a novel cognitive architecture.
- The RL modification (adding a cross-entropy loss on format-valid correct samples) is a simple pragmatic fix. While effective, its technical novelty is limited and could be seen as an implementation detail rather than a core algorithmic contribution.
- The description of data construction (Stage 1 trajectories generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B) raises a question: the distillation from a larger model may partially account for the data efficiency, but the paper does not analyze the contribution of this distillation versus the RL training.

### Trivial

- Table 3 lists "UI-TARS-1.5" twice in the closed-source section (once as "UI-TARS-1.5" and once as "UI-TARS-1.5-7B" in open-source 7B), which is slightly confusing.
- The x-axis labels in Figure 3 (right) show "GSP0" while the text refers to "GSPO"; this typo appears in the figure text.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An analysis of the average number of tool calls per sample, and a comparison of inference time between GUI-Spotlight and single-step baselines.
- Error analysis showing the types of mistakes GUI-Spotlight still makes (e.g., failures in tool selection, cropping too coarsely, etc.). This would strengthen the qualitative understanding.
- A study of scaling behavior: since the model is data-efficient, it would be interesting to see accuracy as a function of training data size (e.g., 5K, 10K, 18.5K).

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own results, the most valuable insight is that multi-turn tool-use policies for visual grounding can be learned effectively from relatively small amounts of high-quality data when the training is stabilized against format collapse. The negative results—showing that GRPO and vanilla GSPO diverge on this multi-tool task, while an auxiliary cross-entropy loss prevents oscillation—are a practical finding that may benefit other agentic RL settings where output format violations cause reward sparsity. The ablation also shows that a simple sparse reward for the final click outperforms dense center-shaped rewards, suggesting that intermediate positional rewards may misalign the policy.

## Suggestions

- Add confidence intervals or statistical significance tests (e.g., bootstrapped standard errors) for the main results to verify that the improvement over top baselines is not within noise.
- Include an analysis of inference latency and number of tool steps, and discuss the practical trade-off between accuracy and speed. This would make the contribution more actionable for real-world GUI agents.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a solid, well-executed system with clear data efficiency and useful experimental documentation. However, the accuracy gain over the strongest 7B baselines is small, the inference cost is unexamined, and the core technical ideas (iterative cropping with RL + cross-entropy stabilization) are incremental. On balance, these qualities suggest a borderline acceptance rather than a strong one.
