## Summary

CP4D presents a compositional framework for text-to-4D scene generation that separates scenes into static 3D backgrounds and physically grounded dynamic foreground objects. The pipeline proceeds through three stages: (1) synthesizing 3D Gaussians for background and foreground using image editing for stylistic coherence, (2) a hybrid motion synthesis combining physics simulators (MPM, rigid-body, PBD) with video diffusion priors via SDS for trajectory refinement, and (3) an automated composition mechanism using monocular depth and optimization. The problem — that existing 4D generation lacks physical consistency — is well-motivated, and the decomposition into background + physically simulated foregrounds is a sensible engineering choice.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem framing (Sections 1, 4).** The observation that existing 4D generation methods treat temporal consistency as a visual/signal-processing problem rather than a physical one is genuine and timely. The paper correctly identifies that this leads to outputs that "look right" per-frame but violate basic dynamics.

- **Sensible decomposition strategy (Section 4.1).** Reformulating 4D scene generation as static background + dynamic foregrounds is a practical way to constrain an otherwise ill-posed problem. The specific pipeline of generating a composite image via editing before reconstructing independent 3D representations cleanly addresses the stylistic incoherence that would result from naive independent text-to-3D generation.

- **Hybrid motion synthesis (Section 4.2) is a well-motivated design choice.** Using physics simulators for coarse trajectories and video diffusion priors (via SDS) to refine material parameters and correct spurious collisions addresses two identifiable failure modes (imprecise VLM parameters and grid-approximation artifacts), as shown in Figure 2.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Evaluation metrics measure 2D video quality, not the claimed 4D scene properties.** The paper claims CP4D generates "explorable and interactive 4D scenes" that "support flexible viewpoint changes" (line 66). Yet all quantitative metrics (VBench, WorldScore, GPT-4o scoring in Tables 1–2) are evaluated on fixed rendered videos. There is no novel-view synthesis evaluation, no multi-view consistency analysis from arbitrary camera trajectories, no free-viewpoint exploration assessment, and no interactivity evaluation. The paper's central differentiating claim — that this is a 4D method supporting exploration rather than just video generation — goes untested. This is the most significant gap between the paper's claims and its evidence.

- **Small evaluation set (17 examples) with no disclosed selection criteria or statistical rigor (line 160).** For an open-ended text-to-4D generation task where output quality varies with prompt difficulty and random seeds, 17 examples is insufficient to draw reliable conclusions. No error bars, confidence intervals, or per-prompt breakdowns are reported. Several margins over baselines are small (e.g., Physical realism 0.694 vs. 0.670 for Runway in Table 2; VBench Imaging 0.644 vs. 0.641). Without a disclosed prompt list or selection protocol, the reader cannot assess whether improvements are systematic or incidental.

- **Baseline comparison protocol is underspecified.** The paper compares against image-conditioned physics methods (PhysGen, PhysGen3D, OmniPhysGS) but does not describe how they were adapted to the text-conditioned setting — what image were they provided, and generated by which model? DreamGaussian4D performs dramatically worse (e.g., WorldScore Photo Consistency 14.59 vs. 97.42), which warrants clarification on whether it was configured for scenes vs. single objects. Without a clear description of what input each baseline received and how outputs were post-processed, the comparison tables are difficult to interpret.

### Minor

- **The scene composition optimization (Eq. 9) only enforces consistency from the reference viewpoint (lines 150–154).** The objective minimizes discrepancy between the rendered reference view and the composite image. There is no mechanism ensuring the composition holds up under novel views, which is a gap for a method claiming "explorable" 4D scenes with "flexible viewpoint changes."

- **The ablation study (Section 5.3, Figure 5) is purely qualitative.** Given the modest 17-example evaluation, adding quantitative ablation results on the same VBench/WorldScore/GPT-4o metrics used in the main comparison would be straightforward and would substantially strengthen the evidence for each component's contribution.

- **No discussion of limitations or failure cases (Section 6).** The pipeline chains seven or more expert models (LLM, text-to-image, image editing, segmentation, depth estimation, two image-to-3D models, video diffusion), making error propagation a real concern. A limitations section would help calibrate expectations about when and why the pipeline succeeds or fails.

- **The claim of "differentiable simulators" (line 38) is stated but the mechanism is not described in the main text.** Equations 4–5 require gradients through the physics simulator (∂V/∂Θ), but the paper does not explain how differentiability is achieved for the specific solvers used (MPM, rigid-body, PBD — where PBD is not natively differentiable). The main text defers to Appendix C. Even with appendix details, the core mechanism should be summarized at a high level in the main paper.

## Suggestions

1. Add a novel-view synthesis evaluation (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across random camera trajectories) and/or a user study for free-viewpoint exploration to substantiate the 4D claims.
2. Expand the evaluation set and disclose the full prompt list with per-prompt results and variance estimates.
3. Add quantitative ablation results on the same VBench/WorldScore/GPT-4o axes.
4. Clarify the baseline adaptation protocol — what input did each image-conditioned method receive, and was the 4D baseline configured for scenes or single objects?
5. Add a limitations/failure-cases section to discuss error propagation across the many chained models.
6. Clarify in the main text (not just the appendix) how gradient backpropagation through the physics simulator is achieved.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a well-motivated system design for physics-aware 4D generation. The problem framing is strong, and the three-stage pipeline (coherent 3D synthesis → hybrid motion via physics + SDS → automated composition) is thoughtful and addresses real limitations of prior work. However, the evaluation has three significant structural weaknesses: (1) the metrics evaluate 2D rendered video quality, not the claimed 4D scene properties (novel-view synthesis, free-viewpoint exploration, interactivity); (2) the 17-example evaluation set with no statistical rigor or disclosed criteria is too thin to draw reliable conclusions; and (3) the baseline comparison protocol is underspecified, making the quantitative comparisons difficult to interpret. These evaluation gaps mean the paper's core claims — that the method produces "explorable and interactive 4D scenes" with advantages over prior work — are not adequately supported by the presented evidence. The method itself has merit, but the paper in its current form requires substantially stronger empirical validation.
