## Summary

This paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a metric to quantify whether a time-series representation preserves the recoverability of individual semantic components (trend vs. seasonality) from a composite embedding. It then introduces Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW), which uses the asymmetry between directional SDE values to dynamically reweight contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The goal is to prevent dominant components from suppressing weaker ones during contrastive training.

## Strengths

- **The SDE diagnostic metric is well-motivated and empirically demonstrated on synthetic data (Table 1).** The idea of measuring component recoverability via a vector arithmetic test (Eq. 1) is simple and intuitive. The controlled experiment in Table 1 convincingly shows that TS2Vec embeddings exhibit asymmetric recoverability of trend vs. seasonal components when the amplitude ratio is skewed. This is a genuine finding about existing methods and is the paper's clearest empirical contribution.

- **The logic chain from SDE asymmetry (Δ) to the reweighting mechanism is conceptually clean.** The diagnosis-to-intervention flow — if component B is harder to recover, upweight its loss — is clearly stated (Section 3.4) and easy to follow. The connection between the diagnostic and the intervention is transparent.

- **The paper identifies a real and plausibly under-studied problem.** Semantic imbalance — where dominant temporal components suppress weaker ones in contrastive embeddings — is a genuine concern in time-series representation learning, and the diagnosis of this phenomenon in TS2Vec is a useful observation.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The experimental results do not support the claimed "consistent gains" and on some settings the method is substantially worse than the baseline it extends.** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" (line 10) and the conclusion claims "superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines" (line 300). However, Table 3 tells a different story:
  - **Electricity:** CoST+APW is substantially worse than CoST on 3 of 5 horizons. At horizon 168: 0.566 vs 0.425 MSE (**33% relative degradation**). At horizon 336: 0.677 vs 0.576. At horizon 720: 1.010 vs 0.911.
  - **Weather:** CoST+APW is worse than CoST on 3 of 5 horizons (336: 0.266 vs 0.256; 720: 0.299 vs 0.278; 48: tied).
  - **ETT1 (ETTh1):** mixed — APW wins on 2/5 horizons, loses on 2/5, ties on 1/5.
  - **ETT1 (ETTm1):** the only dataset where APW shows consistent but small improvements over CoST.
  
  Only 1 of 4 datasets shows consistent improvements. The central claim of "consistent gains" and "superior forecasting accuracy" is contradicted by the paper's own data.

- **No statistical uncertainty is reported.** Every result in Table 3 is a single number. No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multiple-seed results are provided. Most of the positive differences are <0.01 MSE — well within the noise range for neural forecasting — making even the positive findings uninterpretable. Given the small magnitudes at stake, this is not a formatting preference but a barrier to evaluating the core claim.

- **Key hyperparameters γ and γ' are never reported.** The asymmetric weighting mechanism (Eq. 3, line 257) depends on two hyperparameters that "control sensitivity to Δ." The paper never states what values of γ and γ' were used in the experiments, how they were selected, or whether results are sensitive to their choice. For a paper whose core contribution is a hyperparameter-mediated reweighting scheme, this is a critical reproducibility gap.

- **Conceptual inconsistency between the SDE definition and its training-time computation.** Section 3.2 defines SDE using v(a+b) = f_θ(a+b) — the encoder applied directly to the composite signal. Section 4.4.2 replaces this with v(a+b) = g_φ([v(a)||v(b)]) — a separately trained MLP on concatenated separate embeddings. These are fundamentally different quantities: the original tests whether the encoder preserves linear recoverability from the actual composite input, while the training-time version measures the reconstruction error of an auxiliary MLP. The paper does not acknowledge or argue why the latter formulation is valid for the same purpose.

### Minor

- **The fusion MLP architecture (g_φ) is underspecified.** Section 4.4.2 introduces "a small MLP" that maps concatenated trend and seasonal embeddings to a composite embedding, but provides no information about number of layers, hidden dimension, activation function, or output dimension. Since this MLP's output is used in the SDE computation that drives reweighting, the architecture directly affects the method's behavior.

- **The metric name is inconsistent.** The paper uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title, abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2, Figure 1), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3) to refer to the same quantity. While not lethal, this signals a lack of precision.

- **The two "ETT1" blocks in Table 3 are ambiguously labeled.** The first block uses horizons {24,48,168,336,720} (ETTh1) and the second uses {24,48,96,288,672} (ETTm1), but both are labeled "ETT1," making the table harder to interpret.

- **SDE values are claimed to be reported in Table 3 but are not visible.** The text states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" but only MSE/MAE columns appear. This may be a parser artifact, but in the available material, the paper provides no SDE evidence for the proposed method on real datasets despite claiming the method reduces SDE.

## Suggestions

1. Re-evaluate the central claim: the paper's strongest asset is the SDE diagnostic metric and the empirical finding of semantic imbalance in TS2Vec. Consider reframing the contribution around the diagnostic tool and what it reveals, and honestly characterizing the APW intervention as a preliminary attempt with mixed results rather than a validated improvement.
2. Report SDE values on real datasets. Without evidence that APW actually reduces the imbalance it diagnoses, the intervention cannot be evaluated.
3. Specify γ, γ' and show sensitivity to their values.
4. Add error bars or multiple-seed reporting.
5. Clarify the relationship between the SDE definition (encoder-on-composite) and the training-time approximation (MLP-on-concatenated-embeddings).

## Score and Decision

The paper identifies an interesting problem (semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations) and proposes a sensible diagnostic metric (SDE). The synthetic experiment in Table 1 is a genuine contribution. However, the paper's central claim about the APW intervention — that it produces "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" — is contradicted by the paper's own data: on a standard benchmark (Electricity), the method causes substantial degradation on long horizons, and on Weather it is roughly a wash. Key hyperparameters are undisclosed, no statistical uncertainty is reported, and the SDE computation used during training is conceptually inconsistent with the definition. The evidence does not support acceptance of the proposed intervention as a validated contribution. The diagnostic metric itself is useful enough to warrant further development, but the paper in its current form cannot serve as a reliable contribution to the literature.
