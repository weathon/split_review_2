## Summary

CaTS-Bench introduces a large-scale, multimodal benchmark for context-aware time series captioning (TSC), constructed from 11 real-world datasets yielding 20k samples (16k train/4k test) with numeric series, metadata, line-plot images, and validated reference captions. The paper contributes a scalable oracle-based captioning pipeline with factual, diversity, and human-indistinguishability validation, new numeric fidelity metrics, a 460-question diagnostic Q&A suite, and an evaluation of VLMs revealing that models largely fail to leverage visual modality for time series understanding.

## Strengths

- **Thorough quality validation pipeline (Section 3.2).** Manual checks on 72.5% of test captions (~2.9k) across statistical claims and trend descriptors show 98.6% accuracy; a human detectability study (35 participants, 41.1% accuracy, near random); and diversity analysis across nine embedding models yields only 2.3% near-duplicate pairs. This provides genuine evidence that the semi-synthetic captions are not systematically biased or templatic.

- **Human-revisited subset and dual ground-truth reporting (Section 3.1, Table 2; Tables 3 and 4).** The 579 human-revised test captions — sampled from multiple LLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o, Gemma, Llama) — provide a meaningful secondary reference. Reporting results against both semi-synthetic (SS) and human-revisited (HR) ground truths throughout lets the reader assess how much the oracle's stylistic signature affects rankings.

- **Numeric fidelity metrics tailored to TSC (Section 3.5).** Statistical Inference Accuracy (penalizing hallucinations but not omissions) and Numeric Score (penalizing omissions over imprecision via λ_A=0.3, λ_R=0.7) fill a real evaluation gap where standard NLP metrics reward fluency but miss numeric faithfulness.

- **Finding about VLM visual neglect (Section 4.3).** The visual modality ablation (Figure 4) shows near-zero or negative performance deltas from adding the plot image; attention analysis confirms minimal visual grounding; and the plot matching Q&A task shows models scoring near random while humans are near perfect. This is a genuine empirical contribution to understanding current VLM limitations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Unexplained data pattern in the finetuning experiments (Tables 3 and 4).** For QwenVL, the SS columns in Table 3 are identical or nearly identical between the pretrained and finetuned variants: DeBERTa F1 (0.643), BLEU (0.082), ROUGE-L (0.249), METEOR (0.260), and Numeric (0.504). Meanwhile HR scores change substantially (e.g., DeBERTa HR: 0.619→0.703, Numeric HR: 0.445→0.683). The same pattern appears in Table 4 for statistical inference metrics (Mean SS: 0.565, Max SS: 0.822, Min SS: 0.657 — identical across pretrained and finetuned). This pattern strongly suggests the finetuned QwenVL was not re-evaluated against SS references. This undermines confidence in the finetuning results and requires immediate clarification from the authors.

- **The ground-truth circularity is structurally inherent, not fully resolved by validation studies (Sections 3.1, 3.2).** Reference captions are generated by Gemini 2.0 Flash, which receives the full numeric series plus pre-computed statistics. Evaluated models are compared against these references, meaning models producing captions stylistically and structurally similar to Gemini 2.0 Flash will score higher, independent of caption quality. The paraphrasing robustness experiment (Spearman ρ=0.9266) only varies style while preserving factual content — it does not test whether the oracle's *selection of what to include and emphasize* is the correct standard. The paper acknowledges this limitation but the fundamental issue is not fully mitigated. This bounds what the benchmark measures: alignment with a single oracle's captioning choices, not the intrinsic quality of time series understanding in any absolute sense.

### Minor

- **Statistical significance is absent from all main results.** Tables 3 and 4 report only point estimates. With 4k test samples and per-domain macro-averaging, confidence intervals or significance tests are needed to determine whether differences between models (e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash at 0.665 DeBERTa F1 vs. GPT-4o at 0.661) are meaningful or noise. This applies especially to the visual modality ablation (Figure 4), where most deltas are small.

- **The Q&A filtering methodology introduces model-specific bias (Section 3.4).** Removing questions correctly answered by Qwen 2.5 Omni from an initial pool of ~4k per type defines benchmark difficulty relative to the failure modes of a single model. If Qwen 2.5 Omni is stronger at certain reasoning patterns and weaker at others, the filtering disproportionately removes questions of the former type, creating a composition that reflects Qwen's blind spots. The paper claims Appendix J.2 addresses this, but the structural concern about single-model filtering stands as a design limitation.

- **Per-domain breakdowns are relegated to the appendix.** Given the massive domain imbalance (COVID alone is 27.5% of test samples, while Demography is 3%), reporting only macro-averages obscures whether models have domain-specific failure modes. A table or figure showing per-domain performance for at least the top models would significantly increase diagnostic utility.

- **The oracle prompt structure is not summarized in the main text.** The prompt (referenced to Appendix N.1) specifies how statistics are provided to the oracle. A brief summary in the main text would help readers assess potential prompt-induced biases in the reference captions.

- **The human-revisited subset is too small and domain-restricted.** At 579 captions (14.5% of test set) from only 4 of 11 domains, it is valuable as a complement but too narrow to serve as a primary reference standard for broad analyses.

### Trivial

- **Misleading "fair representation" phrasing (Section 3.1).** The paper states sample counts depend on total source time steps "ensuring fair representation," but Table 2 shows Air Quality contributes 99.7% of source time steps yet only 22% of samples. The downsampling is defensible, but the phrasing overstates the proportionality.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation or user study grounding the λ weights for the Numeric Score (λ_A=0.3, λ_R=0.7) would strengthen the metric design.
- Reporting computational cost (GPU hours) for finetuning experiments would help practitioners assess practical trade-offs.
- Generating reference captions from 2–3 architecturally distinct oracle LLMs and checking ranking consistency would directly address the ground-truth circularity concern.
