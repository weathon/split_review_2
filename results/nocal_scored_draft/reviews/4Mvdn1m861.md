## Summary

This paper proposes TOKENCOUNT, a training-free object counting framework that works with SAM in its original form (no fine-tuning, no auxiliary models). The method has two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that models object likelihood as a probability distribution over image embeddings and iteratively samples/updates prompts, and (2) output-token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens (which encode both semantic and positional information) with the TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method achieves the best MAE among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147 (16.25) and strong results on CARPK (4.68 MAE, outperforming all training-based methods).

## Strengths

- **Novel use of SAM's decoder output tokens for verification.** The core idea—using SAM's output tokens (which integrate semantic and positional information through the decoder's bidirectional attention) as a verification signal rather than computing similarity in the image embedding space—is genuinely interesting and not seen in prior SAM-based counting work. (Section 3.2, Figure 1)
- **Probabilistic prompt generation with iterative expansion and probability redistribution.** Using the exemplar-derived similarity map as a probability distribution, sampling via multinomial, and dynamically updating the distribution (zeroing out positive regions, decreasing negative ones) is a sensible improvement over fixed grid or superpixel prompting. It is well-motivated by the observation that output tokens have limited spatial scope and need iterative expansion. (Section 3.1, Figure 3)
- **Genuinely training-free.** The method uses only SAM's pre-trained weights with no fine-tuning, no additional trainable modules, and no auxiliary foundation models. This is a legitimate practical advantage. The method achieves the best MAE among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147 (16.25 vs. TFOC's 19.95, C-Anything's 27.97, and raw SAM's 42.48).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Abstract and claims substantially overstate the results.** The abstract claims the method "outperforms existing training-based and training-free counting methods" with MAE 16.25, but the paper's own Table 1 on FSC-147 shows this is false: training-based methods BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), LOCA (10.79) and the training-free TFCAC (12.26) all achieve lower (better) MAE. The proposal ranks 9th out of 12 methods on FSC-147 MAE. While the body text is more measured (correctly noting it is the best among SAM-only methods), the abstract's unqualified claim is contradicted by the paper's own evidence and needs major revision.

- **The RMSE on FSC-147 (133.11) is the second-worst in Table 1** — higher than every method except raw SAM (137.50). This is more than double TFCAC's RMSE (56.33) and LOCA's (56.97). A low MAE with very high RMSE indicates the method produces catastrophic errors on certain images. The paper only briefly acknowledges this in the Discussion ("dense distributions of very small instances") without analyzing which categories fail or quantifying the severity. A method with this error profile cannot honestly claim "superior accuracy."

### Minor
- **The TS-SS metric improvement over standard cosine similarity is marginal and overclaimed.** Table 3 shows TS-SS (MAE 16.25) vs. Cosine Similarity (MAE 16.48) — a 0.23 MAE difference (~1.4% relative improvement). No statistical significance test, confidence intervals, or error bars are provided, and the prompt sampling process is stochastic. Section 4.4 frames this as "breaking through" a "performance plateau," which is not supported by the evidence.

- **The claimed computational advantage over TFCAC is unsubstantiated.** Section 4.2 states the method "features an inference time that is more than twice as fast" as TFCAC, attributing this to TFCAC's additional model. However, no inference-time numbers for TFCAC are reported anywhere, no comparison of prompt counts is given, and no wall-clock comparison is provided. Only the proposal's own processing time (1.69s per image) is reported.

- **The token verification threshold of 300 is unexplained.** The implementation (Section 4.1) states: "Based on validation experiments, we set the token verification threshold to 300." Since TS-SS is a product of triangle area and sector area (Eq. 2-3), the value 300 is never contextualized — what it represents geometrically, how it was validated, and how sensitive results are to this threshold are all unspecified.

- **Several method details are underspecified.** (a) The format of the exemplar prompt e in Eq. 1 (point, box, or segmentation) is not stated, which affects the quality of the entire pipeline. (b) When a region is classified as negative, the probability value is "decreased" but no update rule, discount factor, or amount is given. (c) When the exemplar list grows across iterations, the aggregation rule for multi-exemplar verification (average? max?) is not specified. (d) How a mask of arbitrary shape maps to the 64×64 probability grid for the update step is not explained.

- **Evaluation is limited to two datasets** (FSC-147, CARPK). For a method claiming to be class-agnostic, evaluation on additional benchmarks (e.g., ShanghaiTech crowd counting, TRANCOS vehicle counting) would strengthen the claim of generality. The one-shot results (Table 2) show a very high RMSE of 135.86, nearly double TFCAC's 69.16.
