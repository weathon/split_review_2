## Summary

This paper proposes a tracklet-level jersey number recognition pipeline for soccer broadcast footage that replaces a conventional multi-stage baseline (legibility classifier + pose estimation + PARSeq OCR) with a streamlined architecture: Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, OWLv2 open-vocabulary number detection, collage-based temporal aggregation, and a Qwen vision-language model fine-tuned with LoRA. The collage approach feeds up to 25 crops per tracklet into the VLM as a single grid input, shifting aggregation burden from hand-crafted confidence heuristics to the model. LoRA fine-tuning improves accuracy from 49.7% (zero-shot) to 76.41%. The paper also presents extensive but ultimately-abandoned legibility classifier comparisons (ResNet34, EfficientNetV2, MobileNet, Swin-T, ConvNeXt).

## Strengths

- **Creative pipeline redesign with a coherent rationale**: The paper replaces four separate stages (legibility classification, pose estimation, per-crop OCR, confidence aggregation) with an integrated flow where OWLv2 serves as both detector and implicit legibility filter and the collage + VLM handles temporal aggregation. Each design choice is motivated by specific limitations of the prior pipeline (Sections IV.A, V.A).

- **Convincing evidence that LoRA fine-tuning substantially improves VLM performance on this task**: Zero-shot Qwen achieves 49.7% accuracy; after two epochs of LoRA fine-tuning, accuracy rises to 76.41% (Section V.C.1). The loss curve (Figure 12) shows convergence, supporting that the fine-tuning is effective and not simply memorizing.

- **Transparent error analysis and limitations discussion**: The paper identifies five specific failure modes — single/double-digit confusion, OWLv2 detection failures, Real-ESRGAN artifacts, ground-truth label errors, and aggressive legibility filtering — each with concrete examples and explanations (Section IV.D). The limitations section (V.B) honestly acknowledges processing time, upscaling overhead, OWLv2 failure modes, and the 25-crop cap.

- **Qualitative evidence for Real-ESRGAN's utility**: Figures 9 and 10 provide clear side-by-side comparisons showing that 4× super-resolution substantially sharpens digit boundaries, making jersey numbers more legible for both detection and recognition stages.

- **Collage-based aggregation eliminates hand-tuned confidence heuristics**: By feeding a grid of crops into the VLM, the approach removes the need for per-frame confidence scores and their associated hyperparameter tuning, offloading the aggregation logic to the model itself (Sections IV.A.3, V.A.3).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **No accuracy comparison against the baseline pipeline**: The paper explicitly frames Koshkina & Elder (2024) as its baseline and describes how every design choice departs from it, yet never reports the baseline's accuracy on the same test set. The only head-to-head comparison is a runtime measurement on a single tracklet (Table I), which covers only the OCR recognition stage. Without knowing whether the baseline achieves 60%, 76%, or 85% on this data, the central claim that the proposed approach is "useful" cannot be evaluated. The 76.41% number — the paper's only quantitative result — is uninterpretable in isolation. This is the single most important omission and substantially weakens the paper's contribution.

- **Method description is incomplete for the core recognition model**: The LoRA configuration (rank, alpha, learning rate, batch size, number of trainable parameters) is never specified. The prompt given to Qwen is described only abstractly ("a prompt asking for the most frequent number in the collage"). The training data split size is not stated beyond "1,840 sample set." The model name alternates between "Qwen3-VL," "Qwen-VL," and "Qwen-3.5" without resolution. These omissions prevent a reader from reproducing the main result or fully understanding what was done.

### Minor

- **Swin-T vs. ResNet34 pipeline decision rests on a 2-tracklet test**: The paper devotes considerable space (Section III-A-2, Figures 5–7) to comparing Swin-T and ResNet34 legibility classifiers at the image level, but the decision to abandon Swin-T for the final pipeline was made after testing on only two tracklets where the difference was 0.34%. While the paper is transparent about this, such a minuscule test does not reliably support a design choice, and the extensive legibility comparison that precedes it is left disconnected from the final pipeline.

- **Confusion matrix is unlabeled and uninterpretable**: Figure 13 shows a 40×40 confusion matrix with no class mapping, no color scale, and no explanation of what the 40 classes correspond to (jersey numbers 0–99 would be 100 classes). The text mentions only that "the most common error pattern across the model being mistaking single- and double-digit jersey numbers," but readers cannot verify this or extract any other pattern from the figure.

- **Runtime comparison is limited to a single tracklet and only the OCR stage**: The paper is honest that OWLv2 and super-resolution time are excluded, but testing on only one tracklet means the speedup claim is anecdotal rather than systematic. Variability across tracklets of different lengths could significantly change the conclusion.

- **The 1,840-sample evaluation set is not precisely characterized**: It is unclear whether this is the full test set, a subset, how it relates to the tracklets used in legibility experiments, and what the class distribution looks like.

### Trivial

- Model name inconsistency: "Qwen3-VL" in most places but "Qwen-3.5" in Section IV-A-3.
- Training and validation loss in Figure 12 and the accompanying table appear inconsistent — the text says training loss dropped from 1.447 to 0.327, but the table shows training loss starting at 0.325. This may be a parser artifact but should be verified.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation on collage size (e.g., 5, 10, 25, 50 images) would strengthen the claim that 25 is a reasonable cap.
- Quantifying the impact of ground-truth label noise (manual correction on a subset and re-evaluation) would give a clearer picture of true model capability, as the paper itself notes this as a problem.
- Reporting OWLv2 detection failure statistics (what fraction of frames yield no detection per tracklet) would help readers understand when the pipeline fails.

The paper cites Qwen3-VL [12] as a real, released model and provides empirical results showing its performance on this task. 2. **"The runtime comparison is misleading because it excludes OWLv2 detection and super-resolution time"** — REMOVED as a standalone criticism. The paper explicitly states: "The measured runtime comparison only considers the OCR inference stage rather than the entire localization-and-recognition pipeline because OWLv2 itself was not timed" (Section V.D). The comparison is transparently scoped. The limitation of being a single-tracklet test is kept as a minor weakness.

3. **"The claim that the method 'moves from frame-wise OCR toward a tracklet-level collage representation' is not backed by any experimental evidence that this change improves performance"** — REMOVED. This is a description of the approach, not a claim of improvement. The paper explicitly frames this as an "alternative formulation" (Section VII), not as a proven better one.

4. **"The legibility classifier material should either have been omitted or presented as a failed ablation"** — REMOVED as a matter of presentation preference. The paper does present it essentially as an exploration that was not adopted, and including these comparisons provides useful context about design exploration. The weakness about the thin evidence for the decision (2 tracklets) is kept.

5. **Strength Finder claims about "important problem" and "genuine challenges"** — REMOVED as generic. These are true of nearly every applied paper and do not constitute specific strengths of this work.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The paper's most interesting conceptual move is treating jersey number recognition not as OCR but as a reasoning-over-multiple-imperfect-observations problem, using a VLM as the aggregation mechanism. This reframing is intuitive but the paper does not deeply analyze why this works or provide comparative evidence that it is better than alternatives.

## Suggestions

- The single most important addition would be running the Koshkina & Elder (2024) baseline on the same test split and reporting side-by-side accuracy. Even a partial comparison (e.g., on 200 tracklets) would dramatically strengthen the paper.
- Specify LoRA hyperparameters and the exact prompt template used with Qwen.
- Label the confusion matrix axes and explain the class mapping, or replace with a more interpretable error analysis (e.g., top-K confusion pairs).
- Clarify which Qwen model variant was used (Qwen3-VL-2B? 7B?) and resolve the naming inconsistency.
