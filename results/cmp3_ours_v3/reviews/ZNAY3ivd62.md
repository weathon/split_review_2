## Summary

GUI-Spotlight introduces an iterative tool-use approach (extract, find_color, crop) for GUI visual grounding, trained via a three-stage pipeline (SFT → RL → RL) with a modified GSPO objective that stabilizes multi-tool RL training. On ScreenSpot-Pro, the method achieves 52.8% accuracy with a 7B backbone and 18.5K training samples — competitive with models trained on orders of magnitude more data. The paper also documents extensive negative results on RL variants and reward designs, providing useful practical guidance.

## Strengths

1. **Impressive ScreenSpot-Pro result with limited data.** The headline result — 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro with a 7B model trained on 18.5K samples — surpasses V2P-7B (50.6% with 9.6M samples) and GTA-1-7B (50.1% with 1.56M samples) (Table 3). This is the paper's strongest concrete evidence and a genuinely competitive result.

2. **Comprehensive RL exploration with negative results.** Sections 4.1–4.2 document seven RL variants and multiple reward designs, including approaches that *hurt* performance (e.g., continuously updating the reference policy, retaining only uncertain prompts). The documentation of what didn't work (tool-filtered positives with cross-entropy loss being the key stabilizer) is genuinely useful for practitioners and goes beyond what most method papers provide.

3. **Training stability analysis.** The right panel of Figure 3 demonstrates that vanilla GRPO/GSP0 collapses around step 300, while the proposed method maintains reward. This is a concrete, measurable advantage independent of final accuracy and makes the RL engineering contribution more believable.

4. **Clear motivation and intuitive design.** The coarse-to-fine "spotlight" idea is well-motivated for high-resolution GUI grounding where a single forward pass may fail to resolve small UI elements. The three-tool toolkit forms a natural decomposition of the problem, and Algorithm 1 describes the inference pipeline cleanly.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Data efficiency claim obscures dependence on a 72B oracle.** The paper frames "18.5K samples vs. 9.6M" as a data efficiency advantage, but the 18.5K count omits that: (a) the Stage-1 SFT trajectories were *generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B* running the inference pipeline on UGround data (Section 3.2.2), and (b) the data filtering pipeline uses Qwen2.5-VL-72B to audit instruction quality, bounding box accuracy, and consistency, retaining only ~50% of the UGround data (Section 3.2.1). On ScreenSpot-Pro itself, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct scores 53.3% (Table 3) — almost identical to GUI-Spotlight's 52.8%. This raises the question of whether the 7B model is recovering the 72B model's knowledge via distilled trajectories and filtered data, rather than learning a fundamentally more sample-efficient grounding strategy. This does not invalidate the contribution, but presenting it as "data efficiency" without acknowledging the 72B model's role is misleading. The framing should be "18.5K carefully curated samples (selected and labeled by a 72B model)" vs. "millions of raw samples."

2. **Generality claim is overstated.** The abstract and introduction frame GUI-Spotlight as broadly improving visual grounding, but gains are highly uneven across benchmarks:
   - ScreenSpot-Pro: +14.1 points (38.7% → 52.8%)
   - UI-Vision: +5.3 points (18.1% → 23.4%), but still trails UI-Venus-Ground-7B (26.5%)
   - OSWorld-G: +0.8 points (61.9% → 62.7%), effectively a rounding error

   The Section 5.2 claim of "outperforming other 7B models" on UI-Vision is inaccurate — UI-Venus-Ground-7B (26.5%) outperforms GUI-Spotlight (23.4%). On OSWorld-G, the Qwen-initialized variant (35.6%) trails UGround-7B (36.4%) and Aguvis-7B (38.7%). The paper would be stronger if it honestly characterized where and why the method helps rather than claiming broad generality from one strong benchmark result.

### Minor

3. **Inference cost is not reported.** Iterative tool-use requires multiple model calls per query. The paper reports only accuracy, not average number of turns, tokens consumed, or wall-clock time. The repeated single-turn baseline (strategy ②, Figure 5) achieves 47.6% — only 5.2 points less than GUI-Spotlight's 52.8% — and presumably uses fewer model calls. Without cost data, practitioners cannot assess whether the gain is worth the overhead. (Related: T_max in Algorithm 1 is never stated.)

4. **find_color tool has an unaddressed practical limitation.** The tool requires the model to output a target RGB value. For a natural language instruction like "Click the Send button," the model must infer the button's color without having seen it — a chicken-and-egg problem: the tool requires color knowledge that the model can only acquire by looking at the right region. The paper never discusses how the model learns to predict plausible RGB values, or how often this tool is actually invoked in practice.

5. **No variance or statistical significance reported.** Every accuracy number in Tables 3, 4, and 5 is a single point estimate. While this is common in the field, it matters more here because the headline claim is "higher accuracy with less data" — a claim that would benefit from evidence that the result is not stochastic, especially since 400 RL steps are used and the gap to some baselines is small.

### Trivial

6. **Stage numbering inconsistency between text and Figure 2.** The text describes "Stage 1" (SFT, 2561 trajectories), "Stage 2" (RL, 12K samples), "Stage 3" (RL, 4K samples). Figure 2 labels these as "Stage 0, 1, 2, 3" with different sample counts. Additionally, the 21.5-point accuracy drop from 39.3% to 17.8% after SFT is presented without discussion.

7. **UGround dataset size before filtering is not stated.** The paper says "approximately 50% retained" but not what 50% of. Since Stage 2 uses 12K from filtered UGround, the original must be ~24K — but the paper should state this explicitly.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A controlled experiment training GUI-Spotlight on (a) raw UGround data without 72B filtering, (b) 72B-filtered data, and (c) 72B-generated trajectories, all at matched sample sizes, would clarify whether gains come from curation or from the RL/tool-use design.
- Characterizing why the method helps dramatically on ScreenSpot-Pro but minimally on OSWorld-G (e.g., analyzing resolution distribution, element density, or annotation quality) would strengthen the contribution considerably.
- A brief deduplication analysis between the self-collected 4K web samples and ScreenSpot-Pro test images would address a natural reviewer concern, even if the domains are different.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the data efficiency claim.** Instead of "18.5K vs. 9.6M," acknowledge that the 18.5K samples were curated and partially generated using a 72B model. Distinguish between sample efficiency of the RL/tool-use design and the upfront cost of the 72B oracle.

2. **Provide a more honest discussion of benchmark results.** The abstract and introduction should acknowledge that gains are concentrated on ScreenSpot-Pro and are modest to negligible on other benchmarks. This does not diminish the contribution — it makes it more credible.

3. **Report inference cost.** Average number of turns, tokens consumed, or wall-clock time per query would let practitioners assess the accuracy-overhead trade-off. Also specify T_max.

4. **Discuss the find_color tool's practical usage.** How often is it invoked? How does the model learn to predict target RGB values? If usage is negligible, consider whether this tool simplifies into the existing framework.
