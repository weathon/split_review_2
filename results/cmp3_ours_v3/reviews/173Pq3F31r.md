## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework for joint expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT), disentangling note-level score content from global performance style. A diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module generates style embeddings from score content alone. Evaluated on ASAP and ATEPP datasets, the model achieves competitive results on both tasks with convincing disentanglement evidence (Table 4) and eliminates the need for note-level alignment preprocessing.

## Strengths
- **Well-motivated joint formulation**: The paper correctly identifies EPR and APT as inverse tasks, drawing a clean analogy to ASR/TTS joint modeling (§1). The content-style disentanglement is architecturally simple and directly motivated by this duality — content is what both domains share, style distinguishes a performance from a score.
- **Strong disentanglement evidence (Table 4)**: A classifier trained on style embeddings achieves 42% performer accuracy vs. ~10% from content embeddings, and 77% composer accuracy vs. ~30% from content. This gap is large and unambiguous.
- **Seq2Seq EPR without note-level alignment works (Table 3)**: Ours(PSR) achieves 92.27% alignment rate, competitive with alignment-dependent DExter (91.27%) and VirtuosoNet (91.88%), showing the alignment preprocessing bottleneck can be avoided.
- **Subjective evaluation shows strong performance (Figure 2)**: Ours(Target) achieves the highest ratings across dynamics, tempo, style, and overall human-likeness, with Ours(PSR) closely following.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **Confounded EPR-Only ablation**: The EPR-Only variant is trained solely on ASAP, while Ours(Target) is trained on ASAP + 75K unpaired scores + YouTube performances. The paper states that "the consistent improvement of Ours-Target over the EPR-Only variant indicates that joint modeling, together with training on additional unpaired data, leads to better EPR performance" (§5.1). Because EPR-Only differs in *both* joint modeling and training data, the specific benefit of joint modeling cannot be isolated. An ablation trained on the same data mix without the APT decoder/loss is needed. This weakens a core claim about the framework's motivation.

### Minor
- **Selective variance claims**: The paper claims "our models exhibit duration and velocity variances that more closely match those of human performances" (line 235). In Table 2, DExter's velocity variance (238.86) is objectively closer to human (241.04) than Ours(Target) (151.03) or Ours(PSR) (161.51). Similarly, DExter's onset variance (0.20) is closer to human (0.12) than either Ours variant (0.02). The claim holds cleanly only for duration. The post-hoc explanation ("unstable dynamics rather than meaningful expressiveness") is interpretive, not measured.
- **PSR module evaluation is largely qualitative**: The PSR module is a stated contribution (§1, §3.4), yet its evaluation rests primarily on a 2D visualization (Figure 4) and indirect subjective EPR ratings (Figure 2). There is no quantitative comparison against baselines such as average training-set style embeddings, nearest-neighbor retrieval, or unconditional diffusion samples. Whether the diffusion process adds value over simpler alternatives is unclear.
- **OOD framing does not match experiments**: The paper introduces ATEPP "to evaluate the generalization of disentangled representations in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings" (§4.1), but the ATEPP experiments only measure performer/composer identification (Table 4) and visualizations (Figures 3-4). No EPR or APT task performance is reported on ATEPP. The claim about OOD generalization is about representational structure, not task transfer performance, and this distinction is not clearly made.

### Trivial
- **Statistical notation ambiguity (Tables 2-3)**: The superscript letter notation appears to use Tukey-style grouping, but this is not stated, and the reader cannot reconstruct which pairs differ significantly.
- **GPT-4o mini for era labeling (§5.3)**: Using GPT to assign historical era labels from composer metadata when composer identity directly determines era (e.g., Bach → Baroque) is an odd choice that is not explained.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Include inter-rater agreement metrics (e.g., Fleiss' κ) for the subjective listening test (11 participants, 5 pieces).
- Add a limitations section discussing what the model cannot do (e.g., operates on symbolic MIDI, not audio; limited to classical piano).
- Show a data scaling curve to verify the unpaired data's utility via the masked reconstruction objective.
- Identify the YouTube transcription model and report its accuracy to characterize noise in the unpaired performance data.

## Suggestions
1. **Fix the confounded ablation**: Retrain the EPR-Only variant on the same data mixture (ASAP + unpaired scores + YouTube performances). If Ours(Target) still improves, the joint modeling benefit is isolated; if not, attribute improvements to additional data honestly.
2. **Add quantitative PSR evaluation**: Measure cosine similarity or MSE between PSR-generated and ground-truth style embeddings, compared against (a) average training-set style, (b) nearest-neighbor retrieval, (c) unconditional diffusion.
3. **Report EPR/APT task performance on ATEPP** to substantiate OOD generalization claims, or reframe the ATEPP experiments as representation analysis rather than OOD evaluation.
4. **Correct the variance claim in §5.1** to reflect that DExter matches human variance better on onset and velocity, while the proposed model excels on duration and overall KL/MAE.
