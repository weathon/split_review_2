## Summary

This paper identifies a real issue in contrastive time-series representation learning: dominant semantic components (e.g., trend) can suppress weaker but semantically important components (e.g., periodicity). It introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a diagnostic metric to quantify this imbalance and proposes asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) to dynamically rebalance contrastive loss terms within the CoST framework. Experiments on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets compare CoST+APW against TS2Vec, TNC, and vanilla CoST.

## Strengths

- **The problem framing is well-motivated and convincingly demonstrated.** The synthetic experiment in Table 1 systematically varies the amplitude ratio between trend and periodicity (r from 0.1 to 10) and shows that TS2Vec's SDE values become severely imbalanced (Δ ranging from −1.29 to +1.11). This cleanly validates that semantic suppression is a genuine issue in existing methods.
- **The SDE metric is a simple, interpretable diagnostic tool.** Defined as 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)), it provides an intuitive, grounded way to measure component recoverability on synthetic data. The link to vector arithmetic in word embeddings is a sensible conceptual anchor.
- **Table 2 honestly reports a negative result.** The paper shows that adding SDE as a direct regularizer to TS2Vec does not help (and sometimes hurts). This is transparent reporting and correctly motivates the need for a different approach.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The main results (Table 3) contradict the paper's claim of "consistently lower SDE and superior forecasting accuracy."** On Electricity at horizons 168–720, CoST+APW is strictly worse than *every* baseline including vanilla CoST (e.g., horizon 720: CoST+APW MSE=1.010 vs CoST=0.911, TS2Vec=0.863, TNC=0.859 — roughly 10–17% worse). On Weather at horizons 336–720, CoST+APW underperforms both TS2Vec and TNC (e.g., horizon 720: 0.299 vs 0.233 and 0.219). On the first ETT1 block, CoST+APW is worse than vanilla CoST at horizons 48 (0.07 vs 0.06) and 720 (0.152 vs 0.148). The paper's headline claim of "consistent gains" is not supported by its own primary evidence table.

- **Missing ablation that isolates the central contribution.** The paper's key methodological innovation is the asymmetric weighting mechanism. Table 3 compares CoST+APW against vanilla CoST, but this confounds two changes: (i) the addition of an MLP fusion layer (Section 4.4.2) and (ii) the asymmetric weighting (Section 4.4.3). Without an ablation comparing CoST+MLP (no weighting) vs CoST+MLP+APW, the claimed improvements cannot be attributed to the weighting mechanism. The paper's stated contribution of "systematic ablations" (line 32) does not deliver on this.

### Minor

- **Claim of "no architectural changes" is contradicted by the MLP fusion layer.** The abstract (line 10) states the approach "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes." However, Section 4.4.2 introduces a trainable MLP g_ϕ that maps concatenated embeddings to a composite embedding — an architectural addition not present in vanilla CoST. Even if the MLP is small, this conflicts with the stated claim.

- **No measures of uncertainty.** No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported for any result. This is problematic because many differences in Table 3 are marginal (e.g., ETTm1 horizon 24: 0.012 vs 0.015 — a 0.003 MSE gap), and without variance information it is impossible to distinguish signal from noise. This also makes it hard to assess the severity of the failure cases on Electricity and Weather.

- **The SDE-based Δ computation on real data involves a feedback loop that is not analyzed.** On real-world data where ground-truth components are unknown, Δ is computed entirely from CoST's own learned representations: v(a)=pool(V_T(x)), v(b)=pool(V_S(x)), v(a+b)=g_ϕ([v(a)||v(b)]). This Δ is then used to reweight the encoder's own losses. The paper does not discuss whether this closed loop could reinforce existing biases, nor does it report Δ dynamics during training to show the mechanism converges sensibly.

- **SDE acronym is used inconsistently.** The same quantity is called "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3). This is confusing.

- **ETT labeling ambiguity in Table 3.** The label "ETT1" appears twice (rows 274–278 and rows 284–288) with different horizon sets (24/48/168/336/720 vs 24/48/96/288/672). The second block appears to be ETTm1 based on the horizons. This makes the table harder to interpret.

### Trivial

- The weighting formula in the figure caption (line 128) uses a single γ for both losses, while Sections 3.4 and 4.4.3 use two separate hyperparameters γ and γ'. The relationship between these formulations is not explained.
- The values of γ and γ' are never reported, making the method harder to reproduce.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report SDE values for CoST+APW alongside forecasting metrics to directly support the claim that the weighting mechanism reduces semantic imbalance on real-world data.
- Show Δ dynamics during training (e.g., a plot of Δ over epochs) to demonstrate the weighting mechanism is active and converges sensibly.
- Compare against simpler alternative weighting strategies (fixed reweighting, curriculum weighting) to test whether SDE-based adaptation is specifically beneficial.
- Report the computational overhead of the MLP fusion layer and SDE computation.

## Removed Points

- **Criticism about encoder additivity assumption:** The harsh critic questioned whether neural network encoders are approximately additive. This is a conceptual concern but the paper's synthetic experiment (Table 1) empirically validates that SDE behaves as expected, partially addressing this. Additionally, the additive assumption underlying SDE is similar to well-known properties of word embeddings, making this a standard rather than fatal limitation.
- **Criticism about "time-domain-only" limitation being already addressed by CoST:** The paper builds on CoST, and correctly frames this as a limitation of TS2Vec (not of the field as a whole). This framing is appropriate.
- **Request for limitations section:** This is a presentation concern that does not affect technical validity.
- **Missing appendix/related works references:** These are parser stripping artifacts, not author omissions.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The harsh critic's main finding — that the experimental results contradict the paper's claimed "consistent gains" — is a verification of content already present in Table 3, not a novel conceptual insight.

## Suggestions

1. Add an ablation disentangling the MLP fusion layer from the weighting mechanism: CoST → CoST+MLP → CoST+MLP+APW.
2. Report standard deviations and/or confidence intervals for all main experimental results.
3. Resolve the contradiction between the "no architectural changes" claim and the MLP addition, or rephrase the claim.
4. Clarify the ETT labeling in Table 3.
5. Report Δ dynamics during training to empirically validate that the weighting mechanism is active.
6. Report SDE values for CoST+APW on real-world benchmarks.
7. Report the values of γ and γ' used in the experiments.
