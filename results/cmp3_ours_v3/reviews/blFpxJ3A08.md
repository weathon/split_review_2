## Summary

LPFQA proposes a benchmark of 505 questions sourced from professional technical forums across 20 fields, designed to evaluate LLMs on long-tail professional knowledge. The benchmark is constructed via a pipeline of forum crawling, MLLM-based QA generation from screenshots, expert verification, and difficulty calibration. 12 LLMs are evaluated. The core idea — using real practitioner forum posts as raw material — is genuinely novel and addresses a real gap in the existing benchmark landscape.

## Strengths

- **Genuine gap identification (Sections 1-2).** The paper correctly identifies that existing benchmarks (MMLU, HLE, Arena-Hard) each miss at least one of: long-tail knowledge, professional depth, and real-world authenticity. The framing of this gap is clear and well-motivated.

- **Forum-sourced data as a source of authentic professional questions (Section 3.2.1).** Using real technical forum posts is a legitimate and novel approach to obtaining questions that reflect actual practitioner challenges rather than exam-style textbook questions. The two examples shown (endplate potentials on muscle fibres; orchestral tremolo notation) are genuinely non-trivial and validate the core premise.

- **Expert verification in the pipeline (Step 7, Section 3.2.3).** Having domain experts verify the automatically generated QAs is a meaningful quality-control step that many automated benchmark pipelines omit.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **DeepSeek-V3 "overall best-performing" claim contradicts reported data (Section 4.1 vs. Table 1).** The paper states: *"Among all evaluated systems, DeepSeek-V3 demonstrates the most balanced and consistent performance across disciplines, with no apparent weaknesses, and can thus be regarded as the overall best-performing model."* However, Table 1 shows DeepSeek-V3 scoring **32.60** — the second-lowest among all 12 models, barely above GPT-4o (32.40), while GPT-5 scores 47.28 (highest). The text then adds that GPT-5 "in some cases surpasses DeepSeek-V3" — a model that leads by 14.68 points globally is not merely "competitive." This is a clear internal inconsistency between the textual claim and the numerical evidence that undermines confidence in the paper's analysis.

2. **Scoring metric never defined (Tables 1-4).** The "Score" column is never defined. The paper does not state whether these are accuracy percentages, raw correct counts out of 505, or a weighted metric. All results are reported as "averaged over three trials" (Section 4), but what quantity is being averaged is not specified. While context strongly suggests accuracy percentages (scores of 32–47 on the full set, shifting to 37–54 after filtering), a benchmark paper must explicitly define its evaluation metric. This omission makes the results technically uninterpretable and unreproducible as-is.

3. **Knowledge-vs-reasoning framing contradiction (Abstract/§1 vs. §4.2.2).** The abstract and introduction frame LPFQA as evaluating "complex reasoning" and list "reasoning ability" as a key evaluation dimension. Yet Section 4.2.2's ablation study concludes: *"These findings suggest that LPFQA primarily reflects a model's mastery of domain knowledge rather than its reasoning ability."* The paper's own experiment directly undercuts its central framing. The Conclusion acknowledges this finding honestly, but the abstract and introduction overclaim what the benchmark measures. This must be resolved: either reframe the benchmark's purpose as evaluating professional long-tail knowledge (still a valid and interesting contribution) or provide a more nuanced interpretation of the ablation.

### Minor

4. **Task count inconsistency (Abstract vs. Sections 3.1/3.3).** The abstract says "covering 502 tasks" while Sections 3.1 and 3.3 consistently state "505 questions." This is a small but unnecessary discrepancy that suggests sloppy editing.

5. **Small per-field sample sizes undermine per-field analysis.** Seven fields have ≤10 questions: DS (3), AI (8), Aero (8), En (9), EIS (10), EIE (10), ICE (7). With 3–10 questions per field, any per-field accuracy comparison is statistically unreliable — a single question changes the score by 10–33%. The paper nonetheless conducts detailed per-field analysis with radar charts and draws conclusions about model strengths/weaknesses in these fields, giving a false impression of precision.

6. **AI-generated content weakens "authentic" framing (Section 3.2).** Questions are generated by MLLMs from forum screenshots (Step 4), including distractor options (Step 6). The paper claims "authentic professional tasks," but the actual question wording, answer selection, and distractor design are all MLLM outputs subsequently human-verified. This is not the same as naturally occurring professional questions. A more careful framing of what "authentic" means in this pipeline would strengthen the paper.

### Trivial
7. The "502 tasks" vs "505 questions" discrepancy in the abstract versus the main text (duplicate of #4 — minor copyediting issue).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Human performance baseline to calibrate benchmark difficulty
- Correlation analysis with existing benchmarks (MMLU, GPQA, HLE) to demonstrate what LPFQA adds
- Confidence intervals or variance estimates for the 3 reported trials
- A dedicated limitations section acknowledging the small per-field samples, the AI-generated nature of questions, and other caveats
- Clearer provenance in the main text: showing how the 4 example forums in Figure 1 relate to the 20 claimed fields, or listing additional source forums explicitly

## Suggestions

1. Define the scoring metric explicitly in Section 4 (it appears to be accuracy percentage — state this clearly).
2. Fix or explain the DeepSeek-V3 claim in Section 4.1. If the claim is about "balance" rather than absolute score, state this clearly and use a different term than "overall best-performing model."
3. Reframe the paper's claims to honestly reflect what the benchmark primarily measures: professional long-tail knowledge. The "reasoning" framing is contradicted by the paper's own ablation.
4. Add per-field sample sizes to the radar chart discussion and caution readers about overinterpreting fields with fewer than ~15 questions.
5. Add a limitations section.
6. Correct the "502 tasks" / "505 questions" inconsistency.
