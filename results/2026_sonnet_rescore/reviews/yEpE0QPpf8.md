## Summary

Grounding-IQA introduces a new image quality assessment (IQA) paradigm that integrates multimodal referring and grounding with IQA to achieve fine-grained spatial quality perception. The paper defines two subtasks — GIQA-DES (quality descriptions with bounding boxes) and GIQA-VQA (quality QA with spatial referring/grounding) — and contributes: (1) an automated annotation pipeline, (2) GIQA-160K (~167K instruction-tuning samples, ~43K images), and (3) GIQA-Bench (100 images, 250 test samples). Fine-tuning existing MLLMs on GIQA-160K confers grounding-IQA capabilities that the base models lack.

---

## Strengths

- **Well-designed automated annotation pipeline with verified gains.** The four-stage pipeline (tag extraction via Llama3 → Grounding DINO detection → IQA-Filter + Box-Merge refinement → coordinate discretization) produces quality-aware bounding boxes without manual labeling. Ablation (Table 2a) shows box refinement raises mIoU from 0.5624 to 0.5851 and Tag-Recall from 0.5045 to 0.5497 on the held-out benchmark, directly validating the pipeline's contribution.

- **Dataset is architecture-agnostic.** Fine-tuning on GIQA-160K yields grounding-IQA capabilities across all four tested models (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LLaVA-v1.5-13B, LLaVA-v1.6-7B, mPLUG-Owl2-7B; Table 4), demonstrating general-purpose utility beyond a single model family.

- **Coordinate discretization improves performance.** Discretized coordinates (up to 9 tokens) outperform continuous normalized coordinates on BLEU@4 (23.67 vs. 22.03), LLM-Score (61.75 vs. 61.00), and Tag-Recall (Table 2b), showing that the simplification eases learning without sacrificing accuracy.

- **Paradigm is well-motivated.** The paper makes a clear and genuine case that existing MLLM-based IQA methods (e.g., Q-Instruct) cannot localize quality-affecting regions or answer spatially grounded quality questions, filling a real gap in fine-grained quality assessment for applications like targeted image editing.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Benchmark scale is too small for the precision of claims made.** GIQA-Bench has only 100 GIQA-DES samples and 90 "Yes/No" + 60 open-ended VQA samples. At 90 samples, one misprediction shifts Acc(Y) by ~1.1 percentage points; cross-method differences of 1–3 pp in Table 5 are indistinguishable from noise. BLEU@4 on 100 samples with values in the 0–23 range makes 1–2 point differences similarly unreliable. No variance estimates, confidence intervals, or bootstrap statistics are reported anywhere. This is not a minor omission — it is structural: a benchmark paper's quantitative claims are only as credible as its evaluation scale, and multiple comparative conclusions in Table 5 cannot be meaningfully asserted at this sample size.

- **Distribution overlap between training and benchmark inflates description quality metrics.** Section 3.4 states that GIQA-Bench GIQA-DES descriptions come from Q-Pathway "adjusted" by experts. Section 3.3 states GIQA-160K is built from Q-Pathway descriptions via the same annotation pipeline. While the paper correctly excludes the benchmark *images* from training, it does not address the fact that a model trained on Q-Pathway-derived text will naturally generate outputs closer to Q-Pathway vocabulary and phrasing — precisely what BLEU@4 and LLM-Score reward. The inflated description quality metrics are unacknowledged and not disentangled from the reported gains.

- **LLM-Score evaluation circularity.** The GIQA-VQA training data in GIQA-160K was generated by Llama3. The LLM-Score in GIQA-Bench is also computed by Llama3 (comparing model outputs against Q-Pathway ground truth). Models fine-tuned to produce Llama3-style text are likely to receive higher Llama3 judge scores regardless of actual quality perception fidelity. No human preference correlation study or inter-annotator agreement for the LLM-Score is provided to validate that the metric captures quality assessment capability rather than stylistic proximity to the judge model's own generation patterns.

- **Q-Ground — the most directly comparable prior work — is absent from Table 5.** Section 2.2 explicitly identifies Q-Ground (Chen et al., 2024b) as the closest existing method for degradation-region grounding, noting only that it "lacks referring capabilities." Despite this explicit acknowledgment, Q-Ground does not appear in any quantitative comparison. Omitting the most natural baseline leaves the paper's core claim — that grounding-IQA is a new capability advance over prior work — unverified against the one prior method that directly addresses quality-region localization.

### Minor

- **Ferret-7B (zero-shot) outperforms three of four fine-tuned Grounding-IQA models on GIQA-DES grounding, contradicting the "Ours outperforms existing MLLMs" framing.** According to Table 5, Ferret-7B achieves mIoU 0.6458 and Tag-Recall 0.6778 on GIQA-DES — exceeding Grounding-IQA variants based on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (0.5763/0.5283), mPLUG-Owl2 (0.5955/0.5474), and LLaVA-v1.5-13B (0.6302/0.5548). Section 4.3's conclusion that "our method outperforms existing MLLMs" is too broad without this qualification. The paper should discuss why a general grounding model surpasses fine-tuned IQA-grounding models on box precision, and what the fine-tuning adds beyond raw grounding accuracy (e.g., quality-relevance of grounding vs. salient-object grounding).

- **VQA class imbalance in benchmark is undisclosed.** Section 3.4 reports 35 "Yes" and 55 "No" answers in the 90 binary VQA samples (39%/61% imbalance). A constant "No" baseline achieves 61% Acc(Y), yet this majority-class baseline is not reported. Several general model baselines (mPLUG-Owl2: 58.9%, LLaVA-v1.6: 48.9%) are thus near or below this trivial baseline, which the paper does not acknowledge. Acc(Y) gains are difficult to interpret without this context.

- **Circularity in IQA-Filter.** The pipeline uses Q-Instruct to filter bounding boxes (by asking "Is the image quality ⟨Tq⟩?"). Q-Instruct was trained on Q-Pathway, the same dataset whose descriptions seed the pipeline. This creates a closed-loop quality signal: the filtering step is guided by a model already shaped by the same text distribution. The practical impact may be modest, but it is unacknowledged.

### Trivial

None worth flagging.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **A concrete downstream-task experiment.** Section 3.1 mentions targeted image editing as a motivating application. The paper references downstream task experiments in the supplementary material but only vaguely. A head-to-head comparison showing that grounding-IQA outputs enable better targeted restoration or editing compared to non-grounded IQA descriptions would make the paradigm's practical value much more compelling.
- **Independent holdout for benchmark images.** Including even 20–30 benchmark images drawn from a source entirely outside Q-Pathway (e.g., a DQ-495K-only held-out split) would serve as a distribution-independence check and strengthen confidence in the description-quality metrics.
- **Engagement with the Ferret comparison as an analysis.** Rather than avoiding the result, analyzing *why* Ferret achieves competitive box-precision on GIQA-DES — and what grounding-IQA fine-tuning specifically adds (quality-relevant grounding vs. generic salient-object grounding) — would clarify the paradigm's identity and strengthen the contribution.
- **Majority-class and inter-annotator agreement statistics.** A "No-always" baseline for Acc(Y) and inter-annotator agreement numbers for expert-annotated samples are standard for benchmark papers and would sharpen credibility.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely insightful observation in the combined reviews is the Ferret paradox: a general grounding model (zero-shot) outperforms fine-tuned grounding-IQA models on GIQA-DES box precision. This reveals a fundamental distinction between *spatial grounding ability* and *quality-relevant grounding* — a dedicated IQA-trained grounding model need not be better at placing boxes than a general grounding model; it must be better at selecting *which* boxes to place (quality-affecting vs. merely salient regions). This distinction is the conceptual core of the grounding-IQA paradigm but is neither demonstrated nor discussed in the paper. Making this argument quantitatively (e.g., showing that fine-tuned models ground quality-degraded regions that Ferret misses, even if Ferret's overall mIoU is higher) would substantially strengthen the paper.

## Suggestions

1. **Expand GIQA-Bench** to at least 500 images across diverse distortion types, or report bootstrap confidence intervals on all key metrics in the current Table 5. Without this, the quantitative comparisons cannot support the claims made.
2. **Add Q-Ground as a baseline in Table 5.** Even if it cannot perform GIQA-VQA, comparing its GIQA-DES grounding precision against fine-tuned Grounding-IQA models is the most relevant prior-work comparison and should not be omitted.
3. **Validate LLM-Score.** Provide a human preference study showing that LLM-Score ranks model outputs in agreement with human judgments, or replace it with a judge model not used in training data generation.
4. **Acknowledge and analyze the Ferret result.** Add a targeted ablation or analysis comparing quality-relevant grounding (objects that truly affect image quality) vs. generic saliency-based grounding to show that fine-tuned models select quality-affecting regions more reliably than Ferret, even if raw box overlap is lower.
5. **Report a majority-class "No" baseline** for Acc(Y) in Table 5 to contextualize VQA accuracy gains.
6. **Explicitly acknowledge the Q-Pathway distributional link** between training and benchmark, and propose or implement an independence check (e.g., DQ-495K-sourced held-out images).

## Score and Decision

**Originality:** The paradigm is novel — combining spatial grounding with IQA is a meaningful extension — but it is an engineering combination of established components (Grounding DINO, Q-Instruct, standard MLLM fine-tuning) rather than a conceptual breakthrough. Moderate originality.

**Importance:** Grounded quality assessment has clear practical value for targeted image enhancement and quality-aware editing. The research question is important.

**Claims supported:** The core claim — that GIQA-160K fine-tuning confers grounding-IQA capability — is directionally well-supported. However, the precise quantitative claims in Table 5 are compromised by the small benchmark, distribution overlap, and LLM-Score circularity. The "Ours outperforms all" conclusion in Section 4.3 is overstated given the Ferret result.

**Soundness:** The pipeline design is reasonable and the ablations are informative. However, the evaluation methodology has three significant problems (benchmark scale, distributional overlap, metric circularity) that reduce confidence in the quantitative evidence.

**Clarity:** Writing is generally clear; the paradigm and pipeline are well-explained. The missing discussion of the Ferret anomaly and the imbalanced benchmark are presentation omissions.

**Community value:** GIQA-160K (167K samples) is a genuinely useful resource. The benchmark, as designed, is too small to serve as a reliable evaluation standard for the community.

The paper makes a legitimate and useful contribution but needs a more credible benchmark, more honest framing of comparisons, and resolution of the evaluation circularity concerns before its quantitative claims are trustworthy at the level implied by current tables.
