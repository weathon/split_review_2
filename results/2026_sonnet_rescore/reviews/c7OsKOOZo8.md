## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading that eliminates reliance on external lesion annotations. The two core modules are GALP (Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal), which uses stage-wise auxiliary classifiers and class activation maps to derive grade-conditioned evidence maps and select top-K lesion-salient regions as proposals, and LGRF (Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion), which routes those proposals through a mixture-of-experts pool gated by the current view's context and performs top-K-weighted cross-view attention. On two benchmarks (MFIDDR, four-view; DRTiD, two-view), the annotation-free variant matches or outperforms most externally supervised baselines, while an optional lesion-fused variant achieves new state-of-the-art on MFIDDR.

## Strengths

- **Novel self-generated lesion proposal mechanism (GALP):** Stage-wise auxiliary classifiers (Eq. 2) and LayerCAM-style grade-conditioned evidence maps (Eq. 3–7) enable the model to locate grade-relevant regions at each resolution scale without any external annotation. This is a concrete, mechanically described contribution, not a generic claim.

- **Expert-gated cross-view fusion (LGRF):** The routing network (Eq. 9) selects which of the M experts should process adjacent-view proposals based on the current view's context, and Top-K-weighted cross-view attention (Eqs. 12–15) fuses only lesion-salient proposals. Ablation in Table 4 confirms both GALP and the expert pool contribute: removing LGRF entirely drops accuracy from 83.9% → 82.3%, and removing the expert pool gives 82.6%, demonstrating the expert routing's independent contribution.

- **Performance parity with externally supervised methods, annotation-free:** As shown in Table 1, the annotation-free variant ("Ours w/o lesion") at 83.9% Acc / 70.9% Kappa outperforms end-to-end baselines (best prior: ETMC at 81.5%) and matches or exceeds externally-supervised methods (CVSA 82.6%, LFMVDR-with-lesion 82.2%). On DRTiD (Table 3), the end-to-end variant reaches 76.0% Acc, surpassing all compared methods including CrossFIT (75.6%) which uses OD and macular coordinates.

- **Thorough hyperparameter sensitivity study:** Figure 3 shows genuine optima at α=0.5, K₂=2, M=6 with meaningful degradation on both sides, supporting the chosen configuration.

- **Grade-wise analysis (Table 2):** The annotation-free model achieves competitive F1 across all five grades, including Grade 3 (F1=74.1%), which is among the most clinically important intermediate grades.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The ablation does not isolate GALP's two distinct effects, leaving the mechanism claim unverified.** The paper describes GALP as contributing via two mechanisms: (a) auxiliary stage-wise supervision that makes intermediate features more grade-discriminative, and (b) Top-K regional selection that removes non-lesion background tokens from cross-view fusion. Per Section 4.3, "w/o GALP removes the GALP mechanism and directly uses all tokens for LGRF fusion," which eliminates both (a) and (b) simultaneously. A missing row — LGRF with auxiliary loss but without Top-K selection (all tokens but better-supervised features) — would disambiguate the two effects. Auxiliary supervision in intermediate layers is a well-known performance boost in multi-exit networks; if it alone accounts for the 82.7% → 83.9% gain, the proposal-selection mechanism (the more novel part of GALP's contribution) is unsupported. This is the most significant evidential gap in the paper.

- **The "lesion proposal" narrative is asserted but not verified spatially.** The paper's core claim, stated explicitly in Section 3.2 ("Since the grade evidence in DR is predominantly localized to lesions, selecting Top-K peaks within these maps yields lesion proposals, which is the spatial regions most predictive of the grade"), connects high-CAM-activation regions to actual lesion locations. The MFIDDR dataset provides lesion segmentation masks ("The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model," Section 4.1), yet the paper does not compute or report any spatial overlap between GALP-selected regions and these masks. In DR grading, discriminative features can include vascular caliber changes, global retinal texture, or optic disc appearance — not only traditional focal lesions. Without the overlap analysis, the paper demonstrates that a CAM-based token-selection scheme improves multi-view fusion performance, but has not shown the mechanism is lesion-driven as claimed. This weakens the interpretability narrative throughout the paper.

### Minor

- **Headline result is "Ours (with lesion)," which contradicts the annotation-free framing.** The abstract and introduction are organized around eliminating external annotation dependency, yet the bold top row of Table 1 and the phrase "establishing new SOTA performance on this benchmark" (Section 4.2) refer to "Ours (with lesion)" at 84.6% / 72.3% Kappa. The annotation-free variant at 83.9% / 70.9% is the result that matches the stated contribution. The with-lesion result could instead be framed as an upper bound / oracle experiment showing the architecture is compatible with external signals when available, rather than the primary claim.

- **The cyclic adjacency design in LGRF lacks justification.** Section 3.3 specifies that view i fuses with view j = (i+1) mod N, but no clinical or empirical rationale is given for why cyclic adjacency (as opposed to attending to all other views or learning which view to attend) is the correct choice for four-view fundus imaging, where the anatomical relationship between views is not inherently circular.

- **No variance estimates across runs.** No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or significance tests are reported for any result. For DRTiD, the method's margin over CrossFIT is 0.4% (76.0% vs. 75.6%), and for MFIDDR the margin over the second-best annotation-free result (ETMC 81.5% → 83.9%) is larger, but single-run results on test sets of ~1,100 and ~2,580 eyes could be influenced by random seed and data partitioning.

### Trivial
None requiring separate mention.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Adding a row to Table 4 that retains auxiliary supervision but uses all tokens (no Top-K selection) for LGRF would confirm whether the gain comes from the proposal-selection mechanism specifically. This would fully support the mechanistic claim at low experimental cost.
- Computing spatial overlap (IoU or coverage fraction) between GALP's selected regions and the MFIDDR lesion segmentation masks — even as a qualitative figure — would directly validate the lesion-proposal narrative and substantially strengthen the interpretability argument.
- Reporting mean ± std over at least three runs, particularly for the DRTiD result, would make the performance claims more statistically credible.
- Providing a brief justification (ablation or clinical motivation) for cyclic vs. all-to-all cross-view pairing would clarify this design choice.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **Harsh Critic — Training dynamics / warm-up for noisy early CAMs:** The concern that CAM proposals are noisy early in training since they are computed from predicted (not ground-truth) grades is speculative. The paper does not observe this as an empirical problem, and the hyperparameter study shows stable results. Removed as speculation without a concrete anchor.
- **Harsh Critic — EyePACS pretraining for DRTiD as a confound:** The concern that domain-specific EyePACS pretraining may independently explain DRTiD gains is a valid general concern, but the paper explicitly follows prior SOTA (CrossFIT) in using this initialization for fair comparison (Section 4.1). Intentionally following the prior art's setup is not a methodological weakness.
- **Strength Finder — "Reproducibility and clear design rationale" as a standalone strength:** This is generic and not concrete enough to stand alone; removed as a top-level strength, though the paper is indeed clearly written.
- **Strength Finder — "Comprehensive evaluation on two benchmarks confirms SOTA competitiveness":** While accurate, this is a summary statement rather than a distinct strength with specific evidence not already covered under performance parity.

## Novel Insights

The key novel insight surfacing from this review is that GALP's CAM-derived lesion proposals operate as a soft-attention prefilter for cross-view fusion — conceptually similar to "proposal networks" in detection but applied to feature tokens rather than bounding boxes, and derived from classification gradients rather than a separate head. This creates an implicit coupling between intermediate discriminative capacity (via auxiliary loss) and cross-view attention quality: better-supervised intermediate features produce more accurate CAMs, which yield better proposals, which make LGRF fusion more precise. The paper partially describes this feedback loop but does not analyze it as a training curriculum phenomenon. Whether this mechanism actually converges faster, is more stable, or benefits from explicit warm-up is left unexplored. The unverified link between grade-discriminative regions and actual lesion locations is the main open question; resolving it with the available MFIDDR segmentation masks would either confirm an elegant self-supervisory mechanism or prompt a more honest reframing of GALP as a "grade-discriminative proposal" module rather than a "lesion proposal" module — still a valid and useful contribution.

## Suggestions

1. **Add an ablation row**: Train with auxiliary GALP loss but pass all tokens (α=1.0) to LGRF fusion. This directly isolates the contribution of Top-K regional selection from auxiliary supervision.
2. **Compute spatial overlap with lesion masks**: Use the available MFIDDR lesion segmentation masks to compute IoU or coverage fraction between GALP-selected top-K patches and ground-truth lesion regions, at minimum for Stage 3 features. Report this as a validation figure or table.
3. **Reframe the with-lesion result**: Label "Ours (with lesion)" as "Ours (w/ lesion, oracle)" or move it to a supplementary comparison, with "Ours (w/o lesion)" as the primary result. This aligns the headline result with the paper's stated contribution.
4. **Report run variance**: Provide at least three-run mean ± std for the key metrics on both datasets.
5. **Justify or ablate the cyclic view pairing**: Add a brief comparison (e.g., all-to-all attention vs. cyclic) or provide a clinical/empirical rationale for the current choice.

## Score and Decision

**Originality (3/5):** Combines known techniques (CAM, auxiliary multi-exit supervision, MoE routing, cross-view attention) into a novel pipeline for annotation-free multi-view DR grading. The specific coupling of CAM-derived proposals with expert-gated cross-view fusion is original in this context, though not deeply conceptually new.

**Importance (4/5):** Annotation-free DR grading is practically important for deployment in low-resource clinical settings. The question addressed — can self-generated proposals substitute for external annotations? — is well-scoped and clinically relevant.

**Claims Supported (3/5):** The core performance claim (annotation-free parity with externally supervised methods) is well supported by two benchmarks. The mechanism claim (proposals correspond to lesion locations) is asserted but not demonstrated. The ablation partially but incompletely isolates the contributions.

**Soundness (3/5):** Method description is complete and technically sound. The ablation gap (GALP's two effects conflated) is a genuine issue. No variance estimates weaken confidence in narrow-margin results.

**Clarity (4/5):** Paper is clearly organized, equations are fully specified, and the motivation is logical. The headline result framing is slightly misleading.

**Community Value (4/5):** The paper provides a useful blueprint for annotation-free medical image analysis pipelines and establishes a strong benchmark on both MFIDDR and DRTiD.
