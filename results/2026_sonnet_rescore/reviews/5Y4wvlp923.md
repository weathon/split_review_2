## Summary

This paper introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric for quantifying how linearly recoverable individual temporal components (trend, seasonality) are from a composite time-series embedding. Based on this diagnostic, the authors propose an Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) scheme that dynamically rebalances CoST's seasonal and trend contrastive losses in proportion to the measured imbalance. The method additionally introduces a small MLP to produce composite embeddings from CoST's dual-encoder outputs. Experiments compare CoST+APW against TS2Vec, CoST, and TNC on ETT, Electricity, and Weather benchmarks.

---

## Strengths

- **Synthetic SDE diagnostic (Table 1):** The analysis of TS2Vec embeddings on controlled signals with varying amplitude ratios ($r$) is the cleanest and most principled piece in the paper. Δ shifts directionally and monotonically with $r$, providing concrete, reproducible evidence that semantic imbalance exists and is quantifiable. This is a genuine contribution as a diagnostic tool.

- **Ablation of direct SDE regularization (Table 2):** The paper tests adding SDE directly as a regularization term to TS2Vec and finds it degrades performance, motivating the adaptive weighting design. This negative result is informative and properly reported.

- **The core diagnostic idea is coherent:** Linking measurable asymmetry in component recoverability to an adaptive loss rebalancing strategy is a reasonable and principled design. The concept has merit even if the current execution is incomplete.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Central empirical claim is directly contradicted by the paper's own Table 3.** Section 4.4.4 states: *"This combination leads to consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines."* This is demonstrably false. On the Electricity dataset — which the paper itself describes as having "strong calendar-driven periodicities" and thus being a primary target of the paper's thesis — CoST+APW is the worst method by a substantial margin at horizons 168, 336, and 720:
  - Horizon 168: CoST+APW 0.566/0.450 vs. CoST 0.425/0.405 vs. TS2Vec 0.429/0.392 vs. TNC **0.412/0.384**
  - Horizon 336: CoST+APW 0.677/0.566 vs. CoST 0.576/0.472 vs. TS2Vec 0.565/0.478 vs. TNC **0.548/0.466**
  - Horizon 720: CoST+APW 1.010/0.789 vs. CoST 0.911/0.655 vs. TS2Vec 0.863/0.651 vs. TNC **0.859/0.651**
  
  CoST+APW is ~17-33% worse in MSE than TS2Vec at these horizons. A similar degradation appears on Weather at horizons 336 and 720 (CoST+APW: 0.266, 0.299 MSE vs. TS2Vec: 0.231, 0.233 and TNC: 0.215, 0.219). These are not edge cases — they are systematic failures on the datasets most relevant to the paper's motivation. The headline claim of "consistently superior" results is simply not supported.

- **SDE values are never reported in Table 3, despite the paper claiming they are.** Section 4.4.4 states: *"Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance."* Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE columns — no SDE values appear anywhere in the table. The additional claim that APW achieves "consistently lower SDE values" is entirely unsubstantiated by any evidence beyond the synthetic Table 1 (which evaluates TS2Vec, not the proposed method). Since SDE is the paper's primary novel diagnostic, the failure to measure it on the proposed method and real data is a core evidentiary gap.

### Major

- **Missing ablation between MLP and APW.** The proposed CoST+APW bundles (a) CoST's base architecture, (b) an added MLP fusion layer (Section 4.4.2), and (c) SDE-based asymmetric loss weighting. The comparison in Table 3 is against vanilla CoST (no MLP, no APW). There is no row for "CoST + MLP without APW." Because the MLP adds parameters and a new training objective, any gains over vanilla CoST cannot be attributed to asymmetric weighting specifically. The paper's core claim — that SDE asymmetry is an operative corrective signal — is not isolated by any experiment.

- **SDE computation during training is underspecified.** The training loop requires decomposing each input, running three forward passes, computing Δ, and reweighting losses. The paper never states whether Δ is computed per batch, per epoch, or over a moving window; whether it uses the MLP composite embedding $g_\phi([v(a)\|v(b)])$ or the raw CoST encoder outputs; or how the moving MLP target affects the SDE computation stability. These gaps make reliable reproduction impossible.

- **Table 3 contains a label error.** Two distinct groups of rows are both labeled "ETT1." The first (horizons 24/48/168/336/720) matches hourly resolution. The second (horizons 24/48/96/288/672) matches minute-resolution horizons consistent with ETTm1. This mislabeling casts uncertainty over which dataset was actually used for the second group.

### Minor

- **Three different names for the SDE metric.** The title uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error," Section 3.2 calls it "Semantic Separability Error," and Sections 4.1 and 4.3 call it "Semantic Decomposition Error." The acronym SDE is consistent, but three names in one short paper indicates the manuscript was not coherently finalized.

- **Abstract contradicts method description.** The abstract states the approach "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes," yet Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP $g_\phi$ trained jointly with CoST. This is an architectural addition.

- **Hyperparameters γ and γ′ undisclosed.** Equation 3 introduces two scaling hyperparameters controlling the sensitivity of loss weighting to Δ. Given that Δ ranges from approximately −1.3 to +1.1 (Table 1), the choice of γ and γ′ has a large effect on the magnitude of loss adjustments. Their values are never reported in Section 4.1 or anywhere else.

- **No variance estimates.** No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported across any table. Many of the margins between methods are small (e.g., ETT2 horizon 720 MSE: 0.210, 0.214, 0.208, 0.207), making it unclear whether differences are meaningful.

### Trivial

- Bold formatting in Table 3 is applied inconsistently: at ETT1 horizon 24 (first group), three methods are bolded (0.04, 0.04, 0.039) despite TS2Vec's 0.039 being strictly lower.

## Nice-to-Haves

- The "future work" mentions examining whether APW remains effective with only low-pass filtering, without explicit frequency-domain encoders. However, the proposed method already uses LPF for decomposition in Section 4.3, while CoST provides the frequency-domain encoder backbone. Clarifying the distinction (e.g., replacing CoST with a simpler backbone) would help readers understand the intended direction.
- Reporting per-seed variance for at least one dataset would significantly strengthen confidence in reported margins.
- Analysis of *why* the method fails on Electricity at longer horizons would be more informative than hiding the result — understanding the failure mode would sharpen the contribution.

## Novel Insights

The asymmetry factor Δ as a directional diagnostic is the paper's most original idea. Table 1 provides compelling synthetic evidence that Δ tracks component suppression monotonically and changes sign as dominance switches — this is a clean, reproducible observation that could independently motivate future representation-analysis work, even if the current downstream application is insufficiently validated. The finding that direct SDE regularization hurts performance (Table 2) is also a non-obvious and informative negative result.

## Suggestions

1. **Close the SDE loop on real data**: Report SDE values for CoST, CoST+APW, TS2Vec, and TNC in Table 3. If APW actually reduces Δ on real datasets, showing that would be the paper's most important evidence. If it does not, that requires explanation.
2. **Add the missing ablation row**: CoST + MLP (no APW). This single row determines whether the asymmetric weighting mechanism does any work beyond what the MLP already achieves.
3. **Investigate and explain the Electricity degradation**: The method performs dramatically worse on the dataset most relevant to the paper's thesis at longer horizons. This needs to be explained and bounded, not omitted from the discussion.
4. **Fix the ETT1/ETTm1 label error** and report the correct dataset name for the second group.
5. **Disclose hyperparameter values (γ, γ′)** and provide at minimum a sensitivity table or range.
6. **Unify the metric's name** throughout: pick one (Semantic Disentanglement Error, Semantic Separability Error, or Semantic Decomposition Error) and use it consistently.
