## Summary

The paper proposes EDPA (Embedding Disruption Patch Attack), an adversarial patch attack targeting the latent representation space of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Unlike prior attacks (UADA, UPA), EDPA requires only the encoder parameters—no LVLM backbone access, action space knowledge, or manipulator-specific knowledge—making it applicable across diverse VLA architectures. The paper also proposes an adversarial fine-tuning scheme for the visual encoder as a defense. Experiments on the LIBERO benchmark against OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and π₀ demonstrate substantial attack effectiveness and partial defense recovery.

---

## Strengths

- **Applicability to multiple VLA architectures (Table 3):** Prior attacks (UADA, UPA) are constrained to OpenVLA due to model-specific requirements (action space structure, manipulator geometry). EDPA is evaluated against three architecturally distinct VLA models—OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and π₀—all achieving large failure-rate increases over the random-noise baseline (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT Goal: 5.1%→80.8%; π₀ Goal: 17.5%→44.3%). This is the paper's strongest and most distinctive contribution.

- **Strong attack effectiveness against single-camera VLA (Table 2):** EDPA drives OpenVLA's failure rate to 100% across all four LIBERO suites and outperforms the random noise baseline by 53.0% on average, confirming the optimization provides meaningful signal beyond random perturbation.

- **Defense maintains low clean-accuracy cost:** The adversarially fine-tuned encoder increases clean-condition failure rate by only ~1.6% on average across suites (e.g., Spatial: 14.1%→17.9%, Long: 48.1%→49.0%; Table 2), while substantially reducing EDPA-induced failure rates. The defense also transfers to UADA and UPA patches (−19.1% and −36.0% respectively).

- **Visualizations support the overfitting hypothesis (Figure 2):** Patches generated by EDPA for all three VLA models consistently exhibit structural patterns resembling robotic manipulators, providing visually coherent support for the hypothesis that VLA encoders overfit to manipulator appearances due to limited viewpoint diversity in training data.

- **Multi-camera evaluation (Table 3):** The paper evaluates EDPA under the practically realistic condition where wrist camera observations are also perturbed, demonstrating attack effectiveness even when extra visual context is available.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **"Model-agnostic" title and framing overstates the contribution.** Table 1 confirms that EDPA still requires encoder parameters, just as UADA and UPA do. What EDPA removes is the need for LVLM backbone access, action space knowledge, and manipulator knowledge—a real simplification. But a "model-agnostic" attack in the standard adversarial-ML sense would use a surrogate encoder and demonstrate zero-shot cross-model transfer without accessing the target's weights. The paper never experiments with this; each patch requires per-model encoder access and per-model optimization. The better description is "objective-simplified" or "knowledge-reduced." The title, abstract, and Section 2.2 all use "model-agnostic" in ways that readers will naturally interpret as transfer without target access—which is not demonstrated. This should be reframed accurately.

- **No ablation isolating the contribution of the alignment loss (Eq. 3) in the main paper.** The design story centers on two complementary objectives: patch contrastive loss (Eq. 2, weight α₁=0.8) and image-instruction alignment loss (Eq. 3, weight 1−α₁=0.2). The alignment loss accounts for only 20% of the objective and provides the paper's mechanistic claim—"disrupting semantic alignment between visual and textual representations." However, no experiment in the main text tests whether removing the alignment loss (α₁=1.0) would produce materially weaker patches. It is entirely possible that the contrastive loss alone achieves equivalent results, which would undermine both the dual-objective narrative and the language encoder access requirement. The paper references "Appendix C" for hyperparameter sensitivity but provides no ablation of the individual loss components. This leaves the design rationale without evidential support in the main body.

### Minor

- **Defense effectiveness is presented more optimistically than the numbers warrant for harder task suites.** The abstract states the defense "effectively mitigates this degradation" and Section 4.2 calls it "substantially reduced failure rates." These characterizations are accurate relative to the *attacked* baseline, but relative to *clean* performance, the defense is incomplete on the harder suites: LIBERO-Goal remains at 73.9% failure (vs. 26.9% clean) and LIBERO-Long at 91.2% (vs. 48.1% clean). Against UADA on Long, the defended model reaches 97.4% failure, barely below undefended 99.6%. The framing should acknowledge that the defense is partial, especially on complex tasks, rather than implying broad effectiveness.

- **Eq. 3 (image-instruction alignment loss) uses an unsigned absolute value, inconsistent with its stated motivation.** The loss is defined as |cos(p_i, w_j) − cos(p'_i, w_j)|, which is maximized equally whether the patch *reduces* or *increases* cosine similarity with the language tokens. The stated motivation is to "disrupt the semantic alignment," which is directional (reduce similarity), but the loss is symmetric. Combined with Eq. 2 (which already maximizes latent deviation), the net effect is likely disruptive regardless, but the formulation of Eq. 3 doesn't tightly correspond to its stated motivation.

- **High clean failure rates on harder tasks create unacknowledged ceiling effects.** OpenVLA on LIBERO-Long has a clean FR of 48.1%, and π₀ has 40.8%. These already-elevated baselines compress the attack headroom and affect the interpretation of attack strength on these suites. The paper does not discuss this confound in either Table 2 or Table 3 analysis.

- **The discussion in Section 5 attributing π₀'s superior robustness specifically to wrist camera pretraining is speculative without controlled evidence.** OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and π₀ differ on many dimensions (architecture, training data scale, multi-camera pretraining). The paper presents a hypothesis ("likely because…") but no controlled experiment. This is appropriate for a discussion section and is clearly labeled as a hypothesis, but the reasoning should be qualified more carefully.

None that meet the threshold.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Surrogate-encoder transfer experiment:** Generate a patch using a *publicly available* copy of the visual encoder (e.g., SigLIP-400m from HuggingFace, which OpenVLA uses) without accessing the fine-tuned VLA, then evaluate on the target VLA. If successful, this would dramatically strengthen the practicality argument and bring the method closer to the "model-agnostic" claim.

- **Two-cell ablation table (α₁=1.0 vs. α₁=0.8):** A minimal experiment directly testing whether the alignment loss contributes incrementally beyond the contrastive loss alone would validate the dual-objective design narrative at low cost.

- **Extend defense evaluation to OpenVLA-OFT:** Since OpenVLA-OFT shares OpenVLA's visual encoder backbone, the adversarially fine-tuned encoder could potentially be plugged in directly with minimal overhead, broadening the defense story significantly.

- **Acknowledge physical patch deployment constraints more explicitly:** Under real deployment, a physical sticker can shift in frame, change apparent scale with distance, or be partially occluded. The current limitation section (Section 6) focuses on multi-camera alignment and occlusion of task objects but omits these physical-placement stability considerations, which affect the "readily placeable within the camera's view" claim in the abstract.

## Novel Insights

The visualization finding (Figure 2) that patches generated by all three attacks, across all three VLA architectures, consistently converge to manipulator-resembling structures is a genuinely interesting emergent observation. It provides indirect evidence that VLA visual encoders develop strong priors toward manipulator appearance as a byproduct of the narrow viewpoint distribution in robotics datasets—a finding that could inform future work on data diversity as a defense mechanism. This insight is more impactful than a conventional adversarial robustness finding and deserves deeper investigation. Additionally, the observation that π₀'s multi-stage pretraining provides meaningful robustness compared to OpenVLA-OFT (which adds wrist-camera data only at fine-tuning) tentatively suggests a training-stage interaction effect worth exploring with controlled experiments.

## Suggestions

1. **Rename and reframe**: Replace "model-agnostic" with "encoder-only" or "knowledge-reduced" throughout the paper and align the abstract's contribution statement with what Table 1 actually shows—that EDPA reduces required knowledge (no LVLM, no action space, no manipulator knowledge) but still requires target encoder access.
2. **Add ablation table**: Include a two-row ablation (α₁=1.0 vs. α₁=0.8) in the main text to validate the alignment loss contribution.
3. **Revise defense language**: State explicitly in the abstract and Section 4.2 that the defense provides partial recovery—effective on simpler task suites, but substantially incomplete on Goal and Long suites.
4. **Add clean-baseline confound discussion**: Note in Table 3 analysis that π₀-Long and OpenVLA Long's elevated clean FRs compress attack headroom.
5. **Qualify Section 5 attribution**: Mark the wrist-camera pretraining explanation as an untested hypothesis requiring controlled experiments to validate.
