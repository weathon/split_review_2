## Summary
TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework built on SAM with two components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that iteratively samples from a ReLU-softmax distribution over an image-exemplar similarity map, suppressing already-visited regions, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that classifies segmented instances as positive or negative using the TS-SS geometric similarity metric applied to SAM's decoder output tokens. The key thesis is that output tokens, which fuse semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, provide more discriminable features for exemplar matching than raw image embeddings.

---

## Strengths

1. **Best MAE among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147.** Table 1 shows TokenCount achieves MAE 16.25, surpassing TFOC (19.95), C-Anything (27.97), and even SAM's raw baseline (42.48) — a substantial 18.5% MAE reduction over the closest SAM-only prior (TFOC).

2. **Output-token verification is meaningfully better than image-embedding verification.** Table 5 confirms this directly: output token space (MAE 16.25) vs. image embedding space (MAE 16.94) under identical probabilistic prompting. The PCA visualizations in Figure 5 qualitatively corroborate that output tokens form cleaner positive/negative clusters than image embeddings for the same point prompts.

3. **Ablation in Table 4 establishes credible sensitivity of the iterative sampling strategy.** Systematically varying iterations (8, 16, 32) and prompts per iteration (32, 64, 128) shows consistent MAE improvement with more prompts, validating that the adaptive scheme is a real driver of performance rather than an engineering detail.

4. **Competitive CARPK performance.** MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13 on CARPK outperforms all supervised methods listed and approaches the SAM+α TFCAC (4.39 MAE). The paper appropriately attributes this to SAM's strength with clear boundaries and consistent viewpoints, and correctly positions TFCAC as the state-of-the-art.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Abstract overclaims in a directly contradicted way.** The abstract states the method achieves "superior accuracy… outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is plainly false per the paper's own Table 1: LOCA (MAE 10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62), and TFCAC (12.26) all outperform the proposed method. The accurate claim — best among SAM-only training-free methods, and competitive with SAM+α training-free methods — is well-supported and would be a legitimate contribution, but the current wording will mislead readers and reviewers alike.

- **Misleading bolding of RMSE in Table 1.** The paper bolds all its metrics including RMSE = 133.11 in the "SAM only" section, but C-Anything's RMSE of 131.24 is lower. The method does not achieve best RMSE even within the SAM-only group. The bolding convention suggests the authors' results are best across the board, which is not the case for this metric.

- **CARPK exemplar protocol is non-standard and may compromise the fairness of comparison.** Section 4.1 explicitly states: "we randomly selected 12 objects from the training set to use as exemplars." Standard few-shot counting benchmarks (FamNet, BMNet+, SAFECOUNT, etc.) use 3 exemplar bounding boxes drawn from within the test image itself. The proposed method receives 4× as many exemplars, sourced from a different distribution (training set). Since both the probabilistic prompt generation stage (which computes exemplar-image similarity to seed sampling) and the output-token verification stage (which computes similarity to exemplar tokens) are directly sensitive to exemplar quality and quantity, this protocol difference is a confound. The paper's strongest empirical claim — outperforming all supervised methods on CARPK — cannot be cleanly evaluated without knowing how performance varies with exemplar count or whether a matched-exemplar comparison would hold.

### Minor

- **The contribution of probabilistic prompt generation is not isolated from output-token verification.** Table 5 ablates verification space (output token vs. image embedding) for fixed prompting. Table 4 ablates sampling density. But there is no experiment that holds the verification stage fixed and substitutes uniform grid prompts for the probabilistic scheme. The paper argues in Section 2.2 that grid and superpixel approaches are inadequate, but this is never demonstrated empirically in a direct head-to-head under matched verification conditions. Without this, the marginal contribution of probabilistic prompt generation to the MAE improvement cannot be attributed.

- **TS-SS advantage over cosine similarity is marginal (0.23 MAE).** Table 3 shows TS-SS (16.25) vs. cosine similarity (16.48). The theoretical argument in Section 3.2 — that cosine/L2 "have reached a performance plateau" and a hybrid metric is necessary — is not well-supported by a difference of 0.23 on this benchmark. The theoretical motivation for applying a document-clustering metric (from Heidarian & Dinneen, 2016) to SAM decoder tokens is plausible but would benefit from a more rigorous empirical justification, such as showing correlation between token geometric properties and counting improvement across different difficulty tiers.

- **Token verification threshold (300) and temperature scale (τ = 0.066) have no sensitivity analysis.** These are critical hyperparameters that directly determine which instances are counted. The paper states they are set "based on validation experiments" but provides neither validation curves nor sensitivity analysis. Given that the threshold directly governs positive/negative classification, a reader cannot assess how robust the method is to this choice.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A stratified breakdown of FSC-147 results by object density or image difficulty would help characterize when the output-token approach succeeds or fails — the paper acknowledges degraded performance on "dense distributions of very small instances" in Section 5, but does not quantify how much of the RMSE gap vs. methods like LOCA or TFCAC comes from this regime.
- A direct ablation of "grid prompts + TS-SS output token verification" vs. "probabilistic prompts + TS-SS output token verification" would cleanly attribute credit between the two proposed components and would strengthen the narrative that both contribute independently.
- Providing a CARPK result using 3 in-image exemplars (even as an additional row in Table 1) would either resolve the comparability concern outright or helpfully bound the effect of the non-standard protocol.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*

- **Harsh Critic: "Section 3.1 uses cosine similarity to seed the distribution, but calls cosine similarity unreliable for verification — a contradiction."** Removed. This is not a contradiction. The probabilistic prompt stage uses cosine similarity on image embeddings to determine where to sample prompts. The verification stage applies TS-SS to output tokens, which are a different representation altogether (decoder-output fused tokens, not raw image embeddings). There is no inconsistency — the two stages operate on different feature spaces with different metrics for different purposes.

- **Harsh Critic: "Statistical variance over the FSC-147 test set should be reported."** Removed as scope creep. Single-run evaluation without confidence intervals is the norm for this benchmark and community, and requiring it would be imposing a standard inconsistent with the field.

- **Strength Finder: "Achieves state-of-the-art training-free counting on two benchmarks."** Partially removed. On FSC-147 it is best SAM-only, not overall training-free (TFCAC is better). On CARPK the comparison is confounded by the exemplar protocol. Kept in reduced form above.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel observation is that SAM's decoder output tokens — which fuse spatial and semantic information through bidirectional cross-attention — constitute a more discriminable feature space for exemplar matching than the encoder image embeddings used by all prior SAM-based counting methods. This insight is mechanistically grounded (Sections 3.2 and Figure 1), supported quantitatively by Table 5, and qualitatively by Figure 5. The idea of treating exemplar counting as a clustering problem in token space (rather than in image embedding space) is a meaningful conceptual reframing that could generalize beyond SAM to other decoder-based foundation models. The probabilistic prompt generation mechanism, while not fully isolated in ablation, provides a principled way to handle the localization bias of SAM's output tokens.

---

## Suggestions

1. Rewrite the abstract to accurately reflect the contribution: best SAM-only training-free MAE on FSC-147, competitive with (but not uniformly superior to) SAM+α methods.
2. Fix the bolding in Table 1 for RMSE on FSC-147 — C-Anything (131.24) is better; the bolding implies the authors' result is best, which is incorrect.
3. Add a CARPK experiment using 3 in-image exemplars, or at minimum add a sensitivity analysis varying exemplar count from 3 to 12 to characterize the effect of the non-standard protocol.
4. Add one ablation row: deterministic grid prompts + TS-SS output token verification, to disentangle the two proposed components.
5. Add a hyperparameter sensitivity figure for the verification threshold (300) across a range, even just ±50%.

## Score and Decision

**Originality (3/5):** The insight about output tokens as verification features is genuinely new in the counting context; the TS-SS metric and probabilistic sampling are adapted from prior work.

**Importance (3/5):** Training-free counting is a meaningful research direction, and the token-verification insight is transferable. However, the gaps vs. TFCAC and training-based methods remain large.

**Claims Supported (2/5):** The abstract and presentation overreach the evidence in non-trivial ways (RMSE bolding, "outperforms all training-based and training-free" claim). The CARPK result is the paper's strongest claim but rests on a non-standard protocol.

**Soundness (3/5):** The core method is sound; the ablations are mostly complete but miss the grid-vs-probabilistic comparison. The TS-SS gain is marginal.

**Clarity (3/5):** Writing is generally clear; the table presentation is misleading in places (bolding, abstract phrasing).

**Community Value (3/5):** The output-token insight is a useful contribution to the SAM-for-counting community and could inspire similar work on other foundation models.

The paper has a real core contribution (best SAM-only MAE on FSC-147, a novel and mechanistically justified use of output tokens) but is held back by overclaiming in the abstract, misleading result presentation (RMSE bolding), a non-standard CARPK protocol that undermines its headline claim, and an incomplete ablation. These issues are correctable in revision, but as submitted the evidentiary support for the claims as stated is insufficient.
