## Summary

HASTE is a pipeline framework for code context retrieval under LLM token-budget constraints. It combines AST-aware chunking, hybrid BM25+semantic retrieval via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and call-graph expansion, with the goal of providing LLMs context that is simultaneously token-efficient and structurally coherent. The evaluation uses an LLM-as-judge framework on six curated Python files and twelve SWE-PolyBench instances.

---

## Strengths

- **Coherent architectural design:** The pipeline integrates AST-aware chunking (Section 3.1), hybrid retrieval with RRF (Section 3.3), and call-graph expansion into a unified framework. Each module is motivated and the interplay between them is well-described.
- **Three complementary evaluation metrics:** The paper defines LLM-as-Judge scores, AST Fidelity, and Hallucination Rate (Section 4.2), which collectively target semantic quality, structural integrity, and output noisiness — appropriate for the paper's thesis about the importance of structural coherence.
- **Honest disclosure of failure modes:** Section 5.3 explicitly reports the four low-scoring SWE-PolyBench instances (scores 10, 10, 5, 0), discusses the specific failure modes (task misinterpretation, flawed suggestions), and acknowledges that HASTE cannot fix incorrect upstream suggestions. This transparency is commendable.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Baseline comparisons defined but never executed.** Section 4.1.3 defines three baseline conditions: IR-only retrieval, AST-only retrieval, and naïve truncation. RQ1 is literally framed as "compared to baseline methods." Yet Table 2, Figure 2, and Figure 3 report only HASTE scores — not a single result comparing HASTE to any of these baselines appears anywhere in the paper. The abstract claims HASTE achieves results "significantly improving the success rate of automated code edits," which is a comparative claim, but there is no comparison. The central scientific contribution — that the hybrid, AST-guided approach occupies a better compression-quality trade-off frontier than simpler alternatives — is entirely unsubstantiated. This is not a gap addressable by additional future experiments; the paper as submitted contains no evidence for its primary thesis.

- **A reference is explicitly labeled a fabricated placeholder.** The reference list includes: *"Ziyao Zhang et al. LLM hallucinations in practical code generation... (Placeholder citation for illustrative purposes)."* This citation is actively invoked in Section 2.4 as empirical motivation for HASTE's hallucination-reduction claim ("Zhang et al. [Zhang et al., 2025] identified incomplete or conflicting context as a primary driver of hallucinations"). Using a self-acknowledged fabricated reference as motivation for a core design claim is a scholarly integrity issue that directly weakens Section 2.4.

### Major

- **Evaluation scale too small to support statistical claims.** The curated-dataset analysis (RQ2) covers exactly six Python files. The Pearson correlation r = −0.97 reported in Section 5.2 as a "strong relationship" is computed from these six points, five of which are clustered at compression ratios of 1.2–2.7× with scores of 98–100, and one outlier (test3.py) at 6.8×. A correlation computed from n=6 with a single lever point is not statistically meaningful and cannot support the "trade-off frontier" narrative in Section 5.2.

- **SWE-PolyBench results are misleadingly presented.** Seven of the twelve instances are "POLYBENCH-NOOP" tasks where the goal is merely to produce a non-empty, functionally unchanged patch (e.g., adding a comment). These achieve automatic perfect scores regardless of context quality. The five non-trivial instances yield scores of 95, 10, 10, 5, and 0 — a bimodal distribution with three near-failure scores. Reporting an aggregate over all twelve instances presents a far more favorable picture than the substantive results warrant.

### Minor

- **The judge LLM is never identified.** Section 4.1.4 identifies Gemini 1.5 Flash as the *editor* LLM, but Section 4.2.1 states only that "a general-purpose LLM" serves as judge. Since the primary metric is judge scores, the identity of the judge, its prompt, and any inter-rater validation are necessary for reproducibility.

- **The automatic task generator (Section 4.1.2) is undescribed.** The curated tasks are generated by the system's own "Suggestion Generator," but the paper provides no description of what this component does, what prompts it uses, or how task difficulty is controlled. Since these tasks also serve as retrieval queries, there is a potential self-consistency bias that cannot be assessed without this information.

### Trivial

- **Anonymity inconsistency:** The Data Availability section states compliance with double-anonymous review but also identifies the exact PyPI package name ("HasteContext"), which is searchable. This is a submission policy concern rather than a scientific one.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- The compression-quality trade-off analysis could be made genuinely informative by varying the token budget *intentionally* across the same set of files at multiple compression levels, rather than observing it post hoc across unrelated files. This would give the trade-off curve a controlled experimental basis.
- Cross-file analysis via a repository-wide call graph is mentioned in Section 6 as future work; including even preliminary results on this would significantly strengthen the claim of practical scalability.
- Restricting the SWE-PolyBench primary analysis to substantive (non-NOOP) instances and reporting NOOP results separately would give a clearer picture of HASTE's performance on tasks that actually require contextual understanding.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **Harsh Critic — "The circuit is fully LLM-generated with no external ground truth":** While the curated tasks are LLM-generated, the SWE-PolyBench tasks are provided by an external benchmark, and the paper uses standard LLM-as-judge methodology. The concern about task simplicity is already captured more specifically under the NOOP inflation weakness above. The generic "no ground truth" framing is not a distinct, actionable criticism.

- **Harsh Critic — Observability section inflates the paper:** Section 3.4 is a brief two-bullet description of metrics and logs. While it is primarily engineering infrastructure, it is not misleadingly padded; it occupies minimal space and is relevant to the framework description. Not a meaningful criticism.

- **Harsh Critic — "Section 5.1: qualitative case analysis, not evidence":** The claim about test3.py and the dependent class definition (Section 5.1) is presented as a supporting illustration, not as the primary evidence. Calling it non-evidence is too strict; it is appropriate supplementary analysis. Already subsumed into the observation that baseline comparisons are absent.

- **Strength Finder — "Empirical demonstration on diverse, realistic tasks":** Conflicts directly with the verified weakness about evaluation scale and NOOP inflation. Removed per the rule that weaknesses defeat strengths on the same dimension.

- **Strength Finder — "Quantification of the compression-quality trade-off (r = -0.97)":** Conflicts with the verified weakness that this correlation is statistically meaningless at n=6. Removed.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The architectural combination of hybrid IR with AST-guided selection and call-graph expansion is a reasonable engineering synthesis, but the review process has not surfaced any insight about this design that the paper itself does not contain — and the missing baselines mean the key design question (does the hybrid outperform its components?) remains entirely open.

---

## Suggestions

1. **Run the three already-defined baselines on the same six curated files and twelve SWE-PolyBench instances, then report judge scores and compression ratios side-by-side.** This is the single change that would make the paper's central claim evaluable. The experimental infrastructure already exists; the baselines are already defined.
2. **Replace the placeholder Zhang et al. citation** with a real reference or revise Section 2.4 to not rely on it.
3. **Report SWE-PolyBench NOOP and non-NOOP results in separate tables** to avoid conflating trivial and substantive task performance.
4. **Identify the judge LLM and its prompt** in Section 4.2.1 to make the primary metric reproducible.
5. **Expand the curated dataset** — even to 20–30 files across different sizes and domains — before resubmission, to give the correlation analysis and ablations meaningful statistical support.

## Score and Decision

**Originality:** The idea of combining AST chunking, hybrid BM25+semantic retrieval, and call-graph expansion is well-motivated but synthesizes existing techniques rather than introducing algorithmic novelty. The pipeline design is competent, not pioneering.

**Importance of research question:** Genuine and pressing — context window management for code LLMs is a real, active problem.

**Claims well supported:** The paper's central comparative claims (HASTE outperforms baselines; significantly improves success rate) are entirely unsupported. The only supported claim is that HASTE alone achieves high scores on easy tasks, which does not validate the system's relative merits.

**Soundness of experiments:** The evaluation is too small to be scientifically meaningful (n=6 for RQ2, 5 non-trivial SWE-PolyBench instances for RQ1), uses an unidentified judge, and omits the baselines that would have made the results interpretable.

**Clarity of writing:** The paper is clearly written and the architecture is well-described.

**Value to research community:** As currently submitted, limited — the system may have engineering value, but the paper does not establish it scientifically. The missing baselines, tiny evaluation, and placeholder citation prevent the community from learning whether the design choices actually matter.

The paper has a coherent thesis and reasonable system design, but the evaluation does not test the thesis and one citation is explicitly fabricated. These are not presentation gaps; they are substantive absences that prevent any scientific conclusion.
