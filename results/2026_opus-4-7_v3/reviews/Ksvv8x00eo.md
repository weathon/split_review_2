## Summary
CaTS-Bench introduces the first large-scale, multimodal benchmark for context-aware time series captioning, curated from 11 real-world domains with ~20k samples (~570k timesteps). Each sample includes a numeric time series segment, contextual metadata, a line-chart image, and a reference caption generated by Gemini 2.0 Flash. Beyond captioning, it provides 460 multiple-choice Q&A questions and two novel numeric fidelity metrics. Comprehensive experiments on leading VLMs reveal that finetuning substantially improves performance and — most strikingly — that VLMs extract essentially no useful information from time series plot images.

## Strengths

- **Fills a clearly documented benchmark gap (Table 1, Section 2).** The paper concretely demonstrates via Table 1 that no prior TSC benchmark combines numeric series, expressive captions, rich metadata, and visual modalities. TRUCE and TACO lack metadata; TADACap is visual-only and pattern-constrained. This gap is genuine and well-evidenced.

- **Thorough quality validation of semi-synthetic data (Section 3.2).** Three complementary studies — manual checking of ~2.9k captions with >98.6% accuracy (Table 9), a 35-person human indistinguishability study at 41.1% detection (near-chance), and embedding-based diversity analysis showing only 2.3% near-duplicate pairs (Table 13) — provide above-average validation rigor for a benchmark relying on LLM-generated references.

- **Visual modality ablation is a concrete, actionable finding (Section 4.3, Figure 4).** The result that removing line-plot images causes negligible or positive performance changes across nearly all tested models, corroborated by attention map analysis showing models attend primarily to text elements (axis labels, titles) rather than line trends, is a well-supported empirical contribution that informs future VLM design.

- **Tailored numeric fidelity metrics address a real evaluation gap (Section 3.5).** Statistical Inference Accuracy (measuring hallucination of statistics) and Numeric Score (penalizing omission of numeric details) are well-motivated: standard BLEU/ROUGE-L cannot distinguish whether a caption correctly reports the mean of a series. The design choices (e.g., not penalizing omission in SIA, weighting recall over accuracy in Numeric Score) reflect thoughtful metric engineering.

- **Temporal train/test split prevents subtle leakage (Section 3.1).** Using the first 80% of each source dataset for training and the last 20% for testing is methodologically sound for temporally correlated data.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major

- **Oracle-as-ground-truth circularity insufficiently mitigated (Section 3.1, Tables 3–4).** The primary ground truth is generated by Gemini 2.0 Flash, which is also evaluated as a baseline. On surface-level metrics, Gemini 2.0 Flash shows a notable advantage: BLEU 0.137 vs GPT-4o's 0.112 and its own successor Gemini 2.5 Pro's 0.088 (Table 3, SS column), while on semantic metrics the gap is smaller (SimCSE: 0.858 vs 0.865 for GPT-4o). This pattern is consistent with stylistic alignment inflating n-gram overlap. The human-revisited (HR) subset — the paper's main debiasing mechanism — covers only 579 of ~4,000 test samples (14.5%) across 4 of 11 domains (Crime, Demography, Walmart, Agriculture), excluding the three largest domains (Air Quality: 886, COVID: 1.1k, Border Crossing: 646 ≈ 66% of test data). The paraphrasing robustness check (Spearman 0.9266) demonstrates ranking stability but cannot address absolute score inflation. The paper acknowledges "time series captioning lacks inherent ground truth" but does not directly confront the oracle-bias problem in evaluation.

- **Novel metrics lack human-correlation validation (Section 3.5).** The paper introduces Statistical Inference Accuracy and Numeric Score as key contributions for TSC evaluation, but provides no evidence that higher scores on these metrics correspond to captions humans judge as more accurate or informative. For a benchmark paper proposing new metrics as a core contribution, human-metric correlation is important validation. Without it, the metrics' construct validity is assumed rather than demonstrated.

### Minor

- **Q&A adversarial filtering uses a single-model gate (Section 3.4).** Filtering by removing questions correctly answered by Qwen 2.5 Omni may bias the test set toward that model's specific failure modes. The paper claims Appendix J.2 verifies this produces "genuinely harder questions, rather than reflecting Qwen-specific weaknesses only," which partially addresses the concern, but the main text does not describe the verification methodology. Since the Q&A suite is supplementary to the main captioning benchmark, this is bounded in impact.

- **Low statistical power for binary comparison sub-tasks (Section 3.4).** With only 40 binary-choice questions each for amplitude, peak, mean, and variance comparisons, random chance is 50%. A model could appear 10–15% above chance by luck, making individual sub-task results unreliable without confidence intervals or significance tests.

- **Numeric Score parameters lack sensitivity analysis (Section 3.5).** The weighting λ_A = 0.3, λ_R = 0.7 and 5% tolerance threshold are presented without analysis of how model rankings change under alternative parameterizations. If small perturbations to these values substantially reorder models, the benchmark's conclusions on numeric fidelity become fragile.

- **Finetuned QwenVL anomaly unexplained (Table 3).** Finetuned QwenVL is the only model where HR scores systematically exceed SS scores across all metrics (e.g., BLEU 0.126 HR vs 0.082 SS; ROUGE-L 0.302 HR vs 0.249 SS), the opposite of every other model. Given that the HR subset covers only 4 specific domains and QwenVL was finetuned on Gemini-generated SS training data, this anomaly may reflect a domain-composition artifact rather than genuine performance, but it is not discussed.

### Trivial
None

## Nice-to-Haves

- Expand the HR subset to cover all 11 domains and at least ~1,500 samples to serve as a robust debiasing mechanism against oracle circularity.
- Conduct a human correlation study for Statistical Inference Accuracy and Numeric Score.
- Replace Q&A single-model adversarial filtering with a consensus-based difficulty measure (e.g., questions that ≥N of M diverse models answer incorrectly).
- Add a difficulty breakdown by time series characteristics (length, volatility, trend complexity) to complement the current domain-aggregated results.
- Report bootstrap confidence intervals for the 40-question binary sub-tasks.
- Discuss benchmark evolution strategy as the oracle model (Gemini 2.0 Flash) becomes outdated.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **"Multimodal design is currently non-discriminative" (framed as Critical Issue 3 in input review).** The reviewer argued that because no existing model leverages the visual modality, the benchmark's multimodal framing is "aspirational rather than operative." However, the paper explicitly addresses this in Section 4.3: "the under-utilization of visual inputs observed in our experiments is not a limitation of CaTS-Bench itself, but rather a reflection of current VLM capabilities." The benchmark includes visual data for future models; the current finding is itself a contribution. Removed as the paper already addresses this concern, and the visual data's presence is forward-looking by design.

- **"Abstract misleading about timestamps vs samples."** The abstract says "roughly 465k training and 105k test timestamps" while the benchmark has ~16k/4k samples. The paper uses "timestamps" accurately — each sample contains multiple timesteps. This is a clarity nitpick at most, not a substantive concern.

- **"Domain distribution skew (Air Quality dominance)."** While Air Quality contributes 286M of 287M source timesteps, the sample count is more balanced (4.4k of 20k = 22%), and the paper uses macro-averaging to mitigate imbalance. The skew in source timesteps reflects the original data size but does not directly bias evaluation.

- **"Finetuning fails to improve Q&A accuracy — not investigated further."** This was raised as a missed analysis opportunity, but the paper does note "task misalignment and catastrophic forgetting" as likely causes (Section 4.2) and the Q&A suite is supplementary. This is scope creep rather than a substantive flaw.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most distinctive empirical finding is that VLMs extract essentially no information from time series line plots during captioning — confirmed quantitatively (Figure 4 shows negligible or negative Δ when removing images) and qualitatively (attention maps concentrate on text elements like axis labels rather than line trends). This extends beyond a single-benchmark observation: it reveals that current VLM architectures fundamentally lack fine-grained visual reasoning over numeric patterns encoded in plots. The finding that even alternative visual representations (Gramian Angular Fields, recurrence plots — mentioned in Appendix I.3) fail to trigger visual reasoning strengthens this conclusion. Combined with the near-random plot-matching performance across all models (Section 4.2), this provides strong evidence of a systematic capability gap rather than a benchmark-specific artifact.

## Suggestions

- **Priority 1:** Expand the HR subset to all 11 domains. This is the single most impactful improvement — it directly addresses the oracle-circularity concern, the paper's main methodological limitation.
- **Priority 2:** Validate the novel metrics against human judgments. Even a small-scale study (e.g., 200 caption pairs rated by annotators) showing correlation between Numeric Score and human-perceived accuracy would substantially strengthen the metrics' credibility.
- **Priority 3:** Explicitly quantify oracle style bias by computing per-model stylistic similarity to the reference (sentence-length distribution, vocabulary overlap, structural template usage) and correlating with evaluation scores. This would turn a latent concern into a bounded, characterized one.
- **Priority 4:** Report confidence intervals for all Q&A sub-task results, particularly the 40-question binary comparisons.
