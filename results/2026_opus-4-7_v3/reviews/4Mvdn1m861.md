## Summary
TokenCount proposes a training-free class-agnostic object counting framework built entirely on SAM, with two key components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that iteratively samples prompts from a similarity-derived probability distribution, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that leverages SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method is evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK benchmarks.

## Strengths
- **Using SAM's output tokens for verification is a genuinely novel idea.** The insight that decoder output tokens encode both semantic and positional information through bidirectional cross-attention—making them more discriminative than raw image embeddings—is well-motivated. Table 5 confirms the advantage: output tokens achieve MAE 16.25 vs. image embeddings at 16.94 on FSC-147. The PCA visualization in Figure 5 provides supporting visual evidence that output tokens form more separable clusters between positive and negative instances.

- **The iterative probabilistic prompt generation is a well-designed mechanism** (Section 3.1, Figure 3). Zeroing out probability for verified positives and decaying probability for verified negatives prevents redundant prompting and systematically expands spatial coverage. The early-stopping criterion (IoU > 0.95 between successive mask unions, patience = 5) is practical. Table 4 confirms the contribution with clear performance trends across iteration and prompt counts.

- **Strong CARPK results.** MAE of 4.68 and RMSE of 6.13 using only SAM with no training or auxiliary models outperforms all supervised methods (SAFECOUNT 5.33, BMNet+ 5.76, LOCA 9.97) and is close to TFCAC (4.39) which uses an additional DINO encoder. The paper honestly acknowledges TFCAC's superiority on CARPK while noting the inference time advantage (Section 4.2).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **RMSE on FSC-147 undermines the headline MAE claim.** The method's RMSE of 133.11 (Table 1) is among the worst in the entire comparison—comparable to vanilla SAM (137.50), TFOC (132.16), and far worse than LOCA (56.97), SAFECOUNT (85.54), or TFCAC (56.33). High MAE improvement paired with near-worst RMSE indicates the method fails catastrophically on a subset of images. Section 5 briefly mentions "performance degradation in scenarios with dense distributions of very small instances" but never connects this to the RMSE disparity or provides any quantitative failure analysis. For a counting method, unpredictable catastrophic outliers are a serious practical limitation.

- **Abstract overclaims relative to actual results.** The abstract states: "our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." Table 1 directly contradicts this: LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62), and TFCAC (12.26) all achieve lower MAE on FSC-147. The method only outperforms one training-based method (Counting-DETR at 16.79). The body text in Section 4.2 frames this honestly ("best performance among training-free SAM only methods"), but the abstract's framing is structurally misleading for the paper's central claim.

- **No variance reported for a stochastic method.** The probabilistic prompt generation samples from a multinomial distribution (Section 3.1), making every run non-deterministic. All reported results are single numbers with no standard deviation, confidence interval, or number of runs. Key claims rest on small MAE differences (e.g., 0.23 for TS-SS vs. cosine similarity in Table 3), making it impossible to assess statistical significance.

### Minor

- **TS-SS improvement is empirically marginal.** Table 3 shows cosine similarity achieves MAE 16.48 vs. TS-SS at 16.25—a 0.23 difference. The paper draws strong geometric conclusions from this (Section 4.4: "non-hybrid metrics have reached a performance plateau"), but this conclusion cannot be drawn from differences this small without variance estimates. The larger gap from dot product (19.58) to TS-SS (16.25) is more meaningful but is primarily driven by cosine's angular alignment, not the TS-SS hybrid design.

- **Key hyperparameters not ablated.** The temperature τ = 0.066 and the TS-SS verification threshold of 300 are stated in implementation details (Section 4.1) but never ablated. Given that τ controls the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in prompt sampling and the threshold determines positive/negative classification, sensitivity analysis would strengthen confidence in the method's robustness.

### Trivial
None.

- A quantitative runtime/FLOPs comparison table against baselines rather than the qualitative "more than twice as fast" claim would strengthen the efficiency argument.
- A geometric analysis testing whether token magnitude correlates with spatial position and angle with semantics would ground the TS-SS motivation beyond the current assertion.

## Novel Insights
The paper's central insight—that SAM's decoder output tokens, refined through bidirectional cross-attention with both prompt and image embeddings, can serve as more discriminative verification features than raw image embeddings for counting—is genuinely novel within the training-free counting literature. The PCA analysis (Figure 5) and ablation (Table 5) provide initial evidence for this claim. The iterative probability-based prompt generation with decay/zeroing for explored regions is a practical contribution to prompt engineering for foundation models. However, the paper does not fully explore why this advantage does not translate into RMSE improvement, leaving the most interesting question—under what conditions do output tokens fail—unanswered.

## Suggestions
- **Report variance across multiple random seeds** — essential for a stochastic method, and would immediately clarify whether TS-SS vs. cosine and other small differences are meaningful.
- **Rewrite the abstract** to accurately reflect results: "best among SAM-only training-free methods" rather than "outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods."
- **Add failure analysis** connecting high RMSE to specific image characteristics (dense small objects as hinted in Section 5) with quantitative breakdowns.
- **Ablate τ and the TS-SS threshold** to assess sensitivity of the two key hyperparameters.
- **Consider analyzing per-category or per-density performance** on FSC-147 to identify where output tokens succeed vs. fail relative to image embeddings.
