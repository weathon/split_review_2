## Summary
This paper introduces the problem of *reliability scoring*: assessing how faithfully a reported dataset reflects unobservable ground truth, using only auxiliary observations generated by an unknown statistical experiment. The authors propose the Gram determinant score—det(Q⊤P⊤PQ)—and prove it preserves exact match, Blackwell, and approximate dist orderings, while establishing that it is the unique experiment-agnostic score up to scaling. Impossibility results tightly characterize the boundary of what any score can achieve for two of three orderings.

## Strengths

- **Elegant core decomposition with structural insight.** The factorization Γ(PQ) = det(P⊤P) · det(Q)² (Eq. 4 and line 191) cleanly decouples the unknown experiment P from the misreport structure Q. This makes experiment-agnosticism a *structural consequence* of the determinant's multiplicative property, not an approximation. The parallelepiped volume interpretation (Figure 1, Section 4) provides genuine geometric intuition: misreporting shrinks volume because columns of PQ become convex combinations of columns of PQ_x.

- **Tight impossibility–possibility pairing for exact match and Blackwell orderings.** Proposition 3.1 shows no score preserves exact match ordering on any superset of Q_nonperm, and Theorem 4.2 part 1 achieves preservation exactly on Q_nonperm. Similarly, Blackwell ordering impossibility (single linearly dependent experiment suffices to break it) matches the P_indep requirement. These two pairings are completely tight, which is uncommon and impressive.

- **Uniqueness result (Proposition 4.3).** The characterization that the Gram determinant is, up to scaling by α·det(Q⊤Q)^β, the *unique* continuous, scale-covariant, experiment-agnostic score is a strong structural result. It elevates the score from "one plausible choice" to "the essentially forced choice" within a well-defined function class.

- **Well-structured problem formulation.** The model of true data x, reported data x̂, and auxiliary observations y (Section 2.1) is simple and general. The hierarchy of reliability orderings (exact match → Blackwell → Hamming/dist) with the refinement proposition (Proposition 2.1) and the chain of set inclusions Q_{L,δ} ⊆ Q_L ⊂ Q_dom ⊂ Q_reg ⊂ Q_nonperm (line 139) makes the theoretical landscape transparent.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major
- **The dist-ordering guarantee is far weaker than the "nearly tight" framing suggests.** The paper claims the conditions for preserving reliability orderings are "nearly tight" (line 22) and "nearly matching our impossibility results" (line 187). This is accurate for exact match and Blackwell orderings, which are genuinely tight. However, for the dist/Hamming ordering—arguably the most practically interpretable—there is a substantial gap: the impossibility is on Q_dom (Proposition 3.1 part 3), but Theorem 4.2 part 3 requires Q_{L, 1/(64L²d²)}. For d=10 and L=2, this caps tolerable corruption at ~N/25600 (~0.004% of entries). The preserved ordering is only (1/4LΔ)-dist, meaning one dataset must have 4LΔ times less error than another to be ranked higher. With L=2 and Δ=5 (a moderate non-Hamming distance), this is a 40× factor. The paper should clearly distinguish which of its three orderings have tight results and which do not, rather than characterizing all three collectively as "nearly tight."

### Minor
- **Experiments are illustrative rather than evaluative: no competing scores.** Despite the related work (Section 1.1) discussing f-divergences, determinant-based scores (Zou & Adams, 2012; Xu et al., 2019), Shannon mutual information (Zheng et al., 2025), and data Shapley, none are compared against empirically. Even a simple baseline such as the trace of the estimated Gram matrix or mutual information between x̂ and y would help calibrate the practical advantage of using the determinant. The uniqueness result guarantees theoretical superiority within its function class, but empirical demonstration that this translates to practical gains would strengthen the contribution.

- **Experiment 3 (employment data) is too small to provide meaningful validation.** With N=209 and d=4, the Gram matrix is 4×4 and estimated from very few samples per cell. Figure 3d shows three bars in the expected order but with no error bars, no significance test, and no comparison to alternatives. While this is a valuable real-world illustration, it should not be presented as evidence that the method "effectively captures data quality" (abstract, line 9).

- **The uniqueness result's function class should be stated more prominently.** Proposition 4.3 requires continuity on GL_d and scale-covariance S(tQ) = c(t)S(Q). Alternative experiment-agnostic scores outside this class (e.g., based on condition number of Q) are conceivable. The paper should emphasize that uniqueness holds *within this natural but constrained class*, not among all possible experiment-agnostic scores.

- **Single-source, finite-X model is more restrictive than the motivating examples.** The model assumes X = [d] (finite, line 45) and a single reporting agent. Several motivating examples (insurance telemetrics, investor positions, COVID case counts) involve continuous-valued data and/or multiple sources. The kernel extension (Section 4.3) handles continuous Y but not continuous X. For a paper framing itself as "initiating the study" of reliability scoring (line 17), explicitly acknowledging this boundary and sketching potential extensions would strengthen the contribution.

## Novel Insights
The central novel insight is the multiplicative decomposition Γ(PQ) = det(P⊤P)·det(Q)², which makes experiment-agnosticism a *structural* property rather than an approximation. Combined with the uniqueness characterization and the systematic impossibility–possibility mapping, this establishes that reliability scoring without ground truth is a well-posed problem with a canonical solution—at least for the exact match and Blackwell orderings. The geometric interpretation via parallelepiped volume provides intuition that could inspire extensions to other scoring problems in information elicitation and data valuation.
