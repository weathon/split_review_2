## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) through disentangled note-level score content and global performance style representations. It introduces a diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module that generates style embeddings from score content alone, and formulates EPR as a Seq2Seq task that eliminates the need for note-level alignment. Evaluation includes objective metrics, subjective listening tests, and disentanglement analyses on ASAP and ATEPP datasets.

## Strengths
- **Alignment-free Seq2Seq EPR is a meaningful practical contribution.** Most prior EPR methods (Jeong et al., 2019; Rhyu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024) require fine-grained note-aligned data, which is expensive and fragile for expressive passages. Tables 2–3 show competitive or better results relative to these alignment-dependent baselines, supporting the viability of relaxed supervision. This directly lowers the data barrier for training EPR systems.

- **The PSR module addresses a genuine usability gap.** Most EPR systems require a reference performance or manual parameter control. Modeling style generation as conditional diffusion over a learned style space (Section 3.4) is a natural fit for the one-to-many nature of the problem. Figure 4 provides concrete evidence that PSR-generated styles recover the era-level clustering structure of actual performance styles, with similar centroid locations and separation.

- **Multifaceted evaluation with statistical rigor.** The paper combines APT metrics (MUSTER, ScoreSimilarity), EPR statistics (variance, KL, MAE), accuracy metrics (alignment/insertion/missing rates), subjective listening tests, and downstream classification experiments. Statistical significance is reported via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests in Tables 1–3 with significance thresholds clearly marked. This breadth is appropriate for a multi-claim system.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major
- **No explicit alignment loss between z_x and z_y despite the paper's central claim.** The Figure 1 caption states content features "are encouraged to align," and Section 3.2 defines both encoders as mapping to a shared Z_c. Yet Eq. 6 contains no contrastive, MSE, or mutual information term between z_x and z_y. The only implicit pressure is that z_x feeds the performance decoder (EPR loss) and z_y feeds the score decoder (APT loss), which share no weights. The paper never measures whether z_x and z_y are actually close for paired data (e.g., cosine similarity or CKA). This is a substantive gap between the stated design rationale and the actual training objective — the "shared content space" claim that motivates the entire joint architecture is unsupported by the loss.

- **The sole ablation confounds multi-task training with additional data.** The EPR-Only variant (Table 2) removes both the APT task and unpaired data simultaneously. The paper itself acknowledges this conflation: "joint modeling, together with training on additional unpaired data, leads to better EPR performance" (Section 5.1). Without separating these factors (e.g., a joint model trained only on paired data, or an EPR-only model with unpaired data), the gains cannot be attributed to the paper's primary contribution — the joint modeling duality. No ablation isolates the KL regularization, masked reconstruction, loss weighting, or joint training's effect on APT.

- **Disentanglement evaluation does not control for content leakage into z_s.** Table 4 shows Style→Comp accuracy of 77.46%, which the paper attributes to "professional pianists often align their performance style with the composer's stylistic conventions" (Section 5.2). However, this result is equally consistent with z_s capturing compositional/harmonic content rather than purely stylistic information. The paper acknowledges two confounders (global vs. note-level asymmetry, pianist-composer alignment) but does not attempt to rule out leakage — e.g., by checking whether z_s clusters by performer for same-piece, multi-performer data in ATEPP, which is already available and annotated for this purpose.

### Minor
- **Onset variance σ²(O) = 0.02 for both "Ours" variants vs. Human = 0.12 (Table 2).** This indicates near-metronomic onset timing, yet the paper claims its models exhibit "natural expressive variability" (Section 5.1). The discrepancy is not discussed. While DExter overshoots at 0.20, the proposed model's near-zero variance is also a meaningful deviation from human-like behavior for a system claiming expressiveness.

- **All loss terms in Eq. 6 have unit weights with no sensitivity analysis.** The reconstruction losses (unpaired) and cross-task losses (paired) operate on different data scales and objectives. No justification or sensitivity analysis is provided for equal weighting.

- **The global style vector compresses the entire expressive profile into a single D-dimensional vector.** For long compositions where performers vary their interpretation across sections, this may be too coarse. The design limitation is not acknowledged or discussed.

- **APT pitch error E_p = 3.08 is higher than all baselines in Table 1.** While the paper reasonably attributes this to alignment-based advantages of baselines (Section 5.1), the tradeoff between pitch accuracy and other metrics deserves more explicit discussion.

### Trivial
None

## Nice-to-Haves
- Controlled disentanglement evaluation on ATEPP using multiple performances of the same piece by different performers, to separate content leakage from genuine style-content separation.
- Explicit measurement of z_x/z_y alignment (e.g., cosine similarity, linear CKA) on paired data to validate or refute the shared-content-space claim.
- Ablations isolating: (a) joint training vs. single-task with same data, (b) unpaired data contribution, (c) KL regularization effect on disentanglement quality.
- Style transfer evaluation with more than 3 samples (Figure 5) to draw more generalizable conclusions.
- Larger-scale subjective evaluation (currently 11 participants rating 5 pieces).

## Novel Insights
The paper's framing of EPR and APT as inverse tasks connected through a shared content space is conceptually clean and underexplored in MIR. The PSR module's ability to recover era-level clustering structure from score content alone (Figure 4) is a genuinely interesting finding that, if the disentanglement were more rigorously validated, would be a notable contribution to both style modeling and music generation.

## Suggestions
- Add an explicit alignment loss (contrastive or MSE) between z_x and z_y for paired data, and report alignment metrics to validate the shared-content-space claim.
- Separate the ablation into at least three conditions: (a) joint model on paired-only, (b) EPR-only with all data, (c) full model, to isolate what drives the gains.
- Leverage ATEPP's multi-performer-per-piece annotations to test whether z_s clusters by performer (not piece) for the same composition — this would be a strong, controlled test of disentanglement.
- Discuss the onset variance results honestly; if the model is conservative in timing, this is a known limitation worth acknowledging.
- Consider section-level or segment-level style vectors for future work, to handle within-piece stylistic variation.
