## Summary
TbLTA introduces the first weakly-supervised framework for dense long-term action anticipation (LTA), trained exclusively from video transcripts—ordered action lists without temporal boundary annotations. The architecture combines temporal alignment (ATBA) for pseudo-label generation, cross-modal attention grounded by pseudo-label-derived masks, CTC loss for global transcript consistency, and a CRF-equipped decoder for temporally coherent anticipation. Evaluated on Breakfast, 50Salads, and EGTEA, the method achieves competitive or superior performance to fully supervised methods on Breakfast (avg 29.03 vs. 28.45 MoC) but shows meaningful gaps on 50Salads (~7.5 MoC) and EGTEA (~11 mAP).

## Strengths
- **Genuinely novel problem formulation.** The paper is the first to tackle dense LTA using only video transcripts as supervision, representing a qualitative shift from annotation reduction to a different supervision paradigm entirely. This is clearly articulated in Section 1 and well-grounded in the related work (Section 2), which demonstrates that no prior work has attempted fully weakly-supervised dense LTA.

- **Surprisingly competitive results on Breakfast.** In the deterministic setting (Table 1), TbLTA achieves 40.28 MoC at Obs 30%/Pred 10% vs. 35.79 for ActFusion (best supervised), and the overall average at 30% observation (34.13) exceeds all supervised baselines. For a weakly-supervised method to outperform fully-supervised counterparts on a substantial subset of settings is a notable result.

- **Well-designed cross-modal attention with principled grounding.** The mechanism in Eqs. 1–2 uses pseudo-labels to construct a binary local mask restricting each transcript action's cross-attention to its predicted temporal neighborhood, followed by a gated residual injection back into video features. The ablation (Table 4) confirms this design outperforms both naive cross-attention and no cross-attention.

- **Informative ablation study with consistent trends.** Table 4 shows each component (CRF, duration loss, cross-attention) contributes, with the CRF's contribution most visible at longer prediction horizons (consistent with its purpose of enforcing temporal coherence). Ablations are run on both Breakfast and 50Salads.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major
- **Pseudo-label quality is never directly evaluated.** The entire pipeline—TAS head supervision and LTA decoder training—depends on the quality of pseudo-labels generated by the ATBA temporal alignment module. Yet no intermediate metrics (accuracy vs. ground-truth frame labels, boundary precision, per-class recall) are reported. This prevents diagnosing whether the significant performance gaps on 50Salads (avg 20.92 vs. 28.39 supervised) and EGTEA (65.37 vs. 76.80) arise from poor alignment, poor segmentation, or poor anticipation. For a paper establishing a new supervision paradigm, validating the intermediate representation is essential to understanding when and why the paradigm works.

- **Abstract overclaims relative to the evidence.** The abstract states TbLTA offers "a very robust and less costly alternative to its fully supervised counterpart." On Breakfast this is supported, but on 50Salads the deterministic gap is ~7.5 MoC (20.92 vs. 28.39) and on EGTEA the All-class gap is ~11 mAP (65.37 vs. 76.80). The body text is more nuanced (Section 4.2 acknowledges 50Salads limitations and "supervised models retain a clear edge" on EGTEA), but the abstract's strong claim is not supported by the full experimental evidence.

### Minor
- **The dramatic performance inversion between 20% and 30% observation on Breakfast is unexplained.** At 20% observation, TbLTA's deterministic performance is generally close to but below ActFusion (e.g., 27.47 vs. 28.25 at Pred 10%). At 30% observation, TbLTA suddenly surpasses all supervised baselines by 4–5 MoC points (40.28 vs. 35.79 at Pred 10%). The paper offers only one sentence of explanation: "This result highlights the ability of transcript-based supervision to capture the procedural regularities of activities" (Section 4.2). This is the most striking empirical pattern in the paper and deserves analysis—e.g., does pseudo-label quality improve substantially at 30%, or does the Breakfast dataset's structure interact with this observation ratio?

- **EGTEA evaluation is thin.** Only two supervised baselines are compared (Timeception and Anticipatr), both from 2019/2022. Though TbLTA shows a competitive advantage on Rare classes (60.11 vs. 55.10), the overall comparison would be more informative with additional recent baselines.

- **Stochastic evaluation protocol is not fully transparent.** Table 1 reports TbLTA's Top-1 (best-of-K) stochastic results separately from deterministic baselines, which is appropriate. However, ActFusion itself is a diffusion-based stochastic model, and whether its reported numbers correspond to mean or Top-1 evaluation is not clarified, creating potential ambiguity in stochastic-vs-stochastic comparison.

## Nice-to-Haves
- **Variance/confidence intervals across splits.** Results are averaged over 4 splits (Breakfast) and 5 splits (50Salads), but no standard deviations are reported. Some claimed margins are small (e.g., 29.03 vs. 28.45 overall average on Breakfast). While single-number reporting is common in the dense LTA field (supervised baselines also don't report variance), confidence intervals would strengthen the headline comparisons.
- **Per-class analysis of duration loss.** The paper acknowledges (Section 4.3) that the duration loss is most beneficial for classes with concentrated duration statistics. Reporting which action classes benefit vs. suffer would be informative.
- **Expanded failure analysis.** The single qualitative example (Figure 3) could be supplemented with systematic failure mode identification—e.g., which classes or transition patterns are most affected by the absence of frame-level supervision.

## Removed Points

- **Scalar vs. vector gate in Eq. 2:** The reviewer noted that W_g ∈ ℝ^{d×1} projects to a scalar gate, which "may limit expressiveness." This is a minor architectural design choice with no evidence that it causes a problem, and scalar gating is a common, reasonable choice. Removed as speculation.

This is standard for encoder-decoder architectures. Removed as addressed.
- **I3D features being outdated:** The reviewer themselves acknowledged the comparison is fair since all baselines use I3D. Removed as not a weakness of this paper.
- **Variance reporting as a major weakness:** Downgraded to Nice-to-Have because single-number reporting is standard practice in the dense LTA field; the supervised baselines in the same tables also report no variance.

## Novel Insights
The paper's central insight is that ordered action transcripts—which encode procedural and semantic structure without temporal localization—can serve as surprisingly effective supervision for dense temporal prediction tasks. The finding that this supervision paradigm works competitively on Breakfast (highly structured procedural activities) but degrades on 50Salads (denser action distributions, frequent transitions) and EGTEA (fine-grained egocentric actions) provides a useful empirical characterization of when transcript-level supervision is sufficient vs. insufficient for dense temporal forecasting.

## Suggestions
- Report pseudo-label accuracy (vs. ground-truth frame-level annotations) across datasets, observation ratios, and training stages. This single addition would convert the pipeline's core mechanism from a black box into an interpretable, diagnosable component.
- Analyze the 20%→30% observation performance jump on Breakfast—even a simple analysis showing how pseudo-label coverage or future sequence predictability changes would make this result more credible.
- Temper the abstract's claim from "very robust alternative" to language that reflects the dataset-dependent nature of the results (which the body text already does).
- Clarify whether supervised stochastic baselines (particularly ActFusion) are evaluated under the same Top-1/Mean protocol.
