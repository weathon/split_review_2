## Summary
The paper introduces GMD-25, a compositional-generalisation benchmark for ML interatomic potentials, consisting of four controlled tasks (length extrapolation, functional group composition, duplication, combination) over 118 alkyl-chain molecules with ~296k GFN2-xTB labels generated by a FlashMD pipeline. Five MLFFs (SchNet, PAINN, DimeNet++, GemNet, EquiFormerV2) are evaluated with Bayesian hyperparameter tuning, showing 1–2 orders-of-magnitude OOD degradation and ID/OOD rank reversals across output modalities.

## Strengths
- Tasks are explicitly mapped to a principled compositional-generalisation taxonomy (length generalisation, systematicity) from Hupkes et al. 2020 (Sec 3.1), giving the benchmark a clear diagnostic structure rather than ad-hoc splits.
- Each task isolates a single axis of variation (chain length in Task 1, functional group identity/multiplicity in Tasks 2–4) with matched training/OOD splits, supporting targeted attribution of failures.
- Per-model Bayesian hyperparameter optimisation (Sec 4.2) reduces the risk that observed OOD gaps reflect under-tuned baselines.
- Concrete, non-trivial empirical finding: ID/OOD rankings dissociate across output modality (e.g., EquiFormerV2 best on forces, worst on energy under length extrapolation; PAINN vs EquiFormerV2 trading places on Task 4 in Figs 2, 4).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Total-energy MAE drives the headline OOD claim.** The energy metric in Sec 4.2 is the sum over a molecule (extensive), but in Task 1 base the OOD chains contain ~2× as many atoms as the ID chains. A constant per-atom error therefore inflates the visible OOD/ID gap purely from molecule size, so Figs 2(a) and 3(a,c) — the most dramatic curves — are partly a metric tautology rather than evidence of failure to "learn physical principles." A per-atom (or per-heavy-atom) energy MAE should be the primary headline. (Force MAE in Eq. of Sec 4.2 is already per-atom and is unaffected.)
- **Architectures most aligned with the paper's own argument are absent.** Sec 2.2 motivates the benchmark via algorithmic alignment / physics-informed architectures, and Sec 2 cites MACE and NequIP, yet neither appears in the experiments (Sec 4.1). Excluding foundation models is justified (memorisation confound), but MACE/NequIP can be trained from scratch on the proposed splits identically. The broad claim that "popular MLFFs may not learn physically plausible models" is therefore drawn from a model set that omits the strongest candidates for refuting it.
- **No variance / multi-seed reporting.** Figs 2–4 show single-run means; ID/OOD rank reversals and several model orderings are read off small gaps with no error bars. For a benchmark whose purpose is to rank architectures on OOD reliability, this is a meaningful gap.

### Minor
- **Label fidelity vs framing.** The introduction emphasises DFT-quality reference calculations and "underlying physical principles," but labels are GFN2-xTB (Sec 3). Sec 3 defends xTB on accuracy/efficiency grounds, but a small spot-check of one OOD slice against DFT would much more directly support the "learning physical principles" framing.
- **Task 1 augmented mixes regimes.** The augmented split simultaneously evaluates interpolation (alcohols at C4–C8 with training C2–C3 ∪ C9–C15) and extrapolation (carboxylic acids at the tails). Fig 3 does split the two molecular families, but the prose treats them jointly; separating length-interpolation from length-extrapolation behaviour would make the diagnostic sharper.
- **Aggregated errors.** Tasks 2–4 are about local functional-group changes; the benchmark design directly supports atom-resolved or group-resolved errors, but only molecule-level aggregates appear in the main text. This leaves much of the diagnostic value the design enables on the table.
- **Trajectory QC unspecified.** Sec 3.2 describes 200k × 16 fs Langevin runs at 300 K in vacuum but does not state how unphysical/dissociated configurations are screened, which could disproportionately affect long-chain OOD molecules.
- **Scope vs framing.** 118 molecules built from alkyl chains plus a few O/N functional groups (no aromatics, rings, halogens, charged species) is a narrower slice than the abstract's "MLFFs ... learn physical principles" language; explicitly reframing the conclusions as alkyl-functional-group compositionality would match what is measured.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A classical force-field (GAFF/OPLS) reference on the same splits as a non-learning baseline.
- A reverse-direction sanity check (train on long chains, test on short) to verify the OOD asymmetry is not a data-generation artefact.
- Atom-resolved error maps showing whether errors localise to backbone vs the novel functional group.

## Removed Points
These were considered but excluded; treat with caution.
- "PBE0" appearing in the Fig. 2 legend description is a parser artefact for PAINN, not an author error.
- Generic reproducibility complaints (full hyperparameter listings, training logs) — Sec 4.2 and the appendix already cover this at the standard level.
- "Important problem" / generic significance strengths from the Strength Finder.
- Generic "comprehensive architectural coverage" strength — partly contradicted by the verified Major weakness that MACE/NequIP are missing.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions; the most interesting observation — ID/OOD rank reversal across energy vs force modality — is already reported by the paper itself.

## Suggestions
- Make per-atom (or per-heavy-atom) Energy MAE the primary metric; keep total-energy MAE as a secondary view.
- Add MACE and NequIP, trained from scratch on identical splits.
- Run ≥3 seeds and report variance on Figs 2–4.
- Add atom-resolved error analysis isolating backbone vs functional-group errors.
- Validate xTB labels with a small DFT cross-check on at least one OOD slice.
