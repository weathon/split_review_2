## Summary
This paper proposes STNAdam, a two-track iteration framework for "nonconvex + weakly-convex" composite optimization that combines Nesterov momentum with Adam-style adaptive conditioning across two intertwined trajectories (extrapolation and regular update tracks). The method accommodates variance-reduced gradient estimators (SVRG, SAGA, SARAH) and provides convergence analysis under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property. Experiments are presented on low-light image enhancement (LIE).

## Strengths
- **Novel two-track iteration architecture**: Algorithm 1 Step 5 (lines 103–105) maintains two separate proximal update trajectories — $x^{k+1}$ driven by $\hat{\varpi}^{k+1}$ and $\tilde{x}^{k+1}$ driven by $\tilde{\varpi}^{k+1}$ via an extrapolation point $\bar{x}^{k+1}$. This is contrasted with single-track designs of NAG, Adam, and NAdam in Figure 1 (lines 76–89), showing a structurally distinct framework rather than a minor parameter tweak.
- **Generality of convergence analysis under KL property with explicit rates**: Theorem 2 (lines 273–277) establishes convergence rates depending on the KL exponent $\vartheta$: geometric for $\vartheta \in (0, 1/2]$, polynomial for $\vartheta \in (1/2, 1)$, and finite convergence for $\vartheta = 0$. The analysis accommodates arbitrary variance-reduced gradient estimators through the abstract conditions in Lemma 1 (equations 3–5, lines 148–168).
- **Strong empirical gains on LIE benchmarks across all three image quality metrics**: Table 2 (lines 289–298) shows STNAdam-SARAH achieves PSNR 22.26, SSIM 0.9062, and LPIPS 0.0501 — improvements of ~5 dB PSNR and ~0.11 SSIM over SNAdam (17.14, 0.7945), also outperforming domain-specific LIE methods like Retinex-Net (18.44, 0.8205). Gains are consistent in the denoising variant (Table 3, lines 313–321).
- **Well-structured convergence proof**: The analysis follows a clear five-step structure (Lemmas 2–5, Theorems 1–2) building on a carefully constructed energy function $G^k$ (equation 9, lines 200–204) with a precise expected decrease bound (Lemma 2, equation 10) exhibiting eight explicit positive coefficients.

### Major
- **Severe mismatch between deep learning motivation and experimental scope**: The introduction repeatedly frames the contribution around deep learning — "achievements are closely tied to the Adam algorithm" in "computer vision," "NLP," "quantitative finance" (line 13); "developing an enhanced stochastic variant of Adam that can handle the complexities of modern deep learning tasks" (line 17); contribution (iii) claims "removing hand-tuning…reducing training time and improving generalization" (lines 44–48). Yet the entire empirical evaluation tests STNAdam on exactly one application — low-light image enhancement on one dataset (LOL) — which is a variational optimization problem decomposing images into reflectance and illumination layers (Eq. 14, line 285), not a deep learning task. No neural network is trained anywhere. No standard ML benchmarks are attempted. This is a fundamental disconnect: the paper has not established that its contribution matters for the setting it claims to target.

- **Missing ablation isolating the two-track mechanism (the core novelty)**: The paper's primary novelty is the "two-track iteration framework" (contribution (i), lines 43–44). Yet no experiment isolates this contribution. A natural ablation would compare: (a) single-track Adam + variance reduction, (b) single-track NAdam + variance reduction, (c) STNAdam (two-track + variance reduction). The comparisons against SGD, SAdam, and SNAdam conflate the benefit of the two-track mechanism with that of variance reduction — which is not novel in itself — making it impossible to attribute the observed gains.

- **Abstract and contribution statement overstate the convergence result**: The abstract (line 9) claims "the sequence generated by STNAdam almost surely converges to a stationary point" and contribution (ii) (line 44) claims "almost-sure global convergence." However, Theorem 1(ii) (line 263) establishes convergence only "in expectation," and the conclusion (line 336) correctly states convergence "in expectation." The "almost surely" in Lemma 4 (line 234) applies only to subsequence differences ($\|\bar{x}^k - \bar{x}^{k-1}\| \to 0$ a.s.), not to convergence to a stationary point. This is a clear discrepancy between the abstract and the actual theorem statements.

### Minor

- **No error bars or variance reporting for stochastic methods**: Table 2 reports single numbers with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or repeated runs for stochastic methods (SGD, SAdam, SNAdam, STNAdam variants). This makes it impossible to assess whether the observed differences are statistically significant.

- **Narrow optimizer baseline comparison**: Only three optimizer baselines are used (SGD, SAdam, SNAdam). Missing are Adam itself, AdaGrad, AMSGrad, RAdam, AdamW, and other contemporary optimizers. For a paper positioning itself as improving Adam, this is insufficient to contextualize the contribution.

- **Unclear "Time(s)" reporting**: The reported times are on the order of $10^{-5}$ seconds (Table 2), which is implausible for total training time. These appear to be per-iteration or per-pixel times but are never clarified, making the timing comparison uninterpretable.
