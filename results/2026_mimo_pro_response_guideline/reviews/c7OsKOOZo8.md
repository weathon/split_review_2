## Summary
This paper proposes an end-to-end multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading framework that self-generates lesion proposals via a Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module using CAM-derived grade-conditioned evidence maps, and fuses them across views via a Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion (LGRF) module using context-gated mixture-of-experts and top-K weighted cross-view attention. On MFIDDR (4-view), the lesion-free variant achieves 83.9% accuracy, surpassing all end-to-end baselines and matching several externally-informed methods; on DRTiD (2-view), it achieves 76.0% accuracy, outperforming all compared methods including externally-informed ones.

## Strengths
- **Strong empirical results without external annotations**: On MFIDDR (Table 1), the lesion-free variant achieves 83.9% accuracy, surpassing all end-to-end baselines (e.g., MVCINN 80.1%, ETMC 81.5%) and matching or outperforming externally-informed methods (CVSA 82.6%, LFMVDR 82.2%). On DRTiD (Table 3), it achieves 76.0% accuracy, exceeding CrossFIT (75.6%, using OD/macular coordinates) and CVSA (74.7%, using vessel masks). These results directly validate the paper's practical contribution.
- **Systematic ablation confirms complementary contributions**: Table 4 shows that removing GALP (→82.7%), the expert pool (→82.6%), or LGRF entirely (→82.3%) each causes clear accuracy drops from the full model (83.9%), with corresponding declines in Kappa and F1, demonstrating the modules address complementary aspects.
- **Dual-mode capability**: The optional "with lesion" variant (Table 1, 84.6% accuracy, 72.3% Kappa) establishes new SOTA on MFIDDR, showing the architecture gracefully incorporates external cues when available while remaining fully functional without them — a practical advantage over purely end-to-end or externally-dependent methods.
- **Cross-dataset generalization**: Strong performance on both a 4-view dataset (MFIDDR, ImageNet pretraining) and a 2-view dataset (DRTiD, EyePACS pretraining) demonstrates robustness across dataset configurations.
- **Strong grade-wise performance on clinically important intermediate grades**: Table 2 shows the lesion-free model achieves F1=69.7% on Grade 1 and F1=74.1% on Grade 3, and the lesion-augmented variant reaches best-in-class F1=65.2% on Grade 2 and F1=74.8% on Grade 3 — these intermediate grades are the hardest and most clinically consequential.
- **Practical hyperparameter guidance**: Figure 3 explores retention ratio, number of routed experts, and total experts with clear optimal values (α=0.5, K₂=2, M=6).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Central claim that GALP produces "lesion proposals" is unsupported by direct evidence**: The paper asserts that "since the grade evidence in DR is predominantly localized to lesions, selecting Top-K peaks within these maps yields lesion proposals" (Section 3.2). However, CAMs highlight regions discriminative for the predicted class, which could equally be the optic disc, major vessel junctions, or image artifacts that correlate with grade. The MFIDDR dataset explicitly provides lesion segmentation masks (Section 4.1: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), which would enable direct quantitative comparison (e.g., IoU/precision/recall). No such evaluation is provided, nor is there any qualitative visualization of proposals overlaid on fundus images. Without this evidence, the paper's framing of "lesion proposals," claims about "recovering small, low-contrast lesions," and "micro-lesion sensitivity" (abstract, contribution 3) are naming conventions rather than demonstrated facts. The method may work, but the paper does not show it works for the stated reason.

- **Backbone confound prevents clean attribution of gains**: The authors use Swin-B, while many end-to-end baselines use weaker architectures (MVCNN_V: VGG19, MVCNN_R: ResNet50). The ablation (Table 4) is valuable but insufficient: "w/o LGRF" (82.3%) shows Swin-B + GALP, and "w/o GALP" (82.7%) shows Swin-B + LGRF, but there is no "Swin-B + simple multi-view fusion" baseline to anchor the contribution of the proposed modules against the backbone alone. The 3.8% gap over MVCINN (80.1%) could partly stem from the backbone upgrade. Adding this baseline would make gains attributable to GALP+LGRF rather than to the architecture change.

### Minor
- **No variance or statistical significance reporting**: All results are single-point numbers with no confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multi-seed runs. Margins over competitors are sometimes small (e.g., 0.4% over CrossFIT on DRTiD, 1.3% over CVSA on MFIDDR), making stability unclear. Even 3 seeds would substantially strengthen the evidence.

- **Adjacent-only cross-view fusion is unexplored**: In the 4-view MFIDDR setting, cyclic-adjacent fusion (Section 3.3) means view 1 never directly interacts with view 3, and view 2 never with view 4. For contralateral or orthogonal views that may carry complementary lesion information, this seems limiting. No justification or comparison against all-pairs fusion is provided.

- **Auxiliary loss excludes stage 4 without justification**: Eq. 2 sums the auxiliary focal loss over stages 1–3 but not stage 4, while the final classifier operates on stage-4 features. This asymmetry is not discussed.

### Trivial
- **Narrative tension around "with lesion" variant**: The paper's strongest argument is annotation-free grading, yet the headline SOTA number (84.6%) in Table 1 uses external lesion annotations via SPADE. The lesion-free results (83.9%) should be the primary headline, with the "with lesion" variant presented as supplementary.

## Novel Insights
The paper's genuinely novel observation is that CAM-derived grade-conditioned evidence maps from stage-wise auxiliary classifiers can serve as effective surrogates for expert lesion annotations in multi-view DR grading, achieving competitive performance with externally-informed methods while eliminating annotation dependency. The dual-mode design (with/without lesion annotations) and the context-gated cross-view expert routing are also meaningful contributions.

## Suggestions
- Add quantitative evaluation of proposal quality against MFIDDR's ground-truth lesion segmentation masks (IoU, precision, recall) — this single experiment would resolve the most significant evidential gap.
- Add a Swin-B backbone + simple concatenation baseline to isolate GALP+LGRF contributions from the backbone upgrade.
- Report results over 3 random seeds with standard deviations.
- Lead with the lesion-free results as the primary narrative.
