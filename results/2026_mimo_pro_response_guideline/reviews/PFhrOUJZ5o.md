## Summary

This paper presents LAION-Comp, a large-scale dataset of 540K+ images from LAION-Aesthetics annotated with scene graphs (objects, attributes, relations) generated by GPT-4o, along with four baseline models (SDXL-SG, SD1.5-SG, SD3.5-SG, FLUX-SG) and a compositional benchmark (CompSGen Bench). The core contribution is a dataset and benchmark paper for compositional image generation.

## Strengths

- **Large-scale SG dataset with quantitatively verified annotation quality**: LAION-Comp provides 540K+ image-SG pairs—substantially larger than COCO-Stuff or Visual Genome. Table 1 shows scene graph annotations achieve markedly higher accuracy than original LAION captions (SG-IoU+: 0.422 vs 0.306, Ent-IoU+: 0.810 vs 0.631, Rel-IoU+: 0.749 vs 0.557). Partial human verification reports 98.8% object, 97.5% attribute, and 95.7% relation accuracy.

- **Consistent cross-architecture improvements from LAION-Comp training**: Table 2 shows that training on LAION-Comp consistently improves both existing SG2IM methods (SGDiff, SG-Adapter) and the authors' baselines across diffusion and flow-matching backbones. For example, SG-Adapter achieves SG-IoU 0.538 on LAION-Comp vs 0.485 (COCO) and 0.515 (VG), and SDXL-SG achieves 0.558 on LAION-Comp vs 0.497 (COCO) and 0.546 (VG). This cross-architecture consistency provides meaningful evidence that the benefit stems from the data.

- **Informative ablation study**: Table 4 shows monotonic performance improvement with data proportion (10%→100%) for both SG-Adapter and SDXL-SG, supporting the value of scaling data while controlling for training iterations.

- **Richer semantic diversity than prior SG datasets**: LAION-Comp has 77.48% non-spatial relations versus VG's 41.98% (Section 3.2), and the top-10 most frequent relations each account for less than ~4% of total distribution (Figure 4b), indicating broad vocabulary coverage.

- **Dedicated compositional benchmark**: CompSGen Bench (20,838 samples filtered for 4+ relations) specifically targets complex scene generation and discriminates between model capabilities (Table 3).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major
- **Evaluation circularity — benchmark uses same GPT-4o annotation pipeline as training data**: CompSGen Bench is drawn from LAION-Comp's 50K test set, annotated by the same GPT-4o pipeline used for training data. The accuracy metrics (SG-IoU, Entity-IoU, Relation-IoU) measure overlap with GPT-4o-generated scene graphs. Models trained on LAION-Comp have learned GPT-4o's annotation conventions, while baselines trained on COCO-Stuff or Visual Genome face a distributional mismatch in both image content and annotation style. The most informative comparison in Table 2 (SG-Adapter on COCO vs VG vs LAION-Comp) shows LAION-Comp performs better, but this advantage is confounded: the evaluation ground truth is GPT-4o's output, and models trained on GPT-4o's style naturally align better with it. The paper mentions T2I-CompBench results in the appendix (Sec. A.6) to address this, but this key evidence is not in the main text.

- **Annotation quality verification insufficiently substantiated in main text**: The entire value proposition of LAION-Comp rests on GPT-4o-generated annotations being high quality. The paper claims "partial human verification" achieving 98.8%, 97.5%, and 95.7% accuracy, but details (sample size, selection protocol, error categorization, annotator instructions) are deferred to Appendix A.5. For a dataset paper, this is the central question. The reported 95.7% relation accuracy implies ~4.3% erroneous relations; across 540K images with ~5 relations each, this is ~116K erroneous annotations—a non-trivial number that could affect model training.

### Minor
- **T2I vs SG2IM comparisons on SG-overlap metrics are expected**: Table 2 observes that "the SG-IoU of T2I model is significantly lower than that of SG2IM models" and concludes this "highlights the necessity of constructing a large-scale, high-quality structured annotation dataset." A model given explicit scene graph inputs will naturally satisfy those specifications better than one inferring from text. This comparison demonstrates the value of structured inputs, not the value of this specific dataset.

- **FID degradation after fine-tuning not fully discussed**: In Table 3, SDXL achieves FID 25.2 while SDXL-SG achieves 26.7 on CompSGen Bench—slightly worse image quality. The paper acknowledges "fine-tuning pre-trained T2I models inevitably increases FID scores" but doesn't discuss this accuracy-vs-quality tradeoff in depth.

- **Ablation has confounding factor**: In Table 4, "total training iterations remain constant" across settings. The 10% setting sees each sample ~10× more, potentially overfitting. No discussion of learning curves or convergence behavior.

- **No error bars or statistical significance**: All metrics in Tables 2-4 are single numbers.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Evaluate on established benchmarks (GenEval, T2I-CompBench full results) in the main paper to break the circular evaluation concern.
- Report inter-annotator agreement and error categorization for human verification.
- Discuss failure modes of the GPT-4o annotation pipeline (hallucinated objects, misidentified relations).

## Novel Insights
The paper's key observation is that large-scale structured annotations from VLMs can serve as a practical substitute for expensive human annotation in building scene-graph datasets for compositional generation, yielding consistent improvements across diverse model architectures. The diversity analysis (77% non-spatial relations vs VG's 42%) highlights a meaningful qualitative difference between LAION-Comp and prior SG datasets.

## Suggestions
- Move human verification details (sample size, protocol, error analysis) from Appendix A.5 to the main paper — this is the single most important improvement for a dataset paper.
- Add T2I-CompBench and/or GenEval full results to the main paper to break the circular evaluation concern.
- When comparing across datasets (Table 2), explicitly discuss the annotation-distribution matching confound.
- Add confidence intervals to all metrics in Tables 2-4.
