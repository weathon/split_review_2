## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models Expressive Performance Rendering (EPR) and Automatic Piano Transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content from global performance style representations, trained on both paired and unpaired data. A diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module generates style embeddings from score content alone. The framework is evaluated on standard benchmarks with objective metrics, subjective listening tests, and representation analysis probes.

## Strengths
- **Well-verified disentanglement of content and style**: Table 4 shows dramatic gaps between style and content representations for performer/composer identification (Style→Perf F1=25.82 vs Cont→Perf F1=0.74; Style→Comp F1=52.45 vs Cont→Comp F1=3.03), providing strong quantitative evidence of successful disentanglement.
- **Competitive APT without note-level alignment**: Table 1 shows statistically significant improvements (p<0.01) over the end-to-end Seq2Seq baseline (Beyer & Dai, 2024) on E_extra, E_onset, E_offset, E_pitch, and E_avg (12.48, the best overall), demonstrating the viability of the alignment-free formulation.
- **Comprehensive multi-faceted evaluation**: The paper evaluates through objective metrics (KL, MAE, variance), subjective listening tests with music-trained participants, performer/composer identification probes, 2D visualizations, and style transfer tests — covering the paper's claims from multiple angles.
- **Scalable framework leveraging unpaired data**: The masked reconstruction objectives (Eq. 4) allow training on 75,913 unpaired score files and YouTube-derived performances beyond the 967 ASAP paired samples, demonstrating practical scalability.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Factually incorrect claim about DExter's velocity variance** — Line 235 states "DExter shows even larger duration and velocity variance" compared to human. Table 2 shows DExter's velocity variance is 238.86 while Human is 241.04 — DExter's value is actually *smaller* and nearly a perfect match (|diff| = 2.18). Meanwhile, Ours (Target) = 151.03, far from human (|diff| = 90.01). The paper then characterizes DExter's near-perfect human match as "unstable dynamics rather than meaningful expressiveness," which is not credible for velocity variance. This factual error undermines the paper's credibility.

- **Large KL(D) gap with DExter buried** — DExter achieves KL(D) = 1.48 vs. Ours (Target) = 5.51 — a 3.7× difference and the largest performance gap in Table 2. The paper hides this behind "lower KL and MAE scores than most baselines." DExter also outperforms on KL(V) (2.32 vs. Ours-PSR 2.67). The paper should discuss these gaps honestly.

- **Weak quantitative evaluation of PSR module** — PSR is one of three stated contributions but: (1) there is no direct quantitative style-appropriateness metric (e.g., cosine similarity or retrieval accuracy between PSR-generated and actual performer styles); (2) PSR consistently underperforms Target on objective metrics (KL(D) 6.19 vs 5.51, KL(V) 2.67 vs 1.76, MAE(V) 15.24 vs 10.33) without acknowledgement; (3) there are no comparisons to simpler baselines (random/average/genre-based style vectors). Figure 4's qualitative visualization is suggestive but does not substitute for a quantitative PSR-appropriateness evaluation.

- **Missing ablation conflates joint training with unpaired data** — The EPR-Only variant differs from the full model in two ways simultaneously: joint EPR-APT training and access to 75K+ additional unpaired data. The paper attributes improvement to "joint modeling, together with training on additional unpaired data" but cannot distinguish between these explanations. A joint model trained on paired data only vs. EPR-Only would clarify whether the duality itself provides benefit — the paper's central motivating claim.

### Minor
- **Worst pitch edit distance (E_p) in APT not discussed** — Ours achieves E_p = 3.08, the worst among all methods in Table 1 (end-to-end baseline = 2.73, Liu et al. = 2.02). While E_avg is the best overall, this specific regression is not acknowledged or analyzed.

- **Small subjective evaluation with no inter-rater statistics** — 11 participants with no reported inter-rater agreement (Cronbach's alpha, Fleiss' kappa) or confidence intervals. Standard practice in music/audio subjective evaluation requires these to assess reliability.

- **Style transfer Sample 2 contradicts intended narrative** — Figure 5 shows that for Sample 2, the Target condition achieves lower similarity (~2.4) than Mean (~3.0), contradicting the claim that transferred styles improve similarity. The paper does not discuss this outlier.

## Novel Insights
The paper's key contribution is demonstrating that EPR and APT can be jointly modeled as inverse mappings through disentangled content-style representations using only sequence-level supervision. The empirical finding (Table 4) that style representations strongly encode performer/composer identity while content representations do not, combined with the modest composer leakage into content (29.99%), reveals that style-genre boundaries in music are inherently fuzzy — a finding the paper acknowledges but could exploit more deeply.

## Suggestions
- Correct the factually wrong claim about DExter's velocity variance and present a balanced discussion of where the method wins vs. loses across all metrics.
- Add the missing ablation: joint model trained on paired data only vs. EPR-Only, to validate the central claim about the benefit of EPR-APT duality.
- Add a direct quantitative metric for PSR appropriateness (e.g., style retrieval accuracy or cosine similarity between PSR-generated and actual performer styles).
- Add comparison to simple PSR baselines (random style vector, average training style, genre-conditioned style).
- Report inter-rater agreement and confidence intervals for subjective studies.
- Discuss the E_p regression in APT results alongside the strong E_avg.
