## Summary

This paper proposes SIGMAP, a multimodal foundation model for wireless localization that combines (1) a cycle-adaptive masking strategy for self-supervised pretraining on CSI data and (2) a "map-as-prompt" framework that uses a GNN to encode 3D building geometry into a soft prompt token prepended to a frozen transformer. The model is pretrained on DeepMIMO O1\_3p5 and evaluated on single-BS and multi-BS localization, achieving consistent improvements over baselines (e.g., 1.564 vs 2.382 m MAE on single-BS). Generalization experiments on DeepMIMO O2 and WAIR-D (100 real city layouts) show substantial gains over LWLM. Parameter efficiency is genuinely demonstrated (0.085M trainable params, 30 min fine-tuning).

## Strengths

- **The "map-as-prompt" idea is well-motivated and architecturally clean.** Using a GNN to encode 3D building geometry into a soft prompt token prepended to the input sequence of a frozen pretrained transformer is a natural way to condition localization on environmental geometry. The Delaunay triangulation for graph construction (Section 3.4) is a principled choice, and the ablation comparing 3D mesh, 2D bird's-eye, and no-map (Table 4) convincingly shows the prompt mechanism delivers substantial benefit (1.564 m MAE vs 2.275 m w/o map in single-BS localization) that is mostly retained under geometric simplification.

- **Parameter efficiency is genuinely demonstrated.** Only 0.085M parameters (≈0.7% of total) are updated during fine-tuning, and the 1000-epoch fine-tuning completes in 30 minutes. This is a practically meaningful result for deployment in new environments where labeled data and compute are scarce.

- **The generalization experiments cover genuinely distinct scenarios** (DeepMIMO O2 and WAIR-D with 100 real city layouts), and the improvements over LWLM are substantial (1.026 vs 2.213 m MAE on O2; 1.880 vs 3.375 m on WAIR-D). This provides reasonable evidence that the pretrained representations transfer across diverse environments.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Framing error: "zero-shot generalization" claim contradicts the actual protocol.** The abstract states the model "exhibits strong zero-shot generalization in unseen environments," and the contributions list similarly claims "strong zero-shot generalization to unseen environments and base station configurations" (line 43). However, Section 4.5 explicitly states that "only the downstream task heads are fine-tuned using limited target samples (approximately 100 instances per scenario)" and even labels it a "few-shot learning setup." Zero-shot means no task-specific training data from the target environment. The paper's actual contribution is few-shot adaptation, which is a meaningfully weaker claim. The abstract and contributions should be corrected to match the evaluation protocol.

- **NLoS-aware attention mechanism (Eq 11) is introduced as "the key advantage" but is never described in the methodology.** In Section 4.2 (line 247), the paper states: "The key advantage stems from our NLoS-aware attention mechanism that explicitly models multi-path propagation" and presents Equation 11 with notation ($\phi$, $\mathbf{o}_s^{(i)}$, $\mathbf{W}_{\text{NLoS}}$) that appears nowhere else. This mechanism is not mentioned in the framework overview (Section 3.1), the masking strategy (Section 3.3), the geographic prompt tuning (Section 3.4), or the task-specific adaptation (Section 3.5). The reader cannot determine whether this is a separate attention head, a modification to self-attention, a post-hoc weighting scheme, or something else. A mechanism described as "the key advantage" must be defined in the methodology, not appear without context in the results section.

- **Cycle-adaptive masking algorithm is underspecified to the point of irreproducibility.** The paper states that masks are generated by "comput[ing] shift patterns using cross-correlation analysis" (line 133) and mentions "row-wise cross-correlation" (line 41), but provides no algorithm for how $d_{\text{final}}$ (the detected periodicity shift) is actually computed from the CSI data. Which dimensions are cross-correlated? How is the dominant periodicity extracted from the cross-correlation? How are $j_0$ (starting offset) and $w$ (mask width) set relative to the detected periodicity? Without these details, a core claimed contribution cannot be reproduced.

### Minor

- **No variance or confidence intervals are reported for any result**, despite averaging over 5 runs. The large gap between MAE and RMSE in several conditions (e.g., Single-BS: MAE 1.564 vs RMSE 5.675 for SIGMAP w/ map) indicates heavy-tailed errors where a few large outliers dominate the RMSE. In such settings, variance across runs is meaningful for assessing whether improvements are statistically reliable.

- **The interpretability claim for geographic prompts is unsupported.** The paper claims that prompts "enable interpretable fusion of environmental constraints" (line 42), but provides no interpretability analysis — no attention maps, no ablation of prompt dimensions, no analysis of which geometric relationships the prompt encodes. The prompt is a single global-pooled vector, which by construction discards spatial structure.

### Trivial

- **Text-table inconsistency for WAIR-D Scenario-2**: line 340 reports 1.580 m MAE while the table (line 336) shows 1.880 m. The 44.3% improvement computation from the table value confirms the table is correct and the text contains a typo.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Provide analysis of what the cycle-adaptive masking actually changes.** Showing the distribution of $d_{\text{final}}$ values across samples, or visualizing masked vs. unmasked regions for representative CSI samples, would concretely demonstrate the benefit of adaptive over fixed masking.
- **Expand the baseline set** to include some of the SSL-based methods cited in related work (CrowdBERT, signal-guided MAE) as comparisons.
- **Acknowledge the simulation limitation** explicitly and discuss how results might transfer to real-world CSI data.
- **Provide more granular ablation** of the geographic prompt — what happens with different prompt dimensions, or with prompts encoding partial geometry?
