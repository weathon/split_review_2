## Summary

This paper introduces the Perceptually-Grounded Geospatial Chain-of-Thought (Geo-CoT), a reasoning framework for remote sensing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that mandates each analytical step to be verifiably linked to visual evidence. The authors construct Geo-CoT380k, the first large-scale dataset of structured Geo-CoT rationales, and train RSThinker via a two-stage alignment strategy: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to instill the cognitive architecture, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to refine factual correctness. RSThinker achieves state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive suite of remote sensing tasks including visual grounding, object counting, detection, classification, captioning, and VQA.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated and important problem**: The paper correctly identifies the critical need for verifiable, transparent reasoning in high-stakes remote sensing applications (disaster response, environmental monitoring) and proposes a concrete framework to address it.
- **Novel and principled framework**: The Geo-CoT paradigm with its Planning–Grounding–Synthesis structure and strict perceptual grounding is a clear conceptual advance over existing end-to-end or abstract-reasoning approaches in remote sensing.
- **Large-scale dataset contribution**: Geo-CoT380k (384,591 rationales) is a substantial resource that covers multiple tasks and benchmarks, enabling the training of structured reasoning in this domain.
- **Strong empirical results**: RSThinker consistently and significantly outperforms a wide range of baselines (including closed-source models like GPT-5, Gemini, Claude, and open-source RS VLMs) across all evaluated tasks, with particularly large margins on fine-grained perception tasks.
- **Well-designed ablation study**: The ablation clearly demonstrates the additive value of each component: SFT without CoT gives gains, SFT with CoT gives much larger gains, and GRPO further improves reasoning-intensive tasks. The KL regularization analysis (Figure 4) convincingly shows its stabilizing role.
- **Insightful qualitative analysis**: The failure case (Figure 7) is particularly valuable—it shows that even when the model makes an error, the explicit grounding makes the error auditable and falsifiable, which is a key advantage for high-stakes use.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Potential unfair comparison due to training data overlap**: RSThinker is trained on Geo-CoT380k, which is constructed from the training splits of many of the same benchmarks used for evaluation (e.g., VRSBench, DIOR-RSVG, DOTAv2, HRRSD). The paper does not clarify whether the baseline models (especially open-source RS VLMs like GeoChat, VHM, SkySenseGPT, EarthDial) were fine-tuned on the same training data or evaluated zero-shot. If baselines are evaluated without task-specific fine-tuning while RSThinker is trained on the exact evaluation benchmarks, the comparison is not apples-to-apples. This is a significant concern that needs to be addressed.
- **Lack of evaluation of rationale quality**: The Geo-CoT380k dataset is generated by GPT-4V conditioned on ground-truth annotations. While this conditioning reduces hallucination risk, the paper provides no human evaluation or quantitative analysis of the faithfulness, correctness, or stylistic quality of the generated rationales. The SFT stage instills the cognitive architecture from these rationales, so their quality is critical. The paper should include at least a small-scale human evaluation or comparison with human-written rationales.

### Minor
- **Overclaimed novelty**: The paper states it is the "first VLM for geospatial reasoning" and the "first large-scale SFT dataset for remote sensing chain-of-thought." Prior works (SegEarth-R1, RemoteReasoner, SkySense-O, Ringmo-Agent) have explored reasoning in remote sensing VLMs. While the paper's specific formulation of perceptual grounding and two-stage training is novel, the "first" claims should be tempered or more precisely scoped.
- **Incomplete baseline coverage**: The paper does not compare with SkySense-O (Zhu et al., 2025) or other recent reasoning-focused RS VLMs mentioned in the related work. Including these would strengthen the evaluation.
- **GRPO training details are deferred to appendix**: While the main paper provides the core formulation, details such as group size \(k\), number of training steps, learning rate, and the exact composition of the RL dataset are not given. The appendix is stripped, so these details are unavailable. This is a minor issue given the instruction to treat missing appendix as a parser artifact, but it would be helpful to have at least a summary in the main text.

### Trivial
- The paper uses "Visioned-Language Models" in the conclusion (Section 5) instead of "Vision-Language Models"—a minor typo.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A human evaluation study assessing the faithfulness and usefulness of the generated Geo-CoT rationales compared to baselines.
- Analysis of the computational cost (training time, GPU hours) for the two-stage pipeline.
- Zero-shot evaluation of RSThinker on unseen datasets to further demonstrate generalization.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the key insight is that explicit perceptual grounding in reasoning chains serves a dual purpose: it improves task accuracy *and* transforms errors from opaque failures into auditable, falsifiable outputs. The failure case (Figure 7) illustrates this elegantly—the model makes a visual mistake, but the structured reasoning exposes exactly where and why the error occurred. This property is particularly valuable for high-stakes remote sensing applications where trust and verifiability are paramount. The paper also demonstrates that a two-stage alignment (SFT then RL) is essential: SFT instills the cognitive structure, and RL refines factual correctness, with KL regularization preventing catastrophic collapse of the learned format.

## Suggestions
- **Clarify the evaluation protocol**: Explicitly state for each baseline whether it was fine-tuned on the same training data as RSThinker or evaluated zero-shot. If baselines were not fine-tuned, provide a fairer comparison by fine-tuning a representative baseline (e.g., GLM-4.1V-Base) on the same task-specific data without CoT rationales.
- **Add a human evaluation of rationale quality**: Sample 100–200 rationales from Geo-CoT380k and have human annotators rate them for factual correctness, grounding accuracy, and overall coherence. Report inter-annotator agreement.
- **Tone down the "first" claims**: Rephrase to "the first large-scale dataset of *perceptually-grounded* CoT rationales for remote sensing" and "the first VLM to explicitly externalize a verifiable, perceptually-grounded reasoning process."
