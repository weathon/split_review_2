## Summary

This paper introduces three contributions to multimodal process reward modeling: (1) VisualPRM400K, the first large-scale multimodal process supervision dataset (~400K samples, ~2M step annotations); (2) VisualPRM, an 8B multimodal Process Reward Model (PRM) trained on this dataset; and (3) VisualProcessBench, a human-annotated benchmark (2,866 samples, ~27K step labels) for evaluating step-wise correctness detection in multimodal reasoning. The central claim is that VisualPRM serves as an effective critic for Best-of-N (BoN) test-time scaling, and the paper demonstrates consistent BoN improvements across six models from three families at four scales (8B–78B).

## Strengths

1. **First large-scale multimodal process supervision dataset.** Prior process supervision datasets (PRM800K, MathShepherd, OmegaPRM) were text-only. VisualPRM400K at ~400K samples and ~2M step-level annotations fills a clear gap in the literature (Section 3.1, line 120, line 144).

2. **Consistent BoN improvements across all six evaluated policy models.** Table 2 shows VisualPRM improves MiniCPM-V2.6 (+8.0), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+3.7), InternVL2.5-8B (+8.4), InternVL2.5-26B (+8.9), InternVL2.5-38B (+6.3), and InternVL2.5-78B (+5.9) overall points across seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks. The consistency across families (MiniCPM, QwenVL, InternVL) and scales (8B–78B) is strong evidence for general effectiveness.

3. **PRM advantage over ORM and Self-Consistency widens with N.** Figure 4 shows the gap between VisualPRM and the next-best comparator grows from 1.5–2.4 points at N=8 to 3.1–4.3 points at N=128, demonstrating that PRMs scale better with test-time compute than simpler selection methods.

4. **Well-documented human annotation pipeline with quality controls.** Section 3.3 (lines 168–170) provides concrete details: 13 human experts (minimum university degree), 39 person-days, ~$37/person-day, per-split 10% author review with re-annotation of erroneous splits, annotators permitted to skip unfamiliar questions.

5. **Transfer to text-only reasoning.** Table 5 shows VisualPRM improves text-only benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond) for both LLMs (Qwen2.5 series) and MLLMs (InternVL2.5 series), including +9.4 on MATH-500 for InternVL2.5-8B, demonstrating general step-level quality estimation beyond multimodal patterns.

6. **Systematic ablation of PRM design choices.** Table 4 compares value-based vs advantage-based PRM, three aggregation methods (min/max/average), and early-stop vs no-early-stop training. The finding that value-based PRM with average aggregation and no early stop performs best (BoN: 41.1; VisualProcessBench: 62.0) provides actionable guidance for future work.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

### Major
None. The verified weaknesses are addressable and do not threaten the core claims.

### Minor

1. **Monte Carlo rollout model is not specified.** Section 3.1 (Eq. 1, line 136) states "the model is required to complete the solution" when sampling continuations to compute mc_i, but never identifies *which* model performs these rollouts. The solutions are stated to be generated by InternVL2.5 series models (line 130), but whether the same model (and which specific size) is used for MC rollouts is not stated. The quality of mc_i estimates depends critically on this choice, and the pipeline cannot be reproduced or assessed without this detail.

2. **No statistical significance or variance reporting.** All results in Tables 2–5 are reported as single numbers with no error bars, standard deviations, or confidence intervals. BoN evaluation at temperature 0.7 is inherently stochastic. While most improvements (e.g., +16.9 on MathVerse-VO, +14.6 on WeMath) are large enough to be clearly real, some (e.g., +0.7 on MMMU for InternVL2.5-78B) would benefit from variance estimates. This is a standard expectation for empirical papers.

3. **Missing discussion of potential overlap between training data source and evaluation benchmarks.** VisualPRM400K draws its questions from MMLR v1.1 (a multimodal reasoning preference dataset). The evaluation benchmarks include MMMU, MathVision, MathVerse, DynaMath, WeMath, MathVista, and LogicVista. VisualProcessBench itself draws questions from MMMU, MathVision, MathVerse, DynaMath, and WeMath. The paper does not discuss whether MMLR v1.1 contains questions overlapping with any of these benchmarks, nor does it provide decontamination analysis. This is a gap the authors should address — not because contamination is known to exist, but because the lack of discussion leaves an unresolved question about result integrity.

### Trivial

1. **Naming inconsistency for the source dataset.** The same dataset (Wang et al., 2024c) is referred to as "MMLR v1.1" (Section 3.1, line 130), "MMRP v1.1" (Abstract, line 21), and "MMPR" (Section 2, line 110). This should be standardized.
