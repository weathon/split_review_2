## Summary

This paper identifies and addresses a training-inference mismatch in tree-based speculative decoding: existing draft model training (EAGLE, HASS) uses sequential token targets, while inference uses tree-structured drafts. The authors propose TALF (tree-aware loss function), which aggregates cross-entropy over draft tree nodes generated by the target LLM during training, and SALF (stopping at low further gains), a dynamic tree construction algorithm with an early-stopping criterion provably based on monotonically decreasing probability sums. Experiments on 3 target LLMs, 5 benchmarks, and 2 sampling temperatures show consistent speedups over EAGLE-2 and HASS.

## Strengths

1. **Factorial ablation cleanly isolates SALF and TALF contributions (Table 2).** A 3×3 design (three loss functions × three tree-construction methods) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B shows that holding the tree method fixed, TALF improves τ over EAGLE-2 and HASS by 11–13% under beam/optimal/SALF trees. Holding the loss fixed, SALF improves speedup over optimal tree search by 14–19%. This provides direct evidence that each component independently contributes to the gains.

2. **Empirical diagnosis of the claimed training-inference mismatch (Section 3.1, Figure 2).** The paper quantitatively demonstrates that HASS degrades on lower-ranked tokens (5th-ranked: near-zero accuracy gain over EAGLE, high ECE), while TALF achieves ~5% accuracy improvement and ~0.05 ECE reduction. Figure 2(a) shows tokens ranked ≥5th constitute >10% of draft tree nodes, grounding the motivation in measured data rather than intuition.

3. **Provable monotonicity for SALF's stopping criterion (Theorem 1).** The probability sum of nodes selected for expansion is proven to be monotonically decreasing across drafting iterations when B < |Vocab|. This formal guarantee ensures the SALF threshold is a well-behaved tunable parameter rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.

4. **Comprehensive evaluation spanning 3 model families, 5 tasks, and 2 sampling temperatures (Table 1).** SALF & TALF achieve the highest speedup in all 30 configurations tested. Margins are especially pronounced on stronger models (e.g., +39.4% over EAGLE-2 on Llama3-8B at T=1), supporting the claim of generalizability.

5. **SALF threshold sensitivity reveals interpretable behavior (Table 4).** Sweeping th from 0.0 to 0.9 shows speedup peaks at th=0.5 (2.62× mean) while τ decreases monotonically. The peak is not at an extreme value, indicating the method finds a genuine operating point rather than merely trimming waste.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Training-epoch confound inflates reported improvements over EAGLE-2 for 2 of 3 models.** For Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B (§4.1, lines 196–197), the EAGLE/EAGLE-2 draft model is trained for 10 epochs, while HASS and TALF are fine-tuned from that checkpoint for an additional 3 epochs — a 30% longer training budget. Thus the gap between SALF&TALF and EAGLE-2 in Table 1 conflates (a) the tree-aware loss, (b) additional training iterations, and (c) SALF tree construction. The comparison between TALF and HASS is fair (same epochs), and TALF consistently outperforms HASS, so the core loss-function claim survives. But the headline speedups of "15.6–39.4% over EAGLE-2" (abstract, line 27; Table 1) are partially inflated. The DeepSeek-R1 setup (equal wall-clock time, line 197) partially addresses this, but the paper should either equalize epochs for all comparisons or acknowledge the confound explicitly and quantify its likely contribution.

2. **No ablation isolating the effect of dropping the feature regression loss (§3.2).** Unlike EAGLE and HASS, TALF removes L_reg = ||f_s − f_s^{(d)}||_1. The paper states "training solely on the token probability distributions across multiple nodes was sufficient for the model to learn to use features in an autoregressive manner, yielding better performance" (line 114) without a controlled experiment adding L_reg back. Since the draft model still produces features f_s^{(d)} that are fed back during multi-step drafting, feature alignment matters. Without this ablation, it is unclear whether TALF's gains come from the tree-aware structure, from dropping the regression loss, or from an interaction between the two. Given that HASS uses both losses and underperforms TALF, isolating the driver is essential for understanding the method.

### Minor

3. **No variance or confidence intervals reported for any speedup measurement.** All speedups in Tables 1, 2, and 4 are point estimates with no indication of run-to-run variability. Speculative decoding measurements can exhibit noise, especially at temperature > 0. However, single-run evaluation is the prevailing standard in the SpD literature, so this is a weakness that strengthens the paper if addressed, not a fatal gap.

4. **SALF threshold selection may involve test-set feedback (Table 4, lines 244–264).** Table 4 shows th=0.5 yields the highest mean speedup (2.62× vs 2.59× for th=0.6) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. The paper selects th=0.6 as default based on "more consistent performance improvements for the tested target LLMs" (line 264) without clarifying whether a held-out validation set was used. This is a minor concern since the sweep is informative and performance is robust across 0.4–0.7.

5. **Secondary training-inference mismatch from precomputed tree structure (lines 110–111).** The tree used for TALF training is precomputed by the target model and fixed across epochs. The draft model is trained on the target's tree structure, not its own. The paper does not discuss whether this creates a secondary mismatch when the draft model's posterior diverges from the target's. This is a reasonable discussion point but does not invalidate the empirical results.

### Trivial

6. The diagnostic analysis in Figure 2 compares EAGLE (10 epochs) with HASS (10+3 epochs) on lower-ranked tokens; some of the calibration gap could be due to training duration. However, the critical comparison for the paper's thesis — TALF vs HASS (both 10+3) — is fair, so this does not affect the central claim.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An estimate of the preprocessing cost for precomputing the target model's training tree (lines 110–111) relative to total training time would help practitioners assess deployability.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the most notable observation from the reviews is the tension between the paper's strong factorial ablation design and its training-epoch confound. The 3×3 factorial design (Table 2) convincingly shows TALF > HASS (fair comparison) and SALF > beam/optimal search (fair comparison), yet the headline comparisons against EAGLE-2 in Table 1 are weakened by unequal training budgets. This suggests the paper would benefit from explicitly separating "component-level contribution claims" (TALF over HASS, SALF over prior tree methods) from "system-level benchmark claims" (SALF&TALF as the best combined system), with appropriate caveats for the latter.

## Suggestions

1. **Ablate regression loss.** Add L_reg back into the TALF objective and compare τ and speedup to isolate whether the tree-aware structure or the removal of feature regression drives the gains.
2. **Equalize training epochs.** Either train EAGLE-2 for 13 epochs as well for Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B comparisons, or report a 10-epoch-trained TALF to separate the effect of the loss function from additional training.
3. **Report variance.** Provide multi-run (≥3) mean and std for at least the main DeepSeek-R1 configuration at both temperatures.
4. **Clarify threshold selection.** State whether the SALF threshold (th=0.6) was chosen using a held-out validation set, or acknowledge test-set feedback in the selection.
5. **Discuss secondary mismatch.** Address the potential issue of training on the target's tree structure versus the draft model's own tree structure.

## Score and Decision

The paper makes two well-motivated and empirically supported contributions (TALF loss for tree-aware training, SALF early-stopping for tree construction). The factorial ablation cleanly validates each component independently. The main concerns — a training-epoch confound that partially inflates EAGLE-2 comparisons for 2 of 3 models, and a missing ablation for the removal of the regression loss — are significant but addressable and do not undermine the core claims (TALF > HASS on fair comparisons, SALF > prior tree methods on fair comparisons). This is a solid paper with clearly demonstrable contributions that would benefit from additional rigor in the experimental design.
