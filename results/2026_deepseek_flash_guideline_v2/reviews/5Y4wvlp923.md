## Summary

This paper introduces Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric that quantifies whether trend or seasonal components are suppressed in contrastive time-series representations, and Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW), which uses the SDE-derived \(\Delta\) factor to dynamically reweight CoST's contrastive losses. The method targets semantic imbalance — the tendency of dominant components to suppress weaker ones — in time-series representation learning.

## Strengths

- **SDE provides a validated, monotonic diagnostic for component-level semantic imbalance.** Table 1 empirically validates the metric across 9 amplitude ratios (r=0.1 to r=10) on 1,000 synthetic sequences. When components are balanced (r=1), Δ ≈ -0.0276; when trend dominates (r=10), Δ = +1.1072; when periodicity dominates (r=0.1), Δ = -1.2934. This clean, graded relationship confirms that SDE captures which component is underrepresented and by how much — a capability not offered by prior methods (CoST, TS2Vec, TNC).

- **The paper reports a transparent negative result (direct SDE regularization fails).** Section 4.3 and Table 2 show that adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec degrades performance (e.g., Electricity horizon 168: MSE 0.449→0.508; ETTm1 horizon 288: 0.101→0.126). The authors explicitly acknowledge that SDE "fails to provide constructive optimization gradients when directly used as a regularizer." This honest documentation strengthens the empirical narrative by showing that a straightforward approach fails, motivating the more nuanced APW strategy.

- **The APW mechanism is architecturally lightweight.** The core modification (Eq. 4, line 101) is a scalar reweighting of CoST's existing loss terms: \(L = (1+\gamma\cdot\Delta)L_{\text{season}} + (1+\gamma'\cdot(-\Delta))L_{\text{trend}}\). No new encoders or model redesign is needed beyond the addition of a small MLP fusion layer.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The paper's central claim of "consistent gains" is contradicted by its own evidence.** The abstract promises "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" (line 10) and the conclusion claims "superior forecasting accuracy" (line 300). However, Table 3 shows CoST+APW performs *substantially worse* than vanilla CoST on the Electricity dataset at long horizons: horizon 168 (MSE 0.566 vs. 0.425, **33% worse**), horizon 336 (0.677 vs. 0.576, **18% worse**), and horizon 720 (1.010 vs. 0.911, **11% worse**). Results on Weather are also mixed, with CoST+APW losing at horizons 336 and 720 (0.266 vs. 0.256 and 0.299 vs. 0.278). The claim of "consistent gains" across multiple benchmarks is not supported — the method helps on some datasets (ETTm1, ETT2) but actively hurts on others. This overstatement is a significant credibility issue.

- **SDE values are not reported in the main results table despite being explicitly promised.** Line 263 states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance." However, Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE values appear anywhere. The paper further claims "consistently lower SDE values" (line 300) as a key result of the method, but provides zero evidence for this claim. Since SDE is the signature proposed metric and its reduction is supposed to validate the mechanism, this omission is a critical gap. Without SDE values, the reader cannot verify that APW actually reduces the semantic asymmetry it is designed to correct.

### Minor

- **No variance, confidence intervals, or multi-run statistics reported.** Every number in both tables is a point estimate. Many comparisons in Table 3 are within 0.001–0.005 MSE (e.g., ETT1 horizon 24: 0.040 vs. 0.040; ETTm1 horizon 96: 0.037 vs. 0.038). Without run-to-run variance, the reader cannot assess whether these differences are meaningful or within noise. This is especially problematic given that the paper makes strong comparative claims based on marginal differences.

- **"Without architectural changes" claim is imprecise.** The abstract (line 10) states the approach "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes." Yet Section 4.4.2 introduces "a small MLP \(g_\phi\) that maps the concatenated embeddings \([v(\mathbf{a})\|\|v(\mathbf{b})]\) to a composite embedding." This MLP is a learned component added to CoST, which technically constitutes an architectural modification. The claim should be clarified to acknowledge this addition, even if it is small.

- **Unreported hyperparameters.** The loss formulation (Eq. 4) uses hyperparameters \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\), but the paper never reports what values were used, whether they were tuned, or how sensitive results are to these choices.

- **Dataset labeling ambiguity in Table 3.** The label "ETT1" appears twice (lines 274 and 284) for what are likely different subsets (ETTh1 and ETTm1), but this is not clearly distinguished. Similarly, "ETT2" is ambiguous relative to the paper's description of "four subsets (ETT1h/2, ETTm1/2)."

### Trivial

- Minor inconsistency between Eq. 4 (which uses \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\) separately for seasonal and trend weights) and the Figure 1 caption (line 128), which simplifies both to the same \(\gamma\) by writing "the trend loss by \(1-\gamma\Delta\)."

## Suggestions

1. **Honestly characterize the results.** Acknowledge that APW helps on some datasets (ETTm1, ETT2) but degrades performance on Electricity (especially at long horizons) and some Weather horizons. Analyze why this happens rather than claiming "consistent gains."
2. **Report SDE values for CoST+APW vs. CoST in Table 3** (or a supplementary table) so readers can verify the claim of "consistently lower SDE values." This is essential evidence for the core mechanism.
3. **Add variance estimates** (multi-run means and standard deviations or confidence intervals) to all experimental tables.
4. **Clarify or remove the "without architectural changes" claim** — acknowledge that the MLP fusion layer is a minor architectural addition.
5. **Report the values of \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\)** used in experiments, ideally with an ablation study showing sensitivity to these hyperparameters.
6. **Disambiguate dataset labels in Table 3** (use ETTh1 and ETTm1 explicitly rather than "ETT1" twice).
7. **Consider whether the Electricity failure reveals a boundary condition** for APW (e.g., does APW over-amplify seasonal components that are already well-captured? Does SDE become unstable under certain spectral distributions?). Understanding this could substantially strengthen the paper.

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a genuine contribution with the SDE diagnostic (validated in Table 1) and honestly reports a negative result (Section 4.3). However, the central empirical claim of "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" is contradicted by the paper's own Table 3, where the proposed method substantially underperforms vanilla CoST on Electricity and is mixed on Weather. The claim of "consistently lower SDE values" is entirely unsupported — no SDE values appear in the results table despite being promised. These are not minor presentation issues; they directly undermine the paper's headline claims. The core idea has merit, but the paper as written does not support its key assertions.
