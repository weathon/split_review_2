## Summary

This paper proposes a Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) metric to diagnose semantic imbalance between trend and periodic components in contrastive time-series representation learning, and an asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) mechanism that dynamically reweights CoST's component-specific contrastive losses based on this metric. The approach is designed as a plug-in modification to existing frameworks like CoST, requiring no architectural changes.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem**: The paper clearly identifies a real and underexplored issue — that dominant time-series components (e.g., trend) can suppress weaker but semantically important ones (e.g., seasonality) during contrastive training. The preliminary SDE analysis on TS2Vec (Table 1) convincingly demonstrates this asymmetry across different amplitude ratios.
- **Simple and modular design**: The proposed method is easy to implement as a plug-in to CoST, requiring only a loss-reweighting step and a small MLP for composite embedding. This makes it practically accessible.
- **Honest negative result**: The paper transparently shows that direct SDE regularization fails to improve performance (Table 2), which is methodologically valuable even if it weakens one of the three claimed contributions.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Inconsistent experimental gains**: The results in Table 3 are mixed at best. On **Electricity**, CoST+APW performs worse than CoST and even TS2Vec at longer horizons (168, 336, 720), with MSE increasing from 0.425 to 0.566 at horizon 168 and from 0.911 to 1.010 at horizon 720. On **Weather**, CoST+APW is consistently worse than baselines at longer horizons (e.g., MSE 0.299 vs. 0.233 for TS2Vec at horizon 720). On ETT datasets the improvements are modest and inconsistent — CoST+APW loses at some horizons (e.g., ETT1 horizon 48 and 720). The claim of "consistent gains" is not supported by the data.

- **Bold formatting inconsistencies in Table 3 suggest selective reporting**: For example, at ETT1 horizon 24, both CoST+APW (0.04) and TS2Vec (0.039) are bolded despite TS2Vec having lower MSE. At Electricity horizons 168/336/720, the bolding correctly goes to other baselines, but this means the proposed method is rarely the best. The overall picture is that the method sometimes helps and sometimes hurts, without clear conditions under which it reliably improves.

- **One-third of claimed contributions fails**: The paper claims three contributions, but the SDE regularization approach (contribution related to Section 4.3) demonstrably does not work (Table 2). The remaining contribution — asymmetric weighting on CoST — yields inconsistent improvements, weakening the overall contribution claim.

### Minor

- **Inconsistent naming**: The title uses "Semantic Disentanglement Error" while Section 3.2 defines "Semantic Separability Error." This inconsistency persists throughout the paper and creates confusion.

- **Dataset labeling confusion in Table 3**: "ETT1" appears twice with different data, likely corresponding to ETTm1 and ETT1h, but this is never clarified. This makes it difficult to interpret the results accurately.

- **No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: The scaling hyperparameters γ and γ' are introduced but never ablated. Given that the method's behavior depends critically on these values, this is a notable omission.

- **Unclear role of the MLP in downstream tasks**: The composite embedding MLP is introduced for computing SDE, but it is unclear whether this MLP's output is used during downstream forecasting or only during pre-training. If only used for SDE computation, its joint training with CoST could introduce optimization instabilities.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A clearer analysis of **when** asymmetric weighting helps vs. hurts — e.g., characterization of datasets or signal properties where the method reliably improves performance.
- Evaluation on additional downstream tasks beyond forecasting (classification, anomaly detection) to support the broader claim of "representational robustness."
- Visualization of learned embeddings before and after APW to directly demonstrate that weak components are better preserved.

## Novel Insights

The identification of semantic asymmetry as a measurable and actionable property of contrastive time-series representations is a genuine contribution. The idea that one can quantify component recoverability via embedding-space arithmetic (SDE) and use it as a training signal is interesting. However, the finding that direct regularization via SDE fails (Table 2) while indirect reweighting partially succeeds is the most novel empirical observation — it suggests that diagnostic metrics and optimization objectives in representation space may require fundamentally different formulations, a distinction worth deeper investigation.

## Suggestions

- Conduct a thorough ablation study varying γ and γ' to understand sensitivity and identify regimes where the method consistently helps.
- Clarify the experimental setup — standardize dataset naming and provide details on how components are decomposed for SDE computation during training.
- Consider testing on datasets with known severe semantic skew to better demonstrate the method's value proposition, rather than standard benchmarks where the imbalance may be moderate.

## Score and Decision

The paper tackles an interesting and relevant problem, but the experimental evidence is insufficient to support the claims. The gains are inconsistent across datasets and horizons, one of the three proposed mechanisms is shown not to work, and the remaining mechanism provides only marginal and unreliable improvements. The idea has merit but needs substantially stronger experimental validation.
