## Summary

This paper systematically investigates how three major compression methods—quantization, distillation, and pruning—affect the reasoning capabilities of large reasoning models (LRMs), specifically DeepSeek-R1 and its distilled variants. Through performance benchmarking on four reasoning datasets and mechanistic interpretability analysis (difference of means and attribution patching), the authors identify which weight components are most critical for reasoning and demonstrate that protecting just 2% of all weights (final-layer MLP modules) during 3-bit quantization yields a 6.57% average accuracy improvement, substantially surpassing existing methods.

## Strengths

- **Comprehensive multi-paradigm benchmarking.** The paper evaluates dynamic quantization, four AWQ/GPTQ/GPTAQ/ANY variants at multiple bit-widths, two pruning methods (SparseGPT, AlphaPruning) at multiple sparsity levels, and distillation across four R1 distilled models (70B, 32B, 8B, 7B) on four reasoning benchmarks of varying difficulty. This is a genuinely thorough scope that goes well beyond what existing compression studies on LRMs provide.

- **Practical, validated finding on selective weight protection.** Table 4 demonstrates that protecting only the final-layer MLP modules (~2% of weights) during 3-bit AWQ quantization improves average accuracy by 6.57%, outperforming all 3-bit baselines in Table 1 by at least 4.77% (up to 23.17%). This is a simple, actionable, and well-verified result that could directly influence future compression practice.

- **Fine-grained interpretability beyond layer-level analysis.** The adaptation of difference-of-means steering vectors and attribution patching to compute per-module importance scores across all linear components in every layer provides more granular insight than prior layer-wise analyses. The finding that `up_proj` in the final layer is the single most important component, validated by the dramatic 16.3% accuracy drop from quantizing only that matrix (Table 3), is a compelling and well-supported claim.

- **Interesting collapse-point analysis.** The observation in Table 2 that collapse points correlate with benchmark difficulty (e.g., AIME 2024 collapses at 40-50% sparsity for Llama-70B while FOLIO collapses at 60-70%) is a novel and practically useful observation for practitioners deciding on compression levels.

- **Cross-model generalization claim.** The authors state that key findings generalize to non-R1 model families (referencing Appendix J), and within the main text show consistent patterns across R1 distilled Llama and Qwen variants, which strengthens confidence in the findings.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Limited validation of the selective protection finding.** Table 4 only demonstrates the protection mechanism for one model (R1-Distill-Llama-8B) with one quantization method (3-bit AWQ). Given that this is the paper's most practically impactful result and the core validation of the interpretability findings, showing this across at least one more model (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-7B or the 32B variant) and one more quantization method (e.g., 3-bit GPTQ) would substantially strengthen the claim. The absence of this broader validation makes the 6.57% figure feel somewhat cherry-picked.

- **Interpretability methodology relies on GPT-4o annotation with limited scale.** The reasoning behavior annotations (backtracking, uncertainty estimation, example testing, adding knowledge) are generated by GPT-4o over only 120 instances (30 per benchmark). While the paper references Appendix G for annotation robustness, the sensitivity of the entire interpretability pipeline to this annotation quality is a fundamental concern. If the GPT-4o annotations have systematic biases or inconsistencies, the steering vectors and subsequent importance scores could be misleading. A small human validation study on the annotations would strengthen confidence.

### Minor

- **Single LRM family in main experiments.** All primary experiments are on DeepSeek-R1 and its distilled variants. While the generalization claim is made, the main text does not substantiate it, making the findings narrower than the title suggests. Showing even one non-R1 LRM experiment in the main text would help.

- **Distillation knowledge vs. reasoning claim is partially intuitive.** The finding that "weight count has a greater impact on knowledge memorization than reasoning" (Takeaway 3.3) is supported by the comparison between Qwen (fewer params, better reasoning, worse MuSiQue) and Llama (more params, worse reasoning, better MuSiQue). However, this conflates model architecture and parameter count with distillation effect, and the comparison is between different model families, not a controlled experiment. The MuSiQue score differences could also reflect training data differences.

- **Pruning analysis is acknowledged as limited but underexplored.** The paper notes that 50% sparsity causes severe degradation and defers detailed pruning interpretability to Appendix I. While this is honest, it means one of the three compression paradigms is not fully addressed in the main text.

### Trivial

- The formula in Section 2.2 uses both $s_i^\ell$ and $s_i^c$ notation inconsistently (likely a parser artifact for the latter, but worth noting for clarity).

## Nice-to-Haves

- A visualization comparing the actual weight distributions of the final-layer `up_proj` across original, distilled, and quantized models could provide complementary insight into why this module is both important and vulnerable.
- An ablation study varying the number of annotation instances (120 vs. more/fewer) to show the stability of the importance scores would strengthen confidence in the interpretability pipeline.
- Discussion of computational cost of the interpretability analysis itself—how practical is it to run this on larger models?

## Novel Insights

The finding that distillation, not the base model's pre-trained weights, is the primary driver of the final-layer `up_proj` importance pattern (Section 4.3) offers a genuinely novel perspective on how reasoning capabilities emerge during distillation. The comparison of importance heatmaps between R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Llama-3.1-8B (Figure 2, lower portion) shows that the distilled model's critical weight structure is largely created by the SFT process rather than inherited. This has implications for understanding what distillation actually learns and could inform more targeted distillation strategies.

## Suggestions

- Expand the selective protection experiments (Table 4) to cover at least 2-3 additional model/method combinations to make the practical recommendation more robust.
- Include a small human validation study (e.g., 20-30 instances manually labeled for the four reasoning behaviors) to cross-check GPT-4o annotations.
- If space permits, add one non-R1 LRM experiment in the main text to substantiate the generalization claim directly.

## Score and Decision

The paper presents a solid, comprehensive study that combines benchmarking with interpretability analysis to produce actionable insights for LRM compression. The selective protection result is practical and well-validated within its scope, and the identification of the final-layer `up_proj` as a critical component is a genuine contribution. However, the limited breadth of validation for the main practical finding (only one model + one method), the reliance on GPT-4o annotation for the interpretability pipeline without human validation, and the confinement of main experiments to the R1 family hold the paper back from a stronger score. The methodology is incremental (adaptation of existing interpretability techniques) rather than novel, though the application domain and findings are valuable.
