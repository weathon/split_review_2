## Summary

This paper proposes LDP, a lightweight denoising autoencoder plug-in that enhances generalization of single-image super-resolution (SR) models to unseen degradations. LDP conditions a denoiser on LR high-frequency components to control degradation type, adds patch-dependent Gaussian noise to HR features, and enforces LR cyclic consistency. It can be applied either as a training-time loss to improve reconstruction quality or as an inference-time post-processing module via diffusion posterior sampling.

## Strengths

- **Broad applicability and consistent improvements**: LDP is evaluated on four diverse SR architectures (GAN-based FeMaSR, diffusion-based StableSR, Transformer-based SwinIR, Mamba-based MambaIR) and consistently improves performance across five degradation types and three real-world benchmarks. For example, StableSR+LDP gains +2.16 dB PSNR on Hybrid degradation (Table 3), and gains are also observed for diffusion posterior sampling across LDM, StableSR, ResShift, and UPSR (Table 5).

- **Lightweight and modular design**: With only 642k parameters and ~16 hours of training on a single GPU, LDP introduces minimal overhead. Its plug-in nature—requiring no architectural changes to the base SR model—is a significant practical advantage over methods like Lway or DualSR that require joint training or test-time fine-tuning.

- **Principled use of LR high-frequency conditioning**: The paper identifies a well-motivated solution to the conditioning problem—using LR high-frequency components rather than the full LR image avoids shortcut learning while being discriminative for different degradations. Table 1 and Table 2 demonstrate that LDP captures genuine degradation patterns rather than collapsing to trivial downsampling, which DRN effectively does.

- **Dual-mode application**: The ability to use LDP both as a training loss and as inference-time correction via posterior sampling provides practitioners with flexibility, and the experimental validation across both modes strengthens the contribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Inconsistent improvements with selective reporting**: In real-world benchmarks (Table 4), LDP frequently degrades certain metrics for specific models. For example, FeMaSR+LDP on DPED shows MUSIQ dropping from 49.14 to 44.07, CLIPIQA dropping on RealSRSet (0.6874→0.5683), and NIQE worsening on DPED and RealSRSet. Similarly in Table 5, several cells show degradation (e.g., LDM+LDP on RealSR shows NIQE worsening from 6.651 to 6.830). The paper acknowledges some cases but does not provide systematic analysis of when and why LDP fails, making it difficult to assess its reliability as a general-purpose plug-in.

- **Limited evaluation scope**: All experiments are restricted to ×4 scale factor. There is no evaluation on ×2 or ×8 scales, which is a notable gap for an SR method claiming broad applicability. The real-world test sets (RealSR, DPED, RealSRSet) are also quite small and may not be representative of diverse real-world conditions.

- **Fairness of DRN comparison in Table 1**: DRN uses only bicubic downsampling in its degradation branch, while LDP was explicitly trained with BSRGAN degradation patterns matching the test conditions. This creates a significant advantage for LDP. The comparison is informative but should be caveated more clearly, since DRN was not designed for these degradation types.

### Minor

- **Timestep sampling range [500, 1000] is under-justified**: The paper states this range is chosen "to align the noisy HR and LR features" but provides no empirical analysis of sensitivity to this choice. An ablation on timestep ranges would strengthen the methodological contribution.

- **Inference-time computational cost not quantified**: While LDP itself is lightweight, the posterior sampling mode requires gradient computation through LDP at each diffusion step. The paper does not report the wall-clock time overhead for diffusion models, which is practically important.

- **Table 2 interpretation needs clarification**: The "similarity between LR images generated by degradation models and downsampled SR images" is presented as evidence that LDP does not degenerate to downsampling. However, LDP having the *lowest* similarity to bicubic-downsampled SR in some cases (e.g., 28.41 vs DRN's 34.02 for "Down") could alternatively be interpreted as LDP producing less accurate degradation in these specific cases, since the ground truth degradation is indeed downsampling.

### Trivial

Minor formatting inconsistencies in figure captions (repeated "Figure 2" and "Figure 1" text) that are likely parser artifacts.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A comparison of wall-clock inference time overhead when using LDP as posterior sampling with diffusion models, which would help practitioners decide whether the quality gains justify the computational cost.
- Evaluation on additional scales (×2, ×8) and a broader set of real-world datasets.
- Analysis of how LDP's training degradation distribution (BSRGAN) affects generalization to degradations outside its training regime.

## Novel Insights

The paper's genuinely novel observation is that LR high-frequency components serve as an effective conditioning signal for degradation modeling within a denoising autoencoder framework—satisfying the criteria of being non-trivial (not the full LR image), discriminative across degradations, and easily obtainable. Combined with the insight that patch-dependent noise injection enables spatially-varying degradation modeling without assuming uniform degradation, this yields a practical and interpretable degradation model. The demonstration that this simple framework outperforms both explicit degradation branches (DRN) and adversarial training (DualSR) across diverse degradation types suggests that the denoising autoencoder formulation captures degradation structure more effectively than prior approaches.

## Suggestions

- Add ablation studies on the timestep sampling range [500, 1000] and number of CRB layers to justify these design choices.
- Include inference time comparison (with and without LDP posterior sampling) for diffusion-based SR models to quantify the practical overhead.
- Provide more systematic analysis of failure cases where LDP degrades metrics (particularly for generative models like FeMaSR) and characterize the conditions under which LDP helps versus hurts.
