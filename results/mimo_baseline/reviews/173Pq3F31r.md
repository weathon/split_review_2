## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models Expressive Performance Rendering (EPR) and Automatic Piano Transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content and global performance style representations. A diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module is additionally introduced to generate style embeddings directly from score content, enabling automated and stylistically appropriate rendering. The framework is trained using only sequence-aligned data, removing the need for fine-grained note-level alignment.

## Strengths
- **Well-motivated joint modeling**: The paper clearly identifies the duality between EPR and APT and makes a compelling case for joint modeling, drawing an informative parallel to ASR/TTS duality in speech. The formalization of EPR as a one-to-many mapping and APT as many-to-one is clean and well-articulated (Section 3.2).
- **Thorough disentanglement evaluation**: The paper goes beyond simply proposing disentangled representations and rigorously validates them through multiple complementary analyses: (1) performer/composer identification (Table 4 showing Style→Comp at 77.46% vs Cont→Comp at 29.99%), (2) 2D visualizations showing clear clustering by composer and performer (Figure 3), and (3) style transfer subjective tests (Figure 5). This multi-pronged validation is strong.
- **Alignment-free Seq2Seq formulation for EPR**: The paper successfully formulates EPR without note-level alignment, a practical contribution given that alignment tools struggle with expressive techniques like trills and mordents. The competitive APT results (Table 1, best average MUSTER score of 12.48) and strong EPR accuracy (Table 3, highest alignment rate of 92.27% for PSR) validate this approach.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: The paper combines objective metrics (variance, KL divergence, MAE, MUSTER, ScoreSimilarity) with subjective listening tests across multiple composers, representation analyses, and style transfer evaluations. The subjective study covers dynamics, tempo, style, and overall human-likeness dimensions.
- **The PSR module is a valuable practical contribution**: Generating appropriate style embeddings from score content alone addresses a real usability gap in existing EPR systems that require explicit composer/performer labels or manual parameter control.

## Weaknesses
### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Small subjective evaluation pool**: The listening test involves only 11 participants. While the objective metrics largely corroborate the subjective findings, a larger and more diverse participant pool would strengthen confidence in the subjective results, particularly given that subjective quality is a central claim of the work (naturalness of rendered performances).
- **Limited baseline coverage for EPR**: The paper compares against VirtuosoNet (2019) and DExter (2024) as alignment-based EPR baselines but does not include other recent methods such as Renault et al. (2023) which is discussed in related work as an unsupervised alignment-free approach. Including this baseline would better contextualize the performance of the alignment-free formulation.

### Minor
- **Unpaired performance data quality**: The unpaired performance dataset is constructed by transcribing YouTube piano cover videos using an off-the-shelf audio-to-MIDI model. While practical, this introduces transcription noise that is not quantified or ablated. An ablation studying the impact of unpaired data quantity/quality would strengthen the contribution.
- **Style vs. genre distinction**: The paper draws a distinction between style (expressive realization) and genre (structural/harmonic characteristics) in the introduction but the boundary between these concepts is not deeply explored empirically. The PSR module implicitly conflates them since it infers style from score content, which presumably encodes genre information.
- **Limited composer diversity in subjective evaluation**: The subjective test covers 5 composers (Bach, Rachmaninoff, Schubert, Scriabin, Ravel), all classical. This is acknowledged in future work but remains a constraint on generalizability claims.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation study isolating the contribution of joint training vs. unpaired data vs. disentanglement regularization would help readers understand which components drive the improvements.
- Analysis of failure cases or limitations of the PSR module (e.g., when score content is ambiguous regarding appropriate style).

## Novel Insights
The paper's key insight—that the inverse relationship between EPR and APT can be exploited through shared content representations to achieve mutual supervision—is well-supported by the empirical results. The observation that composer information is more strongly encoded in performance style than in score content (Style→Comp accuracy of 77.46% vs. Cont→Comp at 29.99%) is a genuinely interesting finding that suggests professional pianists systematically adapt their expression to match compositional conventions, providing empirical grounding for the PSR module's design philosophy. The era-level clustering of both actual and PSR-generated style embeddings (Figure 4) further suggests that the model captures meaningful musical structure beyond surface-level features.

## Suggestions
- Expand the subjective evaluation to at least 20-30 participants for more statistically robust conclusions.
- Add an ablation table showing the contribution of each training component (paired loss, unpaired loss, KL regularization) to EPR and APT performance.
- Include the unsupervised baseline from Renault et al. (2023) in EPR comparisons.

## Score and Decision
This paper presents a well-executed, clearly motivated contribution to a meaningful problem in music information retrieval. The joint EPR/APT framework is novel and well-designed, the disentanglement analysis is thorough, and the PSR module addresses a genuine usability gap. The experimental results are comprehensive and largely support the claims. The main limitations are the small subjective evaluation pool and limited baseline coverage, but these do not undermine the core contributions.
