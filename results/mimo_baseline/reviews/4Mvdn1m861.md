## Summary
The paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that repurposes SAM without architectural modifications. It introduces two components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that samples prompts from a probability distribution derived from cosine similarity between exemplar and image embeddings, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method achieves MAE of 16.25 on FSC-147 and 4.68 on CARPK among SAM-only training-free methods.

## Strengths
- **Novel use of output tokens for verification.** The key insight that SAM's decoder output tokens, which jointly encode semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, provide a richer representation for discrimination than raw image embeddings is well-motivated and supported by the PCA visualization in Figure 5 and the ablation in Table 5 (16.25 vs. 16.94 MAE).
- **Strong results on CARPK.** The method achieves 4.68 MAE on CARPK, outperforming all supervised methods (e.g., LOCA at 9.97, SAFECOUNT at 5.33) and most training-free methods, demonstrating the value of the approach in structured scenarios with clear object boundaries.
- **Well-structured ablation studies.** The paper provides ablations on verification metrics (Table 3), iteration/prompt counts (Table 4), and verification criteria (Table 5), each isolating a specific design choice and supporting the paper's claims about the contribution of each component.
- **Clear methodological presentation.** The distinction between SAM-only and SAM+α methods is a useful organizational framework, and the iterative probability distribution update mechanism (Figure 3) is well-explained.

## Weaknesses
### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Extremely high RMSE on FSC-147.** The method achieves RMSE of 133.11, which is barely better than raw SAM (137.50) and dramatically worse than training-based methods like LOCA (56.97), BMNet+ (91.83), and SAFECOUNT (85.54). This indicates severe variance in per-image errors—likely catastrophic failures on certain images—yet the paper never discusses or analyzes this. A method with MAE 16.25 but RMSE 133.11 has some images with errors exceeding 100, which undermines the practical reliability claims.
- **Overstated claims in the abstract.** The abstract claims "comparable performance to training-free approaches that require additional models," but on FSC-147, TFCAC (12.26 MAE) outperforms TokenCount (16.25) by ~24%. The abstract also claims "superior accuracy" without qualifying that this is only among SAM-only training-free methods. The CARPK result is genuinely strong, but the FSC-147 result is not "comparable" to TFCAC.
- **Insufficient hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The method has numerous hyperparameters: temperature τ (0.066), verification threshold (300), IoU threshold (0.95), patience (5), max iterations, and prompts per iteration. Only iteration count and prompts per iteration are ablated (Table 4). The verification threshold of 300 is particularly critical and appears dataset-specific—no sensitivity analysis or validation-set procedure is described for selecting it.

### Minor
- **No systematic computational cost comparison.** The paper mentions 1.69 seconds per image and claims TFCAC is slower, but provides no FLOPs, memory usage, or inference time comparisons across all baselines. For a method that emphasizes training-free efficiency, this is a notable gap.
- **TS-SS metric selection is weakly justified.** The paper argues that output tokens have dual semantic-positional nature requiring a hybrid metric, but doesn't compare TS-SS against other hybrid metrics or explain why the specific geometric formulation of TS-SS (triangle area × sector area) is particularly suited to this token space. The improvement over cosine similarity is modest (16.48 → 16.25).
- **One-shot counting results are underwhelming.** Table 2 shows MAE of 18.57, which is worse than TFCAC (15.15) and training-based methods like BMNet+ (16.89) and LOCA (12.53). The paper doesn't discuss why one-shot performance degrades relative to few-shot.

### Trivial
- The paper mentions "1.69 seconds per image" but doesn't specify whether this includes the exemplar processing or just query image processing.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A per-image error distribution plot (histogram of absolute errors) would illuminate the RMSE issue and reveal whether failures are concentrated in specific image types (e.g., very dense scenes, as acknowledged in the Discussion).
- Comparison of inference time across all methods on the same hardware would strengthen the efficiency claims.
- Analysis of failure cases with qualitative examples showing where and why the method fails catastrophically.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most novel observation is that SAM's output tokens provide a more discriminative representation for counting than image embeddings, because the decoder's bidirectional attention integrates prompt-specific semantic information with spatial context. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) compellingly shows that output tokens separate positive and negative instances where image embeddings fail. This insight—that the decoder's internal representations are underutilized in prior SAM-based counting work—is a genuine contribution that could inspire further research on leveraging foundation model internals for downstream tasks.

## Suggestions
- Add a detailed error analysis section examining the distribution of per-image errors, particularly identifying the high-error cases that drive the RMSE up. Include qualitative failure case analysis.
- Provide sensitivity analysis for the verification threshold (300) and temperature τ (0.066), showing how performance varies across a range of values.
- Tone down the abstract's claims to accurately reflect that the method is best among SAM-only training-free methods, with CARPK being the standout result.
- Add computational cost comparisons (inference time, peak memory) for all methods on the same hardware.

## Score and Decision
The paper presents an interesting idea with genuine insight about using SAM's output tokens, and achieves strong results on CARPK. However, the extremely high RMSE on FSC-147 (suggesting unreliable predictions on some images), overstated claims in the abstract, and insufficient hyperparameter analysis weigh against acceptance. The method is a reasonable contribution to the training-free counting literature but needs more rigorous evaluation and honest positioning of its results.
