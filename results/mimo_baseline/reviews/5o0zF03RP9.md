## Summary

The paper introduces IncentRL, a reinforcement learning framework that augments extrinsic rewards with a KL-divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s). The central claimed novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable updated online, eliminating manual tuning. Experiments are conducted on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar-v0, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8.

## Strengths

- **Clear conceptual framing.** The idea of penalizing misalignment between predicted and preferred outcomes via KL divergence is intuitive and well-motivated through cognitive/neuroscience analogies. The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow.
- **Bayesian β adaptation is a reasonable idea.** Automatically tuning the trade-off between extrinsic and intrinsic signals is a genuine pain point in intrinsic motivation methods, and the paper identifies this clearly.
- **Honest reporting of mixed results.** The MountainCar experiment shows that higher β values (0.3, 1.0) significantly degrade performance (-52.76% and -75.01%), which the paper acknowledges rather than hiding.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Theoretical contributions are trivial.** Proposition 1 (for small β, the optimal policy is unchanged) is a straightforward continuity argument. Proposition 2 (as β→∞, the KL term dominates) is immediate from the definition. Neither provides genuine insight into when or why IncentRL helps, nor do they characterize convergence rates or sample complexity improvements.

- **The Bayesian adaptation mechanism is critically underspecified.** This is the paper's primary claimed novelty, yet the method section never specifies: the prior over β, the likelihood model, or the posterior update rule. The experiments show adaptation across "rounds" (suggesting a batch scheme rather than online per-step adaptation), but the paper does not explain this structure. Without a clear algorithmic specification, the contribution cannot be reproduced or evaluated.

- **No comparison to relevant baselines.** The experiments compare only against vanilla Q-learning/DQN (β=0). There is no comparison to: (1) any intrinsic motivation method (curiosity-driven exploration, RND, ICM, VIME), (2) any reward shaping method, (3) any adaptive hyperparameter tuning method (meta-gradient, population-based training), or (4) any KL-regularized RL method. This makes it impossible to assess whether IncentRL's gains come from the specific mechanism or simply from having any auxiliary reward signal.

- **Experiments are too limited.** Only three environments are tested, all very simple. The toy MDP has 2 states, MountainCar is a standard introductory benchmark, and MiniGrid 8×8 is a small gridworld. Only 3 seeds are used for MountainCar and MiniGrid. The paper claims IncentRL improves "long-term planning" but provides no evidence on temporally extended or complex tasks.

### Minor

- **Bayesian adaptation results appear inconsistent with the narrative.** Figure 3 shows the posterior mean of β decreasing from ~0.11 to ~0.02 across rounds, moving *away* from the claimed "effective" β≈0.1 (dashed red line). The paper states the posterior "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β≈0.1)" but the data shows it concentrating near 0.02. This contradiction undermines the key claim.

- **Sensitivity to β is not resolved.** MountainCar shows dramatic performance swings across β values (from +18% at β=0.1 to -75% at β=1.0), and MiniGrid uses β=0.01 (not tested in other environments). This suggests the method is highly sensitive to β, which is precisely the problem the Bayesian adaptation is supposed to solve, yet the paper does not demonstrate that Bayesian adaptation reliably navigates this sensitivity.

- **The cognitive/neuroscience connections are superficial.** The analogies to dopamine RPE and the Free Energy Principle are stated but not developed into testable predictions or concrete mechanistic claims. They serve more as motivation than as substantive contributions.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A clear algorithmic box specifying the Bayesian β adaptation procedure (prior, likelihood, update rule, round structure).
- Comparison to at least one strong intrinsic motivation baseline (e.g., ICM, RND) on the same environments.
- Experiments on harder exploration benchmarks (e.g., Montezuma's Revenge, key-door tasks in larger grids, or multi-room MiniGrid levels).

## Novel Insights

The paper's core insight—that KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcomes can serve as a dense intrinsic signal—is conceptually appealing but not sufficiently novel given the extensive prior work on KL-regularized RL and reward shaping. The Bayesian adaptation of β is the most distinctive element, but its underspecification prevents it from being a clear contribution. The neuroscience connections, while interesting as motivation, do not yield novel testable hypotheses.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the Bayesian adaptation algorithm completely.** Provide the prior, likelihood, and update equations. Clarify whether adaptation is online (per-step) or batch (per-round), and justify the chosen structure.
2. **Add strong baselines.** At minimum, compare against ICM or RND on the same environments to demonstrate that IncentRL's gains are not simply from having any auxiliary reward.
3. **Resolve the inconsistency in Figure 3.** Either correct the narrative or explain why the posterior concentrating near 0.02 (not 0.1) is still evidence of successful adaptation.
4. **Scale up experiments.** Test on at least one challenging exploration benchmark to support claims about broader applicability.

## Score and Decision

The paper identifies a real problem (manual tuning of intrinsic motivation trade-offs) and proposes a conceptually clean framework. However, the theoretical analysis is trivial, the Bayesian adaptation mechanism (the main novelty) is underspecified, the experiments lack comparisons to relevant baselines, and the results are limited to simple environments. The inconsistency between the Bayesian adaptation results and the paper's narrative further weakens confidence in the core claims. The paper needs substantial additional work in specification, evaluation, and experimental rigor before it is ready for a top venue.
