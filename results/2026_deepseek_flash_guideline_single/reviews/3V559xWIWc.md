## Summary

This paper proposes two complementary improvements for tree-based speculative decoding: (1) TALF, a tree-aware loss function that trains the draft model by aggregating cross-entropy losses over a tree of candidate tokens (generated by the target LLM) rather than over individual sequences; and (2) SALF, a dynamic tree construction algorithm that stops drafting when the estimated aggregate probability gain falls below a threshold, balancing tree quality against drafting overhead. Experiments on Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B across five datasets show consistent wall-clock speedups over EAGLE-2 and HASS.

## Strengths

1. **Well-motivated problem with concrete evidence (Section 3.1, Figure 2).** The paper identifies a genuine limitation in prior work: HASS improves accuracy and calibration for 1st-ranked tokens but not for lower-ranked tokens (Figure 2b), even though lower-ranked tokens constitute ~45% of tree nodes (Figure 2a). This analysis directly motivates TALF and is specific, clean, and convincing.

2. **Clean ablation design isolating TALF and SALF contributions (Table 2).** The factorial crossing of three loss functions (EAGLE-2, HASS, TALF) with three tree construction methods (beam search, optimal tree search, SALF) unambiguously shows that TALF improves τ under any tree construction method, and SALF trades τ for lower drafting overhead to improve end-to-end speedup. This is the correct experimental design for this paper.

3. **Consistent improvements across a broad evaluation (Tables 1, 2).** The evaluation covers three model families, five datasets, and two temperatures. SALF & TALF beat both baselines in every single setting in Table 1. The non-greedy (temperature=1) results confirm the gains hold under higher-entropy conditions.

4. **Honest sensitivity analysis (Table 4).** A full sweep of the SALF threshold from 0.0 to 0.9 shows the expected τ-vs-speedup tradeoff, and the paper transparently notes that the best threshold for Deepseek (0.5) differs from the default (0.6) used for cross-model consistency.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Unequal training budget confounds EAGLE-2 comparison on two of three model families (Section 4.1).** For Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B, the EAGLE-2 draft model receives 10 epochs of training while HASS and TALF receive 10+3=13 epochs. The paper explicitly states: "we first trained the draft model for ten epochs using the original EAGLE loss. Then, we performed additional training with the ten-epoch-trained draft model using either HASS or TALF as a loss function for three epochs." The headline abstract numbers ("15.6–39.4% … over EAGLE-2") are therefore partially confounded by unequal training budget on these models. The Deepseek experiments use a fair time-matched 24-hour setup, and the HASS comparison (same 13-epoch budget) is clean, showing 6.5–24.4% improvement. But the paper's primary EAGLE-2 claim is overstated for two of three model families. The authors should either (a) train EAGLE-2 for an equal number of epochs, or (b) explicitly qualify which numbers are confounded throughout the paper.

2. **Removal of the feature regression loss is not ablated (Section 3.2).** TALF drops the regression loss (L_reg = ||f_s - f_s^{(d)}||_1) used by both EAGLE and HASS, training solely on tree-aggregated cross-entropy. The paper justifies this with one sentence: "training solely on the token probability distributions across multiple nodes was sufficient for the model to learn to use features in an autoregressive manner, yielding better performance." No ablation compares TALF with and without L_reg, no feature alignment metrics are reported, and no analysis shows whether dropping L_reg changes the number of drafting steps the model can sustain before drifting. This is a methodological gap: the reader cannot tell whether TALF's improvement comes from the tree-aware aggregation or simply from dropping the regression loss.

3. **Second training-inference mismatch from tree source (Section 3.2, Algorithm 1).** TALF fixes the tree structure in advance using the target LLM's probability distributions (for practical reasons — avoiding repeated target model invocations). During inference, the tree is constructed by the *draft model's* distributions (via SALF or beam search). If the draft model's distribution differs significantly from the target's (which it does, by design, for lower-ranked tokens — the whole motivation for TALF), then the training tree structure may not match the inference tree structure. This is a softer version of the same training-inference mismatch the paper criticizes in prior work. The paper does not discuss or analyze this gap. At minimum, evidence that target-generated and draft-generated trees overlap sufficiently would strengthen the argument.

### Minor

4. **No direct measurement of drafting overhead vs. verification time.** A key claim about SALF is that it reduces drafting overhead. The paper reports end-to-end speedup and τ, but never directly quantifies the drafting time vs. verification time. Table 2's observation that SALF trades τ for speedup implies reduced overhead, but the overhead itself is not measured. A breakdown would make the SALF contribution more concrete.

5. **No wall-clock training cost comparison.** TALF requires computing losses at multiple tree nodes, which increases training time and memory. The paper mentions tree attention to accelerate training but provides no actual wall-clock training time comparison between EAGLE, HASS, and TALF. This is relevant for practitioners considering adoption.

6. **Minor inconsistency in SALF threshold selection.** The paper reports that th=0.5 yields the highest mean speedup for Deepseek (Table 4), but uses th=0.6 as the default, citing "more consistent performance improvements for the tested target LLMs." This is acknowledged but not quantified — no table shows the threshold sensitivity for Llama2 or Llama3, so the "more consistent" performance claim cannot be verified by the reader.

### Trivial

7. **Figure 2b x-axis clarity.** The caption uses "1st to 5th" ranks but does not explicitly state that these are ranks from the draft model's predicted probability distribution (as opposed to the target model's). This is clear from context but could be stated directly.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Quantify the overlap between target-model and draft-model trees during training.** Showing convergence would address the second training-inference mismatch concern (Weakness #3) and strengthen the narrative about training-inference alignment.
- **Ablation adding the regression loss back into TALF.** This would cleanly resolve Weakness #2 and pinpoint which design choice drives the improvement.
- **Fix the training budget for EAGLE-2 on Llama models,** or at minimum report the improvement over EAGLE-2 after 3 additional epochs of EAGLE loss fine-tuning, to quantify the confound.
- **Measure drafting overhead directly** (time spent on the draft model vs. the target model) to substantiate the SALF claim.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.

- "Theorem 1's practical significance is overstated" — The theorem is stated as a formal guarantee of monotonic decrease; the paper does not overclaim its practical implications, and the empirical evaluation (Table 4) already answers the practical question about the rate of decrease.
- "SALF threshold is not dimensionless" — The paper provides a full threshold sweep (Table 4), which is the standard empirical way to handle this. The criticism is speculative and the concern is addressed by the existing sensitivity analysis.
- "No analysis of where SALF cuts nodes (depth vs. breadth)" — This is a speculative question without evidence that a deeper analysis would reveal anything beyond what Table 4 already shows.
- "Different training procedures for Llama vs Deepseek is a confound" — The paper clearly explains why different procedures are used and the Deepseek setup is specifically designed to be fair (time-matched). This is a design choice, not a flaw.
- "The comparison against HASS is more modest than EAGLE-2 comparison" — This is a restatement of results, not a weakness of the paper.
- Various formatting/style nitpicks that are parser artifacts, not author errors.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions

1. Add an ablation where L_reg is added back into TALF, and report feature alignment metrics (e.g., cosine similarity between f_s and f_s^{(d)}) across training.
2. Provide a breakdown of inference time into drafting vs. verification components.
3. Report training wall-clock time for EAGLE, HASS, and TALF on at least one model.
4. Add a cross-model SALF threshold sensitivity table (at least for Llama3-8B) to justify the th=0.6 default choice.
5. Either re-run the Llama2/3 EAGLE-2 experiments with 13 epochs or explicitly qualify the affected numbers throughout the paper (abstract, introduction, conclusion). De-emphasize the EAGLE-2 comparison relative to the HASS comparison.
