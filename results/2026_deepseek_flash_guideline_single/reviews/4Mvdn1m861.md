## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework that uses only SAM (no auxiliary models). It has two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that samples point prompts from a similarity-based multinomial distribution, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with the TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive/negative instances. Evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK.

## Strengths

- **Single-foundation-model design.** Unlike TFCAC (which requires DINO as an additional encoder), TokenCount uses only SAM without architectural modifications or auxiliary models. This is a genuine architectural simplification if accuracy can be maintained (Section 3, Table 1).

- **Clean ablation of verification metrics.** Table 3 systematically compares dot product, cosine similarity, L2 distance, and TS-SS under otherwise identical conditions, allowing the reader to isolate the effect of the metric choice on performance.

- **Strong results on CARPK.** MAE of 4.68 beats all training-based methods shown (best training-based: SAFECOUNT at 5.33) and is close to TFCAC's 4.39 (Table 1). This is the paper's most concrete empirical achievement.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Abstract contains a factual claim contradicted by the paper's own data.** The abstract states the method achieves "superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based… counting methods." However, Table 1 shows four training-based methods with **lower** MAE on FSC-147: BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), and LOCA (10.79). The same overclaim appears in the Conclusion ("superior performance compared to training-based methods"). While the method does outperform some training-based methods (FamNet, CFOCNet+, Counting-DETR), the blanket claim of outperforming "existing training-based methods" is factually incorrect given the paper's own evidence.

- **No variance or error bars despite a stochastic sampling pipeline.** The probabilistic prompt generation stage samples from a multinomial distribution, making the process inherently stochastic. Yet every result is reported as a single point estimate with no standard deviation, no confidence intervals, and no mention of the number of runs. This is a structural gap because: (a) the ablation study's headline improvement — TS-SS (16.25) vs. cosine (16.48) vs. L2 (16.53) — differ by only 0.23–0.28 MAE, which could be within the noise of the stochastic process; (b) the RMSE values across all ablation conditions (132.69–135.41) are even more compressed, further suggesting noise may dominate.

- **The output token verification mechanism is underspecified.** The paper states that output tokens are used as "the primary verification criteria" (Section 3.2) but does not specify: (a) which of SAM's four decoder output tokens are used and how they are extracted for each candidate prompt; (b) how the "exemplar output token" is obtained; (c) what the decision rule is for classifying a candidate as positive vs. negative (fixed threshold? ratio?); (d) what the "token verification threshold to 300" (Section 4.1) means — its units, range, and derivation are unexplained. Without these details, the core verification mechanism cannot be fully assessed or reproduced.

- **Computational efficiency claims are unsupported by comparative data.** The paper claims "inference time that is more than twice as fast" as TFCAC (Section 4.2) and "superior computational efficiency compared to state of the art" (Section 5), but only reports its own runtime (1.69 seconds per image on FSC-147). No runtime numbers are given for any baseline (TFOC, C-Anything, TFCAC, OmniCount, etc.). Furthermore, the best configuration uses ~1,438 prompts per image on average (Table 4, 16 iterations × 128 prompts), each requiring a SAM decoder forward pass — this is a non-trivial computational cost. Without baseline runtimes measured in the same environment, the efficiency claims remain unsubstantiated.

### Minor

- **"Comparable to TFCAC" framing is misleading.** The abstract says the method achieves "comparable performance to training-free approaches that require additional models." On FSC-147, TFCAC achieves MAE 12.26 vs. TokenCount's 16.25 — a ~33% gap. The CARPK gap is smaller (4.39 vs. 4.68, ~6.6%), but FSC-147 is the primary benchmark with 147 categories. Calling a 33% gap "comparable" obscures the actual ranking.

- **The 12-exemplar protocol for CARPK is not justified.** The paper states "we randomly selected 12 objects from the training set to use as exemplars" for CARPK (Section 4.1). The standard protocol in the class-agnostic counting literature typically uses 3 exemplars. The paper provides no justification or ablation showing how performance varies with exemplar count, raising the question of whether the strong CARPK results are driven by the method or by the enlarged exemplar set.

- **Selective comparison to the weakest training-based method.** Section 4.2 highlights that the method "is superior to the training-based method Counting-DETR's 16.79" without mentioning BMNet+, SAFECOUNT, PseCo, and LOCA which all outperform TokenCount on FSC-147. This cherry-picked comparison gives a misleading impression of the method's standing relative to training-based approaches.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface a core tension: TokenCount's most architecturally novel idea — using SAM's output tokens (rather than image embeddings) for verification — is supported by only thin empirical evidence. The ablation shows output token space outperforms image embedding space by 0.69 MAE (16.25 vs. 16.94, Table 5), and TS-SS improves over cosine similarity by 0.23 MAE (Table 3). Without error bars, it is impossible to tell whether these margins reflect a genuine advantage or stochastic noise. Meanwhile, the paper's strongest quantitative result (CARPK MAE 4.68) may be confounded by the non-standard 12-exemplar protocol. This creates a gap where the paper's headline claims (superiority over all training-based methods, computational efficiency) are unsupported, while its defensible contributions (SAM-only architecture, iterative probabilistic prompting) are modest relative to state of the art.
