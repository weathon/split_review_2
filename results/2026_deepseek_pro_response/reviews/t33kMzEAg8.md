## Summary
SWIREASONING proposes a training-free inference framework that dynamically alternates between explicit chain-of-thought reasoning and latent (continuous embedding) reasoning, guided by entropy-trend signals. A switch count controller caps the number of mode transitions to suppress overthinking and enable early answering. The paper reports consistent accuracy gains of 1.8–3.1% across four reasoning LLMs and eleven benchmarks, with larger gains on harder problems, plus claimed token efficiency improvements under constrained budgets.

## Strengths
- **Asymmetric dwell window design with sound motivation**: The paper uses asymmetric dwell windows (immediate Latent→Explicit exit vs. minimum-dwell Explicit→Latent re-entry) grounded in the distinct roles of the two modes — latent reasoning is divergent and should be exited quickly when confidence recovers, while explicit reasoning is convergent and needs protection from oscillation. This is validated by the window-size ablation (Table 3, W=512 optimal) and reflects genuine engagement with the different dynamics of exploration vs. exploitation.
- **Consistent accuracy gains across models, scales, and domains, concentrated on harder problems**: On mathematics and STEM (Table 1), SWIREASONING improves average Pass@1 accuracy by +2.03% (Qwen3-8B), +2.68% (Qwen3-1.7B), and +1.80% (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B). Gains are largest on difficult benchmarks: +5.00%/+5.00% on AIME24/25 for Qwen3-1.7B, +4.04% on GPQA Diamond for Qwen3-32B (Table 4), and +18.18% on hard LeetCode problems (Table 5). The evaluation spans 11 benchmarks, 4 model scales/families, and 4 domains.
- **Switch count control integrates naturally with the switching framework**: Each Latent→Explicit switch marks a natural checkpoint where partial reasoning has been consolidated, making switch boundaries the right place for forced early answering under budget constraints (Section 3.4). This addresses the overthinking problem without adding a separate mechanism.
- **Revealing ablation on signal mixing asymmetry**: The β₀ ablation (Table 2) reveals that the exit bias matters enormously — at β₀=0.0, AIME24 accuracy collapses to 8.33% — while the entrance bias α₀ shows a broad plateau (α₀∈[0.4,0.9] all work). This asymmetric sensitivity is a non-obvious empirical finding with practical implications.
- **Pass@k analysis shows improved sample efficiency**: SWIREASONING reaches peak accuracy with k*=13 vs. 46 for CoT on AIME24 (~72% fewer samples) and k*=16 vs. 22 on AIME25, indicating more diverse and correct reasoning trajectories (Section 4.4, Fig. 5).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Efficiency comparison methodology is underspecified and potentially asymmetric**: The paper's headline efficiency claims (57–79% improvement in the abstract) rely on comparing efficiency curves (Fig. 2, Fig. 4). For SWIREASONING, these curves are generated by varying the switch-count budget C_max (line 226). For baselines (CoT, CoT Greedy, Soft Thinking), the paper does not describe how their corresponding efficiency curves are produced. The efficiency metric Acc_m(ℓ) requires accuracy at specific token counts ℓ; if baselines lack a budget-control parameter, the paper must specify whether baseline traces were truncated at various lengths, run at different max-token limits, or generated by some other method. Without this information, the reader cannot assess whether the comparison is symmetric or whether the efficiency gains reflect genuine reasoning efficiency rather than the artifact of comparing a method with built-in early stopping against methods that run to completion. This directly impacts the paper's most prominent claim.
- **No statistical significance or variance reported for any result**: The paper reports Pass@1 accuracy to two decimal places across all tables with gains typically in the 1–5% range, but never reports standard deviations, confidence intervals, or any measure of variance. Several benchmarks have small test sets (AIME 2024/2025: 30 problems each). On AIME 2024 with 30 examples, an accuracy shift from 75.83% to 79.17% represents roughly 1 more question correct. Without knowing whether results reflect a single sampling run or an average over multiple runs, the reader cannot distinguish signal from noise. Pass@k curves (Fig. 5) also lack error bands. For a paper whose contribution rests on modest accuracy improvements, statistical reporting is essential.
- **Soft Thinking baseline is anomalously weak on DeepSeek without explanation**: Soft Thinking — the only training-free latent reasoning baseline and the most direct point of comparison — achieves 51.52% average accuracy vs. 59.46% for CoT on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (Table 1), a degradation of nearly 8 points. This is substantially worse than even CoT Greedy (52.49%). The paper never discusses this degradation or whether Soft Thinking's hyperparameters were appropriately tuned for reasoning-specialized models. Since SWIREASONING's hyperparameters were extensively tuned (Tables 2–3), an untuned vs. tuned comparison would be misleading. If Soft Thinking genuinely performs poorly on reasoning models, that is itself an interesting finding that should be analyzed, not silently presented.

### Minor
- **No ablation isolating the switching mechanism from signal mixing**: The paper sweeps α₀ and β₀ independently (Table 2) but never reports performance with both α₀=1.0 and β₀=1.0 simultaneously (i.e., pure switching without any signal mixing). The independent sweeps provide partial evidence — β₀=1.0 alone yields 60.04% average, suggesting the switching mechanism contributes even without exit mixing — but a direct "no signal mixing" ablation would cleanly separate the contributions of switching logic from embedding manipulation. This is addressable in rebuttal.
- **Entropy criterion uses an arbitrary reference point without justification**: The switch criterion (Eqs. 2–3) compares current entropy against the block's initial entropy H̄. The paper does not justify why crossing this particular reference point is a meaningful confidence signal — entropy can fluctuate for many reasons during generation. The dwell window partially mitigates this for E→L but not for L→E.
- **Computational overhead of latent steps is not discussed**: Each latent step requires computing a probability-weighted sum over the full vocabulary embedding matrix (Eq. 1). For large vocabularies, this is non-trivial. The paper should report wall-clock time or FLOP overhead relative to standard CoT, especially given that efficiency is a core claim.
- **Linear scheduling of mixing ratios is arbitrary and unablated**: The scheduling α_t = α₀ + (1−α₀)t/T_max (line 107) couples switch timing with mixing intensity, but the choice of linear scheduling is not justified or ablated against alternatives.

### Trivial
- **No limitations section**: The paper would benefit from explicitly acknowledging limitations (sensitivity to β₀, reliance on reasoning-specific tokens like ⟨think⟩/⟨/think⟩, heuristic nature of the entropy criterion).
- **Pass@k analysis limited to one model and two benchmarks**: Extending to additional models would strengthen the diversity claim.

## Novel Insights
The asymmetric sensitivity of entrance vs. exit signal mixing (β₀ swings accuracy by 40+ points while α₀ shows a broad plateau) is a genuinely novel empirical finding. It reveals that *exiting* latent mode cleanly is far more delicate than *entering* it — an insight that could inform future work on latent reasoning and is not obvious from the method's design. The paper also provides converging evidence from multiple angles (Pass@1, Pass@k, efficiency curves, scaling to 32B, cross-domain) that alternating between explicit and latent reasoning yields Pareto improvements over either mode alone, which is a substantive contribution to the training-free latent reasoning literature.

## Suggestions
- Clarify in the main text how baseline efficiency curves are generated. If baselines are truncated at various token counts, state this explicitly with the truncation methodology.
- Report standard deviations or confidence intervals for all main results, at minimum over 3 independent runs. For small benchmarks like AIME, bootstrapped CIs would be appropriate.
- Add an explicit ablation with α₀=β₀=1.0 (no signal mixing, pure switching) to isolate the switching mechanism's contribution. This is a single experiment that would substantially clarify the method's source of gains.
- Discuss or analyze the Soft Thinking degradation on DeepSeek; if it reflects genuine limitations of pure latent reasoning on reasoning-specialized models, this strengthens the paper's motivation.
