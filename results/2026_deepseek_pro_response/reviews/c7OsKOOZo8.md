## Summary
This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading that generates lesion proposals internally via a Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module — which uses auxiliary classifiers to derive CAM-based evidence maps and selects top-K regions as "lesion proposals" — and fuses them across views through a Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion (LGRF) module with gated mixture-of-experts routing. The key claimed contribution is eliminating dependence on costly external annotations (lesion maps, vessel masks, OD coordinates) while maintaining competitive accuracy. On MFIDDR (4-view) and DRTiD (2-view) benchmarks, the annotation-free variant matches or surpasses several externally informed baselines.

## Strengths
- **Novel architectural integration with validated component contributions**: GALP + LGRF combines auxiliary-classifier-derived CAM proposals with cross-view MoE routing in a coherent end-to-end pipeline. The ablation (Table 4) cleanly demonstrates each component matters: removing GALP drops accuracy from 83.9% to 82.7%, removing the expert pool drops to 82.6%, and removing LGRF entirely drops to 82.3%.
- **Strong empirical results on two benchmarks**: On MFIDDR (Table 1), "Ours (w/o lesion)" achieves 83.9% accuracy / 70.9% Kappa / 83.5% F1, outperforming all end-to-end baselines (ETMC 81.5%, MVCINN 80.1%) and surpassing externally informed methods like CVSA (82.6%, requires vessel masks) and LFMVDR (82.2%, requires lesion maps). On DRTiD (Table 3), it achieves 76.0% accuracy, outperforming CrossFIT (75.6%, requires OD/macula coordinates).
- **Grade-wise analysis shows clinically meaningful results**: Table 2 demonstrates strong performance on Grade 3 (severe DR, F1=74.1%), matching externally informed methods — clinically important since accurate severe-DR detection is critical for preventing vision loss.
- **Practical motivation is well-articulated**: The paper clearly frames the annotation bottleneck in DR screening and motivates self-derived proposals as a practical solution, setting up a clear research direction.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **No validation that GALP proposals correspond to actual lesions**: The paper's central framing is built on "lesion proposals," but proposals are generated from CAMs of classifiers trained with image-level DR grade labels only. There is no mechanism ensuring high-activation regions correspond to lesions rather than vessels, optic disc, or spurious correlations. The paper states (§3.2): "Given that DR grades are predominantly determined by lesion evidence, we compute grade-conditioned regions... These regions are interpreted as grade-related (i.e., lesion) areas." This equivalence is asserted, not demonstrated. MFIDDR ships with lesion segmentation masks (§4.1: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), making a validation experiment entirely feasible — yet none is performed. Without lesion-level validation (e.g., IoU/recall of proposals against ground-truth lesion masks), the paper's core terminological and conceptual claim remains unsubstantiated.

- **No variance characterization — reported differences are uninterpretable**: All results are reported as single-point estimates with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical tests. Several key margins are under 1%: on DRTiD the gap over CrossFIT is 0.4% (Table 3); on MFIDDR "Ours (w/o lesion)" at 83.9% trails WGLIN at 84.2% by 0.3% (Table 1). On a test set of ~2,500 eyes, the standard error on accuracy is approximately 0.7%, so these margins could easily fall within run-to-run variance. Without any characterization of variance, the claimed improvements cannot be meaningfully interpreted.

- **Incomplete ablation — no pure backbone baseline**: Table 4 ablates GALP, the expert pool, and LGRF, but never reports the performance of a plain Swin-B backbone with simple multi-view concatenation and a classifier. The "w/o LGRF" variant (82.3%) still concatenates lesion proposals with cross-view tokens — it is not a clean lower bound. The reader cannot determine what fraction of the gain over existing end-to-end baselines (e.g., MVCINN at 80.1%) comes from the proposed modules versus the Swin-B backbone itself, ImageNet pretraining, or other implementation details.

- **Claims of interpretability are unsubstantiated**: Contribution (2) explicitly claims "superior robustness and interpretability," but the paper provides zero qualitative analysis — no attention map visualizations, no comparison of GEMs against actual lesions, no interpretability study of any kind.

### Minor
- **Adjacent-view-only cross-view fusion lacks justification**: LGRF fuses each view only with its cyclic adjacent view (§3.3: "Cross-view fusion is performed between the current view and its adjacent (cyclic) view"). Non-adjacent views (e.g., view 1 and view 3 in a 4-view setup) are never directly fused. No justification or ablation for this design choice is given.
- **50% token retention is high for "lesion proposals"**: With α=0.5, half the feature-map regions are retained as proposals. This is quite diffuse for something framed as focused lesion attention, weakening the conceptual distinction from standard downsampled feature maps.
- **Preprocessing consistency with baselines unclear**: MFIDDR preprocessing uses "code in Karthik et al. (2019)" (a Kaggle pipeline). It is not stated whether all compared baselines were identically preprocessed or whether their reported numbers come from different preprocessing.
- **CAM notation in Eq. (3) could be clearer**: The notation uses s_n as both subscript and superscript, and the relationship between the class-specific weight vector and the predicted grade needs clarification for reproducibility.

### Trivial
- The conclusion restates claims without qualification, overstating what has been demonstrated given the evidential gaps.
- Focal loss usage is not ablated against cross-entropy, though focal loss is a reasonable default for class-imbalanced DR data.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Joint hyperparameter optimization rather than one-at-a-time tuning (Fig. 3) would strengthen confidence in the chosen defaults.
- Discussion of computational cost (parameters, FLOPs, inference time) relative to baselines would support the "scalable solution for large-scale DR screening" claim.
- Failure case analysis — are there grade/lesion types where GALP proposals systematically fail?
- Visual examples comparing GEMs against ground-truth lesion locations to support the qualitative claims.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. ## Suggestions
- **Validate lesion proposals against ground-truth masks**: Use MFIDDR's lesion segmentation masks to measure overlap between GALP top-K regions and actual lesions. Report IoU, precision, and recall. This is the single highest-leverage experiment for the paper.
- **Add variance reporting**: Run 3–5 seeds and report mean ± std for all main results. Apply a statistical test (e.g., McNemar's or paired bootstrap) for key comparisons.
- **Add a pure backbone baseline**: Report Swin-B + multi-view concatenation + classifier to establish a clean lower bound.
- **Add qualitative analysis**: Show GEMs overlaid on fundus images alongside ground-truth lesion locations, and compare against attention maps from a baseline (e.g., MVCINN).
