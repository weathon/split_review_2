## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework that works with SAM without architectural modifications or auxiliary models. It combines two components: (1) iterative probabilistic prompt generation that samples prompts from a similarity-derived probability distribution, adaptively exploring the image while avoiding redundant sampling, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's internal decoder output tokens with a geometric TS-SS (Triangle Similarity-Sector Similarity) metric to distinguish positive from negative object instances. Evaluated on FSC-147 (MAE 16.25) and CARPK (MAE 4.68), the method achieves the best results among SAM-only training-free methods and outperforms all training-based methods on CARPK.

## Strengths

- **Output token space shows measurable improvement over image embedding space for verification.** Table 5 directly quantifies this: using output tokens yields MAE 16.25 vs. 16.94 when using image embeddings for the same prompt-based pipeline. Figure 5 provides qualitative support through PCA visualizations showing cleaner separation of positive/negative instances in output token space.

- **Iterative probabilistic prompt generation demonstrates consistent improvement with more prompts.** Table 4 shows a clear monotonic trend: at 16 iterations, MAE drops from 21.74→18.13→16.25 as prompts per iteration increase from 32→64→128. The probability update mechanism (zeroing out positive regions, reducing negative ones) provides a principled way to disperse prompts across undiscovered objects.

- **Strong CARPK results that outperform all listed training-based methods.** The method achieves MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13 on CARPK (Table 1), beating BMNet+ (5.76), SAFECOUNT (5.33), and LOCA (9.97). The paper offers a plausible explanation — SAM excels at segmenting objects with clear boundaries in consistent viewpoints, characteristic of aerial parking-lot imagery.

- **Training-free operation using only SAM without auxiliary models.** Unlike TFCAC (which adds a DINO encoder) or OmniCount (which combines semantic-geometric priors), the proposed method uses SAM alone. This enables computational efficiency (1.69s/image on an A100) and distinguishes the method within the training-free landscape.

- **Reasonable ablation structure.** The experiments separately ablate the verification metric (Table 3), iteration count and prompts per iteration (Table 4), and the choice of vector space — output token vs. image embedding (Table 5), allowing the reader to attribute performance to specific design choices.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Missing ablation of probabilistic prompt generation against simpler baselines.** The paper criticizes grid and superpixel prompting methods in the Introduction (line 30) and Related Work (lines 73–79), claiming the probabilistic approach is superior. Yet there is no experiment that replaces the probabilistic prompt generation with a grid, superpixel, or random-sampling baseline while keeping the output-token verification stage fixed. Without this ablation, we cannot determine whether the probabilistic prompt generation is a genuine contributor or an elaborate alternative that would perform similarly. This directly undermines the claimed contribution of one of the two core components.

- **Core innovation gains are small and lack statistical characterization.** The TS-SS metric improves MAE by only 0.23 over cosine similarity (16.25 vs. 16.48, Table 3), and the output-token verification improves MAE by 0.69 over image-embedding verification (16.25 vs. 16.94, Table 5). Neither result is accompanied by standard deviations, confidence intervals, or any statistical test. Given that MAE is averaged over 1,190+ test images, a shift of 0.23 could fall within one standard error. The paper's core narrative — that TS-SS breaks through a "performance plateau" and that output tokens are distinctly superior — rests on evidence too thin to carry that weight.

- **The angular↔semantic, magnitude↔positional mapping in output token space is unsubstantiated.** The paper asserts (lines 156–157) that SAM's output tokens jointly encode semantic information in angular direction and positional information in vector magnitude, and uses this claim to motivate the TS-SS metric. No empirical or theoretical evidence is provided for this mapping. Figure 5 shows cluster separability in PCA space but does not demonstrate that angular and magnitude axes correspond to semantic and positional content respectively. This is a foundational claim for the method's motivation that goes unvalidated.

- **Negative-region probability decay rule is unspecified, impairing reproducibility.** Section 3.1 states that regions verified as negative have their probability values "decreased" (line 148) but never specifies the decay factor or formula. Figure 3 shows some values changing across iterations, but the rule governing those changes is absent from the text. A reader cannot reimplement the core iterative algorithm from the paper as written.

### Minor

- **Abstract overstates accuracy.** The abstract claims the method "achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." However, Table 1 shows TFCAC achieves MAE 12.26 on FSC-147 and 4.39 on CARPK — both better than the proposed method. The paper acknowledges this in the body (line 230: "inferior in performance to the state-of-the-art TFCAC"), but the abstract omits this qualification entirely.

- **The verification threshold of 300 is stated without normalization context.** The implementation details (line 180) state the threshold as 300 without explaining what range TS-SS values take, making the number uninterpretable and the method not fully reproducible on this point.

- **Discussion section contains a syntactically ambiguous statement about scalability.** Line 305 states "This limitation affects scalability for real-time applications and large datasets" where "this limitation" appears to reference the preceding sentence about performance degradation on small/dense objects, but the sentence is positioned immediately after a claim about computational efficiency, creating ambiguity about whether the method itself is being called unscalable.

### Trivial

- CARPK exemplar selection ("randomly selected 12 objects," line 174) is described too vaguely — it is unclear whether these 12 exemplars are shared across all test images or selected per-image, which matters for reproduction.

- No runtime comparison table is provided against competitors (TFCAC, TFOC) despite the body text claiming inference time advantages (line 230: "more than twice as fast").

## Nice-to-Haves

- A clean ablation swapping the probabilistic prompt generation for a grid baseline while keeping output-token verification fixed would substantially strengthen the contribution claim and is the highest-leverage improvement available.

- Reporting per-image breakdowns showing on which types of images (dense vs. sparse, small vs. large objects) TS-SS helps most over cosine similarity would add insight beyond aggregate numbers.

- Reporting the TS-SS value range observed across experiments to contextualize the threshold of 300.

- Adding standard deviations or confidence intervals to Tables 3 and 5, and ideally a paired statistical test.
