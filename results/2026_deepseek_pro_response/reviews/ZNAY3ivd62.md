## Summary

GUI-Spotlight introduces a 7B visual grounding model that performs iterative, tool-mediated focus refinement — using *extract*, *crop*, and *find_color* tools to progressively narrow attention to a target UI element — trained via a stabilized GSPO-based RL procedure with an auxiliary cross-entropy anchor loss. The three-stage pipeline (SFT warm-up from a 72B teacher, RL on 12K samples, high-resolution fine-tuning on 4K) achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, 23.4% on UI-Vision, and 62.7% on OSWorld-G, using only ~18.5K training samples. The paper also provides honest documentation of negative results in RL algorithm design and reward engineering.

## Strengths

- **Genuine sample efficiency relative to 7B competitors**: Table 3 shows GUI-Spotlight achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro with 18.5K samples, surpassing V2P-7B (50.6% with 9.6M), GTA-1-7B (50.1% with 1.56M), and other 7B models trained on orders of magnitude more data. Even accounting for distillation from the 72B teacher in Stage 1, the efficiency gap is substantial.

- **Validated training-stability contribution for multi-turn tool-use RL**: Figure 3 (right panel) shows that vanilla GRPO and GSPO exhibit oscillatory collapse after ~300 steps, while the proposed auxiliary cross-entropy term J'(θ) prevents degradation and yields monotonic improvement. This is a concrete, reproducible algorithmic insight backed by clear empirical evidence.

- **Consistent gains across two distinct backbones and three benchmarks**: The method improves UI-TARS-1.5-7B from 38.7%→52.8% (ScreenSpot-Pro), 18.1%→23.4% (UI-Vision), and 61.9%→62.7% (OSWorld-G). When initialized from the non-UI-specific Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, it still delivers +11.9, +7.4, and +4.2 point gains respectively. This cross-backbone transfer distinguishes the approach from methods tightly coupled to a specific pretrained model.

- **Honest documentation of negative results**: Section 4.1 reports that continuously updating the reference policy and selecting top-p% uncertain prompts both degrade accuracy (Figure 3, left). Publishing these failed attempts alongside successes provides genuine practical guidance.

- **Actionable reward-design findings**: Figure 4 (right) shows that rebalancing the Crop/Extract reward ratio from 0.25/0.05 to 0.15/0.15 yields ~10.5% accuracy improvement — a non-obvious finding attributed to Extract being easier to use (requiring only approximate quadrant selection rather than precise coordinates).

- **Clean ablation isolating training from inference effects**: Section 5.4 confirms untrained multi-turn conversational inference achieves only 7.6%, ruling out the possibility that tool-use capability exists in the base model.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **No inference cost analysis**: GUI-Spotlight makes multiple LLM forward passes per example, yet the paper never reports the average number of turns, the value of T_max used at inference, or wall-clock/compute cost relative to single-pass baselines. For a method whose entire premise is iterative refinement, this is a significant gap — the reader cannot assess whether the accuracy gains come at a 2×, 5×, or 10× inference cost, which matters for practical GUI automation where latency is important.

### Minor

- **The gain attributable to RL-trained tool coordination over a simple iterative baseline is modest, and the framing overstates it**: Section 5.4 shows that strategy ② (repeated single-turn cropping, no RL, no tool coordination) achieves 47.6% on ScreenSpot-Pro, while GUI-Spotlight achieves 52.8%. The unique contribution — RL-trained multi-tool coordination — thus accounts for a +5.2 point delta over the training-free iterative baseline, not the full +14.1 point gain over the base model's single-pass accuracy (38.7%). Yet the abstract and introduction compare only against single-pass models without mentioning this baseline. The paper should center the strategy ② comparison and contextualize its contribution honestly.

- **Data-efficiency claim needs contextualization**: The paper foregrounds training on "only 18.5K samples" against baselines using millions. However, (a) the 2,561 SFT trajectories were generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B, a model ~10× larger, making Stage 1 effectively distillation, and (b) multi-turn trajectories contain several tool calls, images, and model responses each, making them richer than single-turn coordinate-prediction examples used by baselines like V2P-7B. The claim is not false, but the comparison is not apples-to-apples in either data volume or supervision quality, and this should be acknowledged.

- **Method is not SOTA on two of three benchmarks**: On UI-Vision, GUI-Spotlight (23.4%) is below UI-Venus-Ground-7B (26.5%). On OSWorld-G, it achieves 62.7%, below GTA1-7B (67.7%). Only on ScreenSpot-Pro does it lead the 7B class. The contribution is better characterized as "a data-efficient method competitive with 7B SOTA on ScreenSpot-Pro" rather than a method that broadly surpasses 7B baselines.

- **No analysis of tool usage patterns**: The paper provides no data on how many turns GUI-Spotlight typically uses, which tools are invoked most frequently, or on what fraction of examples it succeeds after one vs. multiple tool calls. This makes the iterative refinement a black box and limits understanding of when and why the method works.

### Trivial

- **Confusing model naming in Table 3**: UI-TARS-1.5 appears at 61.6% under "Closed-source Models" while UI-TARS-72B appears at 38.1% under "Open-Source Models 72B Level." These appear to be different models in the same family, but the naming inconsistency is confusing and should be clarified.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report the distribution of tool-call sequences: average turns, tool invocation frequencies, success rate by number of tool calls.
- Add a failure analysis with qualitative examples showing what kinds of targets GUI-Spotlight still misses.
- Add an RL-trained but single-tool (e.g., crop-only) ablation to isolate whether the gain over strategy ② comes from RL training or from tool diversity.
- Ablate individual tools at inference time to show which tools actually contribute to accuracy.
- Analyze what fraction of SFT trajectories from Qwen2.5-VL-72B end in a correct final answer, to better explain the Stage 1 regression (39.3% → 17.8%).

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:

- **"The find_color tool seems fragile for UIs"** — Speculative without evidence from the paper. The paper uses CIE Lab color-distance matching, which is a reasonable robustness choice. Without concrete failure data, this is not a valid criticism.

- **"Circular dependency in data filtering"** — Using the same 72B model for data filtering and SFT trajectory generation is standard practice; the filtering criteria (IQ, BA, CON) are distinct from trajectory generation. This is a minor design note, not a weakness.

- **"The introduction claims models achieve only around 50% accuracy but Table 3 shows multiple 7B models at 50%+"** — The paper's statement that "recently released 7B models achieve only around 50% accuracy" is factually accurate: GTA-1-7B (50.1%), V2P-7B (50.6%), UI-Venus-7B (50.8%) are all "around 50%." Not misleading.

- **"The three tools are deterministic, non-learned functions — the paper should be clearer"** — The paper explicitly states tool functionality in Table 1 and Section 3.1. This is already clear.

- **"UI-TARS-1.5 at 61.6% nearly matches the combined gain — the paper should discuss what the remaining gap implies"** — Scope creep. The paper's contribution is a 7B open-source method; matching closed-source models is not the goal.

- **Strength Finder: "The clean isolation of training gains firmly attributes them to RL training"** — Overstated. The +5.2 gain over strategy ② supports attribution but is modest. The strength is reframed in the final review.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most interesting finding is that shifting reward weight from Crop (dense IoU-based) to Extract (sparse quadrant-based) produces a ~10.5% accuracy improvement — suggesting that easier-to-use intermediate tools provide more reliable RL training signals even when they are coarser. This inverts the natural intuition that denser, more precise intermediate rewards should be more helpful. Combined with the finding that the auxiliary cross-entropy anchor loss prevents multi-turn RL collapse (while several other stabilization tricks fail), the paper offers concrete, actionable design principles for anyone building RL-trained tool-using agents.

## Suggestions

- Add inference cost analysis: report average turns per example, T_max, and compare wall-clock time against single-pass baselines. This is the single most important addition for the rebuttal.
- Restructure the abstract and introduction to center the comparison against the training-free iterative baseline (strategy ②), making clear that the unique contribution is the +5.2 point RL-trained tool coordination gain.
- Contextualize the data-efficiency claim by noting the distillation aspect of Stage 1 and the multi-turn nature of training samples.
- Report tool usage statistics (turn distributions, tool invocation frequencies) to make the iterative refinement process interpretable.
