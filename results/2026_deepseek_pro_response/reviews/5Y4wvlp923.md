## Summary
This paper proposes Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a metric for measuring whether time-series components (trend, seasonality) can be linearly recovered from composite embeddings, and uses it to drive asymmetric loss reweighting during contrastive training. Built on CoST, the method adds an MLP fusion layer for composite embeddings and applies adaptive weights to seasonal and trend contrastive losses based on the SDE-derived asymmetry factor Δ.

## Strengths
- **Controlled synthetic SDE analysis (Table 1):** The synthetic experiment varying the amplitude ratio between trend and periodic components shows Δ transitions smoothly from strongly negative to strongly positive as the ratio crosses 1.0. This cleanly demonstrates that SDE captures directional semantic imbalance, making it the paper's most compelling piece of evidence.
- **Honest negative result (Table 2):** The paper transparently reports that directly incorporating SDE as a regularizer into the TS2Vec objective degrades or does not improve forecasting, then uses this to motivate the shift from regularization to dynamic loss reweighting. This progressive-elimination narrative strengthens credibility.
- **Diverse benchmark coverage:** Evaluation spans ETT (multiple resolutions), Electricity (calendar-driven periodicities), and Weather (meteorological indicators), each stressing different trend/seasonality balance aspects.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **SDE conceptual foundation is unvalidated.** SDE is defined as 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)), motivated by word2vec-style vector arithmetic where linear relations hold in embedding space (§3.2). The paper provides no justification for why a nonlinear contrastive time-series encoder should exhibit this property, and never probes whether it holds empirically. Table 1 reports SDE values up to ~1.33 — corresponding to *negative* cosine similarities, meaning v(a+b) − v(b) points opposite to v(a). Under the paper's own interpretation this means the component is anti-recoverable, yet these values are neither acknowledged nor interpreted. The metric that drives the entire reweighting mechanism rests on an untested assumption.

- **Central claim about SDE reduction is unsubstantiated.** Section 4.4.4 states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" and the conclusion claims the method yields "consistently lower SDE values." However, Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE values are reported for CoST vs. CoST+APW on any real dataset. Whether the proposed method actually reduces SDE compared to vanilla CoST is never shown. This is the paper's headline diagnostic claim and it is an evidentiary gap.

- **Forecasting results are inconsistent and partially negative.** On Electricity at horizons 168/336/720, CoST+APW is substantially *worse* than vanilla CoST (e.g., MSE 0.566 vs 0.425 at horizon 168, 1.010 vs 0.911 at 720). On Weather at longer horizons (168/336/720), TNC outperforms all methods. The paper neither acknowledges nor analyzes these failures, instead concluding "superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines." Where gains exist, they are often marginal (0.001–0.01 MSE). The pattern suggests the reweighting may destabilize training on certain datasets, but this possibility is not engaged with.

- **No ablation separates the MLP fusion layer from asymmetric weighting.** Section 4.4 introduces two modifications simultaneously: an MLP fusion layer g_φ (§4.4.2) and asymmetric perceptual weighting (§4.4.3). No experiment isolates which mechanism drives any observed effect. We cannot distinguish whether improvements come from the reweighting scheme or from simply adding an MLP that composes trend and seasonal embeddings.

### Minor
- **Novelty presentation conflates CoST contributions with the paper's own.** The three "limitations" enumerated in §1 — two (no decomposition inductive bias, time-domain-only objectives) are already addressed by CoST, which the paper builds on. Framing CoST's design choices as problems the paper solves inflates the apparent contribution.
- **Hyperparameters unspecified.** γ and γ' control the sensitivity of loss reweighting to Δ (which ranges from −1.3 to +1.1 in Table 1), but their concrete values are never given, creating a reproducibility gap.
- **Abstract contradicts the method.** The abstract claims the approach plugs in "without architectural changes," but §4.4.2 introduces an MLP fusion layer g_φ — an explicit architectural addition.
- **Horizon specification mismatch.** Section 4.1 states forecasting horizons {24, 48, 96, 192}, but Table 3 uses different horizon sets across datasets (e.g., 168, 336, 720 for ETT1/Electricity; 96, 288, 672 for the second ETT block).

### Trivial
- **Naming inconsistency:** SDE is referred to as "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (§3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (§4.1, §4.3).
- **Dataset labeling error in Table 3:** "ETT1" appears as the label for two different dataset blocks. The second block (horizons 24/48/96/288/672, smaller MSE magnitudes) appears to be ETTm1.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Quantify the computational overhead of computing SDE during training (3× encoder forward passes per sequence) relative to vanilla CoST.
- Report variance across runs (standard deviations) to distinguish signal from noise given the often marginal MSE differences.
- Specify the decomposition method concretely (which filter, cutoff frequency, window size).
- Discuss why the method degrades performance on Electricity and Weather at longer horizons rather than leaving these failures unaddressed.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions. The idea of using a vector-arithmetic-inspired metric to diagnose and rebalance component-level semantics in contrastive time-series learning is genuinely novel, even though the execution leaves significant gaps — particularly the unvalidated linearity assumption, the unsubstantiated SDE-reduction claim, and the missing mechanism ablation.

## Suggestions
- The highest-leverage improvement would be to add SDE measurements for all methods on real datasets in Table 3, since this is the paper's central diagnostic claim.
- Add an ablation running CoST+MLP (no weighting), CoST+weighting (no MLP fusion), and CoST+APW (both) to isolate each mechanism's contribution.
- Validate the linearity assumption empirically before using SDE as a training signal — demonstrate that a well-trained CoST encoder actually exhibits the property needed for SDE to measure recoverability.
- Analyze and discuss the negative results on Electricity and Weather rather than leaving them unaddressed. Understanding when and why the reweighting harms performance is essential for the method's credibility.
