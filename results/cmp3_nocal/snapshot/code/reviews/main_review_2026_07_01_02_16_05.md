# Review of papers/main.md

## Summary
ZS-VCOS proposes a zero-shot modular pipeline for video camouflaged object segmentation (VCOS) that integrates RAFT optical flow, OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection, and SAM-2 tracking. By highlighting motion-salient regions with a color overlay and guiding a VLM to localize the moving animal, the method dramatically improves weighted F-measure on MoCA-Mask from 0.315 (best prior zero-shot) to 0.628, surpassing supervised baselines. The paper also provides a useful audit of metric-calculation inconsistencies in prior VCOS work.

---

## Strengths

- **Large, concrete performance improvement.** Table 1 shows F_β^w rising from 0.315 (LLaVA+SAM2-L) to 0.628 — a near-doubling — with consistent improvement across S_α and MAE. The margin is large enough that it constitutes a credible contribution even under some measurement flexibility.

- **Informative ablation study.** Table 2 isolates nine configurations. The jump from no motion detection (S_α=0.643, mIoU=0.301) to optical-flow-guided detection (S_α=0.752, mIoU=0.508) cleanly demonstrates that optical flow is the load-bearing component. Rows (f), (g), and (h) also cleanly show that bidirectional tracking is essential.

- **Honest metric-inconsistency audit.** Section 4.1.2 identifies a concrete and underappreciated problem: E-measure, mDice, and mIoU vary with aggregation strategy. The paper re-evaluates ZoomNeXt under a consistent protocol, color-codes affected columns in Table 1, and explains which three metrics (S_α, F_β^w, MAE) are safe for cross-paper comparison. This is a specific, actionable service to the field.

- **Coherent method design.** The pipeline stages reinforce each other: optical flow creates a motion salience map → OWLv2 localizes within highlighted region → SAM-2 propagates bidirectionally. The design is well-matched to the core intuition that camouflaged objects reveal themselves through motion.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Threshold selection performed on the test set.** Section 4.1.4 states: "we swept the VLM threshold from 0.03 to 0.13 (inclusive) at a step of 0.02 and report the metric at the best threshold." The paper defends this by analogy to SLT-Net's max-over-threshold metric. However, the analogy is imperfect: SLT-Net's sweep is intrinsic to its continuous output; here the threshold is a global pipeline hyperparameter whose optimal value is discovered on the test set and then reported. No held-out validation split is mentioned. This means the headline F_β^w=0.628 is an upper-bound on expected generalization performance, not an unbiased estimate. Given the large margin over the next best zero-shot method (0.315), threshold tuning almost certainly does not account for the entire gap, but it could inflate results non-trivially. The paper should either (a) hold out a validation split for threshold selection or (b) report the full performance curve across thresholds, making sensitivity explicit.

### Minor

- **Ablation configuration selection criterion is opaque.** As Table 2 shows, row (i) — which removes mean subtraction — achieves higher S_α (0.782 vs. 0.776) and mIoU (0.561 vs. 0.550) than the final "Ours" configuration. Section 4.4 notes that the full method achieves the highest E_φ (0.878 vs. 0.859), implying that E_φ drove the choice. But E_φ is not one of the three primary comparison metrics in Table 1 (which uses S_α, F_β^w, MAE), and the rationale for preferring E_φ in this selection context is not stated. If the final configuration was chosen after observing ablation results, Table 2 doubles as a de facto validation set. The selection criterion and its timing should be stated explicitly.

- **Domain-specific prompting advantage not prominently flagged in the main comparison.** Section 3.2 uses "an animal or insect being highlighted in blue" as the positive OWLv2 prompt — explicitly tailored to MoCA's animal content. Table 3 correctly labels this "ZS w/ PK" (with prior knowledge), but Table 1 labels the method simply "ZS," obscuring the asymmetry for readers who focus on the main results table. The paper should make the "ZS w/ PK" qualifier visible in Table 1 or note the distinction in the table caption.

- **"Surpasses supervised methods" framing lacks context.** The abstract and introduction claim the method "surpasses supervised methods" without acknowledging that those supervised methods (SLT-Net 2022, ZoomNeXt 2024) were trained without access to SAM-2, OWLv2, or RAFT pre-trained on internet-scale data. The zero-shot pipeline leverages dramatically more pre-training compute and data. The comparison measures dataset-specific optimization vs. foundation-model capacity, not a principled zero-shot vs. supervised paradigm. This asymmetry should be acknowledged at least once in the introduction.

### Trivial

- **OR combination in bidirectional propagation lacks justification.** Section 3.3 states masks from both directions "are combined using an OR operation" with no rationale. Since OR propagates any false positive from either direction, a brief sentence comparing OR vs. AND behavior would be informative, even if a full ablation is not required.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- The most impactful improvement to evaluation integrity would be a validation/test split or per-video leave-one-out for threshold selection. Even a 50/50 split would make the headline number trustworthy without sacrificing the core finding.
- Section 4.2.2 (MoCA-Filtered) would benefit from more analysis of why improvement is bounded there (0.628 → 0.697) relative to MoCA-Mask. The paper notes the gap but does not explore it.
- Robustness of the pipeline to alternative optical flow methods (e.g., GMFlow) would strengthen the claim that the contribution generalizes beyond one specific RAFT configuration.

## Removed Points
*These points were flagged for removal; treat with caution.*

- **"Section 3.2: How were negative prompts selected?"** — The paper states fixed prompts ("background," "logo or sign," "plant") following LangGas [14]. This is a reasonable design choice disclosed in the paper; the concern about whether they were tuned on the test set is speculative. Removed as speculative reproducibility concern.
- **Table 1 footnote E_φ ambiguity** — The paper explicitly explains in Section 4.1.1 and 4.1.2 that E_m is excluded from cross-paper comparison precisely because of computation inconsistencies. The ablation E_φ values are all computed consistently with each other using the SLT-Net script. The reviewer's concern collapses under the paper's own stated methodology. Removed as a strawman.
- **Strength: "addresses an important problem"** — Generic; removed per filtering discipline. The specific retained strengths above are concrete.

## Novel Insights

The paper implicitly demonstrates that motion — not appearance — is the key supervisory signal that allows zero-shot methods to close the gap with supervised approaches on camouflaged objects. The near-doubling of F_β^w from configuration (b) (no motion, 0.301 mIoU) to configuration (c) (with optical flow, 0.508 mIoU) quantifies this gap precisely and suggests that prior zero-shot methods that relied purely on VLM appearance reasoning were attacking the hardest part of the problem first. This ordering insight — use motion to guide detection, not appearance alone — is transferable to any domain where visual camouflage is the challenge.

---

## Suggestions

1. Add a held-out validation split (e.g., a random 30-video subset of the test set) for threshold selection, and report performance on the remainder. This single change makes the headline number defensible against methodological objections.
2. Add "w/ PK" to the "Ours" row in Table 1 (matching the labeling already present in Table 3) to make the prior-knowledge asymmetry immediately visible.
3. Explicitly state the selection criterion for the final model configuration in Section 4.4 — e.g., "we select the configuration that maximizes E_φ as a tiebreaker" — and explain why E_φ was used for this purpose when S_α and F_β^w are the primary metrics.
4. Add one sentence in the Introduction acknowledging that the foundation models (SAM-2, OWLv2, RAFT) used zero-shot were pre-trained on substantially more data than the supervised baselines.

---

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a genuine, large-scale contribution: a well-designed zero-shot pipeline that substantially outperforms all prior zero-shot methods and supervised baselines on VCOS, with a clean ablation and a valuable metric-consistency audit. The primary methodological concern — threshold selection on the test set — is real but does not plausibly account for a performance gap this large (0.315 → 0.628). The other weaknesses (prompting clarity, ablation selection, framing) are correctable in revision. The core contribution is credible and impactful.
