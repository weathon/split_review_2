## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework that works with SAM without any fine-tuning or additional models. The method has two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation that samples prompts based on a probability distribution derived from SAM's image embedding, and (2) output token-based verification that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with a TS-SS geometric similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. On FSC-147, the method achieves MAE 16.25 (best among SAM-only training-free methods), and on CARPK it achieves MAE 4.68 (outperforming all supervised methods).

## Strengths

- **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for counting.** The core idea — using SAM's decoder output tokens as a verification criterion rather than relying on image embedding similarity — is genuinely novel and architecturally well-motivated. Prior training-free methods (TFOC, Count-Anything) all work in the image embedding space; using decoder output tokens that inherently integrate semantic and positional information through cross-attention is a principled choice.

- **Clean, deployable architecture.** The method requires no modifications to SAM's weights and no additional models. Both the probabilistic prompt generation and output token verification are post-hoc procedures that interact only with SAM's inputs and outputs. This is a genuine practical advantage over methods like PseCo (which trains additional modules) or TFCAC (which requires DINO).

- **Competitive quantitative results for a SAM-only method.** On FSC-147, the method achieves the best MAE among SAM-only training-free methods (16.25 vs. TFOC's 19.95, Count-Anything's 27.97, raw SAM's 42.48). On CARPK, it achieves MAE 4.68, outperforming all supervised methods (BMNet+ 5.76, SAFECOUNT 5.33, LOCA 9.97).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Missing critical implementation details that hinder reproducibility.** Several details essential for reproducing the results are absent: (a) the SAM variant (ViT-B, ViT-L, or ViT-H) is never specified — the paper says only "the official SAM" (Section 4.1); (b) the exemplar selection protocol for FSC-147 (the main benchmark) is not described — for CARPK the paper specifies "randomly selected 12 objects from the training set" (Section 4.1) but nothing comparable is stated for FSC-147; (c) SAM's decoder produces 4 output tokens per forward pass, but which one serves as the "exemplar output token" and how it is selected is never explained; (d) the TS-SS verification threshold of 300 is set globally without any sensitivity analysis, even though TS-SS is unbounded and likely category-dependent.

- **Overclaimed performance in the abstract.** The abstract states the method "achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" (emphasis added). On FSC-147, several training-based methods achieve lower MAE: BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), LOCA (10.79). The method outperforms *some* training-based methods (e.g., Counting-DETR at 16.79) but not all. The claim should be scoped to "SAM-only training-free methods" or qualified accordingly.

- **No direct comparison of probabilistic vs. grid-based prompting at matched prompt counts.** The paper motivates probabilistic prompting as superior to deterministic grids (Section 3.1: "Unlike simple grid or superpixel-based methods") but never provides an ablation comparing them at the same prompt budget. Since the best configuration uses ~1,438 prompts per image, the natural baseline would be a uniform grid with a comparable number of prompts (e.g., 40×40 = 1,600). Without this control, the claimed advantage of the probabilistic strategy is not experimentally validated.

### Minor

- **The TS-SS metric improvement over cosine similarity is marginal and lacks statistical support.** From Table 3, TS-SS achieves MAE 16.25 vs. cosine similarity at 16.48 — a 0.23 difference (~1.4% relative). The method uses stochastic multinomial sampling, yet no repeated trials, confidence intervals, or standard deviations are reported. The paper's claim that non-hybrid metrics "have reached a performance plateau" and that "breaking through this barrier necessitates a new hybrid metric" (Section 4.4) overstates what a 1.4% gap can support. The output token space itself is the primary innovation; the TS-SS framing should be tempered.

- **High RMSE/MAE ratio on FSC-147 is not analyzed.** The method's RMSE of 133.11 against MAE 16.25 gives an RMSE/MAE ratio of ~8.2×, the highest among all comparable methods in Table 1 (Counting-DETR: 7.4×, TFCounter: 7.0×, TFOC: 6.6×). This suggests occasional catastrophic failures. The Discussion (Section 5) acknowledges "performance degradation in scenarios with dense distributions of very small instances" but does not quantify how often this occurs or connect it to the RMSE.

### Trivial

- The temperature parameter τ in Equation (1) is described conceptually, but the "t-scale factor of 0.066" mentioned in the implementation details is not explicitly linked to τ. The connection should be clarified.
