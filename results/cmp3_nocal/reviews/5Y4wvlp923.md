## Summary

This paper identifies the problem of semantic component imbalance (e.g., trend overshadowing seasonality) in contrastive time-series representations. It proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric to quantify how well one semantic component can be linearly recovered from the composite embedding, and an Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) mechanism built on top of the CoST framework to dynamically reweight trend and seasonal contrastive losses. The core claim is that CoST+APW yields consistent forecasting improvements over CoST.

## Strengths

- **The diagnosis of semantic imbalance is well-executed.** Table 1 uses a clean synthetic setup mixing trend and periodic components across a range of amplitude ratios (r=0.1 to 10) and shows convincingly that TS2Vec embeddings become dominated by the higher-amplitude component. The asymmetry factor Δ behaves as expected — near zero when components are balanced, positive when seasonality is suppressed, negative when trend is suppressed. This is a genuine empirical finding that grounds the paper's motivation.

- **The SDE metric is a plausible diagnostic tool.** The definition SDE_{a,b} = 1 − cos(v(a+b)−v(b), v(a)) (Eq. 1) is motivated by the vector-composition analogy from word embeddings and measures whether component a is linearly recoverable from the composite. As a probe (not an objective), it has face validity for the purpose of detecting semantic imbalance.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

**The central empirical claim is contradicted by the paper's own data.** The abstract (line 10) states the method yields "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy," and the conclusion (line 300) claims "superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines." Table 3 tells a different story. On **Electricity**, CoST+APW is substantially **worse** than plain CoST at horizons 168 (0.566 vs. 0.425, +33%), 336 (0.677 vs. 0.576, +18%), and 720 (1.010 vs. 0.911, +11%). On **Weather**, CoST+APW is worse at horizons 336 (0.266 vs. 0.256) and 720 (0.299 vs. 0.278). Across the 25 dataset-horizon pairs, the proposed method is clearly better on roughly 10, clearly worse on roughly 7, and essentially tied on the rest. This pattern does not support "consistent gains" or "superior" accuracy; the claim is directly falsified by data in the paper's main results table.

### Major

- **Table 3 is claimed to contain SDE metrics, but it does not.** Line 263: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets." The table that follows (lines 269–298) contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE values appear anywhere. The paper repeatedly claims "consistently lower SDE values" (lines 300, 304) for CoST+APW vs. baselines, but provides **no evidence** for this claim in the main results. This is a critical evidential gap for a paper whose central mechanism is SDE-driven rebalancing.

- **The "no architectural changes" claim is contradicted by the method itself.** The abstract (line 10) says the approach "can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes." However, Section 4.4.2 (lines 243–245) introduces "a small MLP g_ϕ that maps the concatenated embeddings [v(a)||v(b)] to a composite embedding." This MLP is used to produce the composite embedding v(a+b) required for SDE computation, is trained jointly with CoST, and is a new architectural component. The claim and the method are inconsistent.

- **Three different names for the same concept.** The paper uses "Semantic **Disentanglement** Error" (title, abstract line 10), "Semantic **Separability** Error" (Section 3.2 heading, line 69), and "Semantic **Decomposition** Error" (Section 4.3, line 178) — all referring to the same formula. This undermines reader confidence and suggests the paper was assembled without a final consistency pass.

- **Key hyperparameters are unspecified.** The weighting formula (lines 101, 257) depends on sensitivity hyperparameters γ and γ', whose values are never given. The SDE regularization experiment (line 207) uses a balancing coefficient λ that is never specified. The update frequency for Δ (every batch? every epoch?) is not described. These omissions make the method irreproducible as written.

### Minor

- **The additivity assumption underlying SDE is not validated.** The SDE formula (line 75) assumes that v(a+b) − v(b) ≈ v(a), i.e., that the encoder is approximately additive. The paper describes the metric as measuring "linear recoverability" (line 77) but never tests whether this assumption holds for the neural encoders used. If the encoder is significantly nonlinear, SDE values become uninterpretable — a high SDE could indicate nonlinearity rather than component suppression.

- **No explanation of why asymmetric weighting avoids the gradient pathology that sinks direct SDE regularization.** The paper reports that SDE as a direct regularizer fails (Section 4.3, Table 2) and hypothesizes it "fails to provide constructive optimization gradients" (line 211). Yet the asymmetric weighting formulation also depends on SDE (through Δ) and backpropagates through the MLP and encoders when computing SDE-based weights (line 259). The paper never explains why the same SDE gradients are pathological when used for direct minimization but constructive when used as loss multipliers.

- **No ablation of the MLP fusion layer.** The MLP (Section 4.4.2) is introduced as part of the method, and CoST+APW is compared as a single package. Without an ablation that compares "CoST+APW without MLP" vs. "CoST+APW with MLP," it is impossible to attribute any observed improvement (or degradation) to the weighting mechanism versus the added MLP capacity.

- **No analysis of Δ dynamics during training.** The mechanism's effectiveness depends on Δ meaningfully reflecting suppression during training. The paper provides no plot or analysis of how Δ evolves, whether it correlates with performance, or whether it fluctuates randomly.

- **Table 3 labels two different ETT subsets as "ETT1."** The first block (line 274, horizons 24/48/168/336/720) and the third block (line 284, horizons 24/48/96/288/672) both appear as "ETT1" but correspond to different subsets (likely ETTh1 and ETTm1 based on horizon sets). The paper's setup (line 140) mentions four subsets (ETT1h/2, ETTm1/2), but the table does not distinguish them.

- **Erratic bold formatting in Table 3.** At line 274 (ETT1 horizon 24), all four methods are bolded simultaneously, which is uninformative.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface one insight that goes beyond the paper's own claims: the asymmetry factor Δ is presented as diagnostic for imbalance, but the paper's own results suggest that the relationship between imbalance and forecasting performance is dataset- and horizon-specific. On Electricity (strong calendar-driven seasonality), aggressively reweighing toward the seasonal component *hurts* performance. This suggests that not all semantic imbalance is harmful — some datasets may benefit from the dominant component receiving more weight. The paper's assumption that balance is always desirable may itself be the wrong conclusion to draw from the diagnostic data. This tension is worth exploring in future work.
