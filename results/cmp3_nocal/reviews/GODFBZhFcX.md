## Summary

This paper introduces PCE (Planner-Composer-Evaluator), a framework that converts implicit environmental assumptions buried in LLM reasoning traces into a structured decision tree, then scores each root-to-leaf path by likelihood, goal-directed gain, and execution cost to guide action selection. Unlike prior communication-centric approaches (CoELA, REVECA, CaPo, CoTS) that resolve uncertainty primarily through inter-agent dialogue, PCE treats environmental assumptions as first-class decision variables and reasons over them before acting. Experiments on two multi-agent benchmarks (C-WAH, TDW-MAT) across three diverse backbones (GPT-4o mini, GPT-OSS:20B, Gemma3:4B) show consistent improvements in task completion metrics, and a user study indicates that human partners perceive PCE's communication patterns as more efficient and trustworthy.

## Strengths

1. **Novel problem framing that departs from the communication-centric paradigm.** The core idea — extracting implicit assumptions from LLM CoT traces, organizing them into a decision tree, and scoring paths by likelihood, gain, and cost — is genuinely different from existing work that treats uncertainty as something to be resolved through dialogue. Sections 1 and 4.3–4.4 articulate this clearly: instead of optimizing over action sequences or communication strategies, PCE treats environmental assumptions as first-class decision variables.

2. **Consistently strong task performance across all tested backbones and benchmarks.** In both Table 1 (C-WAH Total Steps) and Table 2 (TDW-MAT Total/Food/Stuff), PCE achieves the best result across all three backbones (GPT-4o mini, GPT-OSS:20B, Gemma3:4B). The improvements are often large — e.g., 87.50% vs. 62.50% (CoELA) on TDW-MAT Total with GPT-4o mini, and 81.25% vs. 55.00% on the same metric with GPT-OSS:20B. Running across three diverse backbones rules out the concern that results hinge on a single model.

3. **Scaling ablation (Figure 3) cleanly separates structural contribution from model-capacity effects.** Showing that PCE + scaling outperforms raw LLM + scaling at every tested scale (4B→12B→27B model size; Low→Medium→High reasoning depth) is a well-designed experiment that supports the paper's central thesis: explicit uncertainty handling provides benefits additive to and distinct from scaling.

4. **Interpretable human-benefit evidence via a user study.** The user study (Section 5.3, Figure 4) adds a dimension often missing in agent papers — actual human perception of the agent's communication patterns. PCE scoring highest on appropriateness, usefulness, efficiency, and trust provides complementary evidence that the approach's benefits extend beyond benchmark metrics.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

1. **Token-usage claims in the abstract and introduction are overstated and inconsistent with the reported data.** The abstract claims "comparable token usage" and the introduction claims PCE "outperforms...in token usage." However, on TDW-MAT (Table 2), PCE's token consumption (*Usages*) is substantially *higher* than CoELA across all three backbones: 1.75× (GPT-4o mini), 1.42× (GPT-OSS:20B), and 1.88× (Gemma3:4B). On C-WAH (Table 1), the picture is mixed — PCE is lower than CoELA on GPT-4o mini and GPT-OSS:20B but higher on Gemma3:4B. The paper's honest trade-off — PCE achieves much better task performance at a moderate token overhead compared to the most token-efficient baseline (CoELA), and better token efficiency than the tree-search baseline (CoTS) — is a still-positive result that should be characterized accurately. The current framing of a win on both dimensions is misleading.

2. **No variance or statistical significance reported for any result.** C-WAH has 10 episodes and TDW-MAT has 24 episodes; all tables and figures report point estimates with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or indication of independent runs. With such small benchmarks, it is difficult for a reader to assess whether the reported improvements are robust. This is a significant evidential gap that should be addressed.

### Minor

3. **The Composer's tree-building procedure is described at a high level that leaves key algorithmic choices unspecified.** Section 4.3 states that "a local ranking policy selects the next assumption to branch on" and that "we approximate these criteria using LLMs' commonsense reasoning," but neither the ranking policy nor the assumption-generation procedure is concretely specified. The paper references Appendix A.12 for prompting details (stripped by the parser), but the main-text description ("the LLM decides") is too vague to constitute a reproducible specification of the paper's central technical contribution.

4. **Potential self-confirmation bias in the Evaluator is not discussed.** The Evaluator uses an LLM to estimate the likelihood of assumptions $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S})$ that were generated by the same or a similar LLM (Planner). When the same model class generates an assumption and then rates its probability, there is a risk of systematic overconfidence. This should be acknowledged and ideally controlled for (e.g., by comparing Evaluator likelihood scores against ground-truth frequencies from environment logs).

5. **Hyperparameter choices lack justification.** The cost coefficients $\alpha=\beta=\lambda=1$ and depth limit $D=3$ are stated as defaults (Section 5) without justification, scale analysis, or sensitivity results in the main text. A 20-meter move costs 20, a 100-character message costs 100 — whether these quantities are on comparable scales is unclear. The paper refers to Appendix A.5 for sensitivity analysis, but the main text should at minimum state whether performance is robust to these values.

6. **The claim that "each candidate action in this trace tends to be grounded in a single partial assumption" (Section 4.2) lacks quantitative support.** This empirical observation grounds a key design decision but is presented without statistics on how often it holds or across how many traces it was verified.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Scaling ablation against structured baselines.** Figure 3 compares PCE to a "Planner only" variant (removing Composer and Evaluator). A stronger control would compare PCE against other structured approaches (CoTS, CaPo) at each scale, to separate the benefits of structured reasoning from the benefits of PCE's specific tree structure.
- **User study size.** Twelve participants is modest; a larger study would strengthen the human-perception claims.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviewers' analyses converge on the same points the paper makes: that its central innovation (surfacing latent assumptions rather than relying on communication) is genuinely novel, and that its experimental evidence is consistently positive but could be strengthened by more precise reporting (variance, method specification, honest token cost characterization).

## Suggestions

1. **Revise the abstract and introduction to accurately characterize the token cost trade-off.** Specifically: PCE achieves significantly better task performance at a moderate token overhead relative to the most token-efficient baseline (CoELA) and better token efficiency than tree-search baselines (CoTS, CaPo). This is still a compelling contribution.
2. **Add variance reporting** (e.g., standard deviations across episodes, or confidence intervals) for at least the main benchmark results.
3. **Concretely specify the Composer's ranking and generation procedures** in the main text with pseudocode or a precise description of the prompts/decision rules, so the method is reproducible without consulting the appendix.
4. **Address the self-confirmation bias concern** by acknowledging it and, if possible, providing a small-scale validation of Evaluator likelihood estimates against environment ground truth.
5. **Report or summarize hyperparameter sensitivity** (α, β, λ, D) in the main text rather than deferring entirely to the appendix.
