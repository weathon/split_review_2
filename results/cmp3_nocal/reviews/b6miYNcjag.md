## Summary

This paper introduces the problem of data reliability scoring: given reported data $\hat{x}$ and auxiliary observations $y$ (generated by an unknown experiment $P$ from unobserved true data $x$), assign a score $S(\hat{x}, y)$ that orders datasets by their deviation from the truth. The paper proposes the Gram determinant score, proves it preserves several ground-truth-based reliability orderings (exact match, Blackwell, approximate Hamming), establishes impossibility results showing these conditions are nearly tight, and proves the score is uniquely experiment-agnostic up to scaling. Experiments on synthetic data, CIFAR-10 embeddings, and employment data provide empirical support.

## Strengths

**Clean problem formalization (Sections 2.1–2.4).** The paper formally defines reliability scoring, introduces misreport matrices $Q$ to summarize $(x, \hat{x})$ pairs, and defines ground-truth-based orderings (Exact Match, Blackwell, Hamming/dist). These provide clear formal targets for evaluating reliability scores.

**Impossibility results (Section 3).** Proposition 3.1 establishes meaningful boundaries — e.g., no score can preserve Hamming ordering under linearly independent experiments even with diagonally dominant misreports. These results prevent overclaiming and delineate the feasible region.

**Multiplicative property and experiment agnosticism (Section 4.1).** The core identity $\Gamma(PQ) = \det(P^T P) \det(Q)^2$ is elegant and implies the Gram determinant score's ranking of datasets is insensitive to the experiment $P$. The uniqueness result (Proposition 4.3) — that any continuous, homogeneous, experiment-agnostic score must be a power of the Gram determinant — is a strong theoretical contribution.

**Near-tightness with impossibility bounds (Theorem 4.2 vs. Proposition 3.1).** The positive results match the impossibility results: exact match for $Q_{\text{nonperm}}$ (any superset is impossible), Blackwell for $Q_{\text{reg}}$ (where it is a strict partial order), and an $\alpha$-dist ordering under $Q_{L,\delta}$ (whose $\delta$ constraint is needed because Hamming ordering on $Q_{\text{dom}}$ is impossible). This coherence signals rigorous thinking.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

**1. The across-dataset ranking claim is not directly tested in the experiments.** The theory's central claim (Definition 2.2, Theorem 4.2) is that the score *preserves reliability orderings across datasets* with different misreport matrices $Q$. Experiments 1 and 2 (Figures 2a–2c, 3a–3c) primarily test monotonicity — whether the score decreases as a single dataset is increasingly corrupted. Figure 2d tests ranking recovery (via Kendall-tau) but uses only the uniform-random manipulation strategy, not the five other structured-noise strategies. The CIFAR-10 experiment has no ranking recovery evaluation at all. The experimental design thus supports a weaker claim than the headline contribution — that the score is monotonic in corruption level, not that it correctly orders datasets with *different* misreport structures.

**2. No baseline comparisons in the main experiments.** The experiments compare the Gram determinant score against ground-truth error metrics ($p$, Hamming, $\ell_2$) that are *infeasible* in practice (they require access to the unobserved ground truth). For a new-method paper, comparing against feasible alternatives (e.g., trace of the empirical Gram matrix, mutual information between $\hat{x}$ and $y$ estimated from the confusion matrix, or other determinant-based measures discussed in the Related Work) is essential to demonstrate the score's advantage. The paper mentions alternative candidates in Appendix G but does not include systematic baseline comparisons in the main experiments.

### Minor

**3. Overclaim on "finite-sample guarantees" in the conclusion.** The conclusion states the paper develops "plug-in and stratified-matching estimators with finite-sample guarantees." In the main text, the plug-in estimator (Definition 4.4) is only shown to *asymptotically* preserve orderings (Proposition 4.5, stated without proof or sketch). The finite-sample result is deferred to the appendix for the stratified-matching estimator. The claim as written is imprecise for the plug-in estimator.

**4. Employment data experiment (Exp 3) does not fully respect the model's causal assumptions.** The model assumes $y$ is generated by a stochastic experiment $P$ from the true data $x$ ($x \to y$). In the employment experiment, $x$ (employment figures) and $y$ (Treasury tax deposits) are co-determined by the economy; the assumed causal direction is not matched. The experiment is a suggestive illustration but does not validate the theory under its stated conditions.

**5. CIFAR-10 experiment uses SimCLR embeddings trained on the same data labels.** The embeddings $y$ are learned from CIFAR-10 images whose labels $x$ are the ground truth. This means the "experiment" $P$ is not fixed and unknown (as the theory assumes) but optimized to be informative about the labels — a setting not covered by the theory. Using pre-trained embeddings from a disjoint dataset would be a cleaner test.

**6. Figure 2d description is inconsistent.** The text reports "the average Kendall-tau distance" but describes the results as "the fraction of correctly recovered rankings"; the figure caption also uses "fraction of correctly recovered rankings." These are different metrics; their relationship should be clarified.

**7. The plug-in estimator's diagonal bias is not discussed.** Definition 4.4's estimator $\tilde{G}$ sums over all $n, n'$ including $n=n'$ where $\mathbf{1}[y_n=y_{n'}]=1$ deterministically. This introduces a bias scaling as $O(1/N)$ relative to the true Gram matrix $\hat{G}$. The paper acknowledges this implicitly ("if $n \neq n'$" in the text) but does not discuss its effect on the asymptotic preservation result.

**8. The $Q_{L,\delta}$ conditions from Theorem 4.2 are not verified in experiments.** Theorem 4.2, part 3 requires misreport matrices in $Q_{L,1/64L^2d^2}$; the experiments do not check whether the corruption policies used produce matrices satisfying this bound.

**9. Kernel extension's theoretical backing is deferred to the appendix.** The kernelized score (Definition 4.6) is used in the CIFAR-10 experiment, but the reliability-ordering result "analogous to Theorem 4.2" is fully deferred to Appendix F (stripped). The main text does not state the conditions under which the guarantees carry over.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Add a ranking-recovery experiment that directly tests Definition 2.2.** Generate datasets with *different* misreport matrices $Q$ and $Q'$ that are ordered under the ground-truth orderings and measure whether the Gram determinant score correctly ranks them. Test this for all manipulation strategies, not just uniform random.
- **Include at least one feasible baseline.** The trace of the empirical Gram matrix or the mutual information between $\hat{x}$ and $y$ are natural candidates.
- **Provide a proof sketch for Proposition 4.5** in the main text to bridge the gap between the partial-knowledge theory and the plug-in estimator.
