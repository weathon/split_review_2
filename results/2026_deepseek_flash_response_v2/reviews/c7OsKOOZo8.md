## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading that generates self-derived "lesion proposals" to replace costly external annotations. Two modules are introduced: GALP (Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal), which uses auxiliary classifiers to produce grade-conditioned evidence maps and selects top-K regions as proposals, and LGRF (Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion), which uses these proposals for cross-view fusion via a gated mixture-of-experts and top-K weighted cross-view attention. Experiments on MFIDDR (four-view) and DRTiD (two-view) datasets show competitive performance: the lesion-free variant achieves 83.9% accuracy on MFIDDR (surpassing all end-to-end baselines and several externally-informed methods) and 76.0% on DRTiD (outperforming CrossFiT by 0.4pp).

## Strengths

1. **Self-generated proposals achieve SOTA competitiveness without external annotations.** On MFIDDR (Table 1), the lesion-free variant (83.9% acc) surpasses all end-to-end baselines (best: ETMC at 81.5%) and several externally-informed methods such as CVSA (82.6%, with vessel annotations) and LFMVDR (82.2%, with lesion maps). This is the paper's core empirical finding and is well-supported by the data.

2. **Both GALP and LGRF modules independently contribute.** The ablation study (Table 4) shows clear, consistent drops when removing each component: −1.2pp (w/o GALP), −1.3pp (w/o Experts), −1.6pp (w/o LGRF) across all four metrics. This confirms both modules provide distinct, measurable value.

3. **Strong performance on the hardest Grade 4 cases.** In the grade-wise breakdown (Table 2), the with-lesion variant achieves Grade 4 F1=51.6%, far exceeding the next-best method SMVDR-W at 40.8% — a 10.8pp absolute improvement on the most challenging DR grade. Even the lesion-free variant (36.0% F1) surpasses multiple externally-informed methods.

4. **Hyperparameter analysis provides operational guidance.** Fig. 3 systematically sweeps retention ratio (α), number of activated experts (K₂), and total experts (M), identifying optimal operating points (α=0.5, K₂=2, M=6). This goes beyond single-value reporting and aids reproducibility.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Unvalidated "lesion" proposals undermine the paper's central framing.** The paper pervasively claims that the top-K regions selected by GALP are "lesion proposals" that "act as surrogates for external cues" (abstract, lines 79, 290). The MFIDDR dataset provides lesion segmentation masks (line 185: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), yet the paper never uses them to check whether the proposals correspond to actual lesions — no Dice/IoU, no qualitative overlays, no overlap analysis of any kind. The proposals are CAM-based top-K selections, which could be picking up any grade-correlated signal (illumination artifacts, field-of-view differences, optic disc atrophy patterns) rather than genuine microaneurysms, hemorrhages, or exudates. This does not invalidate the method's empirical performance (the method clearly works), but it means the core framing — that self-derived proposals substitute for expert lesion annotations — is asserted without evidence. The claims of "interpretability" (Contribution 2) and "micro-lesion sensitivity" (Contribution 3) are similarly unsubstantiated.

- **No variance or significance reporting despite small margins.** All results (Tables 1–4, Fig. 3) are single numbers without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical tests. This is critical because several key margins are small: on MFIDDR, the lesion-free variant (83.9%) trails WGLIN (84.2%) by 0.3pp and leads SMVDR-M (84.0%) by only 0.1pp. On DRTiD, the advantage over CrossFiT is 0.4pp (76.0 vs 75.6), and the AUC breakdown shows the method is not uniformly superior (CrossFiT leads on Grades 0 and 2; Cv-Transformer leads on Grade 1). Without variance estimates, it is impossible to determine whether these differences are meaningful or within random seed variation.

### Minor

- **Ablation lacks a "plain backbone" lower bound.** The ablations (Table 4) remove GALP, experts, or LGRF individually, but all variants still use the Swin-B backbone with the remaining components. The worst ablation (w/o LGRF at 82.3%) remains well above the best end-to-end baseline (ETMC at 81.5%). Adding a "plain Swin-B" without any proposed modules would anchor the marginal contributions more cleanly.

- **DRTiD comparison omits several strong externally-informed baselines.** On DRTiD (Table 3), the paper compares against only two externally-informed methods (CVSA, CrossFiT). The stronger methods evaluated on MFIDDR — WGLIN, SMVDR-W, SMVDR-M, LFMVDR — are not included. The paper states this follows "the protocol established in (Hou et al., 2022)," but it would strengthen the evaluation to include these methods if results are available.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Validating proposals against the available MFIDDR lesion masks (e.g., via IoU/Dice or qualitative overlays) would resolve the most significant framing issue.
- Adding confidence intervals from multiple random seeds would make the small-margin comparisons interpretable.
- A "plain Swin-B" baseline in the ablation would clarify the backbone's baseline contribution.

## Suggestions

1. **Validate the lesion proposals** against the available MFIDDR lesion segmentation masks. A simple overlap analysis (percentage of proposal regions that intersect with lesion masks, or Dice/IoU) would either substantiate or refute the "lesion" framing. This single addition would substantially strengthen the paper.
2. **Add variance estimates** by running each experiment with 3–5 random seeds and reporting means ± std. Given the small margins against top competitors, this is essential to determine whether the improvements are meaningful.
3. **Add a plain Swin-B baseline** to the ablation table to anchor how much gain comes from the proposed modules vs. the backbone itself.
4. **Temper the "lesion" language** unless the proposals can be validated. If the method works by selecting grade-discriminative regions (regardless of whether they are lesions), this is still a valuable contribution — just frame it accurately.
