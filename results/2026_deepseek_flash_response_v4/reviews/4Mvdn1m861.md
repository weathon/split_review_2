## Summary

TokenCount proposes a training-free object counting framework that directly uses SAM's decoder output tokens (rather than image embeddings) for verification, combined with a probabilistic prompt generation stage for spatial exploration. The method requires no additional models or fine-tuning, achieving MAE 16.25 on FSC-147 (best among SAM-only training-free methods by a substantial margin) and MAE 4.68 on CARPK (outperforming all supervised methods reported in the same benchmark).

## Strengths

1. **Novel and well-motivated use of SAM output tokens for counting verification**: The core idea — that SAM's decoder output tokens jointly encode semantic and positional information, making them more suitable for verification than image embeddings — is clearly articulated and supported by PCA visualizations (Figure 5) and by the controlled comparison in Table 5 (output token space MAE 16.25 vs. image embedding space MAE 16.94). This is a genuine contribution to training-free counting.

2. **Substantial improvement over all SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147**: Table 1 shows large margins over SAM (42.48), C-Anything (27.97), and TFOC (19.95). The improvement to MAE 16.25 is a clean ~17% relative gain over the previous best SAM-only method (TFOC). These gains are large enough that variance concerns are less relevant: even with pessimistic variance, the method clearly dominates existing SAM-only approaches.

3. **Strong CARPK results beating all supervised methods**: MAE 4.68 on CARPK surpasses BMNet+ (5.76), SAFECOUNT (5.33), LOCA (9.97), and FamNet (18.19). This is notable since the method requires no category-specific training, unlike the supervised baselines.

4. **Convincing ablation structure**: Tables 3–5 systematically isolate the contribution of each component (verification metric, iterative sampling, output token space), and the ablation trends are clean and monotonic (e.g., Table 4: increasing prompts per iteration consistently improves MAE from 29.10→22.94→18.90 at 8 iterations).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **No variance reporting despite explicit stochasticity in the pipeline**: Every reported metric is a single point estimate with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. This is a critical evidential gap because (a) the probabilistic prompt generation explicitly uses multinomial sampling (Section 3.1), making results inherently stochastic; (b) the central technical claim that TS-SS outperforms cosine similarity rests on a 1.4% relative MAE improvement (16.25 vs. 16.48, Table 3) — a gap that could easily be within run-to-run noise; and (c) the core metric ablation (Table 3) and output token vs. image embedding comparison (Table 5) both involve modest margins. Running the evaluation multiple times (different random seeds or exemplar selections) and reporting mean ± std would resolve whether these improvements are meaningful or noise. Without this, the paper's central claim about TS-SS is not verifiably supported.

2. **Abstract overclaims relative to actual results on FSC-147**: The abstract states the method "achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." On FSC-147, five methods achieve lower (better) MAE: LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), TFCAC (12.26), SAFECOUNT (14.32), BMNet+ (14.62). The method is the best among *SAM-only* training-free methods, which is a legitimate and interesting claim, and the body text is more measured. The abstract also claims the method is "comparable to training-free approaches that require additional models" — TFCAC (SAM+DINO) achieves 12.26 vs. 16.25, a 32.5% relative gap, which stretches the meaning of "comparable." These are fixable presentation issues but need correction.

### Minor

3. **Missing exemplar counts for the main FSC-147 few-shot experiment**: The paper never states how many exemplars were used for the few-shot setting (Table 1). This is a basic experimental detail required for reproducibility and for assessing fairness of comparison with baselines whose exemplar protocols may differ. For CARPK, the paper states 12 exemplars were used, which is an unusually large number for few-shot counting — but the exemplar counts for baselines on CARPK are also undisclosed.

4. **TS-SS metric has an unexamined degeneracy when sin(θ)=0**: TS-SS = TS·SS = (|a||b|sin(θ)/2)·(π·(ED+MD)²·θ/360°). When sin(θ)=0 (θ=0° or θ=180°), TS=0, making TS-SS=0 regardless of ED, MD, or the magnitude terms. This means identical vectors (θ=0°) and exactly opposite vectors (θ=180°) both receive a similarity score of 0. For identical vectors this is plainly wrong — a similarity metric should assign a maximal (or at least non-zero) score to identical inputs. While exact 0° or 180° alignment is unlikely in high-dimensional token space, the discontinuity means close-to-identical or close-to-opposite vectors could produce arbitrarily small scores. The paper neither acknowledges this property nor demonstrates it does not affect practical performance.

5. **Token verification threshold of 300 is presented without justification or sensitivity analysis**: The threshold on the TS·SS product is critical to the pipeline (Section 4.1) but has no ablation or justification. The TS and SS components have different units and dynamic ranges, making the combined threshold especially opaque. How was 300 chosen? How does performance vary across, say, 30–3000?

6. **Probability decrease factor for negative regions is unspecified**: Section 3.1 states that for negative regions "the value of the probability distribution at that mask region is decreased" — but by what factor or formula? "Decreased" could mean halved, reduced to a fraction, or some other function. This affects both reproducibility and the understanding of the iterative process.

9. The Discussion's efficiency claim ("superior computational efficiency compared to state of the art") lacks runtime comparisons to baselines.
