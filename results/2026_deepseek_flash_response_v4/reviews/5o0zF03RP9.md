## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting RL rewards with a KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions, treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable adapted online. Experiments on a toy MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8 show improvements over the β=0 baseline in sparse-reward settings.

## Strengths

1. **Clear, well-motivated framework for preference-prediction shaping.** The core idea — augmenting extrinsic rewards with a KL penalty that aligns predicted outcome distributions $p(o|s,a)$ with preferred distributions $q(o|s)$ — is cleanly presented (Section 3) and grounded in cognitive/neuroscience concepts (dopamine RPE, Free Energy Principle). The differentiation from standard KL-regularized RL (TRPO, PPO, SAC) which constrains policy changes rather than outcome distributions is explicitly stated (Section 2.2).

2. **Substantial MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8 results.** With β=0.01, IncentRL achieves 98% success rate vs 90.5% for the β=0 baseline, and reduces mean episode length from 75 to 29 steps (Figure 2, Section 5.4). These are practically meaningful gains on a standard sparse-reward benchmark.

3. **Empirical evidence of posterior concentration for Bayesian β.** Table 3 and Figures 3–4 show the posterior mean β narrowing from ~0.106 (std 0.057) in Round 1 to ~0.017 (std 0.018) by Round 4, demonstrating that the Bayesian procedure (whatever it is) can converge toward task-relevant values. While the mechanism is unspecified (see Fatal), the phenomenon itself is documented.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **The Bayesian adaptation procedure — the paper's central novelty — is never specified.** The contributions list begins with "Bayesian adaptation (novelty)" (line 21), the abstract calls it the "central novelty," and Figures 3–4 and Table 3 present posterior evolution results. Yet the paper never describes the prior distribution, likelihood model, posterior computation/inference algorithm, what constitutes a "round," or what data drives the updates. The Methods section (Section 3) and Algorithm description (Section 5.1) contain only the fixed-β objective $r^{\text{ext}} - \beta \cdot \text{KL}(p\|q)$. A reader cannot implement, reproduce, or evaluate the headline contribution. Without this specification, the paper's primary claim ("removes the need for manual trade-off tuning") is unsupported by the presented method description.

### Major

- **No comparison to any existing intrinsic motivation method.** Every experiment compares IncentRL only against the β=0 (unregularized) version of the same base RL algorithm. There is no comparison to curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al., 2017; Burda et al., 2018), RND, ICM, empowerment, or any standard intrinsic motivation baseline. The paper criticizes existing methods for requiring fixed trade-off tuning but never demonstrates that IncentRL outperforms them. This would be a serious omission even if the Bayesian procedure were fully specified.

- **How $p(o|s,a)$ is obtained in continuous-state environments is not specified.** For the toy MDP, $p$ is the known transition dynamics. For MountainCar (continuous $(x,v)$ state space) and MiniGrid, the paper does not specify the outcome space $\mathcal{O}$, how $p(o|s,a)$ is parameterized or learned, or how the KL divergence is computed in practice. Section 4.3 discusses Gaussian parameterizations in latent spaces as a future direction, but this is not implemented. The reader cannot reconstruct the experiments.

- **The KL divergence as specified yields infinite values.** In the toy MDP (line 166), $q(o|s_0) = \{s_1: 1.0, s_0: 0.0\}$ while $p(o|s_0,a_1) = \{s_1: 0.3, s_0: 0.7\}$. Then $\text{KL}(p\|q) = 0.3\cdot\log(0.3/1.0) + 0.7\cdot\log(0.7/0.0)$ diverges because $q(s_0)=0$ and $p(s_0)>0$. No smoothing, clipping, or $\varepsilon$-correction is mentioned. While resolvable with standard numerical stabilization, the paper's specification is inconsistent with the reported results, which show IncentRL working well. This undermines reproducibility.

### Minor

- **Internal inconsistency in reporting Bayesian β results.** The text (line 230) says the posterior "concentrates near the effective region ($\beta \approx 0.1$)," but Table 3 shows the posterior mean converging to ~0.02 by Round 4, and Figure 3's caption confirms it "decreases to about 0.02." The MiniGrid optimal fixed β was 0.01, not 0.1 (the 0.1 value comes from MountainCar). The text overstates the proximity to the MountainCar optimum rather than comparing to the MiniGrid optimum, creating unnecessary confusion.

- **Only 3 seeds for MountainCar and MiniGrid experiments.** With MountainCar showing high variance (std 535.41 vs mean 4135.67 for β=0.1), 3 seeds provides weak statistical support.

- **Theoretical analysis is elementary.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimal policy) is a standard continuity argument true of any bounded additive perturbation. Proposition 2 (as β→∞, KL dominates) is definitional. These do not constitute a substantive characterization of what distinguishes IncentRL from other reward shaping approaches.
