## Summary
This paper systematically evaluates nine open-source LLMs (12B–16B parameters) on two reverse engineering tasks — name recovery and type inference — using 40,000 unique functions across four architectures (x86-64, x86-32, ARM, MIPS) at four optimization levels (O0–O3), plus real-world firmware. ## Strengths

The finding that even the best model (Qwen3) achieves only 0.06 F1 without fine-tuning on function name recovery provides concrete empirical grounding.

2. **Multi-architecture and multi-optimization evaluation.** The dataset spans 4 architectures × 4 optimization levels with 40,000 unique deduplicated functions. Prior evaluations were narrower in scope, and this breadth enables the paper to show that performance patterns (e.g., better results at O0; consistently near-zero for assembly-based recovery) hold consistently across architectures (Section 4.1).

3. **Auxiliary-LLM response-normalization pipeline.** Section 3.2 describes a post-processing method in which a second LLM converts heterogeneous model outputs (chain-of-thought reasoning, refusals, multiple candidates) into a uniform schema, enabling automated evaluation that a single parsing algorithm could not handle robustly.

4. **Empirical counterevidence to "larger models are better".** Figure 2 shows near-identical F1 scores across CodeLlama 7B, 13B, and 34B variants on all three tasks, supporting Finding 6 that larger variants of the same model family do not necessarily yield better results.

5. **Real-world firmware evaluation with two commercial decompilers.** Table 6 reports scores on stripped firmware binaries decompiled by both Ghidra and IDA Pro, showing that most models perform better on IDA Pro output — a practical finding that informs practitioner tool choice.

The conclusions are thus about nine mid-sized open-source models, not about "LLMs" broadly. This limitation should be stated more prominently in the title and abstract.
