## Summary

This paper introduces Geo-CoT (Geospatial Chain-of-Thought), a framework for perceptually-grounded reasoning in remote sensing VLMs. It constructs Geo-CoT380k, a large-scale dataset (384,591 samples) of GPT-4V-generated reasoning chains conditioned on ground-truth spatial annotations, then trains RSThinker via a two-stage alignment (SFT + GRPO) that forces the model to output both an answer and a verifiable reasoning trace with explicit bounding box coordinates. Results show very strong performance across visual grounding, object detection, counting, scene classification, captioning, and VQA benchmarks.

## Strengths

1. **Dominant and consistent empirical margins across a comprehensive benchmark suite.** Tables 4–7 show large, consistent advantages over prior work. On DIOR-RSVG visual grounding, RSThinker achieves 93.1% @0.5 vs. 60.8% (SkySenseGPT). On HRRSD object counting, 85.26% Acc vs. 61.48% (EarthDial). On RESISC45 scene classification, 96.89% vs. 91.33% (VHM). Even on zero-shot benchmarks (RRSIS-D, RSOD, RS19), the margins are substantial (94.0% vs. 72.5%, 95.5% vs. 51.5%, and 99.74% vs. 95.53% respectively).

2. **Controlled ablation cleanly isolating each component's contribution.** Table 8 provides clear causal evidence: Base (56.26 mIoU) < SFT w/o CoT (81.80) < SFT w/ CoT (87.70) < SFT w/ CoT + GRPO (89.02). The finding that "SFT w/o CoT + GRPO" (86.47) underperforms "SFT w/ CoT alone" (87.70) supports the paper's claim that the CoT cognitive scaffold is a prerequisite for effective RL, not merely additive.

3. **Dataset construction methodology that conditions on verified spatial evidence.** Geo-CoT380k is generated by providing GPT-4V with known-correct bounding boxes and captions rather than open-ended reasoning, minimizing hallucination risk at the data generation stage. The 384,591-sample scale makes this a substantial resource contribution.

4. **Failure analysis demonstrating the verifiability property in practice.** Figure 7 documents a failure where the model misidentifies a dock extension as a ship, but the explicit bounding box output ([413, 225]) renders the error immediately auditable — a property end-to-end black-box models lack.

5. **Diagnostic analysis of KL regularization in GRPO.** Figure 4 empirically shows that removing the KL penalty causes catastrophic format collapse during GRPO training, providing an operational justification for this design choice.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Main benchmark comparisons are partially confounded by in-domain training.** RSThinker is trained on the training splits of DIOR-RSVG, VRSBench, DOTAv2, HRRSD, NWPU-RESISC45, and AID (Table 1). Baselines include models evaluated zero-shot on these same benchmarks (Claude-sonnet-4, Gemini-2.0-flash, ChatGPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL). The paper presents these side-by-side without clearly flagging which comparisons are in-domain vs. zero-shot for RSThinker. The extraordinary margins on some benchmarks (e.g., 93.1 vs. 60.8 on DIOR-RSVG) are partly attributable to this asymmetry. While the "SFT w/o CoT" ablation provides some control, the headline presentation overstates the CoT-specific contribution.

2. **The "faithful reasoning" claim is asserted but not directly evaluated.** The title, abstract, and contribution statements emphasize "faithful," "verifiable" reasoning, yet the evaluation measures only final task accuracy/IoU/mAP/MAE — not whether the emitted reasoning chains themselves are factually correct. The paper does not include: (a) human evaluation of whether spatial claims in the CoT are correct, (b) consistency checks (perturb inputs and verify the reasoning changes appropriately), or (c) intervention experiments (corrupt a CoT step and check if the answer degrades). The failure analysis (Figure 7) actually demonstrates an unfaithful chain (the CoT is "a stylistic heuristic" justifying an incorrect answer), which the paper reframes as a safety feature. The paper appears to use "faithfulness" to mean *verifiability of the output format* (bounding boxes make errors auditable) rather than *the reasoning trace causally reflects the model's computation*, but this distinction is never made explicit, creating an expectation gap.

### Minor

3. **GRPO's contribution to faithfulness specifically is unsupported.** The GRPO reward functions (Table 3) are standard task metrics (IoU, mAP, BLEU-4), not measures of reasoning faithfulness. Whatever improvement GRPO provides, it optimizes task accuracy, not reasoning quality. The gains themselves are modest from SFT(w/CoT) to SFT(w/CoT)+GRPO: +1.32 mIoU on VG, +3.03 mAP@0.5 on detection, +3.04 on VQA, and essentially flat on counting (-0.15 MAE). The paper's framing that GRPO "refines faithfulness" conflates task accuracy with reasoning quality.

4. **Training data is entirely synthetic and post-hoc.** Geo-CoT380k rationales are generated by GPT-4V conditioned on ground-truth annotations (the answer is known when the rationale is written). The model learns to produce plausible-sounding post-hoc justifications, not necessarily genuine reasoning. The paper acknowledges this briefly (Section 5) but does not ablate against alternatives (e.g., rule-based templates, human-written rationales) to test whether the specific GPT-4V narrative matters or any format-compliant output would suffice.

5. **No variance or statistical significance reported.** Particularly for the ablation results (Table 8) where differences between conditions are small (e.g., MAE 2.93 vs. 2.78), the lack of confidence intervals makes it impossible to assess whether these are meaningful improvements.

### Trivial

6. **The "Δ" rows in Table 8 (lines 287, 290) appear to be formatting artifacts with no data.**

## Nice-to-Haves

- Add an "answer-only" ablation where the CoT output is stripped at test time to test whether the reasoning trace causally contributes to performance.
- Compare against a version of GLM-4.1V fine-tuned on the exact same task data without CoT, to more cleanly isolate the CoT-specific benefit.
- Include RemoteReasoner and SegEarth-R1 as baselines if their checkpoints are available for the evaluated tasks.
- Report variance or confidence intervals for the ablation experiments.
- Ablate the choice of base VLM to show framework generality.

## Novel Insights

The most revealing finding from the cross-review is the tension embedded in the paper's own failure analysis (Figure 7). The paper acknowledges the CoT can act as a "stylistic heuristic" rather than reflecting genuine reasoning — yet treats this as a positive because the error is auditable. This inadvertently exposes that the framework's primary contribution is *output-format verifiability* (the model produces bounding boxes that can be checked by a human) rather than *reasoning-process faithfulness* (the model's internal computation is correctly reflected in its reasoning trace). These are distinct properties, and the paper conflates them under the umbrella of "faithfulness." A model could perfectly mimic the Geo-CoT format while being unfaithful on both dimensions: (a) its internal computation need not follow the Planning-Grounding-Synthesis structure, and (b) the spatial claims in its output could be incorrect but still verifiably so. The paper's evaluation validates (b) only partially (via the failure case) and (a) not at all. A stronger paper would explicitly define which of these two notions of faithfulness it targets and evaluate accordingly.

## Suggestions

1. Directly evaluate reasoning chain correctness — at minimum, a human evaluation of whether spatial claims (bounding boxes) in a random sample of outputs correspond to real objects.
2. Prominently mark which results are in-domain vs. zero-shot in the main tables to avoid misleading comparisons.
3. Add an "answer-only" condition (strip the CoT at test time) to test whether the reasoning trace causally contributes to performance.
4. Report variance for ablation results where differences are small.
5. Reframe the "faithfulness" narrative more precisely: the framework produces *verifiable* outputs (traceable spatial references) rather than *faithful* reasoning (causally correct internal computation).
