## Summary
The paper introduces RisoTTo, a lightweight non-autoregressive (NAR) text-to-speech (TTS) model designed to bridge the performance gap with autoregressive (AR) models in zero-shot scenarios. The authors propose three key components: (1) Soft Alignment Generation (SAG), which uses flow matching to distill the flexible attention-based context vectors of AR models into a NAR framework; (2) an Invertible Encoder (IE) based on normalizing flows to disentangle and model residual acoustic information not captured by text representations; and (3) Prompt-Aware Lightweight Convolution (PAL), which dynamically adapts kernel weights based on a speech prompt. RisoTTo achieves competitive speech quality and speaker similarity with significantly lower latency and parameter counts compared to state-of-the-art models like VALL-E and MaskGCT.

## Strengths
- The paper addresses a critical bottleneck in NAR-TTS: the lack of "soft" alignment and the deterministic nature of upsampling. The SAG module effectively brings the benefits of AR cross-attention to a parallel architecture.
- The use of an Invertible Encoder to model the residual between semantic context and the mel-spectrogram is a theoretically sound way to handle the one-to-many mapping problem in TTS, avoiding the posterior collapse often seen in VAEs.
- The model is highly efficient. With only 33M parameters and sub-second latency for 10s of speech, it is significantly more practical for real-time deployment than the billion-parameter models it is compared against.
- The experimental evaluation is comprehensive, including comparisons against demo-page samples of closed-source models and local evaluations of open-source SOTA models using standardized metrics (MOS, SECS, WER).

## Weaknesses
### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Sampling Efficiency of SAG:** The paper mentions that SAG uses flow matching to generate the soft alignment matrix. While flow matching is generally efficient, the paper does not explicitly discuss the computational overhead of running an iterative ODE solver (even with 2 steps) on a 2D matrix ($N \times T$) compared to standard duration-based upsampling.
- **Clarity on Residual Sampling:** In Section 3.3, the authors state that during inference, residual information $z$ is sampled from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. While this is a common practice in flow-based models, the paper lacks a qualitative or quantitative analysis of how different samples of $z$ affect the prosody or "acoustic richness" of the output.

### Minor
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The loss function includes a weight $\lambda=10$ for the NF loss. There is no ablation or discussion on how sensitive the model performance is to this specific weighting.
- **MMD Comparison:** In Table 2, the comparison between IE and VAE is used to argue for better disentanglement. While the numbers support the claim, the VAE architecture used for comparison is not detailed, making it hard to judge if the improvement is due to the flow-based nature or specific capacity differences.

### Trivial
- The name "RisoTTo" is mentioned in the text before being formally defined as the model name.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A visualization of the generated soft alignment matrix $A_{soft}$ compared to the hard alignment $A_{hard}$ would visually demonstrate the "softness" the model is learning.
- Comparison of the training time/convergence speed relative to AR models.

## Novel Insights
The primary novel insight is the application of flow matching to the *alignment matrix* itself (SAG) rather than just the acoustic features. By treating the transition from hard alignment (duration-based) to soft alignment (attention-based) as a probability path, the authors provide a way for NAR models to inherit the "contextual richness" of AR models without the sequential decoding bottleneck. Additionally, the integration of prompt-aware dynamic kernels in a lightweight convolution framework offers a parameter-efficient alternative to the massive transformer blocks used in current zero-shot SOTA.

## Suggestions
- Provide a visualization (heatmap) of the $A_{soft}$ generated by the SAG module in a revised version to show how it differs from the monotonic hard alignment.
- Clarify the dimensionality of the $A_{log}$ matrix and the specific U-Net architecture used for SAG, as $N \times T$ can be quite large for long utterances.

## Score and Decision
The paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to improving NAR-TTS. The combination of flow-matching for alignment and invertible encoders for residual modeling is a significant contribution to the field, especially given the impressive efficiency results.
