## Summary
The paper investigates the application of Decision Transformer (DT) variants to the Gym-$\mu$RTS environment, a minimalist real-time strategy game. The authors re-implement Online Decision Transformers (ODT) and Critic-Guided Decision Transformers (CGDT), and propose a hybrid approach called Online Critic-Guided Decision Transformer (OCGDT). They contribute a new dataset of 3,000 trajectories generated by high-level rule-based bots on procedurally generated 8x8 maps. The experimental results show that OCGDT matches or slightly exceeds the performance of Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) while requiring significantly fewer gradient updates and less wall-clock time.

## Strengths
- **Strong Empirical Performance:** The proposed OCGDT and its DT-based components achieve competitive win rates against established rule-based competition winners (CoacAI and Mayari). Notably, OCGDT achieves a win rate of 51.6% against a well-tuned IQL baseline.
- **Efficiency:** The paper demonstrates that DT-based methods are significantly more sample-efficient in terms of gradient updates compared to IQL (13k vs 800k steps) to reach similar performance levels, which is a valuable insight for researchers with limited compute.
- **Rigorous Evaluation:** The authors use 95% Wilson score intervals and evaluate across multiple seeds and hundreds of games. The inclusion of cross-agent win rates (Table 2) provides a clearer picture of relative performance than just benchmarking against rule-based bots.
- **Ablation Studies:** The paper includes a comprehensive set of ablations (OCGDT A-G) that investigate the impact of buffer size, context length, and the necessity of the offline pre-training phase.

## Weaknesses
### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Lack of Improvement from Hybridization:** A primary goal of the paper was to see if combining ODT and CGDT (into OCGDT) produces a stronger model. However, Table 1 shows that ODT actually achieves a higher win rate against Mayari (46.3%) than OCGDT (40.1%), and OCGDT is only marginally better against CoacAI. The "Online" component of OCGDT also appears problematic, as OCGDT D (No Fine-tuning) performs similarly to the base OCGDT, suggesting the novel combination doesn't yet provide a clear synergistic advantage over its constituents.

### Minor
- **Overfitting in Fine-tuning:** The authors note that extended fine-tuning (OCGDT B) degrades performance due to "buffer pollution." While they identify the issue, the paper lacks a robust solution to this problem (as OCGDT C only mitigated the degradation rather than improving performance), leaving the "Online" part of the contribution somewhat under-realized.
- **Limited Map Diversity:** The study is restricted to 8x8 maps. While the authors acknowledge this, RTS challenges like long-term planning and pathfinding are significantly amplified on larger maps (e.g., 32x32 or 64x64), where the transformer's context window would be more severely tested.

### Trivial
- The entropy lower bound $\beta$ being recalculated based on valid actions is a sensible implementation detail, but its impact on the objective function's stability during the online phase is not discussed.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A comparison of the attention maps to see if the agent actually attends to long-range "Return-to-go" tokens or primarily focuses on the most recent state-action pairs.
- Testing on at least one larger map size to verify if the $K=20$ vs $K=100$ observation holds when the game duration increases.

## Novel Insights
The paper provides a novel empirical validation of Decision Transformers in the RTS domain, specifically showing that DTs can "stitch" together behaviors from high-level bot demonstrations more efficiently than temporal-difference-based offline RL (IQL) in low-data, sparse-reward settings. A key insight is that for 8x8 $\mu$RTS, long context lengths ($K=100$) do not provide a significant advantage over shorter ones ($K=20$), suggesting the environment remains largely Markovian at this scale, and longer contexts may actually increase the risk of overfitting to specific trajectory sequences in the offline dataset.

## Suggestions
- Investigate a "constrained" online fine-tuning approach, such as using a KL-divergence penalty against the offline pre-trained policy, to prevent the performance degradation observed when the replay buffer is updated with online data.
- Clarify the specific benefit of the Critic in the OCGDT architecture; since the results are similar to ODT, it would be beneficial to show a case (perhaps a more stochastic version of the environment) where the Critic-guidance becomes the deciding factor for success.

## Score and Decision
The paper is a solid empirical study that successfully adapts modern offline RL/RCSL techniques to a challenging RTS benchmark. While the "OCGDT" hybrid doesn't significantly outperform its predecessors, the rigorous benchmarking against IQL and the release of a new $\mu$RTS dataset provide clear value to the community. The efficiency gains (wall-clock time) are particularly relevant for academic RL research.
