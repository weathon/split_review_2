## Summary

This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL with a KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions as an intrinsic shaping signal. The claimed central novelty is a Bayesian scheme that adapts the incentive weight β online to eliminate manual tuning. Experiments on a 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid show that the KL shaping term can improve learning speed and final performance compared to the β=0 baseline.

## Strengths

- **Clean toy experiment (Section 5.2, Table 1, Figure 1).** The 2-state MDP provides a clear, interpretable demonstration that adding the KL-based shaping term with β > 0 improves both learning speed and reduces variance relative to β = 0. This is a well-designed diagnostic experiment that cleanly isolates the effect.

- **Cognitively motivated framing (Sections 2.3, 2.4, 6.3).** The connections to dopamine-based reward prediction error and the Free Energy Principle provide a genuine intellectual motivation for the KL-based preference-prediction alignment. The paper correctly notes its departure from active inference (fixed preferences, behavior shaping rather than belief revision), which is a meaningful distinction rather than an oversight.

- **Honest discussion of failure modes (Section 6.2).** The paper acknowledges preference misalignment, KL dominance, and latent mismatch as real failure modes and does not over-claim robustness.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The Bayesian β adaptation scheme — the paper's claimed "central novelty" — is never described.** The abstract states "The central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable, updated online" (line 9), and the first bullet contribution is "a Bayesian scheme for adapting the incentive weight β" (line 21). Yet the paper provides: no prior over β, no likelihood function, no inference method (analytic, MCMC, variational inference, or otherwise), and no description of how β interacts with agent rollouts (per-episode? per-timestep? per-meta-iteration?). The "Algorithm" subsection (lines 149–155) and the entire Methods section (Section 3) describe only the KL shaping term. Figures 3/4 and Table 3 report posterior statistics, but a reader cannot understand what model produced them, which environment they correspond to, or how to reproduce the scheme. A contribution that is positioned as the paper's central novelty but is not specified cannot be evaluated.

2. **Internal inconsistency between the reported β posterior data and the text claims.** Table 3 (Appendix A) and Figure 3's caption show the posterior mean falling from 0.106 (Round 1) to 0.017 (Round 4) — drifting monotonically away from 0.1 toward zero. Yet the main text (line 239) states the posterior "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)" and Figures 3-4 draw a dashed target line at β = 0.1. The data shows concentration near 0.02, not 0.1. Additionally, the environment that produced these results is unclear: MiniGrid's best fixed β was 0.01 (only β=0.0 and 0.01 were tested), making the β=0.1 target mismatched, while MountainCar's best was 0.1 but the posterior does not converge near it.

3. **The empirical evaluation is too narrow to support the paper's strong claims.** The paper claims to "remove the long-standing need for manual trade-off tuning in intrinsic motivation" (line 289) and to "recover the same effective values discovered by fixed sweeps" (line 247), but:
   - **Only 3 environments**, with 2 being very simple (2-state MDP, MountainCar).
   - **No comparisons to existing intrinsic motivation methods** (e.g., ICM, RND, curiosity-driven exploration) despite positioning the work as addressing their core limitation. The only baseline is β=0 (the same algorithm without the shaping term).
   - **Very few seeds**: 7 (toy), 3 (MountainCar), 3 (MiniGrid), with no statistical significance testing.
   - **MountainCar results show high β sensitivity**: β=0.3 and 1.0 degrade performance by 53% and 75% respectively (Table 2). This undercuts the claim that the method "eliminates manual tuning" without evidence that the Bayesian adaptation avoids harmful β values, but that scheme is itself unspecified.

### Minor

4. **The theoretical propositions add little.** Proposition 1 (for sufficiently small β, the optimal policy is preserved) is a generic continuity argument that holds for any bounded reward perturbation — it is not specific to the KL shaping term and gives no actionable guidance about what "sufficiently small" means. Proposition 2 (as β → ∞, the policy ignores external rewards) is self-evident. Neither proposition meaningfully characterizes when the method helps versus hurts.

5. **The predicted outcome distribution p(o|s,a) is not described for the non-toy experiments.** For the toy 2-state MDP, p(o|s,a) is hand-specified from known transition probabilities (line 166). For MountainCar and MiniGrid, the paper merely states p(o|s,a) is "the predicted outcome distribution" (line 199) or "may be obtained from a forward model" (line 63), with no architecture, training procedure, or loss function provided. Since the KL penalty depends entirely on p(o|s,a), this limits reproducibility.
