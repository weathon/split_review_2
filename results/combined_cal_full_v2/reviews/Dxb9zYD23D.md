## Summary

This paper proposes Spectro-Temporal Diffusion (ST-Diff), a framework that reframes multivariate time series generation as a video generation task. It uses the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to convert a time series into a 3D spectro-temporal video tensor (frequency × covariates × time frames), then applies a custom video diffusion model with tri-axial factorized attention (temporal, frequency, covariate) and learnable bias matrices. The method is evaluated on six benchmarks against GAN, VAE, and diffusion baselines, reporting SOTA results on most metrics.

## Strengths

- **The "time-series-as-video" framing is genuinely novel and well-motivated.** The paper clearly identifies a real gap: time-domain diffusion models lack spectral inductive biases, while image-based methods (like ImagenTime) collapse the temporal axis into a spatial one. The video representation via STFT simultaneously addresses both limitations. This conceptual contribution is the paper's strongest asset.

- **The architectural design is principled and aligned with the problem structure.** The tri-axial factorized attention (temporal, frequency, covariate axes), anisotropic patching (preserving covariate-level granularity), and learnable bias matrices B_C, B_F initialized from empirical data statistics are all defended by clear reasoning about the domain (e.g., covariates are an unordered set; spectral dependencies are non-local). This stands in contrast to applying off-the-shelf video diffusion models without modification.

- **The reported quantitative results are often large in magnitude.** On Context-FID for ETTh at length 64 (Table 2), ST-Diff achieves 0.031 vs. 0.631 for Diffusion-TS — more than an order-of-magnitude improvement. The Discriminative scores on the long-sequence ETTh benchmark (0.030–0.032 across lengths 64–256) are remarkably low and stable. Where comparisons are available, ST-Diff generally outperforms or ties its strongest competitors on Discriminative and Predictive scores.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Incomplete baseline comparisons undermine the headline SOTA claim for two of four metrics.** In Table 1, for Context-FID and Correlational Score, both ImagenTime and Diffusion-TS are marked "—" for all six datasets. The paper's claim of SOTA on these metrics therefore rests on comparisons against TimeGAN and TimeVAE — GAN/VAE baselines from 2019/2021 that the diffusion-based baselines already substantially outperformed. The paper states it "reports performance from the original publications" and claims "ST-Diff establishes a new state of the art" without caveat, while two of four evaluation metrics lack data for the two strongest competitors. (Table 2 does partially mitigate this by providing Context-FID for Diffusion-TS on ETTh long sequences, where ST-Diff wins decisively, but the full Table 1 comparison remains incomplete.)

- **No ablation studies are presented anywhere in the paper.** ST-Diff introduces multiple components — STFT-based video representation, trend-residual decomposition, tri-axial factorized attention, learnable bias matrices with data-derived initialization, anisotropic patching, and a cross-covariance loss — any of which could drive performance. Without ablations, the reader cannot attribute which components matter, and the paper's central thesis (that the video representation itself is the key enabler) is not empirically supported without isolating its effect.

- **The Context-FID metric is never defined.** Despite being listed as a primary result in both Table 1 and Table 2, there is no explanation of what Context-FID is, what feature extractor it uses, or how it is computed. The Evaluation Metrics section (lines 109) defines Discriminative, Predictive, and Correlational scores in detail, but Context-FID is absent from this description. FID is a standard metric for images (using Inception embeddings) but requires specification of the embedding space for time series. This makes a main quantitative result uninterpretable and unreproducible.

### Minor

- **The STFT consistency claim is too strong for unconstrained generated tensors.** The paper states "near-perfect reconstruction" and that samples can be "losslessly converted back" via iSTFT. This is technically true for the *analysis-resynthesis loop* on a real signal, but not for unconstrained tensors generated by a diffusion model, which are not guaranteed to satisfy the consistency condition (i.e., that there exists a time-domain signal whose STFT exactly equals the generated tensor). The paper cites Griffin & Lim (1984) and uses 75% overlap but does not discuss whether post-processing is applied or whether this issue was observed in practice.

- **The main quantitative evaluation (Table 1) is limited to sequence length L=24 for the full cross-dataset comparison.** While Table 2 provides longer-sequence results for ETTh alone, the claim of scalability rests on a single dataset. The STFT representation's dimensionality depends on window/hop parameters scaled to sequence length, and at L=24 the signal produces very few frequency bins and time frames, potentially simplifying the task.

- **Several implementation details needed for full reproducibility are not specified:** (a) the number of transformer layers, hidden dimension, attention heads, and total parameter count are not reported; (b) the weight λ for the cross-covariance loss relative to the standard MSE diffusion loss is not given; (c) the number of random seeds/runs used to compute uncertainty estimates is not stated.

2. **Add ablation studies** isolating at minimum: (a) the video vs. static-image representation on the same STFT input (testing the central thesis), (b) the cross-covariance loss, (c) the bias matrices B_C and B_F, and (d) the trend-residual decomposition.

4. **Report model architecture details**: number of layers, hidden dimensions, attention heads, total parameter count, and the cross-covariance loss weight λ.
5. **Specify the number of random seeds/runs** used for computing uncertainty estimates (standard deviations).
6. **Include a diagnostic** measuring STFT→iSTFT reconstruction error (e.g., mean absolute error between the original and reconstructed signal) on generated samples to address the consistency concern empirically.
