## Summary

This paper introduces Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a cosine-similarity-based metric that quantifies whether individual semantic components (trend, seasonality) are recoverable from a composite time-series embedding. The authors use SDE to derive an asymmetric weighting factor Δ that dynamically re-weights seasonal and trend contrastive losses within the CoST framework. The paper validates SDE as a diagnostic on TS2Vec with synthetic data, demonstrates that direct SDE regularization degrades performance, and shows mixed forecasting results on ETT, Electricity, and Weather benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **Well-designed diagnostic experiment establishing the semantic imbalance problem.** Table 1 provides systematic controlled evidence via synthetic signals (varying amplitude ratios r = 0.1 to 10) that TS2Vec exhibits severe semantic imbalance. The asymmetry factor Δ transitions smoothly from strongly negative (-1.3395 at r=0.2) to strongly positive (1.1072 at r=10), with near-zero Δ only at r=1 (balanced). This cleanly quantifies the problem before proposing a solution.

- **Honest negative result on direct SDE regularization.** Table 2 demonstrates that naively adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec degrades performance across all datasets and horizons. The paper reports this explicitly (line 211: "SDE, defined as a diagnostic metric, fails to provide constructive optimization gradients when directly used as a regularizer"). This negative-to-positive experimental arc is methodologically informative for the field.

- **Structured ablation progression.** The paper follows a clear three-stage design: (1) confirm semantic imbalance exists (Table 1), (2) show direct regularization fails (Table 2), (3) show asymmetric weighting provides partial gains (Table 3). This progression helps the reader understand which design choices matter.

- **Partial gains on ETT datasets at longer horizons.** In Table 3, CoST+APW achieves best MSE on ETT1 at horizons 168 (0.101) and 336 (0.147), sweeps ETTm1 across all horizons, and achieves best MSE on ETT2 at horizons 24, 168, 336, and 720. These improvements are consistent with the claim that preserving weak components matters for longer forecasting windows.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Empirical results contradict the paper's "consistent gains" claim; method degrades on Electricity and Weather at longer horizons.** On Electricity at horizons 168/336/720, CoST+APW MSE (0.566/0.677/1.010) is substantially worse than all baselines (best baselines: 0.412/0.548/0.859) — a 17–37% degradation. On Weather, CoST+APW never achieves the best MSE at any horizon; TNC or TS2Vec wins at every point (e.g., at horizon 720: CoST+APW 0.299 vs TNC 0.219). The abstract (line 10) claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and the conclusion (line 304) claims "improved forecasting accuracy across multiple benchmarks." The paper never acknowledges or discusses these failures. (Verified from Table 3, lines 289–298.)

- **Table 3 claims to report SDE metrics but contains only MSE/MAE.** Section 4.4.4 (line 263) states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance." Table 3 (lines 271–298) contains only MSE and MAE columns. No SDE values are reported anywhere for CoST vs. CoST+APW on real data. The paper's central claim that APW "leads to consistently lower SDE values" (line 300) is entirely unsupported by the presented data. The only SDE values in the paper are in Table 1 for TS2Vec on synthetic data. This is a critical gap: the entire motivation is that semantic imbalance exists and APW fixes it, yet the paper never demonstrates the latter.

- **No ablation isolating the MLP component from the asymmetric weighting.** Section 4.4.2 (lines 241–247) introduces a new MLP g_φ that "maps the concatenated embeddings [v(a)||v(b)] to a composite embedding." This is a substantive architectural addition whose individual contribution is never isolated. The improvement (where it exists) could come entirely from the MLP providing an additional expressive path. An ablation of CoST+MLP (without APW) vs. CoST+APW (without MLP) is essential to validate that Δ-based weighting is the active ingredient. Furthermore, the abstract claims "without architectural changes" (line 10), but adding an MLP *is* an architectural change.

- **SDE computation has an unspecified dimensionality constraint.** The paper defines v(a) = pool(V_T(x)) ∈ ℝ^{d_T} (line 231), v(b) = pool(V_S(x)) ∈ ℝ^{d_S} (line 231), and v(a+b) = g_φ([v(a)||v(b)]) ∈ ℝ^{d_C} (line 245). SDE_{a,b} = 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)) requires d_C = d_T = d_S for the subtraction and cosine similarity to be well-defined. The distinct notation suggests different dimensions, and the paper never specifies that they are equal. The phrase "resides in the same latent space as its constituents" (line 247) is ambiguous when applied to a nonlinear MLP.

### Minor

- **Loss weighting can produce negative weights; no discussion of constraints.** The formula L = (1 + γ·Δ)L_season + (1 + γ'·(−Δ))L_trend (lines 101, 257) can produce negative loss weights when γ'·|Δ| > 1. From Table 1, |Δ| can exceed 1.0 on synthetic data. The paper specifies γ, γ' > 0 (line 103) but never discusses bounds or clamping. Negative contrastive loss weights could destabilize training.

- **Figure/formula inconsistency on γ parameters.** Figure 1 description (lines 128, 130) uses a single γ for both weights ("1+γΔ and 1−γΔ"), while the formulas in Sections 3.4 and 4.4.3 (lines 101, 257) distinguish γ and γ'. This creates confusion about the actual formulation.

- **Failure cases on Electricity and Weather not discussed.** The paper provides no analysis of why APW hurts on these datasets (e.g., whether the seasonal/trend decomposition is inappropriate, whether Δ is unreliable on real data). This undermines the reader's ability to assess when the method is applicable.

- **No error bars or multi-seed evaluation.** All results are single-run point estimates. Given that gains (where they exist) are often small (0.01–0.02 MSE), it is unclear whether improvements are statistically significant.
