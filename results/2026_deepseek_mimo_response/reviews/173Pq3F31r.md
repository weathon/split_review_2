## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based framework that jointly models expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content from global performance style representations, trained on both paired and unpaired data without note-level alignment. It additionally introduces a diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module that generates style embeddings from score content alone, enabling automated style-aware rendering.

## Strengths
- **Competitive alignment-free APT results**: Table 1 shows the model achieves best results on E_extra (7.33), E_onset (16.26), E_offset (27.30), E_avg (12.48), and E_pitch (6.24), all statistically significant (p < 0.01) over the end-to-end baseline. This demonstrates that note-level alignment is not necessary for competitive transcription.
- **Meaningful content-style separation**: Table 4 shows style representations achieve 77.46% composer accuracy vs. 29.99% for content representations (Cont→Comp) and 42.07% vs. 9.94% for performer identification on the ATEPP dataset with 25 composers and 49 pianists, indicating the style encoder captures performance-specific characteristics while content focuses on score-level attributes.
- **Novel PSR module**: The diffusion-based PSR module is a genuinely novel contribution enabling automated style-aware rendering. Figure 4 shows PSR-generated style embeddings replicate the era-wise clustering structure (Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Modern) of real performance embeddings, supporting the claim that style can be inferred from score content.
- **Scalable training via unpaired data**: The masked reconstruction objective (Equation 4) enables leveraging 75,913 MusicXML files and YouTube-sourced performance data alongside 967 paired ASAP performances. Table 2 shows Ours-Target outperforms EPR-Only on KL and MAE metrics.
- **Interesting domain finding**: Style→Comp accuracy (77.46%) far exceeds Style→Perf (42.07%), suggesting professional pianists conform expressive style to composer conventions — a meaningful insight for MIR that directly motivates the PSR design.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None

### Major
- **Conflated ablation undermines the joint-learning claim**: The paper's first contribution claims to "leverage the duality between the two tasks for mutual supervision." The primary evidence is the comparison between EPR-Only and Ours-Target in Tables 2-3. However, EPR-Only "retains only the Score Encoder, Style Encoder, and Performance Decoder, and is trained solely on the ASAP dataset" (Section 5.1) — it differs from Ours-Target in *two* simultaneous ways: (a) removing the APT branch, and (b) removing ~76K unpaired score sequences and YouTube performance data. Without a factorial ablation {EPR-Only, Joint} × {paired only, paired+unpaired}, the improvement cannot be attributed to task duality rather than data scale. The paper itself acknowledges this conflation: "joint modeling, *together with* training on additional unpaired data, leads to better EPR performance" (Section 5.1, emphasis added). This weakens the central contribution claim.

- **Subjective evaluation is small-scale and internally inconsistent**: The subjective listening test uses 11 participants, 5 pieces, and 5 composers (25 stimuli across 6 systems), providing limited statistical power with no inter-rater reliability reported. More critically, Section 4.3 describes "eleven participants *trained in music performance*," while the Ethics Statement says "our human study is based on *online crowdsourcing*" — these describe fundamentally different evaluation populations. If the participants are trained musicians, the study has ecological validity but questionable power; if they are crowdworkers, their ability to judge nuanced dimensions like "style" on a 5-point Likert scale is dubious.

### Minor
- **Disentanglement evaluation uses indirect measures**: The disentanglement claim — central to the paper — relies on a linear classifier (Table 4), 2D t-SNE/UMAP projections (Figures 3-4), and a 3-sample style transfer test (Figure 5). Established disentanglement metrics (DCI, MIG, SAP) are not reported. The 29.99% composer accuracy from content representations (Cont→Comp) is non-trivial (~4% random for 25 classes), and the paper does not quantify what level of cross-talk would indicate disentanglement failure.

- **PSR lacks direct quantitative appropriateness measures**: The PSR module is evaluated via visual era-clustering (Figure 4) and the subjective test, but no quantitative comparison of PSR-generated styles against ground-truth styles is provided (e.g., cosine similarity in learned style space, or retrieval evaluation comparing same-piece vs. different-piece style distances).

- **Loss weighting unspecified**: Equation 6 sums all loss terms with implicit coefficient 1, but the KL regularization weight controls the reconstruction-disentanglement trade-off. This weight is not documented, and sensitivity is not analyzed.

- **Single global style vector limitation unacknowledged**: z_s ∈ R^D assumes style is constant across an entire piece, but real performances exhibit within-piece style variation (e.g., rubato in cadenzas vs. strict tempo). This simplification is reasonable but should be discussed as a limitation.

### Trivial
- The claim that models "more closely match those of human performances" (Section 5.1) overstates the case for variance metrics — Ours-Target velocity variance (151.03) and duration variance (0.58) remain substantially different from Human (241.04 and 1.72), though closer than some baselines.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A factorial ablation isolating joint learning from unpaired data effects would definitively validate the central duality claim.
- Computing DCI/MIG/SAP scores on the ATEPP dataset for quantitative disentanglement evidence.
- A PSR retrieval evaluation: compare PSR-generated styles against ground-truth styles from same-piece vs. different-piece performances.
- Error analysis or failure case discussion.
- Ablation over KL weight sensitivity.

## Novel Insights
The paper's genuinely novel observation is that style representations encode composer information far more strongly than performer information (Style→Comp: 77.46% vs. Style→Perf: 42.07%), suggesting professional pianists conform their expressive style to composer conventions. This domain insight directly motivates the PSR module — if style is partially determined by score content through composer conventions, then style can be inferred from scores alone. This is a meaningful contribution to the MIR community's understanding of the relationship between composition and performance.

## Suggestions
- **Critical**: Add a factorial ablation with four conditions: (1) EPR-Only + paired only, (2) EPR-Only + paired+unpaired, (3) Joint + paired only, (4) Joint + paired+unpaired. This isolates the contribution of joint learning vs. data scale.
- **Critical**: Resolve the inconsistency between "trained in music performance" and "online crowdsourcing" in the participant description, and report inter-rater reliability.
- Add established disentanglement metrics (DCI, MIG, or SAP) computed on the ATEPP dataset.
- Add a direct PSR evaluation comparing generated styles against ground-truth via cosine similarity or retrieval metrics.
