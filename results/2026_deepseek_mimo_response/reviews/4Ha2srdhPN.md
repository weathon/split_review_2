## Summary
GRAID is a framework for generating spatial reasoning VQA datasets using only 2D bounding box annotations from standard object detectors, avoiding 3D reconstruction errors and generative hallucinations. Applied to BDD100k, NuImages, and Waymo, it generates 8.5M+ VQA pairs across 22 template types, with human evaluation claiming 91.16% validity. Fine-tuning four VLM backbones on GRAID data yields improvements on five established benchmarks and demonstrates cross-task generalization from training on only 6 question types.

## Strengths
- **Compelling cross-task and cross-dataset generalization (RQ2)**: Fine-tuning Llama 3.2 11B on only 6 question types from GRAID-BDD yields accuracy improvements on 10+ held-out question types within GRAID-BDD (+47.5pp) and on the entirely unseen GRAID-NuImages (+38.0pp) containing different cities, scenes, and visual contexts (Figure 3). This demonstrates models learn transferable spatial reasoning primitives, not dataset-specific patterns.
- **Consistent benchmark improvements across multiple VLMs (RQ3)**: Four VLM backbones fine-tuned on GRAID vs. OpenSpaces data and evaluated on five benchmarks (BLINK, A-OKVQA, NaturalBench, RealWorldQA, VSR) show GRAID-tuned models consistently outperform. For Llama: +32.5% on A-OKVQA, +15.94% overall on BLINK with +41.13% on Relative Depth and +31.98% on Visual Correspondence. Importantly, GRAID SFT rarely causes regressions on non-spatial tasks, while OpenSpaces SFT frequently does.
- **Well-engineered SPARQ system with measured speedups**: The predicate-based early rejection architecture achieves up to 1,407× speedups on the most expensive templates (Section 3.2). Concrete timing data is provided (e.g., LargestAppearance predicate: 0.02ms vs. full realization). The Algorithm 1 pseudocode for RightOf is clean and correct. This enables generation of 8.5M+ pairs in hours.
- **Well-motivated qualitative-over-quantitative design**: The paper argues convincingly that discriminative questions ("which is closer?") rather than metric questions ("how far?") avoid cascading depth estimation errors. The configurable `margin_ratio` parameter eliminates ambiguous questions from depth-based templates (Section 4).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Inconsistent comparison metrics for the headline claim**: The paper's central comparison — "91.16% human-validated accuracy" for GRAID vs. "57.6%" for SpatialVLM — uses metrics computed differently. For GRAID, 91.16% = (317−28)/317 is the fraction with no combined question+answer issues. For SpatialVLM, 57.6% = 144/250 is specifically the answer *error* rate (only 42.4% of answers were correct). The paper further describes 57.6% inconsistently: the abstract says "57.6% human validation rate" (line 9), Section 1 says "only 57.6% of questions are valid" (line 51), and Section 4 says "57.6% of answers in the dataset were incorrect" (line 182). A consistent comparison on answer validity alone would actually show an even larger gap (93.69% vs. ~42.4%), so the muddled presentation obscures what is genuinely a strong result. Disaggregating the metrics would strengthen the paper.

### Minor
- **No inter-annotator agreement or confidence intervals for human evaluation**: With 4 evaluators assessing 317 pairs (likely different subsets, making per-annotator samples even smaller), the absence of inter-annotator agreement metrics means the paper's central quality evidence (91.16%) lacks statistical grounding. This is a significant omission for the paper's primary empirical claim.
- **Expressiveness-reliability tradeoff not explicitly acknowledged**: GRAID's high accuracy rate is largely by design — given correct bounding boxes and correct templates, QA pairs are deterministically correct. The ~8.4% error rate reflects annotation noise, not the difficulty of the generation task. The paper frames GRAID as superior to SpatialVLM/SpatialRGPT without adequately acknowledging it achieves this by restricting to a narrower, easier class of questions. Explicitly positioning GRAID as trading question expressiveness for answer reliability would be more honest and preempt this criticism.
- **Comparison against community reimplementation, not original SpatialVLM**: The SpatialVLM numbers come from "OpenSpaces one of the more popularly used datasets generated by the community implementation of SpatialVLM" (Section 4). The paper is transparent about this in Section 4, but the abstract and introduction frame it as a comparison against "recent work" without this qualification.
- **Only GRAID-BDD without depth was human-evaluated**: The depth question variant (which uses depth models that the paper criticizes in other methods) and the NuImages/Waymo variants have no human quality assessment. This limits the claimed quality of the 8.5M pairs to only the 3.82M BDD-without-depth subset.
- **RQ3 benchmark tables (Tables 4-6) in appendix**: The most important experimental results are referenced in the main text (line 269: "The results in Table 4, 5, and 6 provide further evidence...") with selective highlights, but the full tables are deferred. The text concedes "lesser gains in Qwen 3" — if GRAID data only marginally helps or harms a stronger model, this affects the contribution's generality.

### Trivial
- Duplicate sentence on line 53: "Table 1 offers a comparison of the differences between GRAID and prior methods." appears twice.

- The interpretability methods discussion (Grad-CAM, Score-CAM, etc.) in Section 3.1 is tangential — these are CNN explainability tools, not validation methods for detection quality.

## Novel Insights
The paper's genuinely novel contribution is demonstrating that 2D bounding box geometry alone — a much simpler and more reliable signal than 3D reconstruction — suffices for generating high-quality spatial reasoning training data at scale. The cross-task generalization results (RQ2) provide concrete evidence that models learn compositional spatial primitives from this data (6 question types → 10+ held-out types, BDD → NuImages). The finding that GRAID-tuned models avoid the non-spatial regressions that SpatialVLM-tuned models exhibit further implicates data quality — not data quantity or model capacity — as a key bottleneck in spatial VLM training.

## Suggestions
- Disaggregate the comparison metrics: report question validity, answer validity, and combined validity as separate rows for both GRAID and SpatialVLM. The current presentation obscures a result that is actually stronger than it appears.
- Add a paragraph explicitly framing the expressiveness-reliability tradeoff. This would preempt the most obvious criticism and strengthen positioning.
- Evaluate at least the depth question variant of GRAID for quality, since these use the depth models the paper criticizes.
- Report inter-annotator agreement for the human evaluation.
