## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting RL rewards with a KL divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s), and claims to treat the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable updated online. The KL shaping mechanism shows positive results on a toy 2-state MDP, while the Bayesian adaptation of β—the paper's primary claimed novelty—is presented through posterior evolution plots and summary statistics but is never mathematically specified.

## Strengths

- **Clean toy MDP demonstration (Table 1, Figure 1):** Any β > 0 yields 69–74% improvement over standard Q-learning with substantially reduced variance (std from 11.30 to 6.00–6.66), providing controlled evidence that the KL shaping mechanism works in sparse-reward settings.
- **Transparent reporting of negative MountainCar results (Table 2):** High β (0.3, 1.0) degrades performance severely (−52.76%, −75.01%), honestly characterizing β sensitivity rather than cherry-picking favorable values.
- **Conceptual distinction from prior KL-regularized RL (Section 2.2):** The paper clearly differentiates by applying KL over predicted *outcome distributions* rather than successive *policy distributions* (as in TRPO/PPO/SAC), which is a genuine conceptual distinction.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Bayesian adaptation of β — the primary claimed novelty — is never specified.** The abstract (line 9), introduction (line 21), and conclusion (line 289) all position Bayesian adaptation of β as the central contribution. Yet the methods section (Section 3) provides no prior distribution, likelihood function, or update rule for β. The algorithm in Section 5.1 (lines 149–155) lists five steps — predict outcomes, specify preferences, compute incentive, shape reward, update policy — none of which involve updating β. The paper shows results of Bayesian adaptation (Figures 3–4, Table 3) and claims "posterior concentration," but the mathematical mechanism producing these results is entirely absent. Without this specification, the claimed contribution is non-reproducible and non-verifiable.

### Major

- **Internal inconsistency: text claims β converges to ~0.1, data shows ~0.02.** The text states (line 230): "the posterior mean quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)." However, the Figure 3 caption (line 237) explicitly states "The posterior mean starts at approximately 0.11 in Round 1 and decreases to about 0.02 by Round 4," and Table 3 (line 321) confirms Round 4 mean β = 0.0173. The text repeatedly claims convergence toward β ≈ 0.1 while the presented data shows the posterior moving away from 0.1 toward ~0.02.
- **The most critical result is stated but "not shown."** Line 228: "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value." This is the paper's headline claim — that Bayesian adaptation eliminates manual tuning — and the evidence is absent.
- **No comparison to any intrinsic motivation or exploration baseline.** The only baselines are vanilla RL and different values of the authors' own β. There is no comparison to ICM, RND, RE3, or any other exploration method, making it impossible to assess whether IncentRL offers anything beyond a simple reward bonus.
- **Limited evaluation with minimal statistical reporting.** Only 3 environments (2-state MDP, MountainCar, MiniGrid 8×8), all simple. Only 3 seeds for MountainCar and MiniGrid; no confidence intervals or statistical tests for MiniGrid success rates (98% vs 90.5%). MountainCar results are mostly negative except β=0.1. Bayesian adaptation results are absent for MountainCar.

### Minor

- **Propositions 1–2 are trivial.** Proposition 1 is a standard continuity argument (small β perturbations preserve optimality in finite MDPs). Proposition 2 states that as β→∞, the KL term dominates. Neither provides meaningful theoretical insight.
- **KL shaping not fully differentiated from broader KL-regularized RL literature.** While distinguished from TRPO/PPO, broader KL-control frameworks already encompass various regularization targets; the paper does not engage with this.

## Novel Insights

The paper identifies a genuine and underexplored distinction: applying KL divergence over outcome distributions (predicted vs. preferred) rather than policy distributions (as in TRPO/PPO/SAC) creates a different type of regularization signal that could serve as a goal-conditioned exploration bonus. However, the paper does not fully develop this distinction, and the core claimed contribution (Bayesian β adaptation) is unspecified, severely limiting the insight's practical value.

## Suggestions

1. **Fully specify the Bayesian β adaptation** — provide the prior, likelihood, update rule, and computational details. This is the single most important fix.
2. **Resolve the text-data inconsistency** — either correct the text to state β converges to ~0.02 (not ~0.1), or explain why ~0.02 is the "effective region."
3. **Show the Bayesian adaptation results for MiniGrid and MountainCar explicitly.**
4. **Add comparisons to at least one intrinsic motivation baseline** (e.g., ICM or RND).
5. **Report error bars and statistical tests** for MiniGrid; increase the number of seeds.
