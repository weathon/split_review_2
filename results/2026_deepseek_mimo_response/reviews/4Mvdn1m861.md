## Summary
This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework built on SAM that uses probabilistic prompt generation and output token-based verification with the TS-SS similarity metric. The method achieves MAE 16.25 on FSC-147 (best among SAM-only training-free methods) and MAE 4.68 on CARPK (outperforming all supervised methods), without any training or auxiliary models.

## Strengths
- **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for verification, with empirical validation**: Table 5 directly demonstrates that output token-based verification (MAE 16.25) outperforms image embedding-based verification (MAE 16.94) under identical conditions. Figure 5's PCA visualizations corroborate this by showing clearer separation of positive/negative instances in output token space. This validates the paper's central technical claim that SAM's decoder tokens, which jointly encode semantic and positional information, are more discriminative for counting verification than raw image features.

- **Probabilistic prompt generation with iterative distribution updates**: Table 4 shows consistent MAE improvement as iteration count and prompts-per-iteration increase (from 29.10 at 8 iter/32 prompts to 16.25 at 16 iter/128 prompts), validating the iterative expansion design that disperses prompts from high-confidence regions outward to discover spatially distant objects.

- **Strong CARPK performance surpassing all supervised methods**: On CARPK, the method achieves MAE 4.68 (Table 1), outperforming all training-based methods including BMNet+ (5.76), SAFECOUNT (5.33), and LOCA (9.97), while using only SAM without additional models.

- **Systematic ablation isolating each design choice**: Tables 3, 4, and 5 validate the TS-SS metric (16.25 vs. cosine 16.48, L2 16.53, dot product 19.58), the effect of iteration/prompt count, and the advantage of output token space over image embedding space. The ablation provides a principled argument for why non-hybrid metrics plateau and motivates the TS-SS approach.

- **Useful method categorization**: The distinction between "SAM only" and "SAM+α" methods (Table 1) provides a fairer comparison framework, highlighting the method's efficiency—achieving competitive results without auxiliary models unlike TFCAC (DINO), OmniCount (CLIP), or TFCounter.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Catastrophic RMSE on FSC-147 is silently ignored**: From Table 1, the method achieves RMSE = 133.11 on FSC-147, barely better than the raw SAM baseline (137.50) and worse than TFOC (132.16) and C-Anything (131.24). The RMSE/MAE ratio of ~8.2 (vs. 4.59 for TFCAC, 5.28 for LOCA, 3.24 for the SAM baseline) indicates extreme outlier errors—some images must be miscounted by 100+ objects. The paper reports all four metrics (MAE, RMSE, NAE, SRE) in Table 1 but selectively discusses only MAE in the body text. The abstract claims "superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25" without mentioning RMSE. The Discussion section vaguely references "dense distributions of very small instances" but provides no quantitative failure characterization (e.g., per-image error distribution, scatter plot vs. ground truth). This selective reporting obscures a fundamental fragility that undermines the "superior accuracy" claim.

- **Abstract makes a false blanket claim**: The abstract states "our method achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is factually incorrect: among training-based methods, LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), and BMNet+ (14.62) all achieve superior MAE. Among training-free methods, TFCAC (12.26) is superior. The body of the paper (Section 4.2) correctly narrows the claim to "best among training-free SAM only methods," but the abstract overstates this significantly, creating an internal inconsistency between the abstract and body.

### Minor
- **TS-SS improvement is marginal**: Table 3 shows TS-SS achieves MAE 16.25 vs. cosine similarity at 16.48 and L2 at 16.53—a difference of 0.23 MAE points (~1.4% relative improvement). While TS-SS is consistently the best metric (also lowest RMSE at 133.11 vs. 133.94 and 133.34), the improvement is small enough that without significance testing, it is difficult to distinguish from noise. The paper presents TS-SS as a core contribution with significant theoretical motivation, but the empirical evidence supports only a modest benefit.

- **Computational efficiency claim lacks supporting data**: The paper claims "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC (line 230) but only reports its own runtime of 1.69s/image on an A100. No baseline is timed on the same hardware, making the comparison unverifiable. The method processes up to ~1438 mean prompts through SAM's mask decoder (Table 4), so direct timing comparison is important for assessing practical utility.
