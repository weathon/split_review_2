## Summary
This paper addresses preference optimization for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that generate intermediate reasoning traces before answers. The key insight is that the standard single-trace DPO gradient estimator has high variance due to stochastic trace sampling. The authors propose BVPO, which mixes a high-variance trace-based gradient with a low-variance empty-trace gradient (conditioned on no reasoning trace), and provides theoretical analysis of the MSE-optimal mixing weight, variance reduction guarantees, and SGD convergence improvements. Experiments on three DeepSeek-R1 distill models across alignment and math reasoning benchmarks show consistent improvements over DPO and SimPO.

## Strengths
- **Well-formalized problem with clear ideal-vs-practical distinction**: Section 3.2 cleanly separates the intractable marginal loss $\mathcal{L}_m$ (requiring summation over all traces) from the practical trace-based loss $\mathcal{L}_t$, establishing trace-induced gradient variance as a concrete, measurable bottleneck for LRM alignment that is distinct from standard preference optimization challenges.
- **Progressive theoretical framework with four interconnected theorems**: Theorem 1 (conditional variance reduction, $\alpha^2$ factor) → Theorem 2 (closed-form MSE-optimal $\alpha^*$ with domination guarantee $\text{MSE}(g_c(\alpha^*)) \leq \min\{\text{MSE}(g_t), \text{MSE}(g_e)\}$) → Theorem 3 (SGD convergence with explicit bias² + variance error floor) → Theorem 4 (under $\eta L = 1$, MSE-optimal $\alpha^*$ is also convergence-optimal). Each theorem supports the next in a coherent chain.
- **Consistent empirical improvements across three model scales**: BVPO outperforms DPO and SimPO on both Arena-Hard and AlpacaEval 2 for all three LRMs (R1-Qwen-7B, R1-Qwen-1.5B, R1-0528-Qwen3-8B) in both Thinking and NoThinking modes (Table 1), with gains up to 7.8 points AlpacaEval 2 and 6.8 points Arena-Hard.
- **Reasoning preservation and improvement after alignment**: Table 2 shows BVPO improves average math reasoning across six benchmarks by up to 4.0 points over the base model, despite training exclusively on general conversational data — a practically important finding.
