## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading that generates grade-salient region proposals internally and uses them to guide cross-view fusion, reducing dependence on external lesion annotations. The Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module extracts top-K high-evidence regions from CAM-derived maps, and the Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion (LGRF) module uses a gated mixture-of-experts and top-K-weighted cross-view attention to fuse these proposals across views. On MFIDDR (four-view) and DRTiD (two-view) datasets, the method achieves competitive accuracy, matching or surpassing several externally-informed methods without requiring external annotations at inference.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem and clear framing (Sections 1, Abstract).** The paper clearly articulates the tension between end-to-end multi-view DR grading (which loses spatial detail through striding/tokenization) and externally-informed methods (which require costly annotations and create deployment brittleness). The proposed approach — generating grade-salient region proposals within the pipeline as surrogates for external cues — is a sensible and well-motivated attack on this tension.

- **Competitive empirical results on two datasets (Tables 1, 3).** The method without external annotations achieves 83.9% accuracy on MFIDDR (vs. 80.4% for the strongest end-to-end baseline LFMVDR-w/o-lesion) and 76.0% on DRTiD (vs. 75.6% for CrossFiT, an externally-informed method). On MFIDDR, the lesion-free variant matches or surpasses several externally-informed methods, supporting the claim that self-generated proposals can approach annotation-augmented performance. When lesion annotations are incorporated, the method achieves 84.6% (new SOTA on this benchmark).

- **Ablation shows each component contributes consistently (Table 4).** Removing GALP, the expert pool, or LGRF each degrades performance across all four metrics (accuracy, specificity, kappa, F1), confirming that the proposed modules are functional and complementary. The degradation pattern is directionally consistent.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The "lesion proposals" are never validated against actual lesion annotations.** The paper relies on CAM to identify grade-predictive regions and interprets them as "lesion proposals" throughout (Section 3.2, Eq. 3-7; Abstract: "self-derived lesion proposals"; Conclusion: "act as surrogates for costly expert cues"). The MFIDDR dataset provides lesion segmentation masks (Section 4.1: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), yet the paper presents no analysis quantifying whether GALP proposals actually overlap with real lesions. A CAM could highlight non-lesion regions (e.g., lighting artifacts, discriminative anatomical features) that correlate with grade but are not lesions. Without this validation, the central conceptual claim — that internally-generated proposals serve as surrogates for external lesion annotations — rests on an untested assertion. The method would still function as an effective attention mechanism for grade-relevant regions, but the motivating narrative is undermined as written.

- **The DRTiD comparison with end-to-end baselines is confounded by unequal pretraining.** The proposed method uses an EyePACS-pretrained Swin-B backbone for DRTiD (Section 4.1, line 208: "for DRTiD, the backbone is pretrained on the fundus dataset EyePACS"). The end-to-end baselines in Table 3 — Binocular Network, Cv-Transformer, MVCNN variants, DeepDR (all from 2020–2021) — almost certainly use ImageNet-only initialization. Fundus-specific pretraining is known to provide substantial gains in DR grading, so the 2.0% accuracy gap (76.0% vs. 74.0% for MVCNN.R) could be partially or fully explained by this pretraining advantage rather than the proposed modules. The method does beat CrossFiT (75.6%), which uses the same EyePACS pretraining, so the advantage over externally-informed methods is on firmer ground. But the claim of outperforming all end-to-end methods (Section 4.2, "experiments on DRTiD") is not cleanly supported without controlling for pretraining. The paper should at minimum acknowledge this confound and ideally report results with ImageNet-only initialization.

### Minor

- **No variance or confidence intervals reported in any table (Tables 1-4).** All results are single numbers. The ablation deltas (1.2-1.6%) and the DRTiD comparisons (~1-2% gaps) could fall within random seed variation. This is particularly relevant for a medical grading application where reliability matters and the headline differences are modest.

- **The "w/o GALP" ablation (Table 4) conflates two changes:** it removes the proposal mechanism AND increases the number of tokens fed to LGRF (all tokens instead of K1 selected ones). The observed 1.2% drop could stem from the optimization burden of processing many more tokens rather than specifically from losing proposal selectivity. A cleaner ablation would hold the token count constant (e.g., random selection).

- **Cross-view fusion is restricted to adjacent (cyclic) pairs** (Section 3.3, line 123: view i ↔ i+1 cyclically) rather than full pairwise fusion across all views. The paper provides no justification for this design choice and does not compare against a full-pairwise variant. For a 4-view dataset, this means only 4 directed fusion pathways instead of 6 unordered pairs, which is a meaningful restriction that could matter for views that are not geometrically adjacent.

- **The claim in the Abstract (line 9) that external annotations "break the end-to-end training" is imprecise.** Methods like CVSA use vessel masks as auxiliary inputs but still train the grading backbone end-to-end; the practical concern is about requiring external inputs at inference time and creating system brittleness, not about architectural two-stage training. The Introduction (lines 39-41) clarifies this as a workflow burden, but the abstract phrasing overstates the issue.

## Suggestions

The single highest-leverage improvement is to validate GALP proposals against the lesion masks available in MFIDDR. This would either substantiate or require reframing the paper's central conceptual claim. The second priority is to acknowledge and control for the pretraining confound on DRTiD. Both issues are addressable with additional experiments, and neither undermines the technical soundness of the method.

## Score and Decision

This paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound framework with competitive empirical results and clean ablations. The core idea — generating grade-relevant proposals internally and using them for cross-view fusion — is novel and effective. However, two significant evidential gaps (unvalidated lesion-proposal correspondence, pretraining confound on DRTiD) prevent the paper's central claims from being fully supported as written. Both are addressable and do not threaten the method's technical validity. The paper merits acceptance conditional on addressing these gaps.
