## Summary

This paper identifies a genuine problem — semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, where dominant components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker ones (e.g., seasonality) — and proposes a diagnostic metric (SDE) to measure it, plus an asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) mechanism built on top of CoST to mitigate it. The paper also honestly reports a negative result where direct SDE regularization fails.

## Strengths

- **Clear problem diagnosis using synthetic data (Section 4.2, Table 1).** The paper constructs controlled synthetic signals with varying trend/periodicity amplitude ratios and shows that TS2Vec's embeddings exhibit progressive representational imbalance. The SDE values cleanly trace the asymmetry across the ratio range (r=0.1 to r=10), and the balanced case (r=1) shows nearly symmetric SDE (~0.44 vs ~0.41). This is the most convincing empirical finding in the paper.

- **Honest reporting of a negative result (Section 4.3, Table 2).** The paper transparently shows that directly regularizing SDE as a training objective does *not* improve forecasting performance and in several cases makes it worse. This kind of negative finding is valuable for the community.

- **The SDE metric itself is conceptually clean.** The idea of measuring whether component information is linearly recoverable from a composite embedding (1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a))) is simple, interpretable, and has intuitive diagnostic appeal.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Central claim of "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" is not supported by the evidence (Table 3).** The abstract promises "consistent gains," but the data tells a different story. On Electricity (horizons 168, 336, 720), CoST+APW is substantially *worse than all baselines* (e.g., Elec 168: 0.566/0.450 vs best 0.412/0.384). On Weather, TNC often outperforms CoST+APW (Weather 336: 0.215 vs 0.266; Weather 720: 0.219 vs 0.299). On ETT1, vanilla CoST beats CoST+APW on two of five horizons (horizons 48 and 720). No variance or statistical significance is reported, so the tiny differences in many settings (e.g., ETT1 horizon 24: 0.040 vs 0.040) could be within random seed noise. The claim of "consistent gains" is simply not supported by the presented numbers.

- **The paper never reports SDE values for the proposed method on real data.** Line 263 states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE columns exist. The paper claims CoST+APW "leads to consistently lower SDE values" (line 300) but provides zero SDE measurements for CoST vs. CoST+APW on any real benchmark. The diagnostic evidence (Table 1) is on TS2Vec with *synthetic* data; the paper never demonstrates that CoST actually has the same imbalance problem, nor that CoST+APW mitigates it. The central causal chain — diagnose imbalance → apply APW → reduced imbalance → better forecasting — is broken at every link by missing measurements.

- **No ablation of the MLP fusion layer (Section 4.4.2).** The method introduces an MLP to produce composite embeddings from concatenated trend and seasonal representations. Its contribution is never isolated. Without an ablation study (CoST → CoST+MLP → CoST+APW), the reader cannot determine whether any observed effect comes from the weighting mechanism, the MLP, or their interaction.

### Minor

- **"Pluggable without architectural changes" claim is contradicted.** The abstract says SDE can be "plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes," but Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP fusion layer that modifies CoST's architecture. This is a direct self-contradiction.

- **SDE naming inconsistency.** The paper uses three different names: "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2, formal definition), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.1 metrics, Section 4.3). These are not minor synonyms — they imply different concepts. This makes the paper appear rushed.

- **No analysis of the asymmetry factor Δ itself.** The method dynamically reweights losses based on Δ, but the paper never reports how Δ evolves during training, whether it stabilizes or oscillates, or whether the weighting scheme ever applies significant reweighting (vs. staying near Δ ≈ 0 where weights ≈ 1).

- **No sensitivity analysis for γ and γ'.** The method introduces two hyperparameters (Section 3.4) that control the strength of reweighting. Their values are not stated, and no robustness analysis is provided.

- **No variance or confidence intervals in Table 3.** Without error estimates, the reader cannot assess whether the small differences between CoST+APW and baselines are reliable.

## Suggestions

1. **Reframe the paper's primary contribution around the SDE diagnostic tool**, presenting APW as an exploratory attempt that needs further development. This would align the claims with the actual evidence.
2. **Add a dedicated experiment reporting SDE values for all methods on real data** — this is essential to support any claim about imbalance mitigation.
3. **Add ablation studies** separating MLP effects from weighting effects.
4. **Fix the SDE naming** to a single consistent term.
5. **Add variance estimates** to all experimental tables.
6. **Report Δ training dynamics** to demonstrate the adaptive mechanism is active.

## Score and Decision

The paper has a genuine diagnostic contribution in the SDE metric and the synthetic-data demonstration of semantic imbalance. The honest reporting of the negative SDE-regularization result is commendable. However, the paper's central claim — that APW produces "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" — is not supported by the evidence presented. The method fails substantially on several benchmarks, key diagnostic measurements are absent (no SDE values on real data for the proposed method), the "pluggable without architectural changes" claim is contradicted, and the MLP is never ablated. The paper oversells its method relative to what the experiments actually show. With major revisions and honest reframing, the diagnostic contribution could form the basis of a stronger paper, but in its current form the evaluation is insufficient to support the stated claims.
