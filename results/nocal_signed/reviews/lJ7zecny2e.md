## Summary

This paper introduces Geo-CoT, a framework for producing structured, multi-step reasoning chains in remote sensing VLMs. It contributes Geo-CoT380k, a large-scale dataset (384,591 samples) of chain-of-thought rationales generated by GPT-4V conditioned on ground-truth annotations, and RSThinker, a model trained via two-stage alignment (SFT followed by GRPO). The empirical results are strong: RSThinker achieves dominant SOTA performance across visual grounding, object counting, detection, classification, captioning, and VQA, including on zero-shot benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **Large-scale, diverse dataset.** Geo-CoT380k (384,591 samples) covers VQA, captioning, classification, visual grounding, object counting, and detection across multiple established benchmarks (Table 1). The structured rationale annotations retrofit ground-truth data with reasoning traces via a scalable GPT-4V pipeline conditioned on verified bounding boxes, reducing hallucination risk. *(impact: +8.0)*

- **Consistent and often dramatic accuracy gains.** RSThinker outperforms all baselines across nearly every task and metric. On DIOR-RSVG visual grounding (Table 4), RSThinker scores 93.1 @0.5 vs. the next best at 59.6 (+33.5 points). On RSOD zero-shot counting (Table 5), it achieves 95.5 Acc vs. the next best at 51.5. *(impact: +8.6)*

- **Clean ablation study (Table 8)** that decomposes contributions: CoT-based SFT is the primary driver of gains, and the KL-divergence analysis (Figure 4) showing format collapse without KL regularization is a useful practical insight. *(impact: +9.3)*

- **Zero-shot generalization is genuinely tested.** Several evaluation columns are marked (ZS) — RSOD, NWPU-VHR for counting; RRSIS-D, RSVG for grounding; RS19, SIRI, UCM for classification — providing clean evidence that capabilities generalize beyond training data. *(impact: +9.0)*

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Faithfulness is central to the paper's framing but never quantitatively evaluated.** The title, abstract, introduction, and method sections repeatedly emphasize "faithful," "verifiable," and "perceptually-grounded" reasoning. The paper claims its outputs are "as verifiable as their final outputs are correct" (line 348) and that GRPO rewards "optimize for the faithfulness of the grounded evidence" (line 65). Yet the evaluation contains no quantitative measure of faithfulness or verifiability whatsoever — no human evaluation of whether reasoning traces match image evidence, no automated faithfulness metric, no systematic comparison of reasoning quality against baselines. The paper's own failure analysis admits that "the textual 'verification' step can occasionally act as a stylistic heuristic" (line 344) but never investigates how often this occurs. While the paper's empirical SOTA contributions stand on their own, the gap between the strong faithfulness framing and the complete absence of any faithfulness evaluation is a significant weakness. *(impact: -9.1)*

### Minor

- **The main qualitative example (Figure 5) does not match the paper's claimed principle of "strict perceptual grounding" (line 31) with "specific, falsifiable spatial references" (line 235).** The model's grounding step outputs vague directional language — "three aircraft parked closely together on one side of the terminal, and two more on the opposite side," "one additional airplane positioned at the far end of the runway" — with no bounding-box coordinates or precise spatial anchors. While the failure case (Figure 7) usefully mentions a coordinate [413,225], the main representative example undercuts the perceptual-grounding claim. *(impact: -5.9)*

- **The GRPO reward functions (Table 3) are based entirely on answer-correctness metrics** (IoU, mAP, BLEU, accuracy), yet the paper claims they are designed to "optimize for the faithfulness of the grounded evidence" (line 65). Since the rewards measure only whether the final answer is correct — not whether the reasoning trace is faithful to image evidence — this claim overstates what the optimization is actually targeting. *(impact: -3.3)*

- **The comparison against baselines for in-distribution benchmarks is confounded by training-data advantage.** RSThinker is trained on the training splits of benchmarks it is evaluated on (Table 1), but the paper does not specify whether RS-specific baselines (GeoChat, VHM, SkySenseGPT, EarthDial) were equivalently fine-tuned on the same training data. The magnitude of in-distribution gains (e.g., +33.5 on DIOR-RSVG) cannot be cleanly attributed to the Geo-CoT method vs. task-specific fine-tuning advantage. (The internal ablation in Table 8 partially addresses this for the GLM backbone, but is not featured alongside the headline comparisons.) *(impact: -2.0)*

- **GRPO provides modest gains over SFT (w/ CoT) alone** (Table 8: +0.22 to +3.04 points), yet the paper frames the two-stage strategy as "essential" (line 37). While the claim that the two-stage combination is essential is defensible (SFT w/o CoT + GRPO underperforms SFT w/ CoT alone), the language somewhat overstates the marginal benefit of GRPO given the small effect sizes. *(impact: -2.9)*

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Add a faithfulness evaluation.** Sample N model outputs and check whether spatial claims in the reasoning trace correspond to actual objects using available ground-truth annotations (e.g., bounding-box precision/recall of claims made in the trace). This would directly test the "perceptual grounding" claim.
- **Clarify the scope of the faithfulness claim.** Distinguish between (a) the architecture enabling verifiability (which is demonstrated) and (b) quantitatively verified faithfulness of outputs (which is not yet evaluated).
- **Include the SFT (w/o CoT) baseline in the main results tables** alongside headline comparisons to clarify the contribution of CoT structure vs. task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Tone down the claim** that GRPO rewards "optimize for faithfulness" since they are standard answer-correctness metrics.
