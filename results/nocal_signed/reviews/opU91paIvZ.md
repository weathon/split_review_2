## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of making chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning more *monitorable* — specifically, more faithful (honest about what influenced the answer) and more concise (short enough to be practically reviewed). The authors formulate CoT monitorability as a constrained optimization problem, show that naive RL fails due to vanishing gradients when monitorable traces are rare under the initial policy (the gradient sparsity diagnosis in Eq. 4–5), and propose a prior-guided distillation pipeline: use an instruct model (Qwen 2.5-7B) to transform raw CoTs into monitorable versions, filter for correctness and monitorability, then SFT-train the base model (DeepSeek R1 Qwen-1.5B) on these traces. Experiments on MMLU-Pro (faithfulness) and GSM8K/MATH500 (conciseness) show improvements.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated theoretical analysis (Section 3).** The constrained optimization formulation (Eq. 1) and the gradient analysis (Eq. 4–5) explaining why naive RL fails are the paper's strongest intellectual contribution. The insight that the gradient L₁ vanishes because f(z)≈0 under π₀ — identifying sampling sparsity, not an inherent accuracy-monitorability trade-off, as the core obstacle — is clean, rigorous, and useful for future work.

- **Elegant proof-of-concept experiment (Section 4, Figure 3).** The key intervention — checking whether π₀ still produces correct answers when conditioned on monitorable traces z_s generated by the prior — cleanly separates the hypothesis from the method. Results (faithfulness: 30%→85%, conciseness: 11.6%→96.6% with accuracy preserved) convincingly show that monitorability and task reward are compatible, transforming the problem from "how do we optimize a trade-off" to "how do we make rare good traces more likely."

- **Strong conciseness results (Section 5.2, Figures 5–6).** Going from 11.6% to 96.6% concise traces on MATH500 and 24.1% to 80.0% on GSM8K while holding accuracy at ~90% relative is a large, systematic effect. The full distribution shift (Figure 6) confirms the behavior is reliable, not occasional. Even with some accuracy loss, the magnitude of the conciseness improvement provides compelling evidence that the method works for this dimension.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Faithfulness metric measures surface-level hint verbalization, not genuine faithfulness, and the training pipeline optimizes for exactly this surface signal.** The metric f(z) = 𝟙{hint verbalized in z} (Section 3) is used to filter training data (Algorithm 1, step 13) and as the evaluation criterion — creating an almost circular loop: prompt the prior to verbalize hints → filter for hint-verbalizing traces → SFT on those traces → evaluate by checking for hint verbalization. The paper's conceptual framing (§1: "the reasoning must honestly reflect the actual factors that led to the answer") implies a much stronger claim than the metric supports. The model could be learning a superficial pattern ("when you see a hint in the prompt, repeat it before answering") without its internal reasoning becoming more transparent. The paper provides no evidence — no probe, intervention study, or human evaluation — distinguishing genuine faithfulness from surface-level compliance. The paper follows the established Chen et al. (2025) evaluation framework, but that framework carries the same limitation.

- **Missing critical baseline: comparison to the prior model itself.** The prior Qwen 2.5-7B Instruct was explicitly prompted to produce faithful/concise traces, and its outputs formed the training data. Yet the paper never reports how the trained 1.5B model compares to the 7B prior on the evaluation metrics. Without this, it is impossible to assess how much of the prior's capability was distilled. If the 1.5B model approaches or matches the prior, that is a strong distillation result. If it falls substantially short, the improvement is only a fraction of what the prior already achieves.

### Minor

- **No analysis of the prior model's output quality.** The method's success depends entirely on π_s (Qwen 2.5-7B Instruct) producing high-quality monitorable traces. The paper does not report: what fraction of π_s's outputs are rejected by the filter? What fraction contain errors (hallucinated steps, incorrect summaries) that could slip through the reward filter and pollute the training data?

- **Conciseness evaluation measures only token length, not whether shortened CoTs remain informative for monitoring.** The paper's own motivation (§1) is that long CoTs hinder monitoring, which implies the goal is CoTs that are *long enough to be informative but short enough to be processed*. A 50-token CoT that skips critical intermediate steps could be less monitorable than a longer one. No human evaluation or analysis of reasoning completeness is provided.

- **No statistical significance or confidence intervals reported.** Given that absolute faithfulness improvements are modest (15.2%→25.0% on average, with individual categories showing small absolute gains like 4→10 and 3→9), it is unclear which differences are statistically reliable.

- **Missing ablation: training on π_s outputs with vs. without the filtering/likelihood-selection steps (Algorithm 1, steps 11–14).** Without this, it is unclear which component drives the result — direct distillation might work as well.

## Suggestions

1. Add a direct comparison to the prior model (Qwen 2.5-7B Instruct) on faithfulness and conciseness metrics to contextualize the distillation result.
2. Add an ablation comparing Algorithm 1's full pipeline vs. direct SFT on unfiltered π_s outputs.
3. For faithfulness, either add human evaluation of whether CoTs genuinely reflect decision processes, or explicitly acknowledge the metric's limitation to hint-verbalization and reframe claims accordingly.
4. Report confidence intervals or significance tests for all main results.
5. Include a brief analysis of π_s output quality (rejection rate, error analysis of accepted traces).
6. For conciseness, verify via a small human study or analysis that shortened CoTs remain informative enough for monitoring.

## Score and Decision

The paper's theoretical contributions and conciseness results are strong and well-supported. The proof-of-concept experiment cleanly diagnoses the core problem. However, the faithfulness claims are weakened by the metric circularity issue (measuring surface-level hint verbalization that the model was directly trained to produce) and the absence of basic comparisons (notably to the prior model itself). The faithfulness dimension needs a fundamentally different evaluation strategy to support the paper's stronger claims. I recommend the paper be accepted as a borderline case — the conciseness results alone plus the theoretical analysis justify interest — but the authors should substantially revise the faithfulness evaluation and claims.
