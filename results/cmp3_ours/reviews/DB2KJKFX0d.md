## Summary

This paper proposes BDSB, a pipeline that combines conformal mapping of cortical surfaces to 2D "brain disks" with an unpaired Schrödinger Bridge diffusion model to enhance 3T fMRI signals toward 7T quality. The method addresses the genuine bottleneck that 7T fMRI is scarce and expensive while 3T is widely available. It evaluates on three settings — synthetic (down-sampled 7T+noise with ground truth), cross-dataset real (3T NOD → 7T NSD, no ground truth), and a small paired dataset (TDM, N=2) — using image-quality metrics and downstream pRF retinotopic decoding.

## Strengths

- **Conformal mapping to brain disks is an elegant and well-motivated solution to cross-subject alignment.** The key difficulty in unpaired fMRI enhancement is that 3T and 7T data come from different subjects with different cortical geometries. Mapping all data to a shared 2D parametric domain via conformal parameterization (Sec. 2.2) creates spatial correspondence without requiring paired subjects. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that conformal mapping substantially outperforms both direct slicing and harmonic mapping (R²: 22.02 vs 16.97 vs 6.10), validating its importance.

- **The evaluation design is structurally thoughtful.** The paper transparently confronts the scarcity of paired 3T/7T data by constructing three complementary experiments (synthetic with ground truth, cross-dataset real, TDM paired) that triangulate evidence from different angles. This is more thorough than is typical for papers in this space, and the limitations of each setting are explicitly acknowledged in Sec. 4.

- **Consistent quantitative improvements across nearly all metric/dataset combinations.** In Table 2, the proposed method achieves the best results on 11 out of 13 metric/dataset combinations. The improvements are substantial on key metrics: FID 42.88 vs 71.40 (next-best) on synthetic; FID 70.65 vs 95.91 (next-best) on cross-dataset; R² 24.00 vs 18.30 (raw LQ) on synthetic and 25.91 vs 20.26 on cross-dataset.

- **Application of Schrödinger Bridge to fMRI enhancement is novel.** While the SB formulation follows prior work (CUNSB-RFIE, Dong et al. 2024), adapting it to fMRI signals — a domain with fundamentally different data structure and quality issues than the retinal fundus images it was originally designed for — is a genuine contribution.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The synthetic experiment tests a substantially easier problem than real 3T→7T enhancement.** Synthetic 3T data is generated by down-sampling 7T NSD data from the 164k fsaverage to 32k fsLR surface and adding Gaussian noise (Sec. 2.1). This simulates a degradation that is spatially uniform, stimulus-independent, additive-white-noise in form, and perfectly invertible in principle since the degradation function is known. Real 3T/7T differences involve different pulse sequences, coil configurations, physiological noise, B0 inhomogeneity, and motion profiles that are far more complex. The paper acknowledges this (Sec. 4: "synthetic 3T-like data cannot fully capture scanner hardware, pulse sequence, or subject-level variability"), but the acknowledgment does not change the fact that the strongest quantitative evidence (SSIM 0.855, PSNR 25.05, R² 24.00) comes from this less realistic setting. The paper's central claim — that the method enhances real 3T fMRI toward 7T quality — is best supported by evidence that addresses a weaker surrogate task.

2. **The cross-dataset real experiment lacks ground truth, making the primary claim partially unverifiable.** The cross-dataset experiment (3T NOD → enhanced, compared to 7T NSD distribution) is the most practically relevant scenario, but without ground truth it cannot distinguish between "the enhanced signal is closer to the true 7T neural response" and "the enhanced signal better fits a pRF model or has more 7T-like distributional properties." Two specific concerns:
   - **FID** measures distributional similarity, not fidelity. A model could improve FID by making outputs look generically "7T-like" (smoother, higher-contrast) without preserving subject-specific neural response.
   - **R² can be mechanically inflated** by variance changes: if the enhanced fMRI has lower total variance (e.g., because the model suppresses noise), the denominator in the R² formula shrinks and R² increases even if the signal is not more accurate. The paper does not report whether variance structure changes.
   These concerns do not invalidate the results, but they mean the cross-dataset evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive for the main claim.

### Minor

3. **The TDM paired experiment is underpowered.** The only experiment with real paired ground truth uses 2 subjects, each with a single session split into 3 training/3 test runs. Results are mixed: the proposed method wins on PSNR (19.24 vs 19.18) and FID (62.09 vs 84.45) but *loses* on SSIM (0.718 vs 0.727 for OTT-GAN). The paper acknowledges this limitation (Sec. 4), but it means the one experiment where ground truth exists does not provide clear support.

4. **No confidence intervals or statistical significance reported.** Table 2 reports only point estimates. With small sample sizes (2–8 subjects depending on experiment), reporting variance or significance would materially affect the credibility of the results. The R² improvement from 18.30 to 24.00 on synthetic data could be meaningful, but without error bars the reader cannot assess its reliability.

5. **No qualitative failure analysis for the cross-dataset experiment.** Since there is no ground truth, the paper should at minimum show examples of NOD subjects' original 3T, enhanced, and compare to NSD 7T qualitatively (as in Fig. 4, which only visualizes synthetic experiment results). Currently, all visualizations are from the synthetic experiment, where ground truth exists.

6. **Ablation trade-off between image-quality metrics and functional metrics not discussed.** In Table 3, the harmonic map (row 2) achieves better SSIM (0.833), PSNR (24.19), and FID (35.56) than the full method with both regularizers (0.855, 25.05, 42.88) but much worse R² (16.97 vs 24.00). This suggests that image-quality and functional metrics trade off, and the conformal mapping + regularizers are specifically beneficial for functional decoding. The paper does not explicitly discuss this pattern.

7. **Novelty distinction from prior SB medical-image work could be clearer.** The method description (Sec. 2.3) closely follows the CUNSB-RFIE framework (Dong et al. 2024). The paper would benefit from a clearer statement of which components are novel (applying SB to fMRI, brain disk representation, BD-SSIM regularization) versus adopted from prior work.

### Trivial
None.

- A controlled experiment with held-out subjects from the same dataset to validate whether enhanced signals preserve stimulus-response relationships
- Statistical testing (confidence intervals or significance tests) on the main results
- Analysis of whether enhanced fMRI has different variance, autocorrelation, or noise structure than LQ data, to verify R² improvements are not mechanical

## Removed Points
These points were raised in the harsh review but are removed after verification against the paper:
- **"Baseline comparisons favor proposed method's inherent advantages"**: The baselines (Cycle-GAN, OTT-GAN, OTE-GAN, SCR-Net, fast-DDPM) are all appropriate 2D image translation methods applied within the same brain-disk pipeline. That fast-DDPM requires paired data and thus cannot run on cross-dataset experiments is a factual constraint of the method, not an unfair comparison. The claim about lacking "simpler baselines" is not substantive — the paper's framework transforms data to 2D brain disks, making fMRI-specific denoising methods in 3D volume space inapplicable.
- **"Different stimulus sets entirely"**: Both NSD and NOD include pRF-fLOC stimuli for the subjects used; the stimulus sets are the same pRF protocol, not entirely different.
- **"Subject count mismatch not explained"**: The paper uses 6 NSD subjects as training targets and all 8 as part of the 7T distribution; this is a standard train/test split, not a mismatch.
- **"Computational considerations not discussed"**: A standard omission for methods papers; this does not affect the validity of the contribution.
- **"The problem is well-chosen and practically motivated"**: A generic strength about importance rather than the paper's specific evidence.

## Novel Insights

The most insightful observation across the reviews is the tension in the ablation study: the harmonic map achieves better image-quality metrics (SSIM, PSNR, FID) than the full pipeline but much worse pRF decoding (R²). This reveals that image-quality metrics and functional decoding metrics are not proxies for each other in this domain — improving SSIM/PSNR/FID does not guarantee better downstream decoding, and the conformal mapping + BD-SSIM regularization specifically benefit functional signal preservation rather than perceptual image quality. This finding is important for future work in fMRI enhancement and suggests that evaluation should always include task-specific downstream validation, not just generic image metrics.

## Suggestions

1. **Add a held-out-subject generalization experiment within NSD** — split NSD subjects into "simulated 3T" (down-sampled) and real 7T groups to create a more realistic cross-subject, same-stimulus validation.
2. **Report vertex-level variance statistics** for enhanced vs LQ signals to rule out mechanical R² inflation and strengthen the pRF evidence.
3. **Provide qualitative comparisons for the cross-dataset experiment** (e.g., enhanced NOD disks alongside NSD 7T disks), even without ground truth, to allow visual assessment.
