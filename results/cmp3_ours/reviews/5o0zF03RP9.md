## Summary

The paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments extrinsic RL rewards with a KL divergence term between predicted outcome distributions \(p(o|s,a)\) and preferred outcome distributions \(q(o|s)\). The headline claim is that the incentive weight \(\beta\) is treated as a Bayesian random variable and adapted online, eliminating manual tuning. Experiments on a 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey compare IncentRL at various fixed \(\beta\) values against standard RL (\(\beta = 0\)).

## Strengths

1. **The KL-based shaping formulation itself is clearly specified.** The augmented reward \(r^{\text{total}} = r^{\text{ext}} - \beta \cdot \text{KL}(p(o|s,a) \| q(o|s))\) is well-defined (Section 3, Eq. 1–2), and the idea of aligning predicted outcomes with preferred outcomes is a reasonable extension of KL-regularized RL and active inference ideas.

2. **The MiniGrid Doorkey result with \(\beta = 0.01\) shows a meaningful improvement** over the \(\beta = 0\) baseline (98% vs 90.5% success rate, Figure 2), demonstrating that the KL shaping term can help in a standard sparse-reward benchmark.

3. **The paper honestly acknowledges its limitations** (preference misalignment, KL dominance, Section 6.2), which is a reasonable practice.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

1. **The Bayesian \(\beta\) adaptation — presented as the paper's central contribution — is never specified.** The abstract, contributions list, and conclusion all emphasize that treating \(\beta\) as a Bayesian random variable is the core novelty. Yet the paper never states: (a) the prior over \(\beta\), (b) the likelihood function, (c) the posterior update mechanism, (d) what inference method is used, or (e) how the adapted \(\beta\) is fed back into the RL agent. The Methods section (Section 3) contains no Bayesian update. The algorithm description (Section 5.1) is a 5-step procedure generic to any shaped-reward method. The Bayesian \(\beta\) appears only briefly in Section 5.4, where figures and a table show posterior statistics of an unspecified model. A reader cannot reproduce, evaluate, or even determine what the Bayesian method actually is. A contribution that cannot be described cannot be accepted.

### Major

2. **The empirical evidence does not support the claim that Bayesian \(\beta\) removes manual tuning.** The paper states: "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of \(\beta\) achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" (Section 5.4, line 228). The RL performance of the Bayesian-\(\beta\) variant is never plotted, tabulated, or compared against any alternative. Showing posterior concentration over \(\beta\) (Figures 3–4, Table 3) is not a substitute for showing that the RL agent using those \(\beta\) values performs well. The central claim of the paper rests on evidence explicitly labeled as "not shown."

3. **No comparison against existing intrinsic motivation methods.** The paper positions itself as improving upon intrinsic motivation and discusses ICM, RND, VIME, and count-based exploration in the Related Work. Yet every experiment compares IncentRL (various \(\beta\) values) only against IncentRL with \(\beta = 0\) — i.e., the baseline is standard RL without any shaping. The paper does not include a single comparison against any established intrinsic motivation baseline. A reader cannot assess whether the benefit comes from the specific KL formulation or from having any dense exploration signal at all.

4. **The instantiation of \(p(o|s,a)\) and \(q(o|s)\) in experiments is underspecified.** For MountainCar (Section 5.3), \(q(o|s)\) is described as "assigning all probability to the goal (\(x \geq 0.5\))" but the paper does not specify the outcome space \(o\), how \(p(o|s,a)\) is obtained (learned forward model? known dynamics?), or what distributional form is used. For MiniGrid (Section 5.4), neither distribution is specified. This underspecification harms reproducibility.

### Minor

5. **The theoretical analysis (Section 4) does not constitute a theoretical contribution.** Proposition 1 (small \(\beta\) preserves optimality, via continuity) and Proposition 2 (large \(\beta\) makes KL dominate, by definition) are basic sanity checks that follow immediately from the formulation. The paper lists "theoretical analysis" as one of four main contributions, but these results provide no insight into learning dynamics, convergence rates, or the behavior of the Bayesian adaptation scheme.

6. **Internal coherence issues.** The title centers on "Bayesian Adaptation," yet the Bayesian component is absent from the method description and most experiments. Section 6.4 lists "Deeper evaluation of Bayesian \(\beta\)" as a *future direction*, which undercuts the paper's presentation of it as an existing contribution.

7. **Posterior statistics not reconciled with labeled targets.** Table 3 reports the posterior mean converging to \(\approx 0.02\) by Round 4, while Figures 3–4 mark \(\beta = 0.1\) as the "target effective" value with a dashed line. The paper does not explain why the posterior converges to a different value than the stated target, or what the "target effective" value represents.

8. **Low number of seeds.** The MountainCar and MiniGrid results use only 3 random seeds, which makes the reported improvements (and the degradation at high \(\beta\) in MountainCar) harder to interpret confidently.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Compare against standard intrinsic motivation baselines (ICM, RND, VIME) to contextualize the benefit of the KL shaping term.
- Compare Bayesian \(\beta\) against simple adaptive baselines (e.g., annealing \(\beta\) or meta-gradient methods) to substantiate the "eliminates manual tuning" claim.
- Ablate the choice of KL divergence vs. other divergences (reverse KL, JS, etc.).
- Test robustness to misspecified or poorly chosen \(q(o|s)\).
- Run the Bayesian \(\beta\) variant and report its RL performance (success rate, reward curves) alongside fixed-\(\beta\) sweeps.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The harsh critic correctly identifies that the Bayesian \(\beta\) framing is neither described nor evidenced, which is the dominant structural problem. The useful observation that the claimed Bayesian contribution is listed as future work (Section 6.4) is a specific coherence failure worth noting.

## Suggestions

**Highest priority:** Either fully specify the Bayesian model for \(\beta\) (prior, likelihood, inference method, integration with RL updates) and provide its actual RL performance across several environments, or drop the Bayesian claim entirely and reframe the paper around the KL shaping formulation with fixed \(\beta\) sweeps. The current presentation promises a contribution it does not deliver. If the Bayesian claim is retained, the paper must include: (1) a complete description of the Bayesian model in a dedicated subsection, (2) RL performance curves comparing Bayesian \(\beta\) against best-tuned fixed \(\beta\) and simple adaptive baselines across multiple environments, and (3) a comparison against at least one established intrinsic motivation method to contextualize the contribution.
