## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of making chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces more *monitorable* along two dimensions: faithfulness (does the trace honestly reflect why the model answered as it did?) and conciseness (is the trace short enough to be easily inspected?). The authors identify that naive RL fine-tuning fails because the monitorability signal f(z) is near-zero under the initial policy, causing vanishing gradients (Section 3). They propose a pipeline: (1) use a stronger prior model (Qwen 2.5-7B Instruct) to transform the base model's (DeepSeek R1 Qwen-1.5B) traces into more monitorable versions, (2) filter for correctness and monitorability, and (3) fine-tune via SFT on the transformed traces. Results on MMLU-Pro (with hint injection), GSM8K, and MATH500 show a ~10pp improvement in faithfulness and dramatic conciseness improvements (e.g., from 11.6% to 96.6% of MATH500 traces under 950 tokens) while retaining most task accuracy.

---

## Strengths

1. **Clean gradient analysis of RL failure (Section 3, Eq. 4).** The decomposition of the gradient into L₁ (monitorability) and L₂ (reward) terms provides a concrete, mathematically grounded explanation for why naive policy optimization fails. The diagnosis — that L₁ vanishes because the initial policy almost never samples traces with positive f(z) — is well-reasoned and supported empirically by Figure 2. This is a genuine conceptual contribution that goes beyond "RL doesn't work" to explain *why* mechanistically.

2. **Elegant proof-of-concept experiment (Figure 3).** The intervention — using a prior model to rewrite traces and checking whether the *base* model can still produce correct answers when conditioned on the rewritten traces — cleanly separates the question of capability from generation probability. It convincingly shows that the base model *can* reason correctly under monitorable CoTs but rarely generates such traces, turning the sparse-gradient diagnosis into actionable evidence that motivates the pipeline.

3. **Striking conciseness results.** On GSM8K, the trained model shifts from 24.1% to 80.0% of responses under the length threshold; on MATH500, from 11.6% to 96.6% (Figure 5). The distribution shift in Figure 6 confirms the effect is systematic. Even accounting for the ~10% relative accuracy drop, these numbers are impressive and substantially exceed what prompting-based baselines achieve.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Inconsistent accuracy retention claims.** The contributions bullet (Section 1, line 55) states "maintaining at least **96%** of the base model's task accuracy," but Section 5.2 (line 296) reports "The accuracy drop remains within **~10%** relative to the base" (i.e., ~90% relative), and the Figure 5 caption (line 307) says "maintaining an average relative accuracy of approximately **90%**." These are inconsistent. Figures 4–5 show only the monitorability metric without the corresponding accuracy numbers for the fine-tuned model, making it impossible for a reader to independently verify either claim.

2. **No ablation studies.** The pipeline has several components: (a) the prior model's rewrite step, (b) the reward filter, (c) the monitorability filter, and (d) the likelihood-based trace selection. Without ablations, it is impossible to attribute the improvement to any specific component. The most informative missing ablation would be to train on traces generated by the *base model itself* (prompted with the same instructions as the prior) with the same filtering, isolating whether the prior's rewriting is necessary or whether filtering + SFT alone drives the improvement.

### Minor

3. **Faithfulness evaluation measures hint-verbalization, not causal faithfulness.** The paper operationalizes faithfulness as f(z) = 1{hint verbalized in z} (Section 3, line 89), following the established protocol from Chen et al. (2025). The training pipeline then generates traces that verbalize hints, filters to keep those that do, and evaluates on hint-verbalization. This creates a risk of circularity: the improvement (15% → 25%) may partly reflect successful instruction-following rather than genuine increases in reasoning transparency. A counterfactual test (e.g., swapping to a misleading hint and checking whether the model's answer tracks the hint) would substantially strengthen the faithfulness claims. The paper acknowledges the LLM-as-judge limitation but not this deeper issue.

4. **Missing accuracy numbers in main figures.** Figures 4 and 5 show only the monitorability metric. Accuracy is referenced only in prose. Adding accuracy columns or overlays would let readers directly verify the "without sacrificing accuracy" claim.

5. **LLM-as-judge metric not validated.** The faithfulness judge relies on an LLM to detect hint-verbalization (Section 5.1), with no reported inter-annotator agreement with humans, no false positive/negative analysis, and no calibration against ground truth. Since the hints were recreated by the authors (not released by Chen et al., 2025), the potential for judge bias compounds the issue.

6. **Missing baseline: SFT on base-model traces.** Figure 4 compares against "Direct Prompting" and "Indirect Prompting" (prompt-only baselines) but not against a model trained via SFT on the base model's own generated traces (filtered by the same criteria). This would control for the effect of SFT training itself versus the prior's guidance.

7. **Constrained optimization framing is motivational, not operative.** The paper derives a Lagrangian (Eq. 3) and a revised objective (Eq. 6), but Algorithm 1 does not optimize these — it is a data-augmentation-plus-distillation pipeline. The Lagrangian, λ, and policy gradients never appear in the training procedure. While many ML papers use constrained optimization as inspiration without directly solving it, the paper should be explicit that Eq. 6 motivates rather than describes Algorithm 1.

### Trivial

8. **Filter inequality direction unclear (Algorithm 1, line 13).** The filter condition "f(z_{si}) ≤ β" appears potentially backward for both the faithfulness setting (where we want f(z)=1) and the conciseness setting (where f(z)=1{Length<β}). Clarification or correction needed.

9. **"10% gain in reasoning faithfulness" is ambiguous.** The abstract claims "about an additional 10%" — this could be read as either absolute or relative percentage points. Figure 4 shows 15.2% → 25.0% (9.8pp absolute, ~64% relative). The paper should be explicit about which interpretation is intended.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's most insightful observation is the structural tension between the paper's framing of faithfulness ("honestly reflect the actual factors that led to the answer") and its operationalization (hint-verbalization). This gap is present in the prior work the paper builds on (Chen et al., 2025) and is not unique to this paper, but it means the faithfulness results should be interpreted as "models learn to explicitly acknowledge hints more often" rather than "models learn to be more causally faithful." The critic also correctly identifies the disconnect between the constrained optimization formalism (Eq. 1–6) and the actual SFT pipeline (Algorithm 1) — though this mismatch is common in ML papers and less concerning than the evaluation gap.

---

## Suggestions

1. **Resolve the 96% vs 90% accuracy inconsistency.** Report absolute accuracy numbers for every model in a table alongside the monitorability metrics. Use a single, consistent figure throughout.

2. **Add at least one ablation.** The most informative single ablation: train on the base model's own traces (generated under the same instructional prompts given to the prior) filtered by the same correctness and monitorability criteria. This separates the effect of distillation from the effect of filtering+SFT.

3. **Add a counterfactual faithfulness test.** For a subset of test examples, swap the hint (e.g., from correct to misleading) and check whether the model's final answer changes with the hint. If the model mentions the hint but ignores it when the hint is incorrect, that suggests genuine acknowledgment; if it follows the hint regardless, that suggests parroting.
