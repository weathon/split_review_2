## Summary

This paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a diagnostic metric that measures whether individual semantic components (trend and seasonality) are linearly recoverable from a composite time-series embedding. It then integrates SDE into an asymmetric weighting scheme that dynamically reweights per-component contrastive losses within the CoST framework to mitigate semantic imbalance. Experiments on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets compare CoST+APW against CoST, TS2Vec, and TNC baselines.

## Strengths

1. **SDE is a conceptually interesting diagnostic tool.** The idea of checking whether each component's information is linearly recoverable from the composite embedding via a cosine-similarity test (Section 3.2) is sensible, novel, and could be useful for analyzing learned representations. The synthetic experiments in Table 1 confirm the expected monotonic trend, validating that SDE behaves as one would hope for a probe of representational balance.

2. **The paper honestly reports a negative result.** Table 2 shows that directly adding SDE as a regularization term to TS2Vec hurts rather than helps performance. Including this null result is valuable scientific practice — it saves the community from a dead end and correctly frames the positive contribution (asymmetric weighting within CoST) as non-obvious.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Claims inconsistent with experimental results (overclaiming).** The abstract states "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and Section 4.4.4 claims "superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines." Table 3 does not support this. On **Electricity** at horizons 168, 336, and 720, CoST+APW is *substantially worse* than CoST (0.566 vs 0.425 at h=168; 0.677 vs 0.576 at h=336; 1.010 vs 0.911 at h=720). On **Weather** at horizons 336 and 720, CoST+APW underperforms CoST (0.266 vs 0.256; 0.299 vs 0.278). On **ETT1**, the method wins on 2/5 horizons, ties on 1, and loses on 2. The overall pattern is mixed at best — the paper's strongest quantitative claim is contradicted by its own data. A reader comparing CoST vs. CoST+APW across the full table would see no systematic advantage.

2. **No variance or statistical significance reported.** Every cell in Tables 2 and 3 is a single number with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or number of seeds. The differences between CoST and CoST+APW are often <0.01 MSE (e.g., 0.040 vs 0.039 at ETT1 h=24; 0.200 vs 0.206 at ETT2 h=336). When the "improvement" margin is 0.001–0.005 MSE with no variance, the result is uninterpretable.

3. **Missing SDE values on real data despite promising them.** Section 4.4.4 states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets." However, Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE columns — no SDE values appear anywhere. The paper's central claim that the method "leads to consistently lower SDE values" on real benchmarks is asserted without the evidence the paper itself promises to provide.

### Minor

4. **SDE naming inconsistency.** The acronym SDE stands for three different phrases: "Semantic *Disentanglement* Error" (title, abstract), "Semantic *Separability* Error" (Section 3.2 heading and body), and "Semantic *Decomposition* Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3). These are not synonyms and reflect imprecision in what SDE actually measures.

5. **SDE definition vs. implementation mismatch.** The theoretical definition in Section 3.2 assumes a single encoder v(·) applied to separated and combined signals (v(a), v(b), v(a+b)). In the CoST+APW setup (Section 4.4), v(a) and v(b) come from separate encoders (TFD and SFD), while v(a+b) comes from an MLP fusion layer. The relationship between the theoretical framing and the practical implementation is never clarified, and these may produce different SDE behavior.

6. **No ablation of the MLP fusion layer.** Section 4.4.2 introduces a learned MLP that maps concatenated component embeddings to a composite embedding. This MLP is trained jointly with CoST, but there is no ablation testing whether the asymmetric weighting works *without* the MLP, or whether the MLP alone (without weighting) produces the observed effects. Without this, we cannot attribute improvements to the claimed mechanism.

7. **Hyperparameters γ, γ' undisclosed and unstudied.** The weighting formula (Section 3.4, Equation L = (1+γ·Δ)L\_season + (1+γ'·(-Δ))L\_trend) has two free hyperparameters controlling sensitivity to Δ. Their values are never reported and no sensitivity analysis is provided. Given the mixed results, knowing how these were chosen is essential.

### Trivial

8. **Framing oversells novelty relative to CoST.** The paper lists three "fundamental limitations of current contrastive approaches." Limitations 1 (no inductive bias for decomposition) and 2 (time-domain-only contrastive objectives) were already addressed by CoST (Woo et al., 2022), which the paper adopts as its starting point. The paper's actual contribution (asymmetric weighting) addresses limitation 3 (isotropic embedding collapse). This inflates the perceived novelty.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Test on naturally "semantically skewed" domains (e.g., medical time-series with rare events, industrial fault signals) where the imbalance problem would be most acute — the synthetic experiment is the only direct evidence of the problem the paper claims to solve.
- Study hyperparameter sensitivity for γ and γ' to understand how robust the method is.

## Suggestions

1. **Revise claims to match evidence.** The abstract and conclusion should accurately reflect the mixed pattern of results — improvements on some settings, degradation on others, particularly Electricity at long horizons. A "diagnostic + preliminary mitigation" framing would be more credible than "consistent gains."

2. **Report SDE values on real data** as an additional column in Table 3 or a separate table (e.g., Figure showing Δ over training). This is the most critical missing piece — the paper's framework connects SDE to downstream performance, but this link is never empirically validated on real benchmarks.

3. **Add variance estimates** (standard deviations across ≥3 seeds) to all reported results. The observed margins are too small to interpret without them.

4. **Ablate the MLP fusion layer** and report the chosen values for γ, γ', including a sensitivity analysis.

5. **Resolve the SDE naming** to a single consistent term.
