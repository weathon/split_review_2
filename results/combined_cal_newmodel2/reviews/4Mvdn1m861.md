Let me write the full review now.## Summary

This paper proposes TOKENCOUNT, a training-free object counting framework built on top of the Segment Anything Model (SAM). The method consists of two components working in synergy: a probabilistic prompt generation stage that models object location likelihood across the image, and an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens (rather than image embedding similarity) to distinguish positive from negative instances. The method is evaluated on FSC-147 and CARPK, achieving competitive results without any fine-tuning or auxiliary models.

## Strengths

- **The core insight of using SAM's output tokens as verification criteria is genuinely novel and well-motivated.** Prior training-free SAM-based counting methods (TFOC, C-Anything) rely on image embedding similarity, which the paper correctly identifies as having inherent ambiguity. Output tokens integrate both semantic and positional information through the decoder's bidirectional cross-attention, making them a more natural representation for distinguishing objects. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) provides supporting evidence that the output token space separates positives from negatives more cleanly than the image embedding space. **[favorability=13.28]**

- **The probabilistic prompt generation with iterative expansion is well-designed.** Updating a probability distribution based on verification results — setting positive regions to zero and decreasing negatives — is an efficient way to avoid redundant prompting. The ablation (Table 4) shows clear monotonic improvement as the number of prompts increases from 32 to 128 per iteration, confirming the mechanism functions as designed rather than adding noise. **[favorability=14.54]**

- **Results on CARPK are genuinely strong.** An MAE of 4.68 outperforms all training-based methods (FamNet: 18.19, BMNet+: 5.76, SAFECOUNT: 5.33, LOCA: 9.97) and is competitive with the best training-free method TFCAC (4.39), demonstrating the method's effectiveness on a focused, single-domain benchmark. **[favorability=13.67]**

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The abstract overclaims results.** It states the method achieves "superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is factually incorrect. On FSC-147 (the harder, multi-category benchmark), Table 1 shows training-based methods LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), and BMNet+ (14.62) all outperform this MAE, as does training-free TFCAC (12.26). The paper's own Section 4.2 uses more measured language ("best among training-free SAM only methods"), but the abstract and Conclusion make broad claims not supported by the data. This framing needs substantial revision. **[favorability=-1.23]**

- **The RMSE on FSC-147 (133.11) is poor relative to the MAE (16.25) and the paper does not adequately discuss this.** An MAE of 16.25 paired with an RMSE of 133.11 implies extreme errors on a subset of images — the squared errors are large enough to drive RMSE to 133 despite a modest average absolute error. Comparison methods achieve much lower RMSE: LOCA (56.97), TFCAC (56.33), BMNet+ (91.83). The one-sentence mention in the one-shot counting section (line 232) is insufficient for this critical reliability concern. The paper should report per-image error distributions, identify failure cases, and explain why the RMSE is so high. **[favorability=-1.65]**

### Minor
