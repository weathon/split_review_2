## Summary

This paper introduces VisualPRM400K, a dataset of ~400K multimodal process supervision samples (~2M step-level annotations) for training multimodal Process Reward Models (PRMs). It also presents VisualProcessBench, a human-annotated benchmark (2,866 samples, 26,950 step labels) for evaluating step-level correctness detection. Building on these resources, the authors train VisualPRM, an 8B multimodal PRM, and demonstrate that it consistently improves Best-of-N reasoning performance across six policy models from three model families (MiniCPM-V, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5) at four scales (8B–78B) on seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **First large-scale multimodal PRM dataset.** VisualPRM400K fills a genuine gap — multimodal process supervision data did not previously exist at this scale. The ~400K samples and ~2M step annotations are sufficient to train practically useful PRMs. (Section 3.1, lines 128–144) [favorability=13.81]

- **Well-constructed human-annotated benchmark.** VisualProcessBench uses paid human annotators with university degrees, 10% per-split author review, a re-annotation pipeline for flagged splits, and a design requiring detection of *all* erroneous steps (not just the first), improving over PRM800K-style evaluation. (Section 3.3, Table 1) [favorability=14.20]

- **Consistent and broad BoN improvements.** VisualPRM improves all six policy models across three families and four scales on all seven benchmarks. The consistency is strong evidence that the trained PRM captures genuine step-level quality signal. (Table 2) [favorability=14.06]

- **Informative ablation on value vs. advantage PRM.** The finding that value-based PRMs substantially outperform advantage-based PRMs (41.1 vs. 37.4 BoN overall, 62.0 vs. 55.0 on VisualProcessBench), together with the honest attribution to noise in the automated data pipeline, is a useful practical insight. (Table 4, Section 4.3) [favorability=12.09]

- **PRM > ORM > SC at higher N, with ORM saturating.** The observation that ORM-based BoN performance saturates and degrades at N > 64 while PRM continues improving is a meaningful finding that goes beyond a standard "our method is better" claim. (Section 4.3, Figure 4) [favorability=16.49]

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The base MLLM from which VisualPRM is initialized is never stated.** The paper describes VisualPRM only as "an advanced multimodal PRM with 8B parameters" and details the training formulation (multi-turn chat, step-level correctness prediction), but never specifies which checkpoint or architecture the 8B model is derived from. Given that solutions are generated by InternVL2.5 series models (Section 3.1) and VisualPRM is consistently compared against InternVL2.5-8B, it is plausible that VisualPRM is fine-tuned from InternVL2.5-8B, but this is not stated. Without this information, readers cannot assess how much of the improvement is attributable to the PRM training regime vs. the base model's innate capabilities. This is a basic reproducibility gap. (lines 25, 146–158)

- **The text-only evaluation protocol is underspecified.** Section 4.3 evaluates VisualPRM on GSM8K, MATH-500, and GPQA-Diamond — text-only benchmarks with no image input. VisualPRM is a multimodal model trained on image+text data, yet the paper provides no description of how text-only inputs are handled (e.g., blank/placeholder image, native text-only capability via architectural design, or some input adaptation). This creates a distribution mismatch with training that is unreported, making the text-only results in Table 5 difficult to interpret. (Section 4.3, lines 304–329)

### Minor

- **No statistical variance reported for any result.** All Tables (2, 3, 4, 5) report single numbers despite BoN evaluation involving stochastic sampling with temperature 0.7. Without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multiple seeds, the reader cannot assess whether smaller improvements (e.g., +0.7 for InternVL2.5-78B on MMMU, +0.2 for Qwen2.5-32B on GSM8K) are meaningful or within noise. The main conclusions are supported by large, consistent improvements, so this does not threaten the core claims, but it is standard to include for stochastic procedures.

- **The "correct completion" criterion in the automated data pipeline is not specified.** Equation (2) defines mc_i as num(correct completions)/num(sampled completions), but the paper never states what constitutes a "correct completion" — whether it is exact answer match, model-based verification by another model call, or some other criterion. (Section 3.1, line 140)

### Trivial

- **Minor naming inconsistency:** the paper refers to "MiniCPM-V2.6" in the introduction (line 25) and "MiniCPM-V-8B" in the results discussion (line 232), while Table 2 lists "MiniCPM-V2.6-8B". These should be harmonized.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A small-scale human evaluation of the automated data pipeline's label quality (e.g., agreement between mc_i > 0 labels and human judgment on 100–200 samples) would strengthen the claim that the dataset is reliable, even though the Monte Carlo methodology is well-established (MathShepherd, OmegaPRM). The paper acknowledges "inherent noise" in the pipeline (line 269) but does not quantify it.
- Reporting breakdown of which error types the PRM catches vs. misses on VisualProcessBench would provide deeper insight into its behavior.

## Suggestions

1. **State the base model explicitly in Section 3.2.** One sentence ("VisualPRM is fine-tuned from InternVL2.5-8B" or whichever architecture was used) resolves the most basic reproducibility question.
2. **Describe the text-only evaluation protocol.** Specify whether a placeholder image is used, whether the model natively accepts text-only inputs, or how the modality gap is bridged. Without this, Table 5 is hard to interpret.
3. **Add variance estimates** (confidence intervals or std dev from multiple seeds) for the main BoN results, at least for the headline claims in Table 2 and the PRM vs. ORM vs. SC comparison in Figure 4.
4. **Clarify the correctness criterion** in Equation (2) — what counts as a "correct completion"?
