## Summary

This paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a metric to quantify semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representations, and uses it to dynamically reweight loss terms in the CoST framework. The goal is to prevent dominant signal components (e.g., trend) from overshadowing weaker but semantically important periodic signals. The synthetic diagnostic for TS2Vec (Table 1) cleanly demonstrates the imbalance problem, but the main claim—that the asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) mechanism yields consistent forecasting gains—is unsupported by the experimental evidence.

## Strengths

1. **The problem is real and well-motivated.** Semantic imbalance—where trend components drown out weaker periodic signals in contrastive embeddings—is a recognizable issue in time-series representation learning, and the paper clearly articulates three structural causes (Section 1).

2. **The synthetic diagnostic (Table 1) is clean and informative.** Using TS2Vec embeddings on controlled trend+sinusoid mixtures, SDE values shift monotonically with amplitude ratio (r = 0.1 through 10), with Δ reaching near-zero at the balanced point. This is the strongest empirical content in the paper.

3. **Honest negative result (Section 4.3, Table 2).** The paper reports that using SDE as a direct regularizer on TS2Vec does not improve forecasting (if anything, it hurts). Reporting a failed attempt is valuable for the community.

4. **The core idea is conceptually elegant.** Diagnosing imbalance with SDE and correcting it via asymmetric weighting would be a lightweight, pluggable contribution if properly validated.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Experimental results do not support the central claim of "consistent gains."** The abstract and conclusion claim CoST+APW yields "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and "consistently lower SDE values." However:
   - On **Electricity** at horizons 168/336/720, CoST+APW is substantially *worse* than vanilla CoST (e.g., 0.566 vs 0.425 MSE at horizon 168—a 33% relative degradation).
   - On **Weather** at horizons 336/720, CoST+APW (0.266, 0.299) underperforms CoST (0.256, 0.278), TS2Vec (0.231, 0.233), and TNC (0.215, 0.219).
   - On ETTh1 at horizon 720, CoST (0.148) beats CoST+APW (0.152).
   - The improvements on ETT datasets where they exist are often marginal (0.001–0.006 MSE) and not monotonic.
   These are not small inconsistencies—they are sign flips, with some degradations large enough to contradict the headline claim.

2. **SDE values are never reported for the proposed method.** Line 263 states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" for CoST+APW, yet Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE columns. No SDE values for CoST+APW or any baseline appear anywhere in the paper. Since "consistently lower SDE values" is a central claim in both the abstract and conclusion, this omission is critical and renders the claim unsubstantiated.

3. **No ablation isolates the proposed weighting mechanism from the MLP fusion layer.** The proposed CoST+APW differs from CoST in *two* ways simultaneously: (a) an MLP fusion layer (Section 4.4.2), and (b) asymmetric weighting (Section 4.4.3). Without ablations such as CoST+MLP (no weighting), CoST+uniform weighting, or CoST+random weighting, any observed differences cannot be attributed to the asymmetric weighting mechanism specifically. The paper claims "systematic ablations" (Section 1) but these essential controls are absent.

### Minor

4. **The synthetic experiment (Table 1) only evaluates TS2Vec, not CoST.** Since the proposed method builds on CoST, there is no evidence that CoST—which already uses frequency-aware contrastive learning and separate trend/seasonal encoders—actually suffers from the semantic imbalance problem the paper aims to solve. For all we know, CoST may already handle this.

5. **No variance or statistical significance reported.** All results (Tables 1–3) report point estimates without standard deviations or multiple seeds. Since many of the CoST vs. CoST+APW differences on ETT are in the third decimal place (e.g., 0.037 vs 0.038 on ETTm1 at horizon 96), the improvements are uninterpretable without error bars.

6. **SDE naming inconsistency.** The acronym is expanded as "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title and abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2 heading), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3, line 150, line 195). While the mathematical definition is consistent, this suggests the framing was not settled during writing.

7. **Table 3 labeling is ambiguous.** Two blocks are labeled "ETT1" with different horizon sets (24/48/168/336/720 and 24/48/96/288/672), making it unclear which ETT variant (ETTh1 vs ETTm1) each corresponds to.

8. **The linearity assumption underlying SDE is not validated.** SDE = 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)) assumes v(a+b) − v(b) ≈ v(a) in embedding space. The paper cites word embedding literature where this property holds by design, but does not validate or discuss whether it holds for contrastive time-series embeddings.

### Trivial
None.
