## Summary

This paper introduces Ambig-SWE, an underspecified variant of SWE-Bench Verified, and an interactive evaluation framework to study how LLM agents handle underspecified instructions in software engineering tasks. Its core contribution is a three-factor decomposition of the problem — detecting underspecificity, acquiring missing information through clarification, and integrating that information to complete the task. The main empirical findings are that (1) interaction improves resolve rates significantly (models recover 54–80% of fully-specified performance), (2) most models fail to detect underspecification reliably (Qwen 3 Coder shows 100% FNR across all prompts), and (3) information integration, not just extraction, is a critical bottleneck.

## Strengths

- **The three-factor decomposition (detection, information acquisition, integration) is the paper's strongest structural contribution.** This framing is non-trivial — most prior work conflates these capacities — and enables the most interesting finding: models that extract comparable information achieve very different resolve rates, isolating information *integration* as a distinct bottleneck. [favorability=13.51]

- **The user proxy design is well-motivated.** The proxy responds with "I don't have that information" when queried beyond the issue's content (Section 2.2), preventing hallucination of missing details. This ensures that measured agent behavior reflects genuine information-seeking rather than proxy cooperativeness. [favorability=10.63]

- **The 100% FNR finding for Qwen 3 Coder across all prompt conditions (Table 2) is crisp and actionable.** If confirmed, this is a clear signal for anyone building agents on this model. [favorability=9.80]

- **The navigational vs. informational information analysis (Table 1) reveals genuinely non-obvious patterns.** Qwen 3 Coder's resolve rate *decreases* when it receives navigational information (52.38% with vs. 55.43% without), and Claude Sonnet 3.5 achieves 59.52% resolve rate *without* file locations. This granularity makes the paper useful beyond its headline numbers. [favorability=8.09]

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Construct validity gap in the RQ2 detection experiment (Section 4).** The paper frames RQ2 as measuring whether models "can identify whether a given task description is missing crucial information" (line 164), but what is actually measured is whether the model *chooses to interact*. A model might detect missing information but not ask (e.g., because it thinks it can infer the answer, or due to conversational norms), or might ask without detecting underspecification. The finding that prompt engineering radically changes measured behavior (Table 2: Claude Sonnet 3.5's FPR goes from 0.00 to 0.36 depending on prompt) confirms that the metric conflates detection ability with willingness-to-act under specific prompt conditions. The claim that "Claude Sonnet 4 and Claude Sonnet 3.5 are the only evaluated LLMs that achieve notable accuracy (89% and 84%, respectively) in making this distinction" (line 37) overstates what the data support — these numbers reflect detection-plus-action under one prompt condition, not detection accuracy as the construct is named. [favorability=-1.38]

2. **Generalizability concern from synthetic dataset construction.** The Ambig-SWE dataset is generated by GPT-4o producing underspecified variants that systematically differ from real underspecified issues (lines 64-66): natural issues contain more code snippets, error messages, file references, and conversational fragments. The generated variants use "more aggressive information removal" (line 66). The paper does not evaluate on naturally underspecified issues because they lack paired ground-truth specifications (line 68). This is a legitimate constraint, but it means all quantitative results rest on an untested assumption that GPT-4o's information removal produces realistic underspecification. Without human validation or a held-out naturalistic subset, the reader cannot assess how far results generalize. [favorability=-2.38]

3. **Claude Sonnet 4's Hidden setting evaluation on only 100/500 instances (footnote 4, line 131) undermines cross-model comparisons.** This means the "improvement from interaction" for Claude Sonnet 4 is computed across different instance populations than for all other models. If the 100 instances are not representative of the full 500, the comparison of interaction gains across models is unreliable. The paper asserts findings are "still statistically significant" without reporting the relevant test statistics or power analysis. This is particularly concerning because the central quantitative claims in the abstract (74% improvement) and introduction (89% relative performance) both involve Claude Sonnet 4 data that mixes different evaluation populations. [favorability=4.40 for the subset fact; merged with -2.09 for the numerical imprecision that depends on it]

### Minor

4. **Headline quantitative claims are not reproducible from the reported data.** The abstract and introduction state "up to 74% improvement" over non-interactive settings (lines 9, 37), and that Claude Sonnet 4 attains "89% relative performance" (line 127). Computing (Interaction − Hidden) / Hidden from Figure 3 gives a maximum of 100% (Claude Haiku), not 74%. Computing Interaction / Full gives 90.3% for Claude Sonnet 4, and gap-closure gives 76.4% — neither is 89%. The lack of clarity about what these numbers actually measure erodes confidence in numerical precision throughout the paper. [favorability=0.96]

5. **The question quality evaluation (RQ3) uses GPT-4o as both the user proxy (Section 2.2) and the LLM-as-judge scoring response quality (Section 5.1), creating a potential circularity.** The LLM-as-judge scores converge around 4/5 for all capable models (Figure 6), which could partially reflect GPT-4o's self-consistency rather than genuine information quality. The cosine distance metric (Section 5.1) partly mitigates this but measures semantic shift in embedding space rather than task-relevant information gain, as the paper acknowledges (line 281). Diversifying either the proxy or the judge would strengthen this analysis. [favorability=3.19 for cosine distance concern; 4.54 for circularity]

6. **Efficiency claims are supported only by anecdotal evidence.** The assertion that "interaction yields no efficiency gains" (line 156) is supported only by step counts for two models (Qwen 3 Coder: 65 steps; Claude Sonnet 4: 65→75 steps). A systematic table showing average steps, interaction turns, and cost across all models would strengthen this central claim. [favorability=4.36]

## Novel Insights

The key insight that emerges from the reviews — beyond what the paper itself articulates — is that the paper's strongest finding (the disconnect between information extraction and task integration, shown by models with similar extraction but very different resolve rates) is actually *more robust* than the paper's weaker quantitative claims. The qualitative and decomposed analyses (Table 1, the three-question-category breakdown in Section 5.3, the Qwen rigid-protocol observation) do not depend on the precise numerical precision of aggregate resolve rates and would survive even if the synthetic data concerns were partially valid. Conversely, the headline "74% improvement" and "89% relative performance" figures are the most fragile claims in the paper because they are not reproducible from the reported tables. This suggests the paper's lasting value will be its analytical framework and qualitative findings rather than its precise numbers.

## Suggestions

1. **Fix the numerical claims**: Clarify what "74% improvement" and "89% relative performance" actually measure and verify they match the reported data. If the numbers are computed differently from what Figure 3 shows (e.g., using only the 100-instance subset for Claude Sonnet 4), explain this clearly.
2. **Ren frame RQ2**: Acknowledge that the detection experiment measures interaction behavior under specific prompt conditions, not detection ability in isolation. The behavioral data is still valuable — it just does not support the "detection accuracy" framing.
3. **Report full efficiency metrics**: Add a table with average steps, interaction turns, and estimated cost per model to support the efficiency claims.
