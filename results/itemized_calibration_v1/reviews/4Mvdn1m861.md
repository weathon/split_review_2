## Summary

This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free framework for class-agnostic object counting using SAM's output tokens (from the decoder) rather than the image embeddings used by prior work. The method has two components: probabilistic prompt generation that iteratively samples prompts from a probability distribution, and output token-based verification using the TS-SS geometric similarity metric. On FSC-147 it achieves MAE 16.25 / RMSE 133.11; on CARPK it achieves MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13, the latter beating all supervised methods.

## Strengths

1. **Novel use of SAM's output tokens for verification (Section 3.2).** Prior SAM-based counting methods (TFOC, Count-Anything) rely on similarity in the image embedding space, which the paper correctly identifies as problematic because the image encoder's embeddings conflate many visual features. Using the decoder's output tokens — which integrate semantic and positional information through cross-attention with the prompt — is a genuinely underexplored direction. The approach is elegant in its minimalism: it does not modify SAM's architecture or add auxiliary models.

2. **Strong results on CARPK (Table 1).** The MAE of 4.68 beats every supervised method in the comparison and is competitive with TFCAC (4.39), which uses an additional DINO encoder. This demonstrates the method works well in consistent-viewpoint scenarios with clear object boundaries and highlights a practical strength of the approach.

3. **Informative ablation on verification metrics (Table 3).** Showing that cosine similarity (16.48), L2 distance (16.53), dot product (19.58), and TS-SS (16.25) all perform similarly on this task, with TS-SS edging ahead slightly, is useful empirical information. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) provides supporting evidence for why output tokens separate positives from negatives better than image embeddings.

4. **Thoughtful design of iterative probability distribution update (Section 3.1).** The idea of suppressing probability mass at locations that have already been verified (setting positive regions to 0, decreasing negative regions) is a simple but effective mechanism to avoid redundant prompting. The IoU-based early stopping with patience is also a sensible efficiency measure.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Abstract overstates results.** The abstract claims the method "outperforms existing training-based and training-free counting methods" with MAE 16.25. However, Table 1 shows that on FSC-147, multiple training-based methods achieve lower MAE: LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), BMNet+ (14.62), and SAFECOUNT (14.32). The training-free TFCAC (12.26), which uses an additional DINO encoder, also outperforms it. The method's MAE of 16.25 only beats FamNet (22.08), CFOCNet+ (22.10), and Counting-DETR (16.79) — and the gap to Counting-DETR (0.54 MAE) is small enough that variance could erase it. This claim is materially misleading. (Section 1, Abstract, Table 1)

2. **High RMSE on FSC-147 is not discussed.** The method's RMSE of 133.11 on FSC-147 is among the worst in Table 1 — only SAM (137.50) is worse; C-Anything (131.24) and TFOC (132.16) are comparable. Training-based methods like LOCA (56.97), SAFECOUNT (85.54), and BMNet+ (91.83) achieve dramatically lower RMSE. A high RMSE relative to MAE indicates occasional large errors (missing or double-counting many objects), which is a significant practical limitation. The paper focuses almost exclusively on MAE and does not analyze why RMSE is so high or characterize the failure modes.

3. **Verification threshold of 300 is undefined.** The implementation details (Section 4.1) state: "we set the token verification threshold to 300" with no explanation of what this value represents, what range of values was searched, what validation split was used, or what criterion determined the final value. The TS-SS metric in Equation (2) produces values from unbounded components; a threshold of 300 is completely opaque without knowing the typical range of TS-SS values for SAM output tokens. This makes the method unreproducible.

4. **Inference time comparison with TFCAC is unsubstantiated.** Section 4.2 claims the method is "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC, but the paper provides no inference time for TFCAC, no controlled speed comparison, and no experimental setup for a timing comparison. The paper reports its own inference time (1.69s per image on an A100) but offers no basis for the relative claim. This assertion should be removed or backed by controlled evidence.

### Minor

1. **No variance information for any result.** The method involves multinomial sampling from a probability distribution (Section 3.1), which makes results inherently stochastic. No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or number of runs are reported for any metric. Without variance, it is impossible to know whether reported differences (e.g., 16.25 vs. 16.48 for different verification metrics in Table 3) are meaningful or within sampling noise.

2. **SAM checkpoint variant not specified.** The paper says "the official SAM" but does not specify which variant (ViT-B, ViT-L, ViT-H) was used. Mask quality and output token properties likely vary across SAM sizes.

3. **Temperature parameter τ is not explicitly reported.** Equation (1) includes a temperature parameter τ, but its value is not explicitly stated. The "t-scale factor of 0.066" from Section 4.1 may be τ, but this relationship is not clarified.

4. **Exemplar selection protocol for FSC-147 not described.** For the main few-shot results (Table 1), the paper specifies exemplar selection only for CARPK (12 random objects from training set). For FSC-147, the exemplar selection is unstated. Similarly, the one-shot setting (Table 2) does not describe how the single exemplar was selected.

### Trivial
None.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's observation that the high RMSE (133.11 on FSC-147) is among the worst in the comparison, while MAE is middle-of-the-pack, is a genuinely insightful point that the paper itself does not engage with. This suggests the method has a "long tail" of large errors — it mostly works but occasionally fails catastrophically — which is a different and more serious limitation than the average error metric suggests. The harsh critic also usefully identifies that the inference speed claim against TFCAC is entirely unsupported, revealing a gap between what the paper asserts and what it demonstrates. Neither of these points is obvious from a casual reading of the paper.

## Suggestions

1. Correct the abstract and introduction to accurately characterize the method's performance: it is competitive among training-free SAM-only methods on FSC-147 (not universally superior), and it excels on CARPK.
2. Define the verification threshold in terms of TS-SS value statistics: report the distribution of TS-SS scores for positive vs. negative pairs in validation data, show how 300 was selected, and report sensitivity to this hyperparameter.
3. Add variance estimates (standard deviations across multiple seeds) for all main results, given the stochastic sampling procedure.
4. Characterize the failure modes underlying the high RMSE on FSC-147 — identify which categories or scenarios produce large errors, and discuss practical implications.
5. Either provide a controlled inference-time comparison with TFCAC under identical hardware, or remove the "twice as fast" claim.
6. Report which SAM variant (ViT-B/L/H) was used and clarify the temperature parameter τ.
