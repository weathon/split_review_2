## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting RL rewards with a KL divergence term between predicted outcomes p(o|s,a) and preferred outcomes q(o|s), weighted by a scalar β. The paper claims as its central novelty that β is treated as a Bayesian random variable adapted online, eliminating manual tuning of the intrinsic/extrinsic reward trade-off. Experiments are reported on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8×8.

## Strengths

- **Clear cognitive framing.** The paper connects the KL-based shaping term to the dopamine reward-prediction-error literature and the Free Energy Principle in a well-articulated manner (Sec. 2.3–2.4, Sec. 6.3), providing coherent interdisciplinary motivation.
- **The KL shaping term itself is clearly formulated.** The augmented reward function rᵗᵒᵗᵃˡ = rᵉˣᵗ − β·KL(p‖q) is stated concisely (Eq. 1, Sec. 3.2), and the roles of its components are explained.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **Bayesian β adaptation mechanism is entirely unspecified.** The paper repeatedly advertises this as its central novelty (Abstract: "treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable, updated online"; Sec. 1 bullet 1; Conclusion). Yet no algorithmic description is provided anywhere in the paper: there is no prior over β, no likelihood function, no update rule (conjugate, MCMC, variational, or otherwise), no definition of what a "round" is, and no description of how β is sampled from the posterior and used during acting. The Methods section (Sec. 3) describes only the KL-shaped reward; the algorithmic steps in Sec. 5.1 list "predict outcomes → specify preferences → compute KL → shape reward → update policy" — none of these steps involve a Bayesian update of β. The Bayesian adaptation is mentioned only in passing in Sec. 5.4 (lines 230–247) where results are displayed, but no mechanism is given. A paper cannot be accepted when its central claimed contribution is not described; the reader has no way to evaluate, understand, or reproduce it.

### Major

- **Toy 2-state MDP uses an undefined KL divergence.** The setup defines p(o|s₀, a₁) = {s₁: 0.3, s₀: 0.7} and q(o|s₀) = {s₁: 1.0, s₀: 0.0}. The KL(p‖q) term 0.7 × log(0.7/0.0) is undefined (division by zero → +∞). The paper never addresses this — no smoothed KL, no alternative direction (KL(q‖p)), no Jensen-Shannon variant is mentioned. The quantitative results in Table 1 and Figure 1 for this experiment are therefore based on a mathematically invalid operation. (Quoted from Sec. 5.2, line 166)

- **Bayesian β posterior evidence contradicts the paper's own claims.** The paper states that the posterior "concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)" (lines 239, 245) and "recovers the same effective values discovered by fixed sweeps" (line 247). However, Table 3 (Appendix) shows the posterior mean moving from 0.106 (Round 1) to 0.045 (Round 2) to 0.019 (Round 3) to **0.017** (Round 4) — *away* from 0.1, not toward it. Even the figure description states: "The posterior mean starts at approximately 0.11 in Round 1 and decreases to about 0.02 by Round 4" (line 237). If the "effective region" is β ≈ 0.1 (as the grid search found best performance), the Bayesian method is not converging to it, directly undermining the paper's core claim. (Quoted from Table 3, lines 316–321)

- **No comparison to any existing intrinsic motivation method.** The paper frames itself as addressing a "core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods" (Abstract, Conclusion) and cites Pathak et al. (2017), Burda et al. (2018), Houthooft et al. (2016), and Mohamed & Jimenez Rezende (2015) as key prior work. Yet **none of these methods are included as baselines**. The only baseline is β=0 (no intrinsic motivation at all). The reader cannot evaluate whether IncentRL offers any advantage over standard intrinsic motivation approaches, which is the very claim the paper makes. (Quoted from Sec. 2.1, line 37)

### Minor

- **Low statistical power.** MountainCar (Table 2) and MiniGrid (Sec. 5.4) experiments use only 3 seeds each. With coefficients of variation as large as ~13% (std 535 on mean 4135 for β=0.1 on MountainCar), individual outliers can shift means substantially. No statistical significance tests are reported.

- **MiniGrid experiment tests only one non-zero β value (β=0.01) without a sweep**, unlike MountainCar where β ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0} was swept. The paper does not explain why β=0.3 or β=1.0 were not tested in this environment. Given that these values degraded performance on MountainCar, the lack of a full sweep raises concerns about selective reporting.

- **Theoretical propositions are basic.** Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimal policy) is a continuity property that holds for any bounded reward perturbation in any finite MDP; Proposition 2 (β → ∞ makes KL dominate) is definitional. Neither provides insight into why the specific KL(p‖q) form is beneficial, what convergence rates look like, or how β should be chosen. Calling this a "theoretical analysis" (Sec. 4) overstates what is presented.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the Bayesian update mechanism in full.** This is not optional — without it, the paper's central contribution does not exist. Describe the prior over β, the likelihood function, how the posterior is computed, what a "round" corresponds to in terms of timesteps, and how β is sampled from the posterior and used during action selection. Provide the update equations.

2. **Fix the toy experiment's KL divergence.** Resolve the KL(p‖q) undefined-divergence issue (use a smoothed q, use KL(q‖p), Jensen-Shannon divergence, or another approach) and rerun the experiment. Report whether the results change qualitatively.

3. **Reconcile the β posteriors with the grid-search results.** The data in Table 3 shows posterior mean converging to 0.017, not 0.1. Either explain why β ≈ 0.017 is the "effective region" (i.e., why different β values are optimal across rounds) or acknowledge and investigate the discrepancy.

4. **Add at least one standard intrinsic motivation baseline** (ICM, RND, or VIME) on the same benchmarks. Without this, the motivating framing is unsupported.
