## Summary

GUI-Spotlight proposes a multi-tool iterative refinement approach for GUI visual grounding, trained via a three-stage pipeline (SFT warmup from a larger model's trajectories, then RL with a modified GSPO objective). The method achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro with only 18.5K training samples, the best result among 7B models on that benchmark, and includes systematic RL/reward ablations validated on two different backbone architectures.

## Strengths

- **Data efficiency on ScreenSpot-Pro.** GUI-Spotlight achieves 52.8% with 18.5K samples, surpassing V2P-7B (50.6%, 9.6M), GTA-1-7B (50.1%, 1.56M), and UI-Venus-7B (50.8%, 107K). This represents a substantial reduction in training data relative to many baselines while achieving higher accuracy (Table 3).

- **Two-backbone validation.** The method is evaluated starting from both UI-TARS-1.5-7B (UI-specialized) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (general-purpose). The Qwen-initialized variant gains +11.9 points (26.8% → 38.7%) on ScreenSpot-Pro, demonstrating that the training recipe transfers beyond UI-specialized architectures (Table 3, Section 5.1).

- **Systematic RL and reward ablations.** Section 4.1 benchmarks seven RL variants under controlled conditions, and Section 4.2 compares answer-reward formulations (sparse vs. dense) and crop/extract reward ratios. The paper documents negative results (discarded variants ④ and ⑥) that provide practical guidance (Figures 3 and 4).

- **Clean ablation of multi-step reasoning.** Figure 5 directly compares GUI-Spotlight (52.8%) with a repeated single-turn inference baseline (47.6%), isolating a 5.2-point gain attributable to the learned multi-tool reasoning policy beyond what simple iterative cropping achieves.

## Weaknesses

### Major

**1. Unexplained accuracy crash during the SFT stage.** Figure 2 shows that after Stage 1 SFT on 2561 trajectories from Qwen2.5-VL-72B, ScreenSpot-Pro accuracy drops from 39.3% to 17.8% — a 54% relative decline. The paper's only explanation is that the model "remains under-aligned" (Section 3.2.2). This is insufficient. If supervised fine-tuning on expert demonstrations causes the model to perform far worse than random guessing on many categories, there is a fundamental mismatch in the training data or the learning dynamics that the paper does not interrogate. Since the SFT data was generated by a 72B model and applied to a 7B model, the drop may reflect a teacher-student capacity mismatch, but this is not discussed. The paper also does not test whether skipping SFT and initializing RL directly from the base model would work as well or better. This weakens confidence in the three-stage pipeline as a principled design rather than a fragile artifact.

### Minor

**2. Factually overclaimed result on UI-Vision.** Section 5.2 states that GUI-Spotlight (UI-TARS) achieves 23.4%, "surpassing its backbone ... and outperforming other 7B models." However, Table 4 shows UI-Venus-Ground-7B at 26.5% — a clear 3.1-point advantage over GUI-Spotlight. The claim is contradicted by the paper's own data. At best this is imprecise wording; in context it reads as a stronger claim than the evidence supports.

**3. Negligible gain on OSWorld-G from the UI-TARS backbone.** On OSWorld-G (Table 5), GUI-Spotlight (UI-TARS) achieves 62.7%, only +0.8 points above the base UI-TARS-1.5-7B (61.9%). This is a marginal improvement, especially compared to GTA1-7B (67.7%), which outperforms GUI-Spotlight by 5 points while also being a 7B model. The paper claims that GUI-Spotlight "remains competitive with 72B-scale models" on OSWorld-G, but the gain over the base model on this benchmark is essentially zero.

**4. "Think-with-image" framing outstrips the evidence.** The paper repeatedly describes GUI-Spotlight as performing "image-grounded reasoning" (abstract, introduction, conclusion), but provides no analysis of what reasoning occurs — no attention visualizations, no chain-of-thought analysis, no case studies of intermediate decisions. Figure 5 shows a 5.2-point advantage over the repeated single-turn baseline, which is a meaningful gain, but attributing this to "reasoning" rather than better-trained tool-use patterns requires evidence the paper does not provide.

**5. No variance or multi-seed reporting.** All experiments report single numbers without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multi-seed runs. For RL training with group-based policy optimization, where Figure 3 (right panel) shows jagged training curves, single-seed results cannot be assumed stable. This is especially problematic for the negative-results documentation (contribution 3), since it is unclear whether discarded variants are genuinely worse or simply unlucky.

**6. SE-GUI-7B not discussed despite being the most data-efficient competitor.** SE-GUI-7B (Table 3) achieves 47.2% on ScreenSpot-Pro with only 3K training samples. Since the paper's data-efficiency claim is a central contribution, the omission of any discussion of this model (in the text or comparison) is notable.

### Trivial

**7. Stage-indexing mismatch.** Figure 2 labels stages 0–3 while the text describes stages 1–3, and the sample counts per stage in the figure do not align with the text's stage descriptions. This does not invalidate the results but indicates sloppy presentation.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Analyze the SFT crash mechanism: is the Qwen2.5-VL-72B teacher mismatched to the 7B model? Would skipping SFT and initializing RL from the base model work?
- Report multi-seed results for the main benchmarks and RL variant comparisons.
- Provide inference cost analysis: average/maximum number of tool calls per example, wall-clock time, and comparison to single-pass baselines.
- Analyze the learned tool-use strategies across different GUI types (e.g., is find_color used primarily for color-coded buttons?).

## Removed Points

These points were raised in the input review but are removed from the main weakness list:

- **Qwen2.5-VL-72B blind spots in data cleaning filter (Section 3.2.1).** This is speculative — there is no evidence in the paper of systematic blind spots. The filtering pipeline is standard and the paper acknowledges using the same model for multiple checks.
- **Strategy ① (7.6%) characterized as a "strawman."** The comparison serves a useful pedagogical purpose: showing the base model cannot use tools at all, then isolating the actual gain in the trained policy. This is a fair experimental design, not a strawman.
- **Notation nitpicks about the auxiliary loss J'(θ).** The formulation, while slightly baroque, is mathematically sound (the reviewer's own analysis confirms this).
- **Missing related works.** Cannot be verified without external literature search; the paper cites relevant prior work (UGround, UI-TARS, OS-Atlas, etc.).

## Novel Insights

The most informative tension in the reviews is the contrast between the paper's legitimate ScreenSpot-Pro result and the fragility revealed by the SFT accuracy crash (39.3% → 17.8%). This drop exposes a potential instability in multi-stage training pipelines that is rarely documented but likely common: initial SFT on teacher-generated trajectories can temporarily destroy performance, and the subsequent RL rescue is itself nontrivial. The paper's documentation of this trajectory (even if unexplained) is itself valuable as a negative result for practitioners. A second insight is that the data-efficiency claim, while real on ScreenSpot-Pro, weakens considerably on OSWorld-G (+0.8 points over the base model), suggesting that the method's benefit is benchmark-specific — a nuance the paper's framing obscures.

## Suggestions

1. Investigate and report the cause of the SFT accuracy drop. At minimum, test an ablated pipeline that skips SFT and initializes RL from the base model to determine whether the SFT stage is necessary or harmful.
2. Correct the UI-Vision claim to "outperforming most other 7B models" or list the models it specifically surpasses.
3. Add multi-seed runs (3 seeds) for all main benchmark results and RL variant comparisons.
4. Contextualize the OSWorld-G result honestly — the +0.8 point gain over the base model should be discussed, not glossed over.
5. Either substantiate the "think-with-image" claim with reasoning analysis or adopt more measured language.
