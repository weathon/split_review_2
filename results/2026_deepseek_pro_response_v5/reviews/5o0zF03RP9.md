## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting standard RL rewards with a KL divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions \(p(o|s,a)\) and preferred outcome distributions \(q(o|s)\), with an incentive weight \(\beta\). The paper positions Bayesian online adaptation of \(\beta\) as its central novelty, claiming it eliminates manual trade-off tuning. Experiments on a toy MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8 show that fixed-\(\beta\) KL shaping improves over standard RL (\(\beta=0\)). The Bayesian adaptation is demonstrated through posterior concentration curves (Figures 3–4, Table 3) but its algorithm is never specified.

## Strengths

- **Concrete KL-based shaping framework (Section 3):** The core objective — \(r^{\text{total}} = r^{\text{ext}} - \beta \cdot \text{KL}(p(o|s,a) \| q(o|s))\) — is clearly formulated and cleanly extends standard RL. The framework is simple to understand and could in principle be combined with any RL algorithm.

- **Meaningful gains on MiniGrid DoorKey 8×8 (Section 5.4, Figure 2):** IncentRL with \(\beta=0.01\) achieves 98% final success rate vs. 90.5% for the \(\beta=0\) baseline, and reduces mean episode length from 75 to 29 steps (~2.6× improvement). This is a non-trivial sparse-reward benchmark and the result directly demonstrates that KL-based preference-prediction alignment can improve policy quality.

- **Clean mechanistic demonstration in the toy MDP (Section 5.2, Table 1):** The 2-state MDP experiment isolates the shaping mechanism clearly: all \(\beta > 0\) values yield 69–74% improvement over \(\beta=0\) with substantially reduced variance (std 6–7 vs. 11.3). This directly illustrates how the KL penalty provides guidance toward preferred (goal) states even before external rewards are experienced.

- **Clear positioning relative to KL-regularized RL (Section 2.2):** The paper draws a crisp distinction between its outcome-distribution KL penalty and the policy-change KL constraints used in TRPO/PPO, clarifying what is genuinely different about the approach.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The Bayesian adaptation scheme — the paper's headline contribution — is never algorithmically specified.** The abstract and introduction position Bayesian \(\beta\) adaptation as IncentRL's central novelty ("Bayesian adaptation (novelty)," line 21). Yet the Methods section (Section 3, lines 55–81) contains no description of any Bayesian mechanism: no prior over \(\beta\), no likelihood, no posterior update rule, no sampling procedure. The paper shows posterior concentration curves (Figures 3–4) and summary statistics (Table 3) as evidence that the scheme works, but never states what algorithm produced them. A paper whose central claimed contribution is a specific adaptation mechanism must describe that mechanism. Without it, the contribution is not evaluable.

- **The RL performance of Bayesian-adapted \(\beta\) is never reported.** The fixed-\(\beta\) experiments (Tables 1–2, Figure 2) are sweeps over hand-chosen values. The Bayesian adaptation results (Figures 3–4, Table 3) show only that the posterior over \(\beta\) concentrates — they do not report success rate, reward, or any task metric achieved under the adaptively chosen \(\beta\). Section 5.4 states: "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of \(\beta\) achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" (line 228). This is the paper's central claim relegated to an unsubstantiated parenthetical. Without seeing these results, there is no evidence that Bayesian-adapted \(\beta\) produces effective RL policies.

- **How \(p(o|s,a)\) is obtained in the deep RL experiments is never specified.** For the toy MDP, \(p(o|s,a)\) is hand-crafted from transition probabilities (lines 166–167). For MountainCar and MiniGrid — environments with continuous or high-dimensional state spaces — the paper says nothing about how \(p(o|s,a)\) is estimated. Section 3.1 says it "may be obtained from a forward model or from environment dynamics" (line 63) but never states what was actually used in the experiments. Is a forward dynamics model learned? With what architecture? At what frequency? If derived from the Q-network, how? The KL term cannot be computed without this estimate, and its quality directly determines whether the shaping signal is informative or harmful. This renders the deep RL experiments unreproducible and their validity unverifiable.

### Minor

- **No comparison against any intrinsic motivation baseline.** The paper frames itself as addressing a "core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods" (line 9) — the need for manual trade-off tuning — and discusses ICM, RND, and VIME in related work. Yet every experiment compares IncentRL only against standard RL with \(\beta=0\). The paper provides no evidence that IncentRL improves over the intrinsic motivation methods it claims to supersede. The fixed-\(\beta\) results do demonstrate that KL shaping adds value over standard RL, so the core mechanism is validated, but the framing relative to intrinsic motivation baselines is unsupported.

- **The theoretical analysis is limited to restating the objective in limit cases.** Proposition 1 (small \(\beta\) preserves the optimal policy) is a continuity argument that follows directly from the objective definition in finite MDPs. Proposition 2 (\(\beta \rightarrow \infty\) ignores external rewards) is similarly definitional. There is no convergence rate, no regret analysis, no characterization of when or by how much the shaping accelerates learning, and no connection between the KL structure and sample complexity. The analysis confirms what is already obvious from the objective function's form.

- **The outcome variable "o" is never concretely defined for the deep RL experiments, and the KL divergence may be ill-posed.** For MountainCar, \(q(o|s)\) is described as putting all probability mass on \(x \geq 0.5\) (the goal). If \(p(o|s,a)\) is a distribution over continuous states that does not put all mass on the goal region, the KL divergence \(\text{KL}(p\|q)\) is infinite — the support of \(q\) is not contained in the support of \(p\). The paper must address how this is handled numerically, or the reported results are mathematically suspect.

- **MountainCar experiment reports only 3 seeds and a single aggregate number** (Table 2: mean goal reaches per 5000 episodes). Standard practice for an exploration benchmark would be to show learning curves with confidence intervals over more seeds. The paper claims sample efficiency gains but reports no sample-efficiency metric (e.g., timesteps to reach a performance threshold, area under the learning curve).

### Trivial

- The neuroscience framing (dopamine, Free Energy Principle) is introduced prominently in the abstract and introduction but remains purely analogical throughout — no substantive connection to the method's mechanism is operationalized.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Specify the Bayesian adaptation scheme: prior, likelihood, update rule, sampling procedure. Show RL performance under Bayesian-adapted \(\beta\) alongside fixed-\(\beta\) sweeps as a primary result rather than an unshown parenthetical.
- Make \(p(o|s,a)\) concrete for the deep RL experiments with architecture and training details, and ablate its quality.
- Add at least one intrinsic motivation baseline (e.g., ICM or RND) on MiniGrid to substantiate the framing.
- Address the KL divergence well-posedness issue for continuous outcome distributions where \(q\) has support not contained in \(p\).

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The core idea — using KL divergence between predicted and preferred outcome distributions as a shaping reward — is a clean and intuitive formulation. The fixed-\(\beta\) results confirm that this shaping mechanism can help in sparse-reward settings, though the paper would benefit from establishing whether the KL penalty is functionally equivalent to potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) in the tested environments.

## Suggestions

- The paper's most defensible contribution is the fixed-\(\beta\) KL shaping mechanism. If the Bayesian adaptation cannot be fully specified and evaluated, the paper should recenter around the fixed-\(\beta\) results, remove or downplay the Bayesian adaptation claim, and add the missing implementation details (\(p(o|s,a)\) specification, intrinsic motivation baselines, proper learning curves with confidence intervals).
- The MountainCar KL divergence issue (potentially infinite KL when \(q\) puts all mass on a subset of continuous outcomes) needs a clear technical resolution — this is a mathematical problem with the method as described for continuous state spaces.
- Consider whether the KL penalty is functioning as potential-based reward shaping; if so, the contribution should be explicitly positioned relative to that well-established literature.
