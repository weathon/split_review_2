## Summary
This paper proposes L-TTA, the first test-time adaptation method for vision-language models (CLIP) under long-tailed test distributions. L-TTA combines three components: (1) Synergistic Prototypes (SyPs) with Deterministic and Exclusionary Prototypes to maintain fine-grained class knowledge even for tail classes, (2) Rebalancing Shortcuts (RSs) with a Class Re-Allocation loss adapted from MoE load balancing for balanced prototype routing, and (3) Balanced Entropy Minimization (BEM), a modified entropy loss with a confidence-based penalty term to favor tail-class adaptation. Experiments span 15 datasets across three benchmarks at three imbalance ratios, with comparisons against 11 VLM-specific TTA baselines and 6 backbones.

## Strengths
- **Novel problem framing with diagnostic evidence**: The paper identifies two concrete failure modes for VLM-based TTA under long-tailed distributions — Text-induced Tail Erosion and Modality-bias Amplification — supported by Figure 1. The observation that unimodal LT-TTA methods like SAR fail catastrophically on VLM backbones (Fig 1b.2) provides clear motivation for a VLM-specific solution.
- **Exclusionary Prototypes as a principled anti-prototype mechanism**: EPs (Eq. 5) update all class prototypes on every sample, weighted by prediction improbability φ_c, ensuring tail-class prototypes are continuously updated. The subtractive use in Eq. 8 (+DP affinity, −EP affinity) provides a clean contrastive signal.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation**: Evaluation across 15 datasets, 3 benchmarks (OOD, Cross-Domain, Corruption), 3 imbalance ratios (10, 20, 50), 12 baselines, and 6 backbones. L-TTA consistently improves both accuracy and macro-F1, with macro-F1 gains typically exceeding accuracy gains, directly validating the class-balancing claim. The efficiency study (Table 4) shows L-TTA completes in 1.45h with 1.89G memory.
- **Thorough ablation studies**: Table 6 ablates each component combination, Figure 4 ablates hyperparameters (λ₁, λ₂, η, K, β), and Table 7 tests robustness to temporal ordering shifts. All components are shown to contribute positively.

### Minor
- **The propositional claims (Props. 1–2) rely on a stripped appendix and present an underspecified link between class cardinalities and gradient signs.** Proposition 1 asserts that E_{i~C_head} ∇_{z_i} H < 0 < E_{i~C_tail} ∇_{z_i} H based on class size ordering. However, whether entropy gradients are negative for head classes and positive for tail classes depends on the model's output distribution, not directly on ground-truth class sizes. The proof is in a stripped appendix and cannot be verified. The paper does not state the auxiliary assumptions needed to bridge class sizes to model behavior, which weakens the theoretical framing — though the empirical results remain independently valid.

- **No standard deviations reported despite claiming 5 runs.** The experimental protocol states "We conduct 5 runs for each experiment" (Table 1 caption, line 155) but no error bars, confidence intervals, or standard deviations appear in any table or figure. Several performance margins are small (e.g., on ImageNet-S at imb=10, MTA achieves 46.50 Macro-F1 vs. L-TTA's 45.99, and WATT achieves 46.83) and without variance it is unclear whether these differences are statistically meaningful.

- **The feedback loop in BEM's pseudo-label-based prior estimation is not discussed.** The class prior π in Eq. 9 is estimated from pseudo-labels generated by the model itself (line 138: "the class prior is continually updated based on the current predicted pseudo-labels"). If the model is biased toward head classes, pseudo-label counts will overestimate head-class cardinalities, potentially reinforcing the bias. The paper does not analyze this effect or compare against oracle priors.

- **The K hyperparameter is described inconsistently.** K is introduced as a count of hyper-class vectors (line 112: "assume there are K hyper-class vectors"), but is implemented as a fraction (line 208: "K = 0.3") and ablated in fractional range [0.1, 1.0] (Figure 4c). This should be clarified.

- **Exceptions in the results are not acknowledged.** In Table 1, MTA achieves better Macro-F1 than L-TTA on ImageNet-S at imb=10 (46.50 vs 45.99) and WATT is also higher (46.83). At imb=50, DPE has better accuracy on ImageNet-A (60.21 vs 60.07) and MTA has better Macro-F1 on ImageNet-V2 (62.69 vs 62.38). While L-TTA wins most comparisons, a balanced discussion should briefly acknowledge where it does not.

### Trivial
- The DP update in Eq. 4 uses (N-1) counter-based weighting rather than a standard EMA decay factor; the rationale is not explained.
- The EP update in Eq. 5 mixes integer count N^{EP}_{c,s} with probability-derived φ_c; for large N the φ_c adjustment becomes negligible, which the paper does not discuss.
- Notation in Eq. 7 is difficult to parse; the parentheses and summation indices are ambiguous (the prose clarifies but the equation itself could be cleaned up).

## Novel Insights
The paper's identification that text embeddings carry pre-training biases that interact destructively with class imbalance (Text-induced Tail Erosion) is genuinely novel and well-motivated.
