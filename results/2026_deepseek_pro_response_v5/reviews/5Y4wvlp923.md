## Summary
This paper proposes the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric that quantifies whether individual semantic components (trend, periodicity) are linearly recoverable from composite time-series embeddings, and Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW), a method that uses SDE asymmetry to dynamically reweight CoST's trend and seasonal contrastive losses. The approach is evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather forecasting benchmarks.

## Strengths
- **Systematic synthetic validation of SDE (Table 1)**: The paper constructs controlled signals mixing trend and periodic components at varying amplitude ratios and demonstrates that SDE and the asymmetry factor Δ shift monotonically, with Δ cleanly flipping sign at the balanced point (r=1, Δ=−0.0276). This provides convincing evidence that SDE captures the intended semantic imbalance phenomenon in a controlled setting.
- **Informative negative result (Table 2)**: Directly adding SDE as a regularization term to the contrastive loss consistently degrades forecasting performance (e.g., ETTm1 horizon 288 MSE worsens from 0.101 to 0.126). This is a genuinely useful finding that motivates why naive minimization of SDE is insufficient and why the APW reweighting approach is necessary.
- **Clean design**: APW is genuinely plug-and-play — it only modifies the loss weighting of CoST's existing objectives without architectural changes, making it easy to adopt.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Core mechanism is never empirically demonstrated**: The paper's central claim is that APW reduces SDE and that this reduction improves forecasting. However, Table 3 reports only MSE and MAE — no SDE values appear anywhere in the main experiments. Line 263 explicitly states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but Table 3 contains no SDE data. The reader cannot determine whether CoST+APW actually reduces SDE relative to CoST, whether Δ changes over training, or whether any forecasting differences are attributable to the reweighting mechanism rather than the additional MLP fusion layer. The paper's central claim goes entirely unevaluated.
- **Mixed results are overclaimed and failure cases are unacknowledged**: The paper states that CoST+APW achieves "superior forecasting accuracy" and "consistent gains," but Table 3 shows significant degradations: on Electricity at horizon 168, CoST+APW produces 0.566 MSE vs. CoST's 0.425 (33% worse); at horizons 336 and 720, CoST+APW is also substantially worse (0.677 vs. 0.576; 1.010 vs. 0.911). On Weather at horizons 336 and 720, CoST outperforms CoST+APW (0.256 vs. 0.266; 0.278 vs. 0.299). These failures are never acknowledged or analyzed. The blanket claim of improvement is not supported by the presented data.

### Minor
- **γ/γ′ inconsistency**: Section 3.4 and Section 4.4.3 define the loss with separate hyperparameters γ and γ′: `(1+γ·Δ)·L_season + (1+γ′·(−Δ))·L_trend`. But the Figure 1 caption (lines 126–132) uses a single γ for both terms: `1+γΔ` and `1−γΔ`. The paper never resolves which formulation is actually used.
- **MLP fusion layer not ablated**: Section 4.4.2 introduces a learnable MLP g_φ that maps concatenated trend/season embeddings to a composite embedding. No ablation isolates its contribution from the APW reweighting, so the reader cannot attribute any gains to APW vs. the additional capacity. The paper itself acknowledges this as future work (line 306).
- **SDE naming inconsistency**: The metric is called "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3). This creates unnecessary confusion.
- **SDE values exceeding 1.0 undiscussed**: In Table 1, when one component dominates (r=0.1, 0.2, 0.5), SDE values reach 1.3281, 1.378, 1.3502. Since SDE = 1 − cos(·), values >1 imply cos(·) is negative — the vector difference points opposite to the target component. This strains the "recoverability" interpretation and is never addressed.
- **Contribution scope overstated**: The introduction lists three limitations (lines 20–22); the first two (no decomposition inductive bias, time-domain-only objectives) are addressed by adopting CoST as the base architecture, not by the authors' APW contribution. Only the third (isotropic collapse) is addressed by the proposed method.

### Trivial
- Table 3 uses two blocks both labeled "ETT1" (likely ETTh1 and ETTm1), making interpretation confusing.
- TNC is listed as a baseline in Table 3 but never discussed in the text.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Report standard deviations across multiple runs in Table 3, as many result differences are within margins explainable by run-to-run variance.
- Include forecasting results on ETTm2, which is listed as an available dataset in Section 4.1 but absent from Table 3.
- Provide hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for γ, γ′.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most interesting observation is in Table 1: under a contrastive encoder (TS2Vec), the asymmetry factor Δ cleanly flips sign at the balanced amplitude ratio, and SDE values exceed 1.0 when one component dominates — suggesting that contrastive objectives don't just underrepresent weak components but can actively invert their semantic direction in embedding space. This "anti-representation" phenomenon is genuinely striking and merits deeper investigation than the paper provides.

## Suggestions
- Report SDE and Δ values alongside MSE/MAE in the main results table, and show Δ evolution over training, to substantiate the claimed mechanism. This is the single most important missing piece.
- Acknowledge and analyze the failure cases (Electricity long horizons, Weather long horizons) — understanding when and why APW hurts would be more scientifically valuable than the current blanket positive framing.
- Ablate the MLP fusion layer to isolate APW's contribution from added model capacity.
- Resolve the γ/γ′ vs. single-γ inconsistency and clarify which is used in experiments.
- Unify the SDE naming across the paper.
