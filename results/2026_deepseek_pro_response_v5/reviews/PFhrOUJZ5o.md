## Summary
This paper introduces LAION-Comp, a 540K-image dataset built on LAION-Aesthetics V2 annotated with GPT-4o-generated scene graphs (objects, attributes, relations) with partial human verification reporting >95% accuracy. The authors train four baseline models (SDXL-SG, SD3.5-SG, FLUX-SG, SD1.5-SG) integrating a GNN-based scene graph encoder into diffusion and flow-matching backbones, and introduce CompSGen Bench, a benchmark of 20,838 complex-scene test samples. Experiments show SG2IM models trained on LAION-Comp outperform those trained on COCO/VG across accuracy metrics, and the ablation study demonstrates monotonic improvement with increased data.

## Strengths
- **Multi-dataset empirical validation (Table 2):** The same SG2IM models (SGDiff, SG-Adapter, SDXL-SG) consistently achieve higher SG-IoU, Entity-IoU, and Relation-IoU when trained on LAION-Comp vs. COCO or Visual Genome. SDXL-SG on LAION-Comp reaches SG-IoU=0.558 vs. 0.497 (COCO) and 0.546 (VG), providing direct evidence that LAION-Comp annotations improve SG2IM performance over prior SG datasets.
- **Rich and diverse annotations (Section 3.2, Figure 4):** LAION-Comp contains 6.39 objects/sample on average vs. 5.33 in original LAION captions (216% more object information excluding proper nouns). The most frequent relation ("surrounded by") accounts for only 3.78% of all relations, and the non-spatial vs. spatial relation ratio (77.48% vs. 22.52%, compared to VG's 41.98% vs. 58.02%) quantifies a meaningful shift toward interaction-based semantics, supporting the claim of richer compositional annotations.
- **Ablation study with monotonic scaling gains (Table 4):** Progressive improvement as training data increases from 10% to 100% (SDXL-SG SG-IoU: 0.530 → 0.533 → 0.547 → 0.558). At 10% data volume (smaller than VG), SDXL-SG already achieves Entity-IoU=0.874, outperforming its VG-trained counterpart (0.813), suggesting annotation quality drives gains beyond scale alone.
- **Cross-backbone generality (Tables 2–3):** The approach is validated across four backbones spanning diffusion (SDXL, SD1.5) and flow matching (SD3.5, FLUX), with FLUX-SG and SD3.5-SG posting the strongest accuracy (SG-IoU=0.583 and 0.578), demonstrating the dataset's value is not tied to a single architecture.
- **CompSGen Bench as targeted evaluation (Section 3.3):** Filtering to samples with >4 relations (20,838 complex scenes) with three complementary accuracy metrics (SG-IoU, Entity-IoU, Relation-IoU) plus FID and CLIP score creates a focused benchmark specifically for the regime where text-only models struggle.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Structure-vs-quality confound weakens the framing claim:** The paper's thesis is that *structural* annotations (scene graphs) specifically enable better compositional generation. However, the comparison between T2I models (trained on noisy web-scraped LAION text) and SG2IM models (trained on GPT-4o-generated scene graphs) conflates annotation structure with annotation quality — the SG annotations are generated by a VLM prompted for accuracy, while the text captions are web-scraped and often loosely related to image content (line 111: "describe information beyond the images"). The paper never includes a control condition (e.g., GPT-4o-generated high-quality text captions for the same images). As a result, the experiments equally support the weaker claim that "better annotations improve generation." This does not invalidate the dataset's practical value — LAION-Comp is clearly a better SG dataset than COCO/VG, as shown in the SG-to-SG comparisons in Table 2 — but it means the paper's claim that structure *specifically* is the key factor is unsupported by the current experimental design. The paper should either add this control or temper its structural-annotation claims (e.g., lines 15, 107, 331).

### Minor
- **Cross-dataset evaluation asymmetry (Table 2):** Models trained on COCO-Stuff and Visual Genome are evaluated only on the LAION-Comp test set. Lower performance of COCO/VG-trained models could partially reflect domain shift (different image distributions, annotation vocabularies) rather than inferior annotation quality alone. The paper mentions CLIP scores on COCO (line 310) for one model pair, but a within-dataset evaluation (LAION-Comp models on COCO/VG test sets) would more robustly isolate annotation quality from domain effects.
- **Evaluation metric pipeline deferred to appendix:** The SG-IoU, Entity-IoU, and Relation-IoU metrics (line 191) rely on a parser that extracts scene graphs from generated images, but the parser is described only in the appendix (Sec. A.2). While the paper cites Shen et al. (2024) for the metric definitions, the main body lacks any summary of the extraction mechanism, which would help readers assess potential circularity concerns.

### Trivial
- SD1.5-SG results (Table 3, FID 56.3) add limited insight given the poor base model quality; the space might be better used for other analyses.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Annotation cost/scalability: GPT-4o inference at 540K scale is expensive; reporting cost would help others considering similar pipelines.

- A qualitative failure analysis showing what kinds of scenes or relations the models still get wrong would improve the contribution's honesty and usefulness.

## Novel Insights
The paper's analysis of non-spatial vs. spatial relation distributions (LAION-Comp: 77.48% non-spatial vs. VG: 41.98%) provides a concrete, quantitative characterization of why existing SG datasets like Visual Genome may be insufficient for complex scene generation — VG is dominated by geometric/spatial relations while real compositional understanding requires interaction-based semantics. This framing, supported by the ablation showing that even 10% of LAION-Comp (a subset smaller than full VG) yields better Entity-IoU than VG-trained models, offers a useful empirical insight for the field about what annotation properties matter for compositional generation.

## Suggestions
- Add a control experiment using the same GPT-4o pipeline to generate high-quality text captions for a subset of images, train a T2I model on those, and compare against the SG-trained model. This would isolate the effect of annotation structure from annotation quality.
- Report within-dataset baselines for COCO/VG (i.e., evaluate COCO-trained models on COCO test, VG-trained on VG test) alongside the cross-dataset evaluation to contextualize the domain shift concern.
- Include a one-paragraph summary of the evaluation parser (SG extraction from generated images) in the main body.
