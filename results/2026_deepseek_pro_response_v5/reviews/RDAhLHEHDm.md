## Summary
This paper proposes a "context-driven" paradigm for Scientific LLMs in biomolecular understanding: instead of feeding raw protein sequences to LLMs, use bioinformatics tools (BLASTp, InterProScan, Pfam) to generate structured textual context and feed only that context. Experiments across seven models on protein function, pathway, and subcellular localization tasks show that context-only input dramatically outperforms sequence-only input. The paper frames this as evidence that the "tokenization dilemma" can be circumvented, and claims that raw sequences "consistently act as informational noise."

## Strengths
- **Strong core result in Table 1 across a diverse model panel**: Context-only achieves dramatically higher scores than sequence-only across all seven models (e.g., Intern-S1: 86.15 vs 43.33; DeepSeek-v3: 84.99 vs 40.77). The magnitude and consistency of this gap across both specialized Sci-LLMs and general-purpose LLMs is a robust empirical finding.

- **Valid mechanistic decomposition through Evolla's pipeline (Figure 3)**: Tracing representation quality from the SaProt encoder (ARI=0.945) through the Q-Former alignment module (ARI=0.916) to the final decoder output (ARI=0.809) provides concrete, within-model evidence for the semantic misalignment claim. This analysis uses the same representation type at each stage, making it internally valid.

- **Temporal analysis (Section 5.4, Figure 4) offers a creative lens on generalization**: Evaluating performance against protein publication year (1995–2024) reveals differential degradation patterns: Evolla shows steep decline for recent proteins, Intern-S1 is flat and low, while the context-driven approach degrades more gracefully. The paper acknowledges the database-coverage confound.

- **Addresses a real and well-motivated problem**: The tokenization dilemma — atomic tokens destroying functional motifs vs. modality alignment challenges — is a genuine conceptual tension in biomolecular Sci-LLMs, clearly articulated in Section 3.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None identified. The core empirical finding (context dramatically outperforms raw sequence) survives the identified issues; the problems are in overclaiming and secondary analyses.

### Major
- **The universal "informational noise" claim is contradicted by the paper's own data for general-purpose LLMs.** The paper states that raw sequences "consistently act as informational noise" and that "the inclusion of the raw sequence alongside its high-level context consistently degrades performance" (Abstract, Section 5.1 Takeaway). However, in Table 1, three of four general-purpose LLMs perform _better_ with sequence added: DeepSeek-v3 (86.03 Seq+Context vs 84.99 Context-Only), GPT-5 (76.45 vs 75.76), and Qwen3 (85.90 vs 84.99). Only Gemini 2.5 Pro shows a negligible drop (86.98 vs 87.19). The "noise" effect holds for specialized Sci-LLMs but reverses for general LLMs — a model-class-dependent result that the paper's narrative ignores entirely. The takeaway message must be substantially qualified.

- **Numerical contradiction in the wet-lab validation (Section 5.6).** The body text states that "Evolla (Figure 6) attains a reasonable 80.0% accuracy on Rhodopsin" and "fails catastrophically on PETase." But the Figure 6 caption reports Evolla achieves 5.00% accuracy on Rhodopsin (1 correct, 19 incorrect) and 83.78% on PETase — the opposite pattern. This is not a rounding error but a complete reversal, and it undermines trust in the wet-lab results. The authors must resolve which numbers are correct.

- **The core comparison between context-only and sequence-only is fundamentally asymmetric.** The "context" is generated by BLASTp (searching Swiss-Prot for homologs and retrieving their GO annotations), InterProScan (detecting conserved domains), and Pfam — tools that distill curated biological knowledge. When asked "What is the function of this protein?", the context text often contains the answer. The task for the context-only model is thus reading comprehension of pre-computed annotations, while the sequence-only model must perform ab initio function prediction. The paper frames its approach as revealing LLMs' "profound capacity for reasoning over structured, human-readable knowledge" but does not adequately acknowledge this asymmetry. Importantly, the Sequence+Context vs Context-Only comparison (within the same model, same information base) remains fair and is the more scientifically interesting result.

### Minor
- **The embedding comparison in Figure 2 is between fundamentally different representation types.** The "Ours" embedding is generated by applying Qwen-embedding (a text embedding model) to the structured context text, while the other models' embeddings are final-layer outputs from processing raw sequences. That text embeddings of functional annotations cluster by functional similarity is expected, not evidence that the context-driven approach produces better representations. The Figure 3 within-model analysis is valid, but Figure 2's cross-approach comparison should not be presented as apples-to-apples.

- **No statistical significance or variance reported for Table 1.** Some claimed effects involve differences of 0.7–3.5 points on a 0–100 scale. Without variance estimates, readers cannot assess whether these differences are meaningful. Dataset size is deferred to Appendix B (stripped), compounding the opacity.

- **Label leakage from BLASTp self-hits is not adequately controlled.** For well-characterized proteins, BLASTp against Swiss-Prot can retrieve the query protein itself or near-identical homologs (e.g., >95% identity) whose GO annotations are effectively the ground-truth answer. The paper's anti-leakage argument — restricting to homolog annotations rather than the query's own record — does not address the case where the homolog is functionally identical to the query.

## Novel Insights
Beyond the paper's own contributions, the most interesting signal — not explored by the authors — is the _differential effect_ of adding raw sequence: it degrades specialized Sci-LLMs but slightly helps general-purpose LLMs. This pattern suggests that domain-specific training may cause models to over-weight sequence-level patterns at the expense of higher-level context, while general LLMs treat the sequence as just another text field and integrate it more flexibly. This model-class interaction deserves focused study and could be more scientifically significant than the paper's universal "noise" narrative.

## Suggestions
- Revise the "consistently acts as informational noise" claim to reflect the model-class split in the data: the pattern holds for specialized Sci-LLMs but reverses for general-purpose LLMs. This nuanced finding is actually more interesting than the universal claim.
- Resolve and correct the wet-lab accuracy contradiction between text (80% for Evolla on Rhodopsin) and Figure 6 (5%).
- Either remove Figure 2's cross-approach ARI comparison or reframe it as a comparison of input representations rather than model quality.
- Report BLAST hit sequence identity distributions to address the label leakage concern.
