## Summary

HYPOGENEAGENT is an LLM-driven framework for selecting the Leiden clustering resolution parameter in single-cell/Perturb-seq analyses. It introduces two annotation-consistency metrics — intra-cluster agreement (ICS) and inter-cluster distinctiveness (ICD) — derived from GO hypotheses generated by GPT-o3, combined into a Resolution Score. The method is demonstrated on a single K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq dataset (Replogle et al.) and compared to silhouette score, modularity, and functional enrichment analysis.

## Strengths
- **Formally defined, reusable metrics.** ICS and ICD (Table 1, Section 3.4) are cleanly formulated; the Resolution Score turns a subjective tuning problem into an optimisable quantity.
- **Systematic Stage 1 ablation.** The paper benchmarks multiple LLMs, embeddings, prompts, and temperatures on 100 non-redundant GOBP gene sets (Section 4.3), establishing a credible, evidence-backed configuration for Stage 2 deployment.
- **Two complementary clustering views.** Both GEX-level (r=0.4) and perturbation-level (r=0.5) clusterings show a clear Resolution Score optimum (Figures 3 and 4), suggesting the pattern is not artefactual of a single data view.

## Novel Insights

The concept of using annotation consistency — semantic self-consistency of LLM-generated functional descriptions — as a proxy for biologically valid clustering granularity remains genuinely novel. The proposal that LLM "opinion stability" across candidates (ICS) and across clusters (ICD) can serve as a quality oracle for unsupervised clustering parameters opens a direction where functional interpretation feeds back into hyperparameter selection. However, the specific claim of superiority over classical metrics remains an assertion rather than an evidence-backed result, and the paper's own functional enrichment analysis (Figure 6a) selects a different resolution, raising questions about internal coherence that the rebuttal does not resolve within the submitted text.
