## Summary

The paper proposes **Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE)**, a directional metric quantifying per-component recoverability in contrastive time-series embeddings, and an **Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW)** mechanism that re-scales CoST's seasonal and trend contrastive losses using an observed SDE asymmetry factor Δ. An MLP fusion layer is also introduced to model nonlinear trend-seasonal interactions. The method is evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather forecasting benchmarks against TS2Vec, TNC, and vanilla CoST.

## Strengths

- **Principled synthetic diagnostic (Table 1):** SDE analysis on TS2Vec across nine amplitude ratios shows monotonic Δ variation, confirming semantic imbalance. SDE=0.44 even at r=1 suggests TS2Vec's embedding is not linearly decomposable under balance.
- **Meaningful negative result (Table 2):** Direct SDE regularization degrades performance on all tested horizons, motivating the adaptive weighting approach.
- **Strong ETTm1 results:** CoST+APW achieves best MSE across all five ETTm1 horizons (e.g., h=672: 0.132 vs CoST 0.154, TS2Vec 0.142), representing a clear improvement on this dataset.

## Novel Insights

The SDE diagnostic framework — measuring component recoverability via cosine similarity in embedding space and tracking asymmetry across amplitude conditions — is a meaningful analytical tool for auditing contrastive time-series encoders. Table 1's finding that TS2Vec is not linearly decomposable even under balanced conditions (SDE=0.44 at r=1) is a genuinely informative result. The negative result in Table 2 (direct SDE regularization degrades performance) is a useful finding that motivates the adaptive weighting approach on principled grounds. The ETTm1 improvements are real and suggest the mechanism works on shorter-period, denser time series. However, the central empirical loop remains unclosed: the paper never demonstrates that the proposed training procedure actually reduces SDE on real data, and the severe degradation on Electricity long-horizon — the paper's primary motivated use case — is both unexplained and unacknowledged in the main text.
