## Novel Insights

The finding that style representations achieve 77.46% composer classification accuracy — versus 29.99% for content representations — implies that professional pianists systematically encode compositional identity into their performance choices to a much greater degree than personal expression alone. This has implications beyond piano rendering: if composer-appropriate style envelopes are the primary driver of the style space, then the PSR module may succeed partly because composers strongly constrain acceptable interpretive ranges, making style-from-score inference easier than the one-to-many framing suggests. This hypothesis, if confirmed across a broader repertoire, would reframe "performer identity" as a second-order effect relative to "composer compatibility" — a potentially important claim for MIR research on expressivity.
