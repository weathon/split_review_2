## Summary

This paper compares three neural network architectures — a plain MLP, a "U-Net-like" residual network, and a DeepONet-inspired model — for surrogate modeling of single-step thermal explosion kinetics in a hydrogen–oxygen–air system. The U-Net variant achieves a statistically significantly lower MSE (1.37×10⁻³) versus MLP (2.03×10⁻²) and DeepONet (1.81×10⁻²) on a 5,000-sample test set, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals. The paper's primary contribution is the empirical finding that architecture choice substantially affects rollout accuracy for stiff combustion kinetics.

## Novel Insights

The paper's central finding — that a residual network with hierarchical skip connections substantially outperforms plain MLPs and a factorized operator-learning model on stiff ODE rollouts for combustion kinetics, with non-overlapping confidence intervals on a 5,000-sample test set — is a real empirical result. The specific contribution of the local intermediate residual (block output added before compression) is marginally novel relative to a plain global skip, but the paper does not isolate this component or frame it in terms of residual ODE integration, which would connect it to the broader literature.
