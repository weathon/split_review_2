## Summary
The paper proposes **HYPOGENEAGENT**, a framework that uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to determine the optimal clustering resolution in single-cell genomics and Perturb-seq data. It treats the LLM as a biological annotator that generates functional hypotheses for cluster-specific gene sets, then calculates a "Resolution Score" (RS) based on the semantic consistency within clusters (Intra-cluster agreement) and distinctiveness between clusters (Inter-cluster separation). The framework aims to replace subjective manual resolution tuning with a biology-aware, quantitative optimization process that simultaneously provides cluster annotations.

## Strengths
- **Introduction of a Quantitative, Bio-Aware Clustering Metric**: The paper defines a "Resolution Score" that formalizes the quality of a cluster partition based on biological interpretability. This addresses a significant gap in single-cell analysis where traditional metrics (Silhouette, Modularity) do not account for biological meaning.
- **Empirical Validation of LLM Reasoning for Genomics**: The authors benchmark multiple LLM backbones (GPT-o3, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-pro) on a curated set of 100 non-redundant GOBP gene sets. They demonstrate that "thinking" models like GPT-o3 achieve high semantic accuracy and provide a validation of the model's self-judgment capability through confidence scores.
- **Novel Feedback Loop for Clustering**: By using LLM-generated annotation consistency to drive hyperparameter selection, the paper successfully closes the loop between traditionally independent steps of unsupervised partitioning and functional interpretation.
- **Cross-Modality Application**: The method is validated on both gene-expression (GEX) levels and perturbation levels in a CRISPRi Perturb-seq dataset, showing utility across different omics summaries.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
- **None.**

### Major
- **Methodological Risk of Circular Logic**: The "Resolution Score" (RS) relies heavily on the similarity between descriptions generated by the *same* LLM for the same gene set. As noted in Section 3.4, Intra-cluster similarity (ICS) is calculated by comparing lower-ranked hypotheses to the top hypothesis from a single agent output. If the LLM generates generic or "template-like" biological terms for a noisy/suboptimal cluster (e.g., "Cell cycle"), the semantic embedding might show high ICS due to model-internal consistency or bias rather than actual biological coherence. The paper lacks a "null model" control (e.g., scoring randomly permuted gene sets) to prove the RS distinguishes biological signal from LLM-specific linguistic patterns.
- **Arbitrary Weighting of Resolution Components**: The Resolution Score formula $RS_k = wICS_k + (1 - w)(1 - ICD_k)$ uses a fixed weighting ($w=1/3$). Section 4.3 acknowledges that "for different clusters, the tendency of resolution score changing with $w$ can be different." This sensitivity suggests the metric may not be stable across different biological systems without re-tuning, which undermines the claim of providing a fully "objective" and "automated" adjudicator.
- **Significant Figure/Caption Mismatch**: Figure 1 at the bottom of page 8 (lines 257-261) and its associated caption describe the effects of "1,25-(OH)2D3 on the expression of IL-17A... in TH17 cells." This appears to be a major artifact or leftover from a different study, as it is unrelated to the K562 CRISPRi dataset or the GO enrichment analysis discussed in the surrounding text (Section 4.4.3). This raises concerns about the rigor of the manuscript preparation.

### Minor
- **Potential Data Leakage via the LLM**: Since LLMs are trained on existing literature (PubMed, GO, NCBI), their "selection" of resolution is essentially an alignment task against known literature rather than an independent discovery. The paper does not address how the agent would handle genuinely novel biological findings or cell states not represented in its training corpus.
- **Cost and Scalability**: The grid search over resolutions requires a significant number of LLM API calls ($C$ clusters $\times$ $R$ resolutions). For large-scale studies with dozens of clusters, the computational cost and time (minutes vs. seconds for traditional metrics) may limit practical adoption without further optimization or local model alternatives.

### Trivial
- **Typographical Inconsistency**: The framework is referred to as both "HYPOGENEAGENT" and "HYPOGENAGENT" (e.g., Section 5).

## Nice-to-Haves
- A sensitivity analysis/heat map showing the stability of the "optimal resolution" as the weight $w$ varies from 0 to 1.
- Benchmarking against a dataset where the "true" biological resolution is agreed upon by human experts (e.g., a standard PBMC dataset) to confirm the agent's selections align with expert judgment.

## Removed Points
- *Reproducibility of implementation details:* Concerns regarding undisclosed training logs or trivial hyperparameters were removed as they are standard to omit or deferred to the appendix, which was stripped in this version.
- *LLM Availability:* Criticisms regarding GPT-5 or other models being unavailable were removed as it is assumed cited entities exist.

## Novel Insights
The framework's primary innovation is the conceptualization of cluster *interpretability* as a mathematically optimizable fitness function. Historically, scientists adjusted resolution parameters "until the clusters look right" based on marker genes; HYPOGENEAGENT formalizes this "looking right" by quantifying semantic coherence and distinctiveness via an LLM agent.

## Suggestions
- Perform a "Null Model" comparison: Run the agent on randomly permuted gene sets of equivalent size. A robust metric should show a significant drop in Resolution Score compared to biologically coherent clusters.
- Correct the figure/caption mismatch at the end of Section 4.4 to ensure the text correctly references the GO enrichment results.
