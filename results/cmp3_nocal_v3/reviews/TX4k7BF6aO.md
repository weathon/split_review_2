## Summary

This paper proposes ARPO, an RL algorithm for training multi-turn LLM-based agents that use external tools. ARPO is motivated by an empirical observation — LLMs exhibit elevated token-level entropy after receiving tool-call feedback — and implements an entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism that branches additional sampling at high-entropy decision points during the rollout phase. The paper additionally discusses "advantage attribution estimation" variants for handling shared vs. individual token segments in branched trajectories. Experiments across 13 benchmarks spanning math reasoning, knowledge-intensive QA, and deep search show consistent improvements over GRPO, DAPO, and REINFORCE++, with a reported efficiency gain of approximately half the tool-call budget.

## Strengths

1. **Well-motivated by concrete empirical evidence.** The pilot experiment in Section 2 (Figures 2 and 4) provides a measurable phenomenon — entropy spikes after tool calls — that directly motivates the algorithm design. Observations about differential uncertainty between search and Python tools add useful granularity. This is the most distinctive and convincingly presented part of the paper.

2. **Consistent positive results across a broad evaluation.** Tables 1 and 2 show ARPO outperforming GRPO, DAPO, and REINFORCE++ on nearly all of 13 datasets, across two backbone families (Llama-3.1 and Qwen-2.5/3) and multiple model sizes. This breadth gives the results credibility.

3. **Practically significant efficiency finding.** The observation that ARPO achieves higher accuracy while using substantially fewer tool calls than GRPO (Figure 7a, roughly 250-300 vs. 400-450 calls) addresses a real operational concern — tool-call costs are a bottleneck for deployed LLM agents.

4. **Diversity analysis provides mechanistic insight.** The clustering analysis (Figure 7b, 54 ARPO clusters vs. 48 GRPO clusters) offers direct evidence that the adaptive rollout changes the sampling distribution, not just reweighting the same trajectories.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Missing ablation: entropy-guided vs. generic adaptive branching.** The paper never tests whether the *entropy signal* drives the gains, as opposed to simply performing *any* adaptive branching at tool-call steps. The critical missing control is a random-branching baseline — branching at tool-call steps with the same budget but without the entropy criterion. Without this, the observed improvement could be attributable to (a) simply having more continuations at decision points regardless of uncertainty, or (b) the adaptive rollout structure effectively increasing the sample count at key steps. This gap undermines the paper's central mechanistic claim that entropy is what matters.

2. **No statistical significance or variance information.** Tables 1 and 2 report single numbers with no confidence intervals, standard deviations, or multi-seed runs. RL training for LLMs is notoriously high-variance (policy collapses, reward hacking, seed sensitivity). Several reported gains are modest (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: ARPO 58.3 vs. GRPO 56.5 average, a 1.8-point gap) and could fall within the noise band of a single training run. Without variance estimates, the reader cannot assess whether results are robust or artifacts of a single seed.

### Minor

3. **Framing of "Advantage Attribution Estimation" overclaims novelty.** Section 3.2 presents Hard and Soft advantage estimation as a new contribution. However, the Soft variant (the default, per Figure 5) is the standard GRPO objective applied to trajectories generated by the adaptive rollout. Equation 4 shows that the importance sampling ratio naturally distinguishes shared/individual tokens — but this is a property of GRPO, not a novel design. The paper acknowledges this ("While we retain the original GRPO loss formulation…") but the section title and framing imply more novelty than exists. The genuine contribution is the rollout mechanism, and centering the novelty claim there would be more accurate.

4. **The theoretical section (Section 3.3) is decorative.** The Generalized Policy Gradient Theorem (Equation 6) restates the well-known result that the policy gradient theorem applies at any temporal granularity of macro actions. The theorem makes no claim specific to entropy-based branching, convergence properties, or exploration efficiency. Calling this "a robust theoretical foundation" for ARPO overstates what an established result provides.

5. **The O(n²) → O(n log n) complexity claim is unsubstantiated.** Line 116 states that ARPO reduces complexity from O(n²) to between O(n log n) and O(n²) without derivation or citation. Since branching is adaptive, the actual complexity depends on how many branches are triggered at which points.

6. **The entropy baseline comparison could be better motivated.** Section 3.1 compares post-tool-call entropy H_t to the initial entropy H_initial (computed right after the question, before any tool interaction). The paper's own Observation (2) partially addresses this — it states that early-reasoning entropy "remains lower than after receiving tool-call feedback" — so H_initial serves as a workable baseline. However, the approach would be more principled if it compared to pre-tool-call entropy directly. As written, the motivation slightly overclaims the precision of the mechanism.

7. **Efficiency comparison (Figure 7a) needs explicit budget-matching details.** The paper claims ARPO uses "only half the tool-call budget" but does not explicitly state whether GRPO and ARPO were matched on total training steps, trajectories per step, and non-rollout hyperparameters for *this specific comparison*. While "Under identical conditions" appears for the main results (line 212), this should be stated directly for the efficiency analysis.

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- An ablation of hyperparameters (α, β, τ in Equation 2; the N:M−N ratio) would strengthen claims about robustness and transferability.
- Observation (3) about search vs. Python feedback causing different uncertainty levels would be more impactful with a quantitative comparison alongside the qualitative statement.
- The multi-tool reward bonus r_M = 0.1 (Equation 5) is a reasonable engineering choice but is not ablated, creating a potential confound with the branching mechanism.

## Removed Points
- **Entropy baseline being "structurally flawed":** Removed from Fatal/Major tier. The paper's own Observation (2) establishes that early-reasoning entropy is lower than post-tool-call entropy, making H_initial a reasonable (if not maximally precise) baseline. The criticism is downgraded to Minor (point 6 above).
- **Section notes about GAIA "—" entries, figure formatting, "missing derivation" of the complexity claim being fatal:** Removed. These are either parser artifacts, presentation preferences, or are already addressed at the Minor level above.
- **"Current RL algorithms performing trajectory-level exploration — imprecise contrast":** Removed. The paper's contrast is accurate: the distinction is mid-trajectory branching vs. complete-trajectory sampling, even if per-token advantages exist.
- **Criticism about missing related work:** Removed per meta-review guidelines.
- **Criticism about "Soft Advantage Estimation is not a new contribution" being structural:** Downgraded to Minor (point 3). The paper honestly states it retains the GRPO loss; the framing can be improved but this is not a structural flaw.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions
- Add the random-branching ablation as the single most informative control experiment. If entropy-guided branching outperforms random branching at the same budget, the central causal claim is validated. If not, revise the explanation.
- Report multi-seed runs (at least 3) with mean and standard deviation for headline results (Table 1).
- Rephrase the "Advantage Attribution Estimation" framing to clearly separate the rollout mechanism (the true novelty) from the standard GRPO objective used on the resulting trajectories.
- Either remove the complexity claim (O(n²) → O(n log n)) or provide a concrete derivation or citation.
- Explicitly state budget-matching details for the tool-call efficiency comparison.
