## Summary

CHATS is a discrete-token-based system that transforms written dialogues into spoken dialogues with natural listener behavior (backchannels, laughter) and turn-taking dynamics (overlaps, pauses, gaps). It extends the dGSLM dual-tower architecture by conditioning on phoneme sequences (including the next utterance) and speaker IDs, and introduces a Turn-Taking Mechanism (TTM) that modifies utterance boundaries during training to teach the model appropriate silence durations and overlap onsets. The system is evaluated through utterance-level PER, dialogue-level backchannel and turn-taking metrics, and human evaluation, showing improved dialogue naturalness over a TTS baseline and dramatically better meaningfulness than textless dGSLM on a 74-hour dataset.

## Strengths

- **Text-conditional generation that simultaneously produces meaningful speaker speech and implicit listener behavior**: CHATS conditions the speaker channel on phonemes while generating listener backchannels/laughter unconditionally. Human evaluation (Table 5) shows CHATS achieves Meaningfulness 3.58 vs. dGSLM's 1.18, while backchannel frequency proportion (23.02%) closely approaches Ground Truth (19.61%) compared to the TTS Baseline (2.08%).

- **Turn-Taking Mechanism (TTM) validated through both distributional and speaker-level metrics**: Figure 4 shows CHATS closely matches Ground Truth distributions for overlaps, pauses, and gaps. Table 4 demonstrates that removing TTM degrades pause duration correlation with human data from 0.42 to -0.09, confirming the mechanism's role in predicting appropriate silence.

- **Preservation of speaker-specific conversational characteristics**: Table 3 shows CHATS achieves the lowest MAE (0.07) for speaker-specific backchannel frequency vs. dGSLM (0.09) and Baseline (0.18). Table 4 shows CHATS achieves highest correlations for IPU and competitive scores for other turn-taking event durations.

- **Data augmentation by context reduction is critical and well-validated**: Table 1 shows removing this augmentation causes PER to skyrocket from 13.03 to 59.35 in the TTS setting, convincingly demonstrating its necessity for robust inference from cold starts.

- **Comprehensive evaluation spanning multiple granularities**: The paper evaluates utterance-level intelligibility (PER), dialogue-level listener events (backchannel frequency/duration), turn-taking distributions (IPU, overlap, pause, gap), speaker-level correlations, and human subjective judgments — a thorough evaluation suite that addresses the multi-faceted nature of spoken dialogue quality.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The backchannel detection methodology for generated audio is unspecified** (Section 4.3.1): To compute backchannel frequencies, durations, and speaker-wise statistics in Tables 3 and 4, the paper must detect backchannels in generated audio. No method is described — whether via VAD, the IPU classifier from Section 2, or manual annotation. The paper describes VAD-based IPU segmentation for turn-taking events (Section 4.3.2), and a similar approach applied to the listener channel could identify backchannels, but this is never stated. This omission makes it impossible to fully assess the validity of the listener-event evaluation.

- **Content fidelity of overlapping speech is not quantitatively evaluated** (Section 4.3, 4.4): The paper claims that conditioning on the next utterance's phonemes prevents unnatural overlap content, but supports this only with a qualitative remark that the "w/o next sentence" ablation "often generated unnatural or meaningless content as overlapping segment." No quantitative metric (e.g., phoneme/word error rate restricted to overlap regions, or human judgment of overlap intelligibility) is reported. The claim about coherent overlap generation therefore remains unsubstantiated.

- **The Turn-Taking Mechanism's inference-time specification is incomplete** (Section 3.2): The conceptual mechanism — modifying time boundaries in training so the uLM learns silence duration and overlap onset through its standard duration prediction — is understandable. However, the paper does not make explicit how silence durations are realized at inference (e.g., whether trailing silence is encoded as the cumulative duration of silence units, or through a separate prediction head). The inference procedure (end of Section 3) describes autoregressive generation until EOS but does not detail how the boundary modifications from training translate into concrete inference behavior. While a capable practitioner could likely infer the intended mechanism from the duration-prediction framework, the description falls short of full reproducibility.

### Minor

- **The baseline comparison isolates only part of the contribution** (Section 4.3): The TTS Baseline cannot generate listener speech by design (listener channel filled with silence units). While the combination of Baseline (showing listener channel matters) and dGSLM (showing text conditioning matters for meaningfulness) together brackets the contribution reasonably, a baseline that also attempts listener speech generation (e.g., a parallel backchannel predictor) would more precisely isolate the value of CHATS's specific architectural choices.

- **IPU classifier accuracy is not reported** (Section 2): The hybrid rule/ML backchannel exclusion pipeline uses a 3-layer bidirectional LSTM classifier whose performance directly affects training and evaluation data quality, yet no accuracy or error analysis is provided.

- **The degradation in PER relative to Baseline is noted but the dGSLM backchannel correlation advantage is not discussed** (Sections 4.2, 4.3): Baseline achieves better PER (12.13 vs. 13.03) — the paper acknowledges this as "minute." However, Table 4 shows dGSLM achieves higher Pearson r for per-speaker backchannel frequency (0.63 vs. 0.54 for Proposed) and several other metrics, which goes undiscussed. This trade-off between text conditioning and speaker-specific backchannel behavior merits analysis.

- **Statistical significance testing is incomplete** (Section 4.4): The human evaluation reports significance only for Proposed vs. Baseline on Dialogue Naturalness (p=0.038). Significance for other metrics and for Proposed vs. dGSLM is not reported, though dGSLM's Meaningfulness (1.18) is clearly floor-level.

### Trivial

- The paper does not explain how the u2s module's speaker embedding mechanism learns to separate speaker characteristics from content/pitch units, though it references prior work (Polyak et al. 2021) for the architecture.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A stronger baseline that also generates listener speech (e.g., a separate backchannel predictor coupled with TTS) would better isolate CHATS's architectural contributions.
- An illustrative waveform/spectrogram example of generated overlap with corresponding next-utterance text would make the overlap-handling behavior more concrete.
- Scaling experiments with a larger spoken-dialogue dataset, as the authors themselves acknowledge 74h is limiting.

## Novel Insights

The paper offers a practical demonstration that text conditioning can rescue textless spoken dialogue models from the meaningfulness collapse they suffer on modest datasets (74h vs. the 2000h used in prior dGSLM work). This is a valuable empirical insight for researchers working with limited spoken-dialogue resources. Additionally, the finding that a simple boundary-modification approach to turn-taking — without an explicit separate prediction module — can produce human-like pause and overlap distributions when integrated into a duration-predicting language model is an elegant design insight.

## Suggestions

- Explicitly describe the backchannel detection methodology used for generated audio (e.g., VAD-based IPU segmentation on the listener channel, applying the IPU classifier from Section 2). Report its reliability if possible.
- Add a quantitative metric for overlap content quality (e.g., PER restricted to overlap regions, or a human judgment study on overlap intelligibility) to support the claim that next-utterance conditioning improves overlap coherence.
- Clarify the TTM inference procedure: explicitly state whether silence duration is implicitly encoded through the cumulative duration of predicted silence units, or through a separate mechanism.
- Discuss the trade-off where dGSLM achieves higher Pearson r for per-speaker backchannel frequency (0.63 vs. 0.54), and what this implies about text conditioning vs. speaker-specific behavior.
- Report IPU classifier accuracy to give readers confidence in the data preparation pipeline.
