## Summary

This paper challenges the sequence-centric paradigm for Scientific LLMs (Sci-LLMs) by proposing a context-driven approach that replaces raw biomolecular sequences with structured, human-readable annotations generated by established bioinformatics tools (InterProScan, BLASTp, ProTrek). Through a systematic empirical comparison across 7 LLMs on three protein QA tasks, the authors show that context-only input dramatically outperforms sequence-only and sequence+context input modes. The paper further provides mechanistic insight via a layer-wise representational analysis of Evolla-10B, efficiency comparisons, and wet-lab validation on genuinely novel protein sequences.

---

## Strengths

- **Layer-wise semantic misalignment diagnosis (Section 5.3, Figure 3):** The ARI tracking across SaProt encoder (0.945) → Q-Former alignment (0.916) → Evolla decoder (0.809) is the most methodologically clean and novel result in the paper. It directly shows that the alignment module, not the encoder, is where functional information is degraded, providing a mechanistic diagnosis of why sequence-as-modality models underperform. This is a genuine and insightful finding.

- **Systematic multi-model empirical comparison (Table 1):** Evaluating 7 diverse LLMs (3 specialized Sci-LLMs, 4 general-purpose) across three input configurations at scale provides a robust empirical foundation. The consistent win of context-only over sequence-only is demonstrated across very different model families.

- **Real-world efficiency gains (Table 2):** The quantitative cost-time-performance analysis showing ~23× cheaper and ~154× faster batch processing for the context-driven approach compared to Evolla is concrete and impactful. This makes the practical case for the proposed pipeline compellingly.

- **Wet-lab validation on truly novel sequences (Section 5.6, Figures 5–6):** Using unpublished proteins absent from major databases provides a genuine holdout test. The context-driven method achieves 100% on Rhodopsin and 97.3% on PETase, vs. Evolla's collapse on one of the families, offering meaningful real-world evidence.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None — the core practical finding (context >> sequence) is robust and withstands the methodological issues below.

### Major

- **The ARI comparison (Section 5.2, Figure 2) compares fundamentally incompatible embedding types.** The paper compares final-layer activations from generative LLMs (Evolla, Intern-S1, NatureLM) against Qwen-embedding, a model explicitly trained for retrieval and semantic discrimination with dense, linearly separable spaces. These objectives produce categorically different representation geometries. The conclusion that context provides "a vastly superior functional representation" cannot be drawn from an ARI comparison between a generation-tuned model's latent space and a purpose-built embedding model. The "weak representation" horn of the tokenization dilemma is not validly tested by this analysis; the analysis would need to compare like-for-like (e.g., embedding the context with a model from the same family as the generative LLMs, or extracting embeddings from dedicated embedding adapters of those models).

- **The "sequences consistently act as informational noise" claim is factually overstated.** Table 1 shows that for general-purpose LLMs, adding the raw sequence alongside context *improves* performance in several cases: DeepSeek-v3 (Seq+Ctx 86.03 > Ctx 84.99), Qwen3-235B (85.90 > 84.99), and GPT-5 (Seq+Ctx 76.45 > Ctx 75.76). The sequence-degradation pattern holds specifically for specialized Sci-LLMs (Evolla, Intern-S1, NatureLM) but not for general-purpose LLMs. The paper's framing that this effect is universal ("consistently") and its interpretation as a fundamental tokenization failure across all models is directly contradicted by the data in the authors' own Table 1.

- **Internal inconsistency in Section 5.6 text vs. Figure 6.** The text states: "While Evolla attains a reasonable 80.0% accuracy on Rhodopsin, it fails catastrophically on PETase." However, Figure 6's caption explicitly reports: "left plot for Rhodopsin shows 5.00% accuracy with 1 correct and 19 incorrect predictions; right plot for PETase shows 83.78% accuracy." These two statements are directly contradictory — the text and figure labels appear swapped. This is a concrete, verifiable inconsistency that undermines confidence in the wet-lab validation reporting.

### Minor

- **The "reasoning vs. retrieval" distinction is not empirically tested.** The paper frames its central contribution as showing that "LLMs excel at reasoning over structured knowledge," but the context pipeline uses BLASTp to retrieve GO annotations from homologous sequences — effectively performing RAG over a database whose annotations are closely related to the ground truth. For well-characterized proteins with high-identity BLAST hits, the LLM may need to do no reasoning at all, merely extracting a near-identical annotation. The paper addresses this philosophically ("homology-based inference rather than direct annotation matching," Section 4), but does not provide an empirical ablation — e.g., restricting to proteins where BLAST finds no hits above 30% identity to test whether InterProScan-only context (without homology retrieval) remains superior. Without this, the paper cannot distinguish "reasoning over structured knowledge" from "highly effective RAG," which would limit the strength of its theoretical framing. (Note: the wet-lab validation partially mitigates this concern for the practical claim.)

- **NatureLM's sequence-only score (6.82) is implausibly low and unexplained.** NatureLM obtains 3.58 on function, 5.52 on pathway, and 10.45 on localization in the sequence-only condition. These scores are near-random and dramatically below even general-purpose models on the same input (DeepSeek-v3: 40.77). This strongly suggests a prompt-format incompatibility or output parsing issue specific to NatureLM in the sequence-only condition, rather than a genuine measure of its biology understanding capacity. The paper presents this value without comment. If this is a setup artifact, its inclusion inflates the apparent gap between sequence-only and context-only conditions.

- **LLM judge identity is not specified in the main text.** Section 5.1 mentions "a general-purpose LLM as an expert judge (LLM-Score)" but defers specification to the appendix. Since LLM-Score is the sole quantitative metric for the headline result, the judging model and prompt design should be stated in the body. If the same model family serves as both answering model and judge (e.g., DeepSeek-V3 judged by DeepSeek-V3), same-family stylistic similarity could systematically inflate one model's scores.

### Trivial

- The temporal analysis (Section 5.4) acknowledges that Evolla's training data cutoff (Swiss-Prot 202303) conflates with sequence novelty as an explanation for temporal performance collapse, but dismisses it somewhat quickly. The paper should more clearly state this as an acknowledged limitation, not a resolved one.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel contribution is the layer-wise diagnostic in Section 5.3: showing that within a sequence-as-modality model (Evolla), the SaProt encoder itself achieves an ARI of 0.945 — near the context-driven approach's 0.958 — but this information is progressively destroyed by the Q-Former alignment (0.916) and further degraded by the decoder (0.809). This directly localizes the failure mode of the sequence-as-modality paradigm to the modality-alignment step rather than the sequence encoder itself. This is actionable for future work: it suggests that if alignment can be improved (e.g., via better cross-modal contrastive training), the encoder's rich representations could be preserved. The implication is that the "tokenization dilemma" may be solvable at the alignment stage rather than requiring a complete paradigm shift to context-driven approaches.
