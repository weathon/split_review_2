## Summary

HYPOGENEAGENT is an LLM-driven framework for selecting the resolution hyperparameter in Leiden clustering of single-cell/Perturb-seq data. The core contribution is two annotation-consistency metrics—intra-cluster agreement (ICS) and inter-cluster distinctiveness (ICD)—computed from GO hypotheses generated by GPT-o3, combined into a Resolution Score that is maximised to choose an "objective" clustering granularity. The method is demonstrated on a single K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq dataset (Replogle et al.) and benchmarked against silhouette score, modularity, and functional enrichment analysis.

---

## Strengths

- **Formally defined, reusable metrics.** ICS and ICD are cleanly formulated (Table 1, Section 3.4) and their combination into a Resolution Score turns a traditionally subjective parameter into an optimisable quantity. The clear mathematical specification makes reimplementation straightforward.

- **Systematic Stage 1 ablation.** The paper rigorously benchmarks multiple LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-o3, GPT-5, Gemini variants), embedding methods (OpenAI, SapBERT, Nomic), prompt designs, and temperature settings on 100 non-redundant GOBP gene sets. The finding that GPT-o3 with the hypothesis prompt yields the highest, most stable cosine similarity and self-consistent confidence calibration (Figures S1–S3) is a credible, evidence-backed basis for the fixed deployment configuration used in Stage 2.

- **Application to two complementary clustering views.** The framework is applied to both GEX-level and perturbation-level clusterings of the same dataset (Figures 3 and 4), and the Resolution Score identifies a clear optimum in both cases, suggesting the approach is not artefactual of a single data view.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
*None that fully invalidate the core idea, but the major issues collectively prevent the comparative claims from being substantiated.*

### Major

- **The headline comparative claim is never empirically demonstrated.** The abstract states the Resolution Score "selects clustering granularities that exhibit alignment with known pathway compared to classical metrics," and the introduction asserts it "recover[s] known perturbation effects better than modularity and silhouette criteria." What the paper actually shows is that HYPOGENEAGENT selects r=0.4 (GEX), silhouette elbows at r=0.5–0.6, and modularity peaks at r=0.7. There is no independent ground truth—no mapping of clusters to known pathway memberships, no recall of annotated co-essential gene modules, no external criterion—that would let one verify which resolution is actually correct. The claim of superiority reduces to "we selected a lower resolution, and here is a UMAP that looks clean." This is not evidence of superiority; it is a circular appeal to the method's own output.

- **The functional enrichment "validation" in Section 4.4.3 is self-referential and in fact contradicts the paper's narrative.** The Figure 6a caption explicitly states "the resolution score peaks at 0.7," while HYPOGENEAGENT selects r=0.4. The paper dismisses this discrepancy with "consider the reasonability of cluster numbers we expected, so the selected resolution can be 0.5 or 0.4." This post-hoc reasoning is undefined and scientifically inadequate: "reasonability of cluster numbers expected" is not an operationalised criterion. More fundamentally, the same ICS/ICD metrics are applied to enrichment results to validate LLM-based ICS/ICD results — both measure semantic coherence/distinctiveness of functional descriptions. Two semantically similar measures agreeing shows consistency, not independent validation.

- **The weight parameter w=1/3 is tuned on the only test dataset.** Section 3.4 states: "w=1/3 was chosen by a small grid search and found to give a stable ordering of resolutions across datasets." The paper uses one dataset for Stage 2. No held-out data, cross-validation, or secondary dataset is provided to verify that the chosen w generalises. This is a free parameter effectively fitted on the sole evaluation instance.

### Minor

- **Single-dataset evaluation.** Results rest entirely on one K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq experiment. The paper acknowledges this ("preliminary test") in the abstract, but then elevates to "superior biological interpretability" in the introduction. These two characterisations are inconsistent; the claims should be uniformly scoped to "preliminary."

- **Stage 1 benchmark is subject to potential data contamination.** GO term descriptions are likely present in LLM training corpora, so cosine similarity between an LLM's outputs and official GO descriptions may reflect memorised retrieval rather than genuine biological reasoning. This does not necessarily invalidate Stage 2, but Stage 1 cannot be treated as an unbiased measure of annotation quality without a brief discussion of this confound.

- **ICD measures linguistic diversity rather than confirmed biological distinctiveness.** If GPT-o3 uses varied language for biologically similar processes (or repetitive language for distinct ones), the ICD score is systematically biased. No analysis of this potential confound is provided.

### Trivial

- The abstract calls the method "a preliminary test" while the introduction uses "superior" without qualification; aligning these framings would strengthen coherence.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Construct an independent external ground truth for resolution quality (e.g., recall of known pathway co-memberships from Replogle et al. at each resolution) and compare HYPOGENEAGENT's selected resolution to the silhouette/modularity-selected ones on that objective criterion. This is the single experiment that would convert the paper's claim from assertion to evidence.
- Apply HYPOGENEAGENT to at least one additional Perturb-seq or scRNA-seq dataset where perturbation groupings are known, to establish generalisability.
- Report API cost (calls for GPT-o3 over a 10-resolution × up to 20-cluster grid). This is a practical constraint directly relevant to the paper's stated scalability goal.
- Report ICS and ICD components separately throughout results, so readers can audit what each contributes independently of the combined score.

## Novel Insights

The framework of using annotation consistency — rather than geometric or graph-theoretic properties — as a proxy for biologically valid clustering granularity is a genuinely novel and practically motivated idea. The observation that semantic self-consistency of LLM-generated functional descriptions can track cluster coherence (ICS) and cluster distinctiveness (ICD) across a resolution grid opens a direction where LLM "opinion stability" serves as a quality oracle. That said, the specific claim — that this oracle outperforms classical metrics — is not yet tested with the experiment it requires: an external, biology-grounded ground truth for which resolution is "correct."

---

## Suggestions

1. Map clusters at each resolution to the known functional groupings of the knocked-down genes (e.g., co-essential pathway modules from the Replogle et al. dataset itself) and compute recall at each resolution. Compare HYPOGENEAGENT's selected resolution to silhouette and modularity selections on this objective measure.
2. Validate w=1/3 on a second publicly available Perturb-seq or scRNA-seq dataset, or show results across a range of w values are qualitatively stable.
3. Unify the abstract's "preliminary test" framing with the introduction's "superior" language — the comparative claim needs either evidence or scoping.
4. Address the Figure 6a discrepancy explicitly: either show why the enrichment peak at r=0.7 is not informative, or revise the claim that functional enrichment validates the agent's r=0.4 selection.
