## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end multi-view diabetic retinopathy grading framework that avoids external lesion annotations by generating internal lesion proposals via a Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module — using stage-wise CAMs to identify high-evidence regions — and selectively fusing them across views with a Mixture-of-Experts cross-view attention module (LGRF). Evaluated on MFIDDR (four-view) and DRTiD (two-view), the annotation-free variant matches or exceeds several externally supervised baselines, and the annotation-informed variant achieves SOTA on MFIDDR.

---

## Strengths

- **Annotation-free performance parity with externally supervised methods (Table 1 & 3)**: The annotation-free variant achieves 83.9% Acc / 70.9% Kappa on MFIDDR, surpassing CVSA (82.6%) and LFMVDR-with-lesion (82.2%), and 76.0% Acc on DRTiD, outperforming all compared baselines including CrossFiT (75.6%) which uses OD/macula coordinates. This is the paper's strongest evidence and it is well-supported by the tables.

- **Novel self-supervised lesion proposal mechanism (GALP, Section 3.2)**: The combination of stage-wise auxiliary focal loss (Eq. 2) and LayerCAM-derived grade evidence maps (Eq. 3) followed by Top-K region selection (Eq. 4–7) is a technically coherent approach to generating internal lesion surrogates without external annotations. The motivating rationale — that DR grades are predominantly determined by localized lesion evidence — is clinically grounded.

- **Expert-routed cross-view fusion (LGRF, Section 3.3)**: The MoE router gated by current-view features (Eq. 9) to select which experts process adjacent-view proposals, followed by Top-K-weighted cross-view attention (Eqs. 12–15), is a principled architectural choice that enables selective, context-aware integration. Ablation shows removing the expert pool drops accuracy by 1.3% (Table 4, "w/o Experts").

- **Thorough hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Figure 3)**: The sweeps over retention ratio α, K₂, and M demonstrate genuine optima with meaningful degradation on both sides, supporting that the chosen settings are not arbitrary.

- **Grade-wise per-class analysis (Table 2)**: The detailed breakdown of F1, precision, and specificity per grade provides transparency about where the method works and where it struggles (e.g., Grade 4 F1 remains lower at 36.0% annotation-free), with honest reporting.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **The ablation does not isolate GALP's two distinct contributions.** Section 4.3 defines "w/o GALP" as removing GALP and passing *all tokens* to LGRF for fusion, thereby simultaneously removing: (a) the stage-wise auxiliary loss that strengthens intermediate feature discriminability, and (b) the Top-K lesion-proposal selection that reduces background noise. These are two separately claimed mechanisms (Section 3.2: "GALP pursues two objectives"), and the single ablation row conflates them. A missing row — LGRF with auxiliary loss active but no Top-K filtering (α=1.0) — would disambiguate whether the gain comes primarily from better-supervised features or from selective proposal routing. Without it, the paper cannot confirm that its more novel claim (spatial proposal selection) is doing meaningful work beyond the well-known auxiliary supervision trick. The 1.2% drop from removing GALP (83.9% → 82.7%) could be entirely attributable to the auxiliary loss.

### Minor

- **No verification that GALP proposals spatially correspond to known lesion regions.** The paper argues (Section 3.2) that because "DR grades are predominantly determined by lesion evidence," CAM-derived top-K regions are lesion proposals. However, MFIDDR provides lesion segmentation masks ("the provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model," Section 4.1), and the paper never computes spatial overlap between GALP proposals and these masks. Grade-discriminative features in DR grading can include vascular caliber changes, optic disc appearance, and global texture — not only discrete lesions. The lesion-proposal framing is asserted, not demonstrated. A simple IoU analysis on MFIDDR would directly confirm or refine this claim.

- **"Ours (with lesion)" is foregrounded as the headline result despite being the annotation-dependent variant.** The paper's stated contribution is annotation-free grading, yet Table 1 bolds "Ours (with lesion)" as the top row, and Section 4.2 claims "establishing new SOTA performance on this benchmark" via this variant. The annotation-free result (83.9%) is the actual contribution and should receive equivalent or primary emphasis; the lesion-informed variant is better framed as an upper bound or optional extension.

- **Cyclic adjacent-view pairing in LGRF is unjustified.** Section 3.3 hardcodes cross-view fusion so view *i* attends to view *i*+1 (mod N). For four-view fundus imaging, the clinical relationships between fields are not inherently cyclic. No ablation compares this to attending all views or learning which view to attend to. This is an arbitrary design choice that should at minimum be acknowledged and ideally ablated.

### Trivial

- **Early training dynamics of CAM-based proposals are not discussed.** Since GEMs are derived from the predicted grade (Eq. 3), early in training when predictions are unreliable, proposals may be noisy. Whether a warm-up schedule is needed is not mentioned, though this is unlikely to affect final results significantly.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation in this paper is that stage-wise auxiliary classification (applied at all four encoder stages, not just the final layer) can generate sufficiently discriminative grade-conditioned evidence maps to serve as lesion surrogates in cross-view attention — enabling annotation-free performance competitive with models that use explicit lesion segmentation masks. If verified by spatial overlap analysis, this would establish a practical principle: in DR grading, internal grade-supervision signal is a viable substitute for external annotation signal for the purpose of cross-view lesion alignment. The MoE routing conditioned on the *current* view's context (rather than static routing) is also a clean and practically motivated design choice for asymmetric multi-view scenarios.

## Suggestions

1. **Add ablation row that decouples auxiliary loss from Top-K selection**: retain GALP's auxiliary loss but set α=1.0 (all tokens to LGRF). This single row resolves the main evidential gap without re-running all experiments.
2. **Compute spatial overlap (IoU or Dice) between GALP proposals and MFIDDR lesion masks** at multiple training checkpoints (e.g., epoch 1, 10, final). This verifies the lesion-proposal claim and adds interpretability.
3. **Reframe "Ours (with lesion)"** as an optional "oracle extension" in Table 1 (e.g., a separate panel or footnote) so the annotation-free variant receives emphasis as the primary result.
4. **Report results as mean ± std** over ≥3 random seeds for Table 3 (DRTiD), where the margin over CrossFiT is 0.4%.
