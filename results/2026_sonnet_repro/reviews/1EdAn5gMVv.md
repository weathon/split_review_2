## Summary

SpatialBoost is a training framework that injects 3D spatial knowledge into pre-trained vision encoders (DINOv2, DINOv3, SigLIPv2, OpenCLIP) by converting depth-estimated and segmentation-derived geometric information into a hierarchical multi-turn Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset, then fine-tuning the encoder through a frozen LLM decoder with a dual-channel attention mechanism designed to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The method is evaluated across monocular depth estimation, semantic segmentation, 3D scene understanding (Lexicon3D), vision-based robot control (CortexBench), image classification, and retrieval benchmarks, yielding consistently positive results across all tested backbones.

---

## Strengths

- **Broad, consistent empirical improvements across four encoders and six evaluation domains.** Every tested encoder improves on every benchmark, including non-spatial tasks: DINOv3's depth RMSE on NYUd drops from 0.31 to 0.25, ADE20K segmentation mIoU rises from 55.9% to 59.7%, robot learning average rises from 72.8 to 80.8, and ImageNet linear probing rises from 88.4% to 90.2% (Tables 1–5). The breadth rules out cherry-picking.

- **Dual-channel attention demonstrably prevents catastrophic forgetting.** Figure 6 directly compares fine-tuning approaches: full fine-tuning drops classification from 86.3% to 79.5%, LoRA drops it to 83.7%, while dual-channel attention raises it to 87.6% — a clear, concrete mechanistic advantage shown on the same encoder and evaluation protocol.

- **LLM-based supervision outperforms direct pixel-level decoders (Table 6).** For DINOv2-ViT-L/14, the LLM decoder achieves classification 88.3% (+2.32%), segmentation 51.5 mIoU (+7.97%), and depth RMSE 0.32 (-15.79%), consistently outperforming linear depth, linear segmentation, SAM decoder, and VGGT decoder on all four metrics. This concretely supports the claim that language encoding provides richer supervision than pixel-level alternatives.

- **Dataset scalability is empirically validated.** Figure 5 shows monotonic improvement in depth (AbsRel, RMSE) and segmentation (mIoU) as training data grows from 50K to 300K for both SigLIPv2 and DINOv3, suggesting headroom with more data.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Potential training/evaluation data overlap on Lexicon3D 3D tasks.** Section 4.1 states that the multi-view VQA dataset is constructed from "3D dataset (Jensen et al., 2014; Dai et al., 2017; Mildenhall et al., 2021; Barron et al., 2022)," which includes ScanNet (Dai et al., 2017). The Lexicon3D benchmark (Table 3) evaluates on ScanNet-derived tasks: ScanQA, SQA3D, ScanRefer, and 3D semantic understanding on ScanNet scenes. The paper does not state that training and evaluation scenes are disjoint. The concern is acute because the improvement for OpenCLIP on 3D semantic understanding mIoU jumps from 6.9 to 54.9 — a near-8x increase that is difficult to attribute solely to general spatial reasoning transfer. If ScanNet scenes in the multi-view VQA training split overlap with Lexicon3D test/evaluation splits, the Table 3 results would partly reflect memorized scene content rather than generalizable spatial understanding. Explicit confirmation that the ScanNet training/test scene splits are respected (or a re-evaluation with held-out splits) is necessary to trust the magnitude of these gains.

- **Missing ablation isolating language-guided reasoning from implicit knowledge distillation.** The single-view CoT training data is generated by running Depth Pro (Bochkovskii et al., 2024) and SAM2 (Ravi et al., 2024) on SA1B images and encoding their outputs as language QA pairs. The downstream spatial evaluations (depth on NYUd/KITTI, segmentation on ADE20K/Pascal VOC) test probing heads on the same encoder features. The mechanism is therefore consistent with implicit feature distillation from Depth Pro and SAM2 into the encoder via language encoding — the LLM may act primarily as an information bottleneck, not as a reasoning facilitator. Table 6 compares LLM against pixel-level alternatives (linear, SAM, VGGT decoders) but uses different training data regimes for each condition, confounding decoder architecture with training signal quality. A direct ablation — e.g., training the encoder to regress Depth Pro's depth maps and SAM2's segmentation masks directly without language, matched on data volume — would isolate what the language-guided CoT component specifically contributes. Without this, the paper's central explanatory claim is not established.

### Minor

- **Classification and retrieval gains lack a mechanistic explanation.** Section 4.5 attributes the ImageNet and retrieval improvements to "dual-channel attention preserving pre-trained knowledge and the inclusion of general scene captions alongside spatial reasoning." But no ablation compares caption-only training (no spatial QA) versus full SpatialBoost. If SA1B images fine-tuned with rich GPT-4o captions alone produce similar classification gains, the spatial training signal is not driving these results. This matters because the paper uses classification/retrieval improvements as evidence that SpatialBoost "enhances general vision capabilities" — a claim that could alternatively be explained by general semantic fine-tuning on SA1B.

- **Table 7 ordering ablation overstates its conclusion.** The paper claims "reasoning order significantly impacts the quality of representation." The actual differences are small: depth RMSE 0.34 (forward) vs. 0.35 (reverse) vs. 0.36 (random), segmentation mIoU 48.9 vs. 48.4 vs. 48.5. No variance estimates are reported for this ablation. "Significantly impacts" is too strong for margins of this size without statistical support.

### Trivial

- **Dual-channel attention novelty not clearly scoped.** Figure 3's caption explicitly cites "(Hong et al., 2023a)" as the source of the dual-channel attention layer. The body text in Section 3.1 reads "we introduce a dual-channel attention mechanism," which could mislead readers into thinking this is a paper contribution. The sentence should clarify that this is an adopted technique.

## Novel Insights

The most practically interesting observation in SpatialBoost is that language-encoded spatial supervision — derived from specialized vision models run on 2D images — can improve pre-trained encoder representations across tasks far beyond the original supervision signal (e.g., robot control and instance retrieval). This suggests that language as a representation format may serve as a useful *compression and generalization* channel for domain-specific knowledge: encoding Depth Pro and SAM2 outputs as natural language and using them to supervise a ViT through an LLM appears to produce more general representations than the source models themselves provide. Whether this generality stems from the compositional and relational structure of language or from the distributional coverage of the SA1B/Ego4D images is not resolved by the paper — but the empirical pattern across all four tested encoders is consistent enough to motivate this as a hypothesis worth testing directly.

## Suggestions

1. **Explicitly confirm ScanNet scene splits.** State in Section 4.1 which ScanNet split was used for multi-view VQA construction, and verify that no test scenes from Lexicon3D appear in that split. If needed, re-run Table 3 with the official held-out split.

2. **Add a direct distillation ablation.** Train one variant of the encoder using direct regression supervision from Depth Pro's depth maps and SAM2's masks (no language encoding, matched data volume and training compute) and compare against full SpatialBoost. This single experiment would resolve or substantiate the paper's central causal claim.

3. **Add a caption-only ablation.** Train with only GPT-4o scene captions (no spatial QA) and report ImageNet and retrieval results. This isolates whether the classification/retrieval gains are driven by the spatial component or by general semantic fine-tuning on SA1B.

4. **Qualify the Table 7 claim.** Either report variance estimates or soften "reasoning order significantly impacts quality" to "forward hierarchical ordering tends to outperform reversed and random orderings."
