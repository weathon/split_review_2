## Summary
SpookyBench is a 451-video synthetic benchmark in which all meaningful content (text, object silhouettes, dynamic scenes) is encoded exclusively through opposing motion patterns in binary noise — individual frames look like random noise, but content becomes visible to human observers through motion-defined form (structure-from-motion) perception. The core finding is stark and reproducible: human observers achieve 98% accuracy on these stimuli while all 28+ tested Video-VLMs achieve exactly 0%, and fine-tuning directly on SpookyBench data does not help.

---

## Strengths

- **Clean, adversarial isolation of motion-defined form perception.** By design (Algorithms 1–2, Section 3.2), every pixel of every individual frame is uninformative noise; content exists only in the coherent temporal flow field. This eliminates spatial shortcuts that contaminate virtually all other video benchmarks, yielding a structurally pure test.

- **Universal, catastrophic failure across architectures and scales.** All 28 evaluated VLMs — spanning 2B to 78B parameters, open-source and closed-source, general-purpose and video-specialized (TimeChat, InternVideo2.5, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash) — score exactly 0% under both direct and chain-of-thought prompting (Table 1). The consistency is unambiguous.

- **Fine-tuning fails to recover accuracy.** After 10 epochs of training on 400 SpookyBench videos, InternVL2.5-8B and Qwen2-VL-7B still achieve 0% on a held-out test set (Section 4.4), ruling out an out-of-distribution data explanation and pointing toward an architectural deficit.

- **Rigorous frame-rate control experiment.** Table 4 and Table 5 show that humans need ≥20 FPS (95%+ accuracy) and fail at 1 FPS (0%), while VLMs fail at every tested frame rate (1–30 FPS). This explicitly rules out temporal subsampling as a sufficient explanation for VLM failure.

- **Deterministic, indefinitely extensible dataset generation.** The dataset is generated by fully specified algorithms with all hyperparameters published, enabling exact reproduction and unlimited scaling via the released generator.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Section 3.3.2 contains an irreconcilable internal inconsistency.** The section states "words exhibited negligible detection (~0%) below 2.5 dB SNR, but jumped to 85.7% accuracy above this threshold" — yet the data table accompanying Figure 4 shows accuracy values of exactly 0.00 below the threshold and 1.00 (100%) above it; the 85.7% figure appears nowhere in the data. More problematically, the section then states "Prompts performed best (40% accuracy), with Chain-of-Thought reasoning improving general identification tasks," language that implies a VLM prompting experiment. But Table 1 records 0% for every VLM under every condition, making a 40% reading impossible for any model. If Figure 4 depicts human detection (which the data curve shape suggests), then the figure title ("with direct prompting and chain of thought prompting") and the in-text reference to prompting strategies are wrong labels. If Figure 4 depicts model performance under some condition, it directly contradicts the paper's headline result. This inconsistency must be resolved with explicit labeling and corrected text.

- **Scope overreach: motion-defined form perception ≠ temporal reasoning broadly.** The paper's title and central claim frame SpookyBench as a test of "temporal understanding" generally, yet the benchmark exclusively requires one specific capability: computing a dense optical-flow field and grouping pixels by flow direction (structure-from-motion / motion-defined form). This is a specific, low-level visual processing sub-skill. Current Video-VLMs demonstrably succeed at action recognition, event ordering, causal reasoning, and temporal grounding — they are not globally "time-blind." The claim that current architectures "lack the architectural mechanisms to process information conveyed through temporal patterns regardless of temporal resolution" (Section 4.3) is too broad; the evidence supports only that they cannot perform motion-defined form perception. This over-generalization risks misdirecting the community about what capability actually needs to be built.

### Minor

- **Effective frame count received by each VLM at "30 FPS" is not documented.** A 7-second video at 30 FPS yields ~210 frames. Most VLMs impose token budgets that internally subsample to 8–32 frames, effectively 1–5 FPS — the range at which humans also fail (Table 4). The paper states models were tested "using identical temporal downsampling" (Section 4.3) but does not report how many frames each model actually ingested at the 30 FPS setting. If models were silently subsampled to ≤5 FPS, the frame-rate experiment would be confounded. The fine-tuning result partially mitigates this concern, but documenting the actual per-model frame count for the FPS sweep would make the main control experiment self-contained.

- **Fine-tuning analysis lacks training-accuracy reporting.** Section 4.4 notes that fine-tuned models "produced outputs that mimicked training examples without correctly identifying test patterns," but does not report training-set accuracy. If fine-tuned models memorize training labels (training accuracy → 100%, test accuracy = 0%), that is a strong evidence of perception-free memorization. If training accuracy is also 0%, that is a different and perhaps even stronger finding about architectural inability. Either way, the distinction would sharpen the architectural conclusion considerably.

- **Abstract states "15 state-of-the-art Video-VLMs" but Table 1 contains 27.** This creates a factual mismatch between the abstract and the actual scope of the evaluation.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation in the review synthesis is the evidential wedge created by the *combination* of the frame-rate experiment and the fine-tuning experiment: the frame-rate experiment shows that 1 FPS is insufficient *for humans*, so VLMs at 0% across all FPS are not merely operating at an insufficient rate — they are architecturally missing the motion-grouping computation entirely. The fine-tuning experiment then eliminates the data-distribution alternative explanation. Together, these two experiments create unusually tight triangulation on an architectural claim for a benchmark paper, even if the fine-tuning analysis itself needs the training-accuracy detail to be fully convincing.
