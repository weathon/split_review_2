## Summary

The paper proposes **Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE)**, a directional metric that quantifies per-component recoverability in contrastive time-series embeddings, and an **Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW)** mechanism that re-scales CoST's seasonal and trend contrastive losses using the observed SDE asymmetry factor Δ. A small MLP fusion layer is also introduced to model nonlinear trend-seasonal interactions. The motivation—that dominant components suppress weaker ones during contrastive training—is validated diagnostically via synthetic ablations (Table 1). The method is then evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather forecasting benchmarks.

---

## Strengths

- **Principled synthetic diagnostic (Table 1):** The SDE analysis on a TS2Vec encoder, varying the amplitude ratio *r* across nine conditions, provides clear quantitative evidence of semantic imbalance. The asymmetry factor Δ varies monotonically with *r*, directly supporting the hypothesis that dominant components suppress the recoverability of weaker ones. This is the most rigorous piece of evidence in the paper.

- **Useful ablation against naive regularization (Table 2):** The paper demonstrates that direct SDE regularization on TS2Vec degrades performance on all tested horizons and datasets. This motivates the adaptive weighting approach over a simpler regularization penalty, and is a meaningful negative result.

- **Lightweight plug-in design:** The weighting mechanism in Eq. (3) re-scales existing CoST losses and does not require new encoder architectures. The MLP fusion is small. The design is practical in principle.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

- **The headline claim is directly contradicted by Table 3.** Section 4.4.4 states: *"This combination leads to consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy compared with both TS2Vec and vanilla CoST baselines."* Table 3 directly refutes this on the Electricity dataset—described in Section 4.1 as having "strong calendar-driven periodicities," the paper's primary stated use case—at horizons 168, 336, and 720:
  - Horizon 168: CoST+APW 0.566/0.450 vs. CoST 0.425/0.405 vs. TS2Vec 0.429/0.392. The proposed method is ~33% worse than CoST on MSE.
  - Horizon 336: CoST+APW 0.677/0.566 vs. best TNC 0.548/0.466. The degradation is ~18%.
  - Horizon 720: CoST+APW 1.010/0.789 vs. TS2Vec 0.863/0.651. The proposed method is ~17% worse on MSE.
  - Similarly, on Weather at horizons 336 and 720, CoST+APW is the worst method by MSE.

  This is not a marginal exception. The proposed method systematically underperforms all baselines on longer horizons for two of three real-world datasets. The failure is worst on Electricity—precisely the dataset most associated with the paper's motivation—which means the method may actively harm representations where it should matter most. The conclusion as written cannot stand, and this failure mode receives no discussion, no qualification, and no explanation anywhere in the paper.

- **SDE values are never measured for the proposed method on real data.** Table 3 is described as reporting "the SDE metrics and forecasting performance" (Section 4.4.4), yet the table contains only MSE and MAE. The claim that CoST+APW achieves "consistently lower SDE values" has zero evidentiary support beyond the synthetic analysis in Table 1, which is computed on a TS2Vec encoder—not on CoST+APW. The SDE metric is the paper's primary novel contribution; demonstrating that the training method actually reduces SDE on real data is a non-negotiable burden of proof that is never met.

### Major

- **No ablation isolating the APW contribution.** Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP $g_\phi$ trained jointly with CoST, and Section 4.4.3 introduces the asymmetric weighting. The comparison in Table 3 is between CoST+APW (MLP + weighting) and vanilla CoST (no MLP, no weighting). There is no CoST+MLP-only row. Any gains over vanilla CoST cannot be attributed to the asymmetric weighting mechanism; they could be entirely from the additional parameters and training signal provided by the MLP. This is not a minor gap—it is the core ablation needed to validate the paper's stated mechanism.

- **Abstract is inconsistent with the method.** The abstract states: *"Our approach can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes."* Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP $g_\phi$ that is trained jointly with CoST and adds composite embedding computation—an architectural addition. This is a direct contradiction.

- **SDE computation during training is underspecified.** Eq. (3) requires computing Δ online from the encoder's own embeddings. The paper never specifies: Is Δ computed per-batch, per-epoch, or with a running average? Does it use the MLP composite embedding $g_\phi([v(\mathbf{a})\|v(\mathbf{b})])$ or the raw CoST pooled embeddings? Since the MLP is trained jointly, Δ shifts throughout training—does this create gradient conflicts or instability? This is a reproducibility gap that prevents independent validation.

### Minor

- **Three inconsistent names for the same metric.** The metric is called "Semantic Disentanglement Error" in the title, "Semantic Separability Error" in Section 3.2, and "Semantic Decomposition Error" in Sections 4.1, 4.3, and 5. All three use the acronym SDE. This inconsistency pervades the paper and creates unnecessary confusion about whether these refer to the same concept.

- **Table 3 has a duplicate ETT1 label.** The first group of ETT1 rows uses horizons {24, 48, 168, 336, 720} (hourly resolution, consistent with ETT1h), while the second group labeled "ETT1" uses horizons {24, 48, 96, 288, 672} (consistent with ETTm1 minutely resolution). These are different datasets; mislabeling them both as "ETT1" undermines the table's credibility and makes it unclear which datasets were actually used.

- **Hyperparameters γ and γ' are uncharacterized.** These control the strength of asymmetric reweighting and appear in Eq. (3), but the values used in experiments are never reported. Given that Δ ranges from approximately −1.3 to +1.1 (Table 1), the scaling effect is not negligible.

- **Future work direction is inconsistent with the current method.** Section 5 states the authors "plan to examine whether asymmetry-aware weighting remains effective when the decomposition is approximated using only low-pass filtering, without explicit frequency-domain encoders." However, Section 4.3 describes extracting trend and periodic components via a **low-pass filter** for the SDE computation, which is exactly this scenario. The planned future direction partially describes the current method.

### Trivial

- **The wav2vec citation for "vector arithmetic in word embeddings."** Section 3.2 cites Baevski et al. (2020) (wav2vec 2.0, a speech model) to motivate the vector arithmetic interpretation of SDE, which is an odd attribution. The word2vec paper (Mikolov et al., 2013) is the standard reference for this property and is cited elsewhere in the paper.

## Novel Insights

The SDE diagnostic framework—measuring component recoverability via cosine similarity in embedding space and tracking its asymmetry—is a meaningful analytical tool for auditing contrastive time-series encoders. The synthetic validation in Table 1 is genuinely informative, showing that TS2Vec's embedding space is not linearly decomposable even under balanced signal conditions. The insight that direct SDE regularization degrades performance (Table 2) is a useful negative result that motivates the adaptive weighting approach on theoretical grounds. However, the central empirical loop is never closed: the paper shows SDE is a problem with TS2Vec, but never shows that CoST+APW actually reduces SDE or reliably improves forecasting, especially on the datasets most motivated by the diagnostic.
