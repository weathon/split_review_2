## Summary
This paper proposes an end-to-end multi-view DR grading framework that self-generates lesion-like proposals via CAM-based grade activation (GALP) and fuses them across views using a gated mixture-of-experts mechanism with cross-attention (LGRF). The method is technically coherent, fully self-contained at inference, and achieves competitive results on two benchmarks (MFIDDR and DRTiD), matching or exceeding several externally-informed baselines.

## Strengths
- **Technically coherent and well-designed architecture.** GALP (auxiliary classifiers → CAM-style evidence maps → Top-K region selection) and LGRF (MoE gating conditioned on the current view, cross-attention on adjacent-view proposals) are clearly described, theoretically sound, and complementary. The pipeline is self-contained at inference, which is a genuine practical advantage (Sec. 3.2–3.3).
- **Competitive empirical results on two datasets.** On MFIDDR, the lesion-free variant (83.9% Acc, 70.9% Kappa) matches or exceeds several externally informed methods (CVSA, LFMVDR, SMVDR-W). On DRTiD, it achieves the highest overall accuracy (76.0%), narrowly edging CrossFIT (75.6%). These results are credible and suggest the approach has practical merit (Tables 1, 3).
- **Clean ablation that controls for backbone.** The ablation (Table 4) varies only the proposed modules while keeping Swin-B constant, isolating their contributions. The consistent drops (1.2–1.6 points) confirm both modules contribute positively.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **No empirical validation that GALP proposals correspond to actual lesions.** The paper's central claim is that GALP generates "lesion proposals" that act as "surrogates for expert cues" (lines 41, 91–99, 282). However, GALP produces *grade-predictive* regions via CAM — spatial locations whose activation most increases the predicted grade logit. This is not the same as identifying lesions; the model could attend to spurious correlates of disease severity. The paper provides **zero quantitative analysis** of whether these proposals overlap with actual lesion regions, despite the MFIDDR dataset releasing lesion segmentation masks (line 185). Without this validation (e.g., Dice overlap between proposals and lesion masks vs. random baselines), the "lesion proposal" claim is an unsubstantiated leap. This matters because the paper's motivation and narrative (title, abstract, Sec. 1) rest on these being lesion surrogates. If they are merely grade-correlated regions, the method still works as an attention mechanism, but the framing is misleading.

- **No statistical significance or variance reporting anywhere.** No standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multi-run statistics are reported for any result (Tables 1–4, Fig. 3). Performance differences against the strongest baselines are very small (0.4% on DRTiD; 0.3% below WGLIN on MFIDDR), and the ablation drops are modest (1.2–1.6 points). Without variance estimates, the reader cannot assess whether these differences are reliable or within noise. This undermines comparative claims like "surpasses" and "outperforms" for the tightest comparisons.

### Minor
- **The annotation-cost framing conflates doctor annotations with automatically-generated maps.** The paper characterizes externally informed baselines as requiring "costly annotations" and "doctor-provided annotations" (lines 38–40, 65). However, on MFIDDR, the comparator methods (WGLIN, SMVDR, LFMVDR) use lesion maps *generated by an automated segmentation model* (line 185), not clinician annotations. The paper's own "with lesion" variant uses these same machine-generated masks via SPADE. The practical distinction is about inference-time model dependency (a valid point), not annotation cost. The rhetorical framing exaggerates the burden difference.

- **Backbone differences confound headline comparisons with end-to-end baselines.** The proposed method uses Swin-B; many baselines use ResNet50, VGG19, or custom architectures. The ablation (Table 4) controls for backbone and shows only 1.2–1.6 point gains, suggesting that some of the headline advantage over end-to-end baselines may partly reflect backbone strength. The DRTiD comparison is cleaner (both use fundus-pretrained backbones), but the margin is only 0.4%.

- **Hyperparameter selection is unclear; no validation set mentioned.** The paper uses the official 70/30 split with no mention of a held-out validation set. It is unclear whether hyperparameters (α, K₂, M) were selected on the test set or via some other procedure.

- **Adjacent-view-only fusion is not justified or ablated.** LGRF fuses each view only with its cyclic adjacent view (view i with view i+1 mod N; line 123). For four-view MFIDDR, this restricts information flow compared to all-pairs fusion. The paper neither justifies this design choice nor ablates it.

- **Missing ablation isolating the auxiliary loss from proposal selection.** The "w/o GALP" ablation removes both the auxiliary loss and the proposal selection together. It does not include a condition that keeps the auxiliary loss but replaces GALP's proposals with all tokens, making it unclear whether the gain comes from the auxiliary supervision or the proposal selection mechanism.
