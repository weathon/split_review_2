## Summary

This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free framework for class-agnostic object counting that leverages SAM's output tokens (rather than image embeddings) as the verification signal. It consists of two components: a probabilistic prompt generation stage that samples point prompts guided by a similarity-derived probability distribution, and an output token-based verification stage that uses the TS-SS metric to distinguish positive from negative instances directly in SAM's decoder token space. The method requires no training and no additional models beyond SAM.

## Strengths

- **Genuinely novel use of SAM's output tokens as the verification signal.** Prior training-free SAM-based counting methods (TFOC, C-Anything, etc.) operate in image embedding space, computing cosine similarity between cropped features. The idea that SAM's decoder output tokens—which integrate positional and semantic information through bidirectional attention—could serve directly as a verification signal is creative and well-motivated. The PCA visualization (Figure 5) provides supporting evidence that output tokens separate positive from negative instances more cleanly, and Table 5 ablates this directly (output tokens MAE 16.25 vs. image embeddings MAE 16.94).

- **Strong CARPK results.** MAE of 4.68 outperforms all training-based methods and all training-free methods except TFCAC (4.39), while using only SAM without additional encoders. This is a concrete, non-trivial result on a structured real-world benchmark.

- **The probabilistic prompt generation with iterative refinement is well-motivated.** Moving beyond fixed grids or superpixels to a sampling strategy guided by a similarity-derived probability distribution, with suppression of verified regions, is principled. The ablation in Table 4 shows clear monotonic improvement as iterations and prompts increase (MAE 29.10→16.25).

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The abstract makes an unqualified claim contradicted by the paper's own Table 1.** The abstract states the method "achieves superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." However, Table 1 shows six methods with better FSC-147 MAE: BMNet+ (14.62), SAFECOUNT (14.32), PseCo (13.05), LOCA (10.79), and TFCAC (12.26). The claim is only accurate within the "SAM only" subcategory. The paper partially hedges in Section 4.2 ("best performance among training-free SAM only methods"), but this qualification is entirely absent from the abstract, making it a misrepresentation of the paper's actual standing.

- **The FSC-147 RMSE (133.11) is dramatically higher than comparable methods and essentially undiscussed.** TFCAC achieves RMSE 56.33 and LOCA achieves 56.97—both roughly 2.3× better. An RMSE-to-MAE ratio of ~8:1 indicates catastrophic failures on a subset of images. The paper only briefly mentions RMSE in the one-shot section (line 232), calling it "higher than some training-based methods," which is a substantial understatement. This omission prevents readers from assessing the method's reliability.

- **Key implementation details needed for reproduction are missing:** (a) The temperature parameter τ in Equation 1 is never given a numeric value; the "t-scale factor of 0.066" is described as a SAM predictor configuration (line 180) but not explicitly tied to τ. (b) The exemplar selection protocol for FSC-147 (number of exemplars, number of random trials) is not stated, unlike CARPK where "12 random objects" is specified. (c) The TS-SS verification threshold of 300 is presented with no sensitivity analysis or justification. (d) When verified positives are added to the exemplar list, how the TS-SS similarity is computed against multiple exemplars (mean pooling, max pooling, voting) is not described. (e) No variance or confidence intervals are reported despite stochasticity from multinomial sampling and random exemplar selection.

### Minor

- **The claimed advantage of TS-SS over cosine similarity is marginal (MAE 16.48 → 16.25, a 1.4% relative improvement) with no statistical grounding.** The paper calls this a "breakthrough" that "breaks through the barrier" of non-hybrid metrics (line 275), which is unwarranted for a 0.23 MAE difference that could easily arise from random sampling noise given the lack of variance estimates.

- **The probabilistic prompt generation is not compared against a simple deterministic baseline** (e.g., uniform grid or superpixels) under the same verification method. The current design confounds the contribution of the sampling strategy with the verification contribution.

- **The paper claims computational efficiency advantages but provides only its own runtime (1.69 s/image on FSC-147) without comparable numbers for TFOC, TFCAC, C-Anything, or other methods.** The claim that TFCAC is "more than twice as fast" is stated qualitatively with no supporting table.

### Trivial

None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Provide a per-category or per-density breakdown of where output tokens outperform image embeddings (beyond the aggregate Table 5 result), to give mechanistic insight.
- Characterize failure modes: identify images where the method produces errors of hundreds of objects and determine whether the root cause is SAM mask quality, sampling misses, or verification threshold issues.
- Perform a hyperparameter sensitivity study for the TS-SS threshold and temperature τ.

## Removed Points

- "C-Anything uses CLIP (debatable under SAM-only taxonomy)" — not verifiable from the paper text; the paper's own categorization is what the authors intended.
- "Alt-text contradicts itself on PCA columns" — this is a parser artifact from figure alt-text extraction, not a paper error.
- "Method still computes cosine similarity in prompt generation, undercutting 'bypass uncertainty' claim" — the claim about bypassing similarity uncertainty refers to the verification stage, not prompt generation. Minor overclaim at worst.
- "Missing appendix content / proofs" — parser strips the appendix; content exists in the original submission.
- "Speculation that TS-SS mapping to output token space 'is actually valid' is unverified" — the paper provides Table 3 and Figure 5 as evidence; this concern is too vague to retain as a specific weakness.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The key insight—that SAM's output tokens can serve as a cleaner verification signal than image embeddings for training-free counting—is already well-articulated in the paper and confirmed by the PCA visualization and ablation. The reviews do not surface an interpretation the authors missed.

## Suggestions

1. **Correct the abstract** to reflect that the method achieves best performance among SAM-only training-free methods, not all methods.
2. **Add a failure-mode analysis** discussing the high RMSE and characterizing when the method fails catastrophically.
3. **Provide all missing hyperparameters** (τ, FSC-147 exemplar selection protocol, exemplar aggregation method) and add a sensitivity analysis for the TS-SS threshold (300).
4. **Report standard deviations or confidence intervals** for all main results.
5. **Add a direct comparison** of probabilistic vs. grid-based prompt generation under the same verification method.
