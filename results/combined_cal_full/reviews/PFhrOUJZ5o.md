## Summary

This paper introduces LAION-Comp, a large-scale dataset of 540K+ aesthetic images with detailed scene graph annotations (objects, attributes, relations) generated by GPT-4o with partial human verification. The authors train four baseline SG-to-image models (SDXL-SG, SD3.5-SG, FLUX-SG, plus SG-Adapter) on this dataset and evaluate them on a newly introduced benchmark (CompSGen Bench) as well as existing benchmarks. The central claim is that large-scale structural annotations are crucial for advancing compositional generation.

## Strengths

- **The scale and purpose of LAION-Comp are well-motivated and fill a genuine gap.** Existing scene-graph datasets (Visual Genome ~108K, COCO-Stuff ~164K) are small and spatially skewed. LAION-Comp covers 540K+ aesthetic images with rich structural annotations via GPT-4o, a genuinely new scale for this domain. The analysis in Section 3.2 (non-spatial relations 77.48% vs. 41.98% in VG) makes a concrete case that this dataset captures a broader and more challenging semantic range than prior SG datasets. This dataset alone would be a meaningful community resource.

- **The ablation on data proportion (Table 4) is properly controlled.** Keeping total training iterations fixed while varying data size from 10% to 100% isolates the effect of data diversity/volume, not compute budget. The monotonic improvements across all four metrics support the claim that more structural data helps.

- **The paper trains baseline models across multiple backbones (diffusion and flow-matching), demonstrating adaptability.** The paper shows the dataset works with SDXL, SD3.5, and FLUX architectures, going beyond a single model evaluation.

- **The paper reports some evaluation beyond its own benchmark.** Results on T2I-CompBench and COCO CLIP scores (Sec. A.6) and a user study (Sec. A.3) provide some independent evidence, though these are deferred to the appendix.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The primary evaluation benchmark (CompSGen Bench) shares the same image pool and annotation schema as the training set, conflating data quality with schema familiarity.** CompSGen Bench is constructed by filtering the LAION-Comp test set (same LAION-Aesthetics source as training) for samples with >4 relations. The SG-IoU/Entity-IoU/Relation-IoU metrics measure overlap with LAION-Comp's specific annotation format (relation verbs, attribute types). Models trained on LAION-Comp inherently know this schema, while models trained on VG or COCO use different vocabularies (e.g., VG has 58% spatial relations vs. LAION-Comp's 22.5%). The paper partially mitigates this with T2I-CompBench results (appendix) and COCO CLIP scores, but the headline results and main paper's strongest claims rest on CompSGen Bench, making it difficult to disentangle whether superior performance comes from data quality or schema familiarity. The SG-IoU gap between SDXL-SG trained on LAION-Comp vs. VG (0.558 vs. 0.546) is particularly thin given this confound, though Entity-IoU (0.884 vs. 0.813) and Relation-IoU (0.856 vs. 0.800) show larger gaps. (*Verifiable from Section 3.3 and Table 2.*)

### Minor

- **No variance or significance reporting.** Tables 2–4 report single-point estimates with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or multiple-seed results. Differences as small as 0.005 in SG-IoU or 0.4 in FID are discussed without any indication of whether they are statistically meaningful. Given that FID can vary by several points across seeds and the IoU-based metrics depend on an automated scene-graph parser (Sec. A.2), this is a significant omission for a benchmark paper. (*Verifiable from Tables 2, 3, 4.*)

- **Overclaim on FID comparison.** Line 285 states "our baseline achieves the best performance among all candidates in both image quality and accuracy." In Table 2, SDXL (T2I, no SG) achieves FID 19.3 while SDXL-SG on LAION-Comp achieves FID 20.1. The claim is inaccurate for FID if "all candidates" includes T2I models. While the paper acknowledges fine-tuning increases FID (lines 281-282), the headline claim on line 285 is inconsistent with the presented data. (*Verifiable at lines 285-286 and Table 2.*)

- **Image editing is framed as a core contribution but has zero supporting evidence in the main paper.** The editing framework (Sec. A.1) and its results (Sec. A.1.2, A.1.3) are entirely deferred to the appendix. The abstract and introduction advertise "remarkable effectiveness and controllability" and "powerful editing potential" for editing, yet a reader of the main paper cannot evaluate these claims. While this is partially explained by space constraints, the mismatch between the paper's framing and its presented evidence is a coherence issue. (*Verifiable: editing content is cross-referenced entirely to Sec. A.1, which is in the stripped appendix.*)

### Trivial

- **The partial human verification (line 169) is underspecified in the main text.** The paper reports 98.8%/97.5%/95.7% accuracy for objects/attributes/relations but does not state the sample size or selection method, making it difficult to assess reliability from the main paper alone. Details are deferred to Sec. A.5. (*Verifiable at line 169.*)

A cross-schema evaluation (train on LAION-Comp, test on VG's schema, and vice versa) would resolve this ambiguity.
