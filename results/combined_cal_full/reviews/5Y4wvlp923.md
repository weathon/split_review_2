## Summary

This paper identifies the problem of semantic imbalance in contrastive time-series representation learning, where dominant temporal components (e.g., trend) suppress weaker but semantically important ones (e.g., seasonality). It introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE), a directional metric to quantify this imbalance, and proposes Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW), which uses SDE-derived asymmetry to dynamically reweight contrastive losses in the CoST framework. The SDE diagnostic is cleanly validated on synthetic data. However, the experimental evidence does not support the broad claims made in the abstract and conclusion, the theoretical grounding is disconnected from the practical implementation, and a missing ablation prevents attribution of results to the proposed mechanism.

## Strengths

- **Well-motivated and validated diagnostic metric.** The SDE is introduced with a clear synthetic validation (Table 1): when trend and periodic components have unequal amplitudes in signals encoded by TS2Vec, SDE values are asymmetric, and Δ correctly tracks the direction of imbalance. This is the paper's cleanest result and a genuinely useful diagnostic tool for the community.

- **The problem is real and clearly identified.** Semantic imbalance—where contrastive objectives bias representations toward high-amplitude components at the expense of weaker but semantically important ones—is a genuine limitation of methods like TS2Vec. The paper articulates this well and provides a synthetic benchmark to measure it.

- **Conceptually appealing feedback loop.** Using a diagnostic metric (SDE/Δ) to directly adjust loss weights is a simple and principled idea. The pluggable framing (no architectural changes to CoST) is a practical virtue if the mechanism works.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Claims-evidence mismatch.** The abstract claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" and the conclusion claims "improved forecasting accuracy across multiple benchmarks." Table 3 tells a different story:
   - **Electricity:** CoST+APW is substantially *worse* than baselines at long horizons (168: 0.566 MSE vs. best 0.412; 336: 0.677 vs. 0.548; 720: 1.010 vs. 0.859).
   - **Weather:** CoST+APW never achieves the best MSE at any horizon (e.g., 336: 0.266 vs. TNC's 0.215; 720: 0.299 vs. TNC's 0.219).
   - Gains are confined to the ETT family. This pattern is never acknowledged, discussed, or explained in the paper. A reader relying on the abstract and conclusion would be misled about the method's effectiveness.

2. **Theoretical-practical disconnect in SDE computation.** Section 3.2 defines SDE on *ground-truth signal components* using a single encoder: SDE_{a,b} = 1 − cos(v(a+b) − v(b), v(a)). In Section 4.4, the actual computation uses separate encoders V_T and V_S that both receive the *full signal* x, and v(a+b) comes from an MLP fusion g_ϕ([v(a)||v(b)]). These are fundamentally different objects, and the claimed theoretical grounding for the SDE as a measure of "component recoverability" no longer holds in the practical setting. The method may still work as a heuristic, but the paper's theoretical foundation is unsupported.

3. **Missing ablation separating MLP fusion from asymmetric weighting.** Section 4.4.2 introduces an MLP fusion layer; Section 4.4.3 introduces the asymmetric weighting. Table 3 only compares CoST+APW (both changes together) vs. vanilla CoST. Without ablations for "CoST + MLP only" and "CoST + weighting only," improvements cannot be attributed to the weighting mechanism specifically—the MLP could independently add useful capacity or capture nonlinear interactions.

### Minor

- **No uncertainty quantification.** All results are single-point estimates with no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or statistical tests. Given small margins between methods (e.g., MSE differences of 0.001–0.005 on ETT), it is impossible to judge whether reported improvements are meaningful.

- **SDE values promised but absent from Table 3.** The text states "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance," but only MSE/MAE are shown. Without SDE values, there is no direct evidence that the weighting actually reduces semantic imbalance as intended.

- **No sensitivity analysis for γ, γ′.** These hyperparameters control how aggressively the weights adjust; the method's sensitivity to them is unreported.

- **Inconsistent naming of SDE.** The acronym is spelled out as "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.3)—three different names for the same term.

- **Incorrect citation.** The paper credits Baevski et al. (2020) (wav2vec 2.0) as the inspiration for "vector arithmetic in word embeddings"; the correct reference would be Mikolov et al. (2013).

- **Inflated contribution framing.** Contribution 3 lists "frequency-domain mechanisms" as part of the proposed framework without clearly delineating that these are inherited from CoST. While CoST is cited, the framing could mislead about the scope of novelty.

### Trivial
None beyond the minor issues above.

## Suggestions

1. Revise the abstract and conclusion to honestly characterize the method's performance profile: it helps on datasets with clear trend/seasonality structure (ETT family) but can degrade performance on others (Electricity long horizons, Weather).
2. Add ablation experiments separating the MLP fusion layer from the asymmetric weighting mechanism.
3. Report SDE values alongside forecasting metrics to demonstrate that weighting reduces semantic imbalance.
4. Report standard deviations over multiple runs.
5. Clarify the relationship between the theoretical SDE formulation (Section 3.2) and its practical computation on CoST's learned embeddings (Section 4.4).
