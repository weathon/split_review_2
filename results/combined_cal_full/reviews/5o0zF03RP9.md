## Summary

This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL with a KL(predicted||preferred) incentive shaping term, and claims as its "central novelty" a Bayesian scheme for adapting the trade-off parameter β online. The core intuition — penalizing the divergence between what an agent predicts will happen (p) and what it prefers to happen (q) — is conceptually clean. However, the paper's central claimed contribution (Bayesian β adaptation) is never specified as a repeatable procedure, and the experimental evaluation is far too thin to support the paper's broad claims.

## Strengths

1. **Clear and well-motivated core intuition.** The idea of rewarding agents for aligning predicted outcomes with preferred ones via KL(p||q) is communicated effectively (Section 3.2), providing a natural dense signal in sparse-reward settings.

2. **Toy experiment cleanly validates the basic mechanism.** The 2-state MDP (Section 5.2, 7 seeds) transparently shows that adding the KL penalty at any β>0 produces faster convergence and lower variance compared to β=0, without confounding factors from learned p or complex environments.

3. **Honest discussion of failure modes.** Section 6.2 openly acknowledges preference misalignment, KL dominance, and latent mismatch as potential failure modes, demonstrating awareness of the framework's limitations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

1. **Bayesian β adaptation — the paper's claimed "central novelty" — is never specified.** The abstract states: "The central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable, updated online." The contribution list (Section 1) repeats this. Yet the paper never describes what Bayesian model is being used: no prior on β, no likelihood function, no posterior update rule, and no definition of what constitutes a "round." Figures 3, 4 and Table 3 show posterior statistics, but the mechanism that produced them is absent from the paper. A reader cannot reproduce this method, and the claimed contribution cannot be evaluated. This is a structural omission that invalidates the paper's headline claim as presented.

### Major

2. **How p(o|s,a) is obtained is not specified for the main experiments.** For MountainCar (continuous state space, Section 5.3) and MiniGrid (Section 5.4), the paper says only that "p(o|s,a) is the predicted outcome distribution" with no description of the architecture, training procedure, loss function, or how KL(p||q) is computed tractably over a continuous outcome space. Section 4.3 discusses latent Gaussian representations in principle, but the actual implementation for the experiments is never given. Without these details, the method is underspecified.

3. **The β convergence data contradicts the paper's narrative.** The paper claims the posterior mean "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β≈0.1)" (Section 5.4, Figure 3 caption). However, Table 3 shows the posterior mean at Round 4 is **0.0173** — a factor of 5 away from 0.1. The figure caption itself states the mean "decreases to about 0.02 by Round 4." Additionally, the key claim that Bayesian adaptation "achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" is relegated to "additional runs (not shown)" (Section 5.4). Results central to the paper's thesis should be presented, not hidden.

4. **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** The paper compares its method only against β=0 (standard RL without any intrinsic motivation) on both MountainCar and MiniGrid. No comparison is made against any existing intrinsic motivation method (curiosity [Pathak et al. 2017], RND [Burda et al. 2018], ICM, or any other exploration bonus) despite discussing these methods in the Related Work. Non-toy experiments use only 3 seeds. Only two non-trivial environments are tested. The paper claims to address sparse-reward exploration but does not benchmark against the exploration methods it surveys.

### Minor

5. **The theoretical analysis is trivial.** Proposition 1 (for sufficiently small β the optimal policy is preserved) is a standard continuity observation that applies to any bounded reward perturbation — it does not leverage any property of the KL term or IncentRL specifically. Proposition 2 (as β→∞ the KL term dominates) is similarly trivial. Neither result provides meaningful insight into the dynamics of IncentRL or its behavior with adaptive β.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Compare against at least one existing intrinsic motivation method (curiosity, RND, etc.) to situate the KL-shaping mechanism against the literature.
- Run with more seeds (currently 3 for non-toy experiments) with confidence intervals.
- Include ablation studies on the KL direction (p||q vs. q||p) and different choices for q.
- Reconcile the β convergence narrative with the actual data: β≈0.02, not β≈0.1.

## Removed Points

These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:
- Criticisms about lack of ablation studies or insufficient statistical rigor (more seeds, confidence intervals) — these are valid but are scope-strengthening suggestions, not core flaws; moved to Nice-to-Haves.
- Criticisms about not comparing against SAC/entropy-regularized RL — demoted since the paper's framing is about preference-prediction alignment, not policy entropy regularization; the framing difference makes direct comparison less critical than comparing against intrinsic motivation methods.

## Suggestions

1. Fully specify the Bayesian adaptation scheme: prior, likelihood, posterior update rule, and definition of "rounds." Without this, the paper's central claim is not verifiable.
2. Describe how p(o|s,a) is implemented for each environment (architecture, training, tractable KL computation).
3. Compare against at least one existing intrinsic motivation method to validate the KL-shaping mechanism against the literature.
4. Present all results supporting the central claim in the paper, not in "not shown" runs.
5. Correct the β convergence narrative to match the data (posterior mean converges to ~0.02, not ~0.1).
