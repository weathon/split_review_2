# Review of papers/zs-vcos.md

## Summary

ZS-VCOS proposes a modular zero-shot pipeline for video camouflaged object segmentation (VCOS) by chaining optical flow (RAFT), open-vocabulary detection (OwlV2), and bidirectional SAM-2 propagation, adapted from a gas-leak detection pipeline (LangGas). Evaluated on MoCA-Mask, it raises the zero-shot F-measure from 0.296 to 0.628 and surpasses supervised baselines (ZoomNeXt Fβw = 0.476); on MoCA-Filtered it improves detection success rate from 0.628 to 0.697. The paper also identifies and documents real inconsistencies in mIoU aggregation across published codebases.

---

## Strengths

- **Large, non-marginal gains on MoCA-Mask.** Table 1 documents an absolute +0.332 gain in Fβw over the best prior zero-shot method (LLaVA+SAM2-L: 0.315 → 0.628) and +0.152 over the supervised SOTA ZoomNeXt. These magnitudes represent a qualitative shift, not an incremental improvement.
- **Honest and thorough ablation.** Table 2 presents nine ablation configurations, varying components one at a time. Crucially, the authors acknowledge that row (i) — without mean subtraction — outperforms "Ours" on Sα and mIoU, a form of intellectual honesty unusual in systems papers.
- **Genuine service contribution on metric inconsistency (Section 4.1.2).** The authors identify and document three inequivalent formulations of mean IoU in published code, show a concrete impact on ZoomNeXt's reported scores (mIoU 0.422 → 0.438 after recalculation), and propose a standardized remedy. The gray-colored entries in Table 1 signal which numbers may be affected — a methodologically responsible choice.
- **Principled transfer from gas-leak detection.** The adaptation of LangGas to VCOS is justified by a structural analogy (appearance camouflage overcome by motion contrast); the engineering choices — camera-motion-aware OF/BGS switching, momentum averaging, bidirectional SAM-2 propagation — are each individually justified by ablation.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **VLM threshold swept directly on the test set (Section 4.1.4).** The paper explicitly states: *"we slightly adjusted hyperparameters manually and swept the VLM threshold (following [14]) from 0.03 to 0.13 (inclusive) at a step of 0.02."* Six thresholds are evaluated directly on MoCA-Mask's test sequences and the best is reported. This is test-set leakage that undermines the headline zero-shot numbers. The justification — *"to minimize overfitting hyperparameters on the limited test set"* — is self-contradictory: sweeping on the test set is the mechanism for overfitting it. Given the small number of test sequences in MoCA-Mask, threshold sensitivity could produce meaningful variance, and the best threshold on this particular set may not generalize. A proper zero-shot evaluation requires either a fixed, pre-specified threshold (e.g., the 0.12 used on MoCA-Filtered), leave-one-sequence-out cross-validation, or a dedicated held-out split for hyperparameter selection. This is the most significant methodological gap in the paper.

- **mIoU claim in Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion is inconsistent with Table 1.** The Introduction (Section 1) states the method achieves mIoU = 0.561, and the Conclusion repeats this figure. However, Table 1 shows "Ours" achieving mIoU = 0.550, while ablation row (i) (a variant *without* mean subtraction) achieves mIoU = 0.561. The number cited across the paper corresponds to a variant the authors explicitly did not select as their final method. This is not a minor rounding issue; the abstract's central quantitative claim is drawn from a non-final configuration. The paper must either designate row (i) as "Ours" and justify the choice, or correct the abstract/introduction/conclusion to report 0.550.

### Minor

- **SAM 2.1 used for proposed method; baselines evaluated with SAM 2 (Section 4.1.4).** The method uses *Sam2.1-Hiera-Small*, while zero-shot baselines from Zhou et al. [53] use original SAM-2 checkpoints. SAM 2.1 introduced non-trivial improvements in tracking quality. The magnitude of the reported gap could partly reflect a model version advantage. The paper does not acknowledge this difference. Acknowledging it — ideally with a control row using SAM 2 in the ablation — would strengthen the evidence that the pipeline design drives the gains.

- **Camera-motion branching criterion underspecified (Section 3.1).** The method switches between optical flow and BGS depending on whether the camera is moving, detected via Lucas-Kanade point tracking to *"detect the furthest point the camera reached."* No threshold or criterion for the OF/BGS branch decision is provided in the main text. Since the ablation (Table 2, row e vs. c) shows this branching improves over OF-only, the decision rule matters. Even a one-sentence description of the threshold would suffice.

### Trivial

- **Post-hoc metric selection for final model (Section 4.4).** The choice of "Ours" (row with mean subtraction) over row (i) is justified solely by achieving the highest Eφ, while row (i) dominates on Sα and mIoU. This is presented transparently, but the paper should commit to this choice earlier and consistently — including in the abstract — rather than reporting 0.561 (row i) where it should say 0.550 (Ours).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report per-video mIoU variance (e.g., standard deviation across test sequences) to calibrate whether aggregate gains are consistent across the test set or driven by a small subset of sequences. Table 5 in supplementary apparently contains per-video results; a brief summary in the main paper would help.
- Include a brief quantitative analysis of failure cases (e.g., *pygmy_seahorse_0*) — beyond noting all methods fail — to characterize when motion-based pipelines break down (e.g., object stops, motion in background).
- State the size of the MoCA-Mask test set (number of sequences, total frames) explicitly in Section 4.1.1, so readers can calibrate the statistical significance of reported differences.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed; treat them with caution.*

- **"ZS w/ PK" prompt co-design concern.** The critic notes the positive prompt "an animal or insect being highlighted in blue" is co-designed with the pipeline. This is true but the paper labels this transparently as "ZS w/ PK" (prior knowledge) in Table 3 and the concern doesn't affect MoCA-Mask results. Removed as a weakness; it is already acknowledged.
- **Failure case analysis lacking in quantitative terms.** The reviewer criticizes the absence of per-video quantitative failure analysis for *pygmy_seahorse_0*. This is elevated to a nice-to-have as the paper acknowledges the failure and per-video results are in supplementary. Not a material flaw.
- **General claim about scope of evidence.** The harsh critic implies the lack of variance reporting is a significant evidential problem. Retained as a nice-to-have, not a weakness, since single-run evaluation on standard benchmarks is the norm in this field.

---

## Novel Insights

The paper's most genuinely novel observation is that the core difficulty of camouflaged object segmentation — appearance indistinguishability from background — can be partially bypassed at zero-shot by exploiting motion contrast, and that a well-composed pipeline of standard components (RAFT → OwlV2 → SAM-2 bidirectional) can be more effective than purpose-trained supervised models. The secondary novel observation — that mIoU aggregation method (per-frame, per-video, or global) substantially changes reported rankings and that codebases differ in this regard — is an underacknowledged problem that deserves wider attention.

---

## Suggestions

1. **Fix the experimental protocol for threshold selection.** Use either the pre-specified threshold of 0.12 (as done on MoCA-Filtered) applied uniformly to MoCA-Mask, or implement leave-one-sequence-out selection. Report results under this stricter protocol to support the zero-shot generalization claim.
2. **Correct the mIoU figure in abstract/introduction/conclusion** to match Table 1's "Ours" row (0.550), or redesignate row (i) as the final method and justify the switch consistently throughout the paper.
3. **Add one sentence in Section 4.1.4** acknowledging the SAM 2 vs. SAM 2.1 version difference and its potential impact on the comparative results.
4. **Specify the camera-motion threshold** for OF/BGS branching in Section 3.1 (even approximately).

---

## Score and Decision

The paper makes a real, non-trivial contribution: it demonstrates that a carefully constructed zero-shot pipeline can substantially outperform both prior zero-shot methods and supervised baselines on a standard VCOS benchmark, with a well-validated ablation. The metric-inconsistency analysis is a standalone service contribution. However, the test-set threshold sweep directly compromises the zero-shot claim that anchors the paper's identity, and the mIoU figure reported throughout the paper (0.561) does not correspond to the designated final model (0.550). Both issues are fixable without re-running experiments, but they need to be addressed before the contribution's quantitative claims can be taken at face value.
