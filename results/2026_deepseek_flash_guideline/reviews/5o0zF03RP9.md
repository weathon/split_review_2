## Summary

IncentRL proposes augmenting RL rewards with a KL divergence between predicted outcome distributions p(o|s,a) and preferred outcome distributions q(o|s), weighted by a parameter β. The paper's central claimed novelty is treating β as a Bayesian random variable that is adapted online to eliminate manual trade-off tuning. Experiments on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey 8x8 show that small positive β values can improve sample efficiency over β=0.

## Strengths

- **Meaningful empirical gains on MiniGrid Doorkey 8x8 (Section 5.4, Figure 2)**: IncentRL with β=0.01 achieves 98% final success rate vs. 90.5% for β=0.0, and reduces mean episode length from 75 to 29 steps — a roughly 2.6× improvement in efficiency. This goes beyond a marginal gain and shows the shaping term qualitatively changes learned behavior toward more direct navigation strategies.

- **KL-based incentive shaping framework is clearly formulated and differentiated from prior work (Sections 2.2–2.3, 3.3)**: The paper cleanly distinguishes its approach from policy KL-regularization (SAC, PPO) and from active inference / the Free Energy Principle. IncentRL keeps preferences fixed and shapes behavior, whereas active inference adapts preferences to reduce surprise. This design choice is articulated precisely and is a legitimate conceptual contribution.

- **Posterior concentration data for the Bayesian β scheme is shown (Figures 3–4, Table 3)**: The paper provides quantitative evidence that the posterior mean of β converges from ~0.11 to ~0.02 across rounds while variance shrinks, and shows narrowing distributions across seeds. This demonstrates that *whatever the Bayesian adaptation mechanism is*, it concentrates on a narrow range near task-relevant values.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Bayesian β adaptation — the paper's central claimed novelty — is never algorithmically specified**: The abstract, introduction, and conclusion all state that treating β as a Bayesian random variable and updating it online is "the central novelty" and that it "eliminates manual trade-off tuning." Yet the method section (Section 3) contains no description of this mechanism. The algorithm listing in Section 5.1 describes the KL shaping term but says nothing about β being a random variable or being updated. The paper never specifies: what prior is placed on β, what likelihood function relates β to observed data, how the posterior is computed (conjugate update? variational inference? MCMC?), what data drives the update, how a sampled β is used during action selection, or what "rounds" mean in relation to RL training steps. The Bayesian adaptation appears only in Section 5.4 as an ex-post analysis described in an "In additional runs (not shown)" clause. A reader cannot implement, evaluate, or verify the paper's central claim. For a method paper whose headline contribution is "Bayesian adaptation," the mechanism must be fully specified in the methods, not deferred to an unreported experiment.

- **KL computation in the toy example is mathematically ill-defined as written**: In Experiment 1 (Section 5.2, line 166), the paper states p(o|s₀, a₁) = {s₁: 0.3, s₀: 0.7} and q(o|s₀) = {s₁: 1.0, s₀: 0.0}. KL(p∥q) requires q assign non-zero probability to every outcome where p assigns non-zero probability. q(s₀)=0 while p(s₀)=0.7, making KL(p∥q) = ∞. An infinite KL penalty for action a₁ would make it never chosen, yet the paper reports that β>0 improves learning (Table 1). Either the actual computation differed from what is stated (e.g., a smoothed q was used) or the results cannot be reproduced. Either way, the simplest demonstration of the method is built on an invalid computation as presented.

- **p(o|s,a) implementation is never specified for non-toy domains**: The method relies on a learned or known predicted outcome distribution p(o|s,a). In the toy MDP this is the known transition function. In MountainCar (continuous state space) and MiniGrid (pixel observations), p(o|s,a) must be learned via a forward model in continuous spaces. The paper never describes: what architecture this forward model uses, how it is trained (online? jointly with the policy? from a replay buffer?), what the outcome variable o represents in these environments (next state? latent encoding?), or whether learning the forward model introduces non-stationarity or additional hyperparameters. Without this information, the empirical results are not reproducible and it is impossible to attribute improvements to the KL shaping vs. the learned forward model.

- **No comparison against standard intrinsic motivation baselines**: The paper frames IncentRL as addressing limitations of intrinsic motivation methods (curiosity, empowerment, information gain — Section 2.1) and claims to "remove the need for manual trade-off tuning, a core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods." Yet every experiment compares only against the β=0 ablation (standard RL without the KL term). There is no comparison against curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak et al. 2017, Burda et al. 2018), RND, ICM, or any other established exploration method, nor against KL-regularized RL methods (SAC, PPO with entropy bonus). The claim that IncentRL improves "over standard RL and fixed-regularization baselines" is only partially supported — the only "fixed-regularization" baselines tested are other β values of the same method, not alternative approaches.

### Minor

- **Bayesian run performance is explicitly "not shown"**: The paper states "In additional runs (not shown), the Bayesian adaptation of β achieved performance comparable to the best fixed value" (line 228). The central empirical claim for the paper's headline contribution is deferred to unpublished runs. Figures 3–4 show posterior statistics but without task-performance curves, the reader cannot assess whether the Bayesian β actually achieves comparable task performance to the best fixed β.

- **MountainCar results show high sensitivity to β that undermines robustness claims**: On MountainCar (Table 2), β=0.1 improves 18% over baseline, but β=0.3 hurts by −53% and β=1.0 by −75%. The Bayesian adaptation was not tested on this task, so there is no evidence it would find the narrow effective range on MountainCar. This directly undercuts the claim that the method eliminates manual tuning.

- **Limited statistical evidence**: 3 seeds for MountainCar and MiniGrid with high variance. On MountainCar, the improvement of β=0.1 (4136 ± 535) over β=0.0 (3502 ± 472) is within one standard deviation — not statistically significant with 3 seeds.

- **Theoretical propositions are basic**: Proposition 1 (small β preserves optimality) follows from continuity of J_β(π) in β for finite MDPs — it is a bounded-perturbation argument. Proposition 2 (as β→∞, policy ignores external rewards) is definitional. These do not constitute substantive theoretical analysis.

- **Only one non-zero β value tested on MiniGrid**: The main MiniGrid comparison is β=0.01 vs β=0.0 only (Section 5.4). A sweep of β values would be needed to demonstrate robustness and verify that β=0.01 is broadly effective rather than a lucky choice.

## Novel Insights

The harsh critic's core observation is that the paper's headline contribution (Bayesian β adaptation) is not actually present as a reproducible method — it appears only as an ex-post analysis in the experiments, and the performance curves for the Bayesian run are explicitly "not shown." This is not a nuanced insight but a straightforward reading of the paper's structure. The critic also correctly identifies that the toy example's KL computation as stated (q(s₀)=0, p(s₀)=0.7) is mathematically invalid, which would mean the results in Table 1 cannot be produced by the described computation unless unreported smoothing was applied. The conjunction of these two issues — the headline contribution is underspecified to the point of non-reproducibility and the simplest validation experiment contains a mathematical error — makes the paper's claims impossible to verify.

## Suggestions

1. **Full algorithmic specification of the Bayesian β mechanism**: Move the Bayesian β adaptation from a brief mention in the experimental analysis to the method section. Provide: the prior distribution placed on β, the likelihood function that relates β to observed data, the exact inference procedure (conjugate update? variational inference? 5. **Increase seeds and add significance testing**: 3 seeds with standard deviations comparable to the reported effect sizes does not constitute statistical evidence.
