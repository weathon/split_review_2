## Summary

DIPOLE proposes a novel RL algorithm for fine-tuning diffusion/flow policies. It starts from a greedified KL-regularized RL objective and shows that the optimal policy naturally decomposes into a positive policy π⁺ (reward-maximizing, weighted by bounded sigmoid σ(βG)) and a negative policy π⁻ (reward-minimizing, weighted by 1−σ(βG)). During inference, actions are generated by linearly combining the scores of both policies as (1+ω)ε⁺ − ωε⁻, matching classifier-free guidance and enabling controllable greediness via ω. The method is evaluated on 39 RL tasks (ExORL, OGBench) in offline and offline-to-online settings, plus a 1-billion parameter VLA model for autonomous driving on NAVSIM.

## Strengths

1. **Novel dichotomous decomposition (Section 3.2, Eqs. 7–8).** The core contribution is showing that the optimal policy in a greedified KL-regularized objective decomposes into π⁺ ∝ μ·σ(βG) and π⁻ ∝ μ·(1−σ(βG)). This replaces the unbounded exponential weight exp(βG) (Eq. 3) with bounded sigmoid weights, directly addressing the optimality-stability trade-off identified in Section 3.1. The derivation is explicit and the connection to prior limitations is clearly motivated.

2. **Principled connection to classifier-free guidance (Section 3.2, Eq. 10).** The score of the optimal policy equals (1+ω)∇log π⁺ − ω∇log π⁻, exactly matching the CFG form. This provides a theoretically grounded way to control greediness via ω during inference, contrasting with the heuristic CFGRL approach. This connection is an elegant and insightful synthesis of two lines of work.

3. **Thorough empirical evaluation.** The paper evaluates across 39 RL tasks on ExORL and OGBench under both offline and offline-to-online settings with 8 random seeds per result, plus a real-world autonomous driving benchmark with a 1-billion parameter VLA model. This is more comprehensive than most diffusion policy RL papers.

4. **Strong offline-to-online results (Table 3).** DIPOLE shows substantial improvements from pre-training to fine-tuning: humanoidmaze-medium-navigate from 61→97, antsoccer-arena from 43→90, scene from 97→100. These gains exceed those of baselines like IFQL (56→82 on humanoidmaze).

5. **Ablation w/o rejection sampling (Table 1).** DIPOLE w/o rs (no inference-time rejection sampling) broadly matches or exceeds CFGRL on most ExORL tasks, showing the performance gain primarily comes from the training objective itself, not expensive inference-time tricks.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Model capacity disparity in RL benchmarks.** DIPOLE trains two separate diffusion models (ε⁺_{θ₁} and ε⁻_{θ₂}) for the positive and negative policies in the ExORL and OGBench experiments, while baselines (IFQL, FQL, IDQL, CFGRL) train a single diffusion/flow model. This roughly doubles the parameter count and per-iteration compute. The paper never acknowledges this discrepancy or provides an ablation controlling for total model capacity (e.g., a single model with 2× parameters, or parameter-sharing between π⁺ and π⁻). Without this, it is unclear how much of DIPOLE's advantage comes from the algorithmic decomposition vs. extra capacity. (The NAVSIM experiment uses LoRA modules, so the overhead is smaller there, but the RL benchmarks use fully separate models.)

### Minor

1. **Theoretical derivation treats G as independent of π without discussion.** Theorem 1 (Eqs. 5–6) derives the closed-form optimal policy by treating the reference distribution μ·σ(βG)/Z(s) as fixed. However, in standard multi-step RL settings, G is the advantage A^π(s,a), which depends on the policy π being optimized. This creates a circular dependency — the reference distribution shifts as π changes. In practice, any implementation uses a frozen estimate of G (standard actor-critic approximation), making the derivation a surrogate optimization rather than a clean closed-form extraction. The paper should explicitly acknowledge this. This is not a fatal flaw (it is standard practice in RL) but the current framing overstates the theoretical rigor.

2. **NAVSIM navtest evaluation protocol is unusual.** The "navtest" variant (Table 4) trains DIPOLE on the NAVSIM test split and evaluates on the same split, achieving 94.8 PDMS (+6.5 over base). The more standard navtrain improvement is modest (+1.4 PDMS). The paper acknowledges this ("without using any ground-truth") but provides no analysis of why the gap between +1.4 and +6.5 is so large. Additionally, the base imitation model (88.3 PDMS) already outperforms all published baselines including Hydra-MDP (86.5), which may reflect significant architectural strength (Florence-2 encoder) as much as DIPOLE's contribution.

3. **No ablation of the ω hyperparameter.** The greediness factor ω is central — it controls the interpolation between π⁺ and π⁻ during inference (Eq. 10). Yet the paper provides no empirical analysis of how performance varies with ω. The claim of "controllable greediness" is not supported by any sensitivity analysis.

4. **Results are strong but not universally dominant.** DIPOLE underperforms IFQL on humanoidmaze-large-navigate (6 ± 2 vs 11 ± 2), underperforms FQL on antsoccer-arena-navigate (57 ± 7 vs 60 ± 2) and cube-double offline-to-online (89 ± 10 vs 92 ± 3). Many standard deviations overlap substantially (e.g., Quadruped walk: 928 ± 55 vs IFQL's 883 ± 12), and no statistical significance tests are reported.

### Trivial
5. The paper states "DIPOLE w/o rs demonstrates better performance compared to CFGRL" — verified against Table 1, DIPOLE w/o rs wins on 5 of 9 ExORL tasks (not universally). The full DIPOLE (with rejection sampling) wins on all 9.

- Wall-clock training time or FLOPs comparison against baselines
- Clarification of how rejection sampling is applied in DIPOLE and how much it contributes

## Suggestions

1. **Add a capacity-controlled ablation.** Train a single diffusion model with the same total parameter count as the two separate models, or share most parameters between π⁺ and π⁻ (as done with LoRA in NAVSIM), to isolate the algorithmic contribution from the capacity advantage.

2. **Acknowledge the actor-critic approximation explicitly.** Add a sentence noting that Theorem 1 treats G as fixed given a frozen critic estimate, and that the policy and critic are updated iteratively in practice — this matches the actual procedure and would strengthen rather than weaken the paper.

3. **Add ω sensitivity analysis.** Show how performance varies with ω on a representative subset of tasks (e.g., one from each domain) to empirically support the claim of "controllable greediness."

4. **Clarify NAVSIM navtest protocol.** Explain why the navtest improvement (+6.5 PDMS) is so much larger than the navtrain improvement (+1.4), and whether the result is reproducible on held-out folds.
