## Summary
This paper proposes IncentRL, a framework that augments standard RL with a KL divergence penalty between predicted outcome distributions and preferred outcome distributions. The claimed central novelty is treating the incentive weight β as a Bayesian random variable that adapts online. Experiments on a toy 2-state MDP, MountainCar, and MiniGrid Doorkey show that some positive β values improve over β=0 (standard RL).

## Strengths
- **Coherent cognitive grounding.** The connections to the Free Energy Principle (fixed preferences vs. adaptive beliefs, line 47) and dopamine-based reward prediction error (distributional KL vs. scalar TD, line 51) are clearly articulated and distinguish IncentRL from related frameworks like active inference. This gives the paper a consistent internal narrative.
- **Candid acknowledgment of failure modes.** Section 6.2 transparently discusses preference misalignment, KL dominance, and latent mismatch — more honest than most papers in this space. The limitations section appropriately caveats the results.

## Weaknesses

### Major
1. **The central claimed contribution (Bayesian β adaptation) is not specified.** The paper bills Bayesian β adaptation as the "central novelty" (abstract, line 9) and first contribution (line 21), yet never describes how it works. The algorithm in Section 5.1 (lines 149–155) lists five steps for the fixed-β version only — none involve Bayesian inference. The Bayesian adaptation subsection (lines 230–247) shows posterior diagnostics (Figures 3, 4, Table 3) but gives no description of the prior, likelihood function, update rule, or inference procedure. Terms like "rounds" are used without definition (Figures 3–4). Even by a generous reading, a reader cannot reproduce, verify, or understand what the method is. For a methods paper whose headline novelty is a Bayesian scheme, this is a structural gap.

2. **The paper's own data contradicts its textual interpretation.** Line 230 states the posterior "quickly concentrates near the effective region (β ≈ 0.1)". Table 3 shows the posterior mean decreasing from 0.106 (Round 1) to 0.017 (Round 4) — converging toward ~0.02, not 0.1. Figure 3's own caption notes the posterior mean "decreases to about 0.02 by Round 4." Yet both the text and the dashed reference line in the figures label β≈0.1 as the target. This internal inconsistency undermines confidence in the reported results. (Ironically, the MiniGrid fixed-β experiment uses β=0.01 as its best value, so the posterior converging to ~0.02 would actually support the central claim — if the text hadn't misstated it.)

3. **No comparison against any existing intrinsic motivation method.** The abstract frames the contribution as addressing "a core limitation of intrinsic motivation methods" (line 9), yet every experiment compares only against β=0 (standard RL with no intrinsic term). No curiosity-driven (Pathak et al., 2017; Burda et al., 2018), empowerment, or information-gain baseline is included. The paper therefore does not demonstrate that IncentRL improves over existing intrinsic motivation approaches — only that some positive β is better than none, which is a much weaker claim.

### Minor
4. **The theoretical analysis is minimal.** Section 4 offers two propositions: (1) for sufficiently small β, the optimal policy under standard RL remains optimal (a continuity argument); (2) as β→∞, the KL penalty dominates (definitional). These provide no convergence rates, sample complexity bounds, or analysis that distinguishes IncentRL from simple additive reward shaping. The paper lists "Theoretical analysis" as a contribution, but the analysis here is too thin to warrant that designation.

5. **Narrow experimental scope.** Three environments (one being a 2-state toy), 3 seeds on the main benchmarks (toy uses 7). On MountainCar, β=0.1 shows a modest improvement (18%) but β=0.3 and 1.0 degrade performance substantially (Table 2), indicating brittle β sensitivity that undercuts robustness claims. The central claim about Bayesian β matching the best fixed value is supported only by "additional runs (not shown)" (line 228), which the reader cannot evaluate. No direct comparison of Bayesian β vs. fixed β on the same performance metric is presented.

6. **Undisclosed KL computation issue in the toy MDP.** The toy experiment (Section 5.2) defines p(o|s₀,a₁) = {s₁:0.3, s₀:0.7} and q(o|s₀) = {s₁:1.0, s₀:0.0}. The KL(p‖q) term 0.7·log(0.7/0.0) is undefined (infinite KL by standard conventions). The paper reports successful learning with β>0 but never explains how this degenerate case is handled (e.g., smoothing, clipping, or reverse KL).

7. **Unclear operationalization for continuous domains.** On MountainCar, q(o|s) assigns "all probability to the goal (x ≥ 0.5)" (line 199), but it is not explained how a binary goal condition translates into a distribution over continuous (x,v) outcomes, nor what forward model produces p(o|s,a). No architecture or implementation details for the forward model are provided.
