# Review of papers/liu.md

## Summary

Hi-Light is a training-free video relighting framework that decouples lighting generation from detail preservation through three plug-and-play modules: a lightness-prior-anchored progressive diffusion scheme, a Hybrid Motion-Adaptive Lighting Smoothing Filter (HMA-LSF) combining optical flow with bilateral filtering, and a LAB-based Detail-Preserving Fusion (LAB-DF) module that transfers only low-frequency lighting information onto the original high-resolution frames. The paper also proposes the Light Stability Score (S_LS), a quantitative metric for temporal light flickering. Qualitative results and human evaluation show clear improvements over existing methods.

## Strengths

- **LAB-DF is a genuinely clever engineering contribution**: The key insight — inverting the direction of detail transfer by adding only the low-frequency (Gaussian-blurred) lighting from the relit output to the input's L-channel (Eq. 9: `L' = L_i + β(G_σ * L_r)`) rather than injecting high-frequency input details into the relit frame — elegantly solves the afterimage/ghosting problem that arises from geometry misalignment in diffusion-based pipelines. The ablation confirms this module is responsible for the dominant fidelity gains (SSIM 0.623→0.943).

- **HMA-LSF is a well-designed filter targeting distinct flicker sources**: The two-stage design (optical-flow-based motion compensation targeting frame-to-frame misalignment flicker, followed by a bilateral filter for spatial compression/processing noise) shows solid engineering judgment. The adaptive α blending (Eq. 7) that reduces temporal smoothing during fast motion is a practical choice that avoids motion blur. The ablation cleanly attributes the largest S_LS gains to this module (0.285→0.462).

- **The Light Stability Score addresses a genuine evaluation gap**: Prior video relighting work had no quantitative metric for temporal lighting consistency, relying entirely on subjective human evaluation. The proposed decomposition into three time-series signals (average intensity I_t, bright-pixel count C_t, frame-to-frame derivative \dot{I}_t), each normalized by dynamic range, is a principled signal-processing approach. The human evaluation (95.6% top-rank agreement with S_LS rankings) provides supporting validation.

- **Frequency-domain analysis (Figure 7) provides metric-independent evidence of detail preservation**: The Fourier magnitude spectra show Hi-Light's output is nearly indistinguishable from the input's, while all baselines exhibit visible high-frequency attenuation. This corroborates the SSIM findings through a physically interpretable, non-learned lens.

- **The human evaluation, while modest in scale, is well-designed**: 30 participants (including domain experts), double-blind setup, separate rankings for light stability and detail quality, with strong preference rates (95.6% / 91.1%).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The interpretation of SSIM/LPIPS/FID results is overclaimed given that all are computed against the original input video**. The paper explicitly states the input video "represents the ideal target for these metrics" (Section 5, Figure 6 discussion). But a correctly relit video *should* differ from the input in its lighting, so the input is not the ideal target for overall relighting quality — it is the ideal target for a "do nothing" baseline. The LAB-DF module (Eq. 9: `L' = L_i + β(G_σ * L_r)`) structurally guarantees high similarity to the input by copying the input's L-channel and adding only low-frequency lighting changes. Consequently, the headline "56% improvement in detail preservation" and the scatter plot in Figure 6 conflate detail preservation (a legitimate use of input-referenced SSIM) with broader relighting quality. The paper does provide independent evidence (frequency analysis, human evaluation, qualitative results) that Hi-Light genuinely preserves details better, but the quantitative framing overstates what these input-referenced metrics demonstrate when presented as primary evidence of superiority. The paper should: (a) acknowledge the tension between fidelity-to-input and relighting quality explicitly, (b) discuss that a method doing no relighting would achieve SSIM≈1.0, and (c) consider no-reference metrics or at minimum calibrate interpretation by placing the input video's own scores in Table 1.

### Minor

- **The S_LS metric is not fully specified in the main text for independent replication**. Section 3.1.1 provides the unsmoothness metric U_norm = M/R and states that "an exponential decay function transforms this metric into a final score S, bounded by 0 and 1," but does not give the actual decay function, its parameters, or the threshold τ value. Section 4 introduces "amplification factors of 20, 20, and 5 for S_L, S_C, S_I" — notation that does not match Section 3.1.1's S_{I_t}, S_{C_t}, S_{\dot{I}_t} and whose role in the computation is unexplained. The metric's conceptual design is clear, and these details may exist in the appendix, but the main paper should at minimum sketch the complete formula chain.

- **The backbone-agnostic claim is not substantiated**: All experiments use IC-Light as the sole relighting backbone. While HMA-LSF and LAB-DF are post-processing modules that could in principle work with any backbone, the lightness prior (Eq. 3–4) is injected during the diffusion process and may interact differently with different denoisers. Testing with at least one alternative image relighting model, or qualifying the claim, is needed.

- **The VBench result is a tie, not a win**: Table 1 shows Hi-Light at 0.736 and LAV-CogVideoX also at 0.736, yet the text presents this as a leading result. The paper also claims Hi-Light is "the only method capable of processing high-resolution video with computational efficiency" without providing any runtime or memory comparison to support uniqueness.

- **The ablation study (Table 2) is missing informative configurations**: Specifically, LAB-DF alone and LAB-DF + Lighting Prior (without HMA-LSF). The text mentions the latter achieves SSIM=0.939 (line 252), but this important data point is not in the table. Given that LAB-DF contributes the vast majority of the SSIM gain, understanding its isolated and pairwise effects would strengthen the attribution.

### Trivial

- The ablation discussion (Section 6) refers to "the derivative term S_j" but this notation is never defined in the paper; presumably it refers to S_{\dot{I}_t} from Section 3.1.1.
- The notation "S_L, S_C, S_I" for amplification factors in Section 4 does not match "S_{I_t}, S_{C_t}, S_{\dot{I}_t}" from Section 3.1.1.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Reporting standard deviations or confidence intervals across the 100 videos would strengthen the quantitative comparisons, especially given that some VBench differences are under 0.01.
- A runtime and memory comparison table would substantiate the "computational efficiency" claim and help practitioners choose methods.
- Including the input video's scores directly in Table 1 (SSIM≈0.95, S_LS≈0.68, from Figure 6) would help readers calibrate the metric scales.

## Removed Points

These points were flagged for removal; treat them with caution.

- **"The core quantitative evaluation is circular" as a fatal flaw**: Removed as a fatal-tier claim. The paper explicitly frames SSIM as measuring "detail preservation," which is a legitimate use of input-referenced comparison — you *want* to preserve the original video's textures and structures. The paper provides multiple independent lines of evidence (frequency-domain analysis, human evaluation, qualitative comparisons) that corroborate the detail-preservation claim beyond the SSIM numbers. The genuine concern about overinterpretation of input-referenced metrics is retained as a Major weakness.

- **"The lightness prior mechanism is undersupported"**: Removed as a stand-alone weakness. The paper provides both a theoretical justification (DC-insensitivity, Eq. 3) and ablation evidence (S_LS improvement from 0.285 to 0.353) for the lightness prior. The residual being static across frames does not preclude it from damping frame-to-frame oscillations — it anchors each frame's high-frequency luminance to a common reference, which is a reasonable mechanism. The ablation text's reference to "S_j" without definition is already captured as a Trivial notation issue.

- **"No 'no relighting' baseline would calibrate SSIM interpretation"**: Removed. The input video is plotted in Figure 6 at (~0.68, ~0.95), which already serves as an implicit no-relighting baseline calibrating the metric space. Explicitly putting it in Table 1 is moved to Nice-to-Haves.

- **Demand for statistical significance testing across 100 videos**: Moved to Nice-to-Haves. Single-run evaluation without confidence intervals is standard practice in video generation/editing benchmarking (VBench, FID reporting conventions). This is not a weakness of this paper specifically.

- **Criticism of SSIM over MS-SSIM choice**: Removed. The paper justifies the choice on efficiency grounds and the specific concern (fidelity regardless of color) — this is a reasonable engineering tradeoff, not a weakness.

- **Criticism that the paper "does not discuss the critical distinction between fidelity-to-reference and relighting quality" in Related Work**: Removed as inflated. The Related Work section is adequate and this distinction becomes the paper's own contribution to grapple with, not a gap in literature review.

## Novel Insights

The review process surfaced an important meta-observation about evaluation in video relighting: input-referenced fidelity metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, FID) and relighting quality metrics (S_LS) exist in inherent tension. A method that perfectly preserves the input (SSIM=1.0) does no relighting, while aggressive relighting degrades SSIM. Hi-Light's decoupled architecture navigates this tradeoff explicitly — LAB-DF maximizes input fidelity while the diffusion backbone handles lighting — but the evaluation framework hasn't fully caught up to this decoupling. Future work on relighting evaluation may need metrics that separately assess (a) lighting plausibility/quality, (b) detail preservation, and (c) temporal stability, rather than collapsing them into a single "better" axis. The scatter plot in Figure 6 is a step in this direction but the framing still implies the top-right corner (near the input) is unambiguously best.

## Suggestions

- Add the input video's scores to Table 1 to calibrate the metric scales and make the "do nothing" baseline explicit.
- In Section 5, add a paragraph explicitly discussing the tension between SSIM-as-detail-preservation and the fact that a method preserving all input details perfectly (SSIM=1.0) would do no relighting at all. This would preempt the obvious criticism and strengthen the paper's credibility.
- Either test with at least one alternative image relighting backbone (beyond IC-Light) or qualify the "backbone-agnostic" claim to "compatible with IC-Light-based diffusion models."
- Complete the S_LS specification in the main paper body by providing the exponential decay formula; even `S = exp(-k · U_norm)` with stated k would suffice for reproducibility.
