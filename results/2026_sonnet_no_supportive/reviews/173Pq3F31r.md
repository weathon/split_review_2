## Summary
This paper proposes a unified transformer-based Seq2Seq framework for joint expressive piano performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT) by disentangling note-level score content from global performance style representations, requiring only sequence-level (not note-level) alignment during training. A separately trained diffusion-based Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module generates stylistically appropriate style embeddings from score content alone, enabling fully automated rendering. The framework is evaluated across objective metrics, subjective listening tests, and latent-space analysis.

## Strengths

- **Statistically significant APT improvement (Table 1):** The joint model achieves $E_{avg}=12.48$ vs. Beyer & Dai (2024) $E_{avg}=14.10$ under the same ASAP split and alignment-free Seq2Seq setup, with $p < 0.01$ significance on $E_{extra}$, $E_{onset}$, $E_{offset}$ (MUSTER) and $E_{pitch}$ (ScoreSimilarity). This is a well-supported, credible gain over the most directly comparable prior work.

- **Quantitative disentanglement evidence (Table 4):** Style embeddings $z_s$ achieve 77.46% composer and 42.07% performer identification accuracy, while content embeddings $z_c$ yield near-chance performer accuracy (9.94%) and substantially lower composer accuracy (29.99%). This is a concrete, falsifiable demonstration — not merely a visualization-level claim.

- **PSR cross-era distribution matching (Figure 4):** PSR-generated style embeddings conditioned only on score content reproduce the Baroque → Classical → Romantic → Modern centroid trajectory and cluster structure observed in real performances. This is specific, falsifiable evidence directly supporting the PSR module's core claim.

- **Principled joint design:** The shared content space $\mathcal{Z}_c$, global style vector $z_s$ conditioning, and masked reconstruction objective for unpaired data follow coherently from the inverse-task duality motivation, with no ad hoc components.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Ours (PSR) regresses on key EPR objective metrics relative to the EPR-Only ablation.** Table 2 shows Ours (PSR) has KL(V)=2.67 vs. EPR-Only's 2.05, and MAE(V)=15.24 vs. 10.65. Section 5.1 states "consistent improvement of Ours-Target over the EPR-Only variant... validating the effectiveness of our joint framework," but this sidesteps the fact that the deployable condition (PSR-conditioned rendering) regresses on velocity metrics relative to the ablation baseline. The subjective results in Figure 2 show Ours (PSR) outperforming baselines, but the discrepancy between objective velocity metrics and subjective ratings is unreported and unanalyzed. The claim that "joint modeling improves EPR" is only supported under an oracle condition (ground-truth style at inference); the intended deployed system does not show this on objective metrics.

- **Missing contemporary EPR baselines.** Rhyu et al. (2022), Borovik & Viro (2023), and Tang et al. (2023) are cited in Section 2.1 as constituting the current state of the EPR field, but none appear in Tables 2 or 3. The paper offers no rationale for their exclusion. Without these comparisons — or a principled justification for their absence (e.g., incompatible data format, no public code) — the competitive-performance claim for EPR cannot be substantiated against the authors' own described state of the art.

### Minor

- **Underpowered subjective evaluation.** The main listening test uses 11 participants rating 5 pieces; the style transfer evaluation (Figure 5) uses the same pool rating only 3 samples. For Sample 2, Target similarity (~2.4) is lower than Mean (~3.0), and Target quality (~2.4) drops from Mean quality (~3.1). The paper claims "this improvement does not compromise overall quality" (Section 5.2), but this does not hold for Sample 2 where both similarity and quality drop. With only 11 participants and 3 samples, style transfer conclusions should be stated more cautiously.

- **Undescribed unpaired performance (YouTube) dataset.** Section 4.1 states an unpaired performance dataset was collected from YouTube and transcribed by "a state-of-the-art audio-to-MIDI transcription model," but footnote 2 is blank (model name missing in the parsed text). Dataset size, transcription accuracy, and filtering criteria are not reported. Since unpaired training is a claimed contribution, the quality and scale of this dataset matter to evaluating that claim.

- **Counterintuitive alignment result in Table 3 is unexplained.** Ours (PSR) achieves higher alignment rate (92.27%) than Ours (Target) (91.55%) despite not using ground-truth style. A likely explanation is that PSR-generated styles are more conservative/mean-like, which would also explain the lower duration variance in Table 2 (0.33 vs. 0.58), but this goes unanalyzed.

### Trivial

- **Tokenization asymmetry between EPR input and output (Section 3.1).** The EPR decoder uses REMI-style tokenization while the input uses note-tuple representation. The implications for training dynamics of sharing an architecture between EPR and APT decoders with different output token spaces are not discussed.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Measure the distribution distance between PSR-generated $\hat{z}_s$ and ground-truth $z_s$ extracted from matching performances; a small gap would substantially strengthen the PSR-to-EPR pipeline validation and help explain the objective metric regression.
- An ablation confirming that removing the EPR objective degrades APT performance would sharpen the joint-modeling-benefits-APT claim; the EPR-Only variant already exists for this purpose.
- A brief ablation removing the KL penalty on $z_s$ would clarify what drives disentanglement — architectural separation vs. regularization.

## Removed Points

*These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution.*

- **"Disentanglement lacks adversarial/MI objective" (Harsh Critic, §3.3):** The paper empirically validates disentanglement quantitatively (Table 4). Architectural separation of sequence-level $z_c$ vs. scalar $z_s$ provides a structural inductive bias sufficient to achieve the reported results. Absence of an adversarial objective is a design choice, not a flaw; this criticism was moved to Nice-to-Have.

- **"Cross-validation for APT joint benefit" (Harsh Critic, §5.1 suggestions):** This is a Nice-to-Have ablation that would sharpen an already-supported claim, not a weakness.

- **Concerns about style transfer "smooth interpolation" claim (Harsh Critic, §5.2):** The Mean condition consistently shows strong quality across all 3 samples — the paper's claim that "the style space is well-structured and supports smooth interpolation" is reasonably supported. Merged into the already-retained Minor about Sample 2's inconsistency.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation — supported by Figure 4 — is that a diffusion model conditioned only on score content can reproduce the historical era clustering found in actual human performance style embeddings, implying that symbolic score structure encodes sufficient compositional identity to indirectly constrain the style space. The Content→Composer accuracy of 29.99% from content-only embeddings (Table 4) corroborates this: even after removing performance-specific style, score structure retains composer-specific signatures, validating the PSR's operating premise. This is a finding with implications beyond this paper for score-based style modeling.

## Suggestions

1. Add a direct comparison of Ours (PSR) vs. EPR-Only in Section 5.1, and analyze why PSR-conditioned rendering regresses on velocity KL and MAE — likely a PSR style distribution mismatch relative to the training distribution.
2. Include Rhyu et al. (2022), Borovik & Viro (2023), or Tang et al. (2023) in Tables 2/3, or provide an explicit, principled justification for their exclusion (e.g., no public code, incompatible dataset format).
3. Report the size, transcription method, and quality filtering criteria for the YouTube unpaired performance dataset.
4. Revise style transfer conclusions (Section 5.2) to acknowledge Sample 2's inconsistency, rather than stating broadly that "this improvement does not compromise overall quality."
