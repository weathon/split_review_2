## Summary

PI-CCA reframes VL-CL forgetting as drift in the CCA canonical invariants (spectrum + subspace) of the whitened image-text cross-covariance, and introduces a replay-free, constant-memory framework that constrains these invariants via compact sketched certificates updated with streaming EMAs. A prompt-invariance mechanism averages text-CCA projectors over prompt perturbations. Evaluated on four VL-CL benchmarks (MTIL, X-TAIL, VLCL, ConStruct-VL), PI-CCA achieves state-of-the-art replay-free performance across all tracks.

---

## Strengths

- **Principled geometric framing (Sec. 3):** Reframing forgetting as CCA canonical invariant drift leads to structurally distinct losses (Eqs. 8–11) rather than distillation of logits or similarities. The connection to the whitened cross-covariance that directly underlies CLIP-style retrieval is well-motivated and non-trivial.

- **Constant-memory certificate (Eq. 4):** Storing $\mathbf{S}_v^* \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times k}$ with a random orthonormal sketch achieves $O(hk)$ memory independent of $d_v, d_t$—a materially better design than replay, teacher-network distillation, or stored logit methods.

- **Breadth of evaluation (Tables 1–2):** Four benchmarks covering task-incremental classification, task-agnostic classification, continual image-text retrieval, and structured concept matching. Consistent SOTA gains across all four tracks is stronger evidence than single-benchmark claims.

- **Component-wise ablation (Table 3):** Single-factor ablation of each loss term, each EMA, sketch type, and pairing strategy with absolute performance drops reported. Among the more thorough ablations in recent VL-CL work.

- **Task-order robustness (Figure 5):** 20 shuffled MTIL sequences with narrow IQRs directly addresses the standard worry that CL papers are tuned to a favorable task ordering.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Figure 3 geometry→performance correlation is circular (verified):** Section 4.3 claims Figure 3 demonstrates that geometry drift "predicts retention rather than being a coincidental regularizer" (caption, p. 8). However, the entire sweep varies only PI-CCA hyperparameter configurations (certificate size, EMAs, invariance strength, whitening, pairing, LoRA capacity/LR, sketch type). Every point is generated by optimizing the same loss that directly penalizes geometry drift; a configuration with lower drift has by definition better satisfied the training objective and will score higher on downstream metrics. The near-perfect r = 1.00 / ρ = 1.00 values are fully consistent with this tautology. Genuine causal evidence would require computing geometry drift for *other methods* (ZSCL, DKR, C-CLIP) and confirming that their cross-method performance outcomes also correlate with cross-method drift. The current analysis cannot establish the causal thesis, only that the PI-CCA training objective is internally coherent. This weakens the explanatory story but does not invalidate the empirical performance contribution.

### Minor

- **Missing variance in Table 1 (verified):** Table 2 reports ±SD; Table 1 (MTIL, X-TAIL) does not. The headline MTIL Avg margin over C-CLIP is 1.6 pp (76.8 vs. 75.2), and over DIKI is 1.9 pp, within a dense cluster of baselines spanning ≈4 pp. Without standard deviations, statistical significance cannot be assessed, and the ranking is not verifiable.

- **Certificate initialization data source unspecified (verified):** Section 3.2 states the certificate is initialized from "the reference (pre-continual) CCA quantities" computed from Eq. 2, but never names what data is used for this computation. If a large calibration corpus (e.g., an ImageNet subset) is used, this constitutes a data dependency that not all baselines share; if only the first task's data is used, the certificate carries a domain bias that interacts with task-order sensitivity in unexplored ways.

### Trivial

- **EMA rate $\alpha$ not reported in main text:** Eq. 13 labels $\alpha$ as "slow" but its concrete value is not given alongside the equation. Given that $\alpha$ governs the fundamental plasticity-retention tradeoff, the specific value used should appear in the main text rather than only in the appendix.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel observation is the design of the sketched CCA certificate: random orthonormal projections compress canonical directions into $O(hk)$ storage while preserving subspace-angle geometry under near-isometric conditions (Eq. 4), and prompt-projector averaging (Eqs. 5–6) simultaneously eliminates sign/rotation ambiguity in CCA directions and provides a variance-reduction mechanism for prompt sensitivity—addressing two otherwise separate problems with a single design choice. The streaming EMA covariance update (Eq. 12) combined with a slow certificate EMA (Eq. 13) creates a two-timescale system that separates "what to preserve" from "how fast to track current geometry," a structure not present in prior VL-CL work.
