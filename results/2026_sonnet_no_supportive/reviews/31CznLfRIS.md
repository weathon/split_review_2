Now I have enough to draft the review.## Summary

VideoJudge introduces a bootstrapping framework for training small MLLM-based evaluators (3B and 7B) specialized for video understanding. A generator–evaluator loop synthesizes 103K training examples without human annotation; fine-tuned judge models are evaluated across pointwise and pairwise benchmarks. The paper additionally trains models to generate instance-specific rubrics at test time, reporting that compact VideoJudge models match or exceed much larger baseline models on several benchmarks.

---

## Strengths

- **Practical bootstrapping pipeline with rigorous data validation.** The generator–evaluator loop produces 103K examples without human annotation. Human validation in §5.2 targets the hardest rating region (2 vs. 3 pairs), achieves 94.8% inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ = 89.5), and demonstrates >92% alignment with bootstrapped labels — directly addressing the most critical failure mode of automated data generation.

- **Concrete empirical finding on LLM vs. MLLM judges.** Table 1 clearly shows that text-only Qwen3 models (including chain-of-thought variants) consistently underperform video-input models on LongVideoBench and VATEX — the two genuinely independent pointwise benchmarks. This is verifiable, actionable evidence that visual grounding is necessary for video evaluation quality.

- **Rubric generation with meaningful human evaluation.** Training a 3B model to produce instance-specific rubrics and evaluating those rubrics via human preference studies (Figure 3) demonstrates a real capability. The ≥50% win rate against Qwen-72B (63.9% by unanimous human vote) on rubric quality is noteworthy given the ~24× parameter disparity.

- **Comprehensive resource release.** The paper provides bootstrapped datasets, trained judge models, and meta-evaluation benchmarks, which are practical and reproducible artifacts for the research community.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Circular evaluation dominates the headline results.** The two primary pointwise meta-evaluation benchmarks — VideoJudgeLLaVA-MetaEval and VideoJudgeVCG-MetaEval — are generated by the same bootstrapping pipeline as the training data (§4.2: "generating additional responses via our bootstrapping pipeline"). Their ground-truth labels are assigned by the same evaluator model that produces training labels. High performance on these benchmarks (e.g., VideoJudge-3B Spearman 0.82 on VideoJudgeLLaVA, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-72B's 0.80) therefore primarily measures how well the model recovers the evaluator's systematic preferences — not generalization to human judgment. The paper acknowledges this as "closed-loop effects" in §7 but continues to treat these benchmarks as headline evidence. Similarly, the VJ pairwise benchmark is derived by converting bootstrapped pointwise ratings into pairs (§4.2), making the 94–98.6% accuracy in Table 3 essentially in-distribution label recovery.

- **Independent benchmark results are modest and mixed, contradicting headline abstract claims.** On genuinely independent benchmarks: (a) *VATEX* (Table 1): VideoJudge-7B RMSE 1.46 is *worse* than Qwen2.5-VL-32B (1.43) and 72B (1.40). VideoJudge-3B is better (RMSE 1.33 vs. 1.40) but the advantage is narrow. (b) *VideoAutoArena* (Table 3, human preferences as ground truth): VideoJudge-3B scores only 71.76 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-32B (80.78) and 72B (89.80). VideoJudge-7B (85.49) trails 72B (89.80) by four points. The abstract states "outperforms or is on par with larger MLLM judge baselines" — but this holds on three of four benchmarks only if one counts the two self-constructed benchmarks. On the most informative independent pairwise evaluation, the claim does not hold.

- **Rubric comparison in Table 2 is structurally unfair.** VideoJudgeR-3B is *fine-tuned specifically to generate rubrics* from synthetic training data, while the 3B, 7B, 32B, and 72B baselines in Table 2 are zero-shot prompted to produce rubrics. The claimed result that a 3B model "outperforms 32B and 72B" measures the advantage of task-specific fine-tuning over zero-shot prompting — not the value of the rubric approach itself. A fair comparison requires either few-shot rubric prompting of the larger models or rubric-supervised fine-tuning of a larger backbone.

### Minor

- **Automatic data validation in §5.1 is circular.** BERTScore and BLEU monotonically decrease as ratings decrease (Figure 2), offered as validation. But the generator was explicitly instructed to produce progressively lower-quality responses; lower scores vs. the gold reference confirm the instruction was followed — not that the quality gradient is meaningful for evaluation training. This is weaker evidence than the paper implies.

- **Overestimation bias is documented but not addressed.** §6.2 reports that 81.3% of rating-4 responses are rated as 5 and 46.6% of rating-3 responses are inflated to 5 — precisely the range where the bootstrapped pipeline generates most training signal. The paper correctly diagnoses this but offers no corrective experiment (e.g., oversampling hard negatives near the top of the scale).

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An ablation comparing the full iterative refinement loop vs. accepting all first-generation responses would clarify the contribution of the feedback mechanism vs. simple SFT on any LLM-generated data.
- The rubric evaluation in Table 2 would be far more convincing with few-shot rubric prompting for larger baselines or rubric-supervised fine-tuning of a 7B backbone.
- The independent benchmarks (VideoAutoArena, VATEX, VJ-H) deserve more prominent framing as the primary evidence for generalization, with candid discussion of why gains there are more modest.
- The specific models used as generator and evaluator in the main pipeline, and sensitivity of data quality to threshold α, would strengthen reproducibility claims.

## Novel Insights

The most genuinely novel finding is that fine-tuned small MLLM judges are *robust to decoding temperature* in ways that zero-shot larger models are not (Figure 4) — VideoJudge-trained models maintain stable or improving Spearman correlation (0.66→0.73) as temperature increases from 0 to 1, while the base model degrades (0.56→0.42). This has direct deployment value. The finding that text-only LLMs with extended reasoning consistently fall short of video-language models on video evaluation (Table 1, LongVideoBench) is also concrete and actionable for practitioners deciding on evaluation infrastructure.

---

## Suggestions

- Reframe main quantitative claims around the independent benchmarks (VideoAutoArena, VATEX, VJ-H); treat the self-constructed benchmarks as supplementary.
- Run the ablation: first-pass acceptance only vs. full iterative refinement — to isolate the bootstrapping loop's marginal contribution.
- Target the overestimation bias directly: oversample or reweight near-top-of-scale pairs in training and measure calibration improvement on the rating-3/4 region.
- Add few-shot rubric prompting for 32B/72B models in Table 2 to make the rubric comparison informative.
