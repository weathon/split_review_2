## Summary
This paper evaluates three neural network architectures—a plain MLP, a "U-Net-style" residual network, and a DeepONet-inspired model—as data-driven surrogates for hydrogen-oxygen-air combustion kinetics. Using 50K training samples on a 13-dimensional chemical state, the paper finds the residual network substantially outperforms the other two (mean MSE ~1.4e-3 vs. ~1.8–2.0e-2) and argues that architectural choice is a critical determinant of surrogate model quality for this class of stiff ODE problems.

## Strengths
- The 95% CI analysis in Table 1 provides minimal but genuine statistical rigor: the U-Net's interval [7.69e-4, 1.98e-3] is non-overlapping with both MLP and DeepONet intervals, confirming the improvement is statistically significant.
- The multi-step rollout training loss (Eq. 4) with inverse-step weighting (1/k) is a sensible choice that incentivizes error-accumulation robustness during recursive prediction.
- Hard-coding dt, N₂, and Ar outputs to their input values (Section 4.1–4.2) is a physically motivated design decision that correctly enforces invariants across all models.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **CO and NO appear in Figures 3 and 4 but are absent from the described mechanism.** Section 2 specifies exactly 9 H₂-O₂ species (H₂, O₂, H₂O, OH, H, O, HO₂, H₂O₂, OH*) plus N₂ and Ar — 11 species total. Yet the figure captions for Figures 3 and 4 explicitly list CO and NO as plotted species. These species are not part of the described mechanism and cannot be outputs of a model trained on that mechanism. This is an unresolved inconsistency between the experimental description and the presented results that raises serious doubt about whether the figures correspond to the experiments described in the paper.

2. **The "U-Net" architecture is not a U-Net, and the mechanism attributed to its success is architecturally unfounded.** Section 4.2 clearly describes: an expansion layer → three dense blocks → local skip (block output + expansion output) → compression layer → global skip (original input added to final output). There is no downsampling, no upsampling, and no encoder mirrored by a decoder — the defining properties of a U-Net. Yet Section 5 explicitly states: *"The U-Net's encoder-decoder design with skip connections appears to capture both global trends and localized transients... This multi-scale representation likely underlies its lower MSE."* The explanation invokes architectural features (encoder-decoder, multi-scale representation) that simply do not exist in the implemented model. The observed improvement is most plausibly attributable to the well-known stabilizing effect of a global residual connection (input-to-output skip), but this is speculative in the absence of ablation.

3. **No ablation to isolate the source of the improvement.** The MLP and the "U-Net" differ by exactly two skip connections: one local (expansion → block output) and one global (input → final output). No ablation is provided. Given that global residual connections are widely known to reduce error accumulation in recursive prediction tasks, the entire gain could come from this alone. The paper's stated purpose is to provide architectural guidance for practitioners choosing surrogate models; without ablation, no such guidance is possible — only the ranking of specific trained models on a single task is established.

4. **No parameter count or inference speed reported.** The entire motivation for building a surrogate model is computational acceleration (Section 2: ODE solving "takes about 90 percent of time resources"). A surrogate paper that reports only MSE, without any inference timing or parameter count for the three architectures, cannot make a meaningful practical recommendation. It is unknown whether the U-Net's accuracy advantage comes at zero computational cost or at a significant overhead.

### Minor

1. **Only 100 epochs reported with no convergence evidence.** Section 4.4 states all models are trained for 100 epochs on 50K samples. No training or validation curves are shown, and no justification is given that 100 epochs is sufficient or equivalent for all three architectures. If the MLP has not converged, the comparison is unfair.

2. **DeepONet trunk/branch terminology is inverted.** Section 4.3 states "the trunk network (for dt) provides coefficients for the basis generated by the branch network." In standard DeepONet, the trunk generates basis functions evaluated at output coordinates and the branch provides expansion coefficients — the opposite assignment from what is described here.

3. **High variance acknowledged but not analyzed.** The U-Net's standard deviation (2.18e-2) is ~16× its mean MSE (1.37e-3). The paper notes that "certain test trajectories remain challenging" (Section 5) but provides no characterization of what conditions produce high-error predictions (e.g., proximity to ignition threshold, temperature range, induction time).

### Trivial
None.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A clean ablation: MLP → MLP + global skip only → MLP + both skips, to establish what architectural feature actually drives the observed improvement.
- Failure-mode analysis: bin test-set errors by thermodynamic regime (temperature, induction time) to characterize where these surrogates break down.
- Inference wall-clock timing and parameter counts for all three models.
- Training/validation loss curves to confirm convergence.

## Novel Insights
The most likely driver of the performance improvement in the "U-Net" is the global residual connection (direct input-to-output skip), a standard stabilization technique for recursive prediction tasks. The paper accidentally demonstrates this effect while attributing it to the wrong architectural principle. The CO/NO discrepancy in the figures may indicate that a more complete mechanism (possibly including nitrogen and carbon chemistry) was actually used in the experiments, which would change the scope and interpretation of the results significantly.

## Suggestions
1. Verify and correct the species list in Figures 3 and 4 to match the described mechanism, or explicitly state if a different (e.g., extended) mechanism was used in the experiments.
2. Add an ablation experiment: run MLP + global skip only to determine whether the single global residual connection accounts for the full improvement.
3. Report inference time (milliseconds per step) and parameter counts in a dedicated table.
4. Show training and validation loss curves to confirm convergence at 100 epochs.
