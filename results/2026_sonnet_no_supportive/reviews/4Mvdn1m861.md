## Summary
TokenCount is a training-free object counting framework built entirely on SAM (no auxiliary models). It consists of two components: (1) probabilistic prompt generation via iterative cosine-similarity-based sampling, and (2) output token-based verification using the TS-SS geometric similarity metric. The key technical claim is that SAM's decoder output tokens encode richer semantic-positional information than image encoder embeddings and thus yield better instance discrimination. Results on CARPK (MAE 4.68, beating all supervised baselines) are strong; results on FSC-147 are more mixed.

---

## Strengths
- **Output token verification is a genuine, well-motivated insight.** Section 3.2 argues that SAM's decoder output tokens integrate semantic and positional information through bidirectional attention, making them superior to raw image embeddings for instance verification. The PCA visualization in Figure 5 directly supports this: output tokens visibly separate positive and negative instances in 2D projection while image embeddings fail to do so.
- **CARPK results are concretely strong.** MAE of 4.68 in Table 1 outperforms all supervised methods (SAFECOUNT 5.33, BMNet+ 5.76, LOCA 9.97, FamNet 18.19) using only SAM without any auxiliary model, representing a meaningful result in a domain-specific scenario.
- **Efficiency framing is appropriately calibrated.** The paper explicitly acknowledges TFCAC's superiority on FSC-147 and attributes it to DINO + more prompts, offering an honest accuracy-efficiency trade-off comparison within the SAM-only paradigm.

---

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None — the paper has verifiable findings, but several major issues prevent straightforward acceptance.

### Major

- **The abstract's central accuracy claim is factually incorrect for FSC-147.** The abstract states the method achieves "superior accuracy…outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods" with "an MAE of 16.25." Table 1 shows this is false: LOCA (10.79), PseCo (13.05), SAFECOUNT (14.32), and TFCAC (12.26) all clearly outperform the proposed method on FSC-147. The paper's actual story — best among SAM-only methods, and competitive with some supervised methods — is legitimate but must be accurately stated.

- **The FSC-147 RMSE anomaly is unaddressed and exposes a structural robustness failure.** The method achieves MAE 16.25 but RMSE 133.11 on FSC-147 — nearly matching vanilla SAM's RMSE of 137.50 and dramatically worse than LOCA (56.97), SAFECOUNT (85.54), BMNet+ (91.83), and TFCAC (56.33). This divergence between MAE and RMSE implies catastrophic failures on a subset of images (dense, small-object scenes). Section 5 mentions qualitative failure on dense scenes but never connects this to the RMSE gap, never estimates how many FSC-147 images fall into this regime, and never quantifies the degradation. The claim of "superior accuracy" is further undermined by these tail errors.

- **TS-SS does not demonstrably outperform cosine similarity.** Table 3: cosine 16.48, TS-SS 16.25 — a gap of 0.23 MAE in a single-run experiment. The paper devotes significant theoretical motivation (Section 3.2) to TS-SS as necessary to "break through the performance plateau," but the data show that the gap between using dot product vs. any normalized metric explains nearly all performance gain; the TS-SS-specific advantage is within noise. The paper's own framing acknowledges "near-parity" between cosine and L2 yet still claims TS-SS is required for a "breakthrough" — this is not supported by the numbers.

### Minor

- **Verification threshold (300) is un-ablated.** Table 4 ablates iterations and prompts-per-iteration, but the threshold that determines what counts as a "positive" instance is never touched. TS-SS has non-trivial scale dependence (combining triangle area and sector area), so 300 has no intuitive interpretation and could substantially affect results.

- **Non-monotonicity in Table 4 is unexplained.** At 32 iterations × 128 prompts, MAE rises to 16.65 from 16.25 at 16 × 128. This reversal is consistent with the early stopping mechanism interacting unexpectedly with oversampling, but the paper does not address it.

- **No variance estimates for key comparisons.** The Table 5 ablation (output token MAE 16.25 vs image embedding 16.94, a difference of 0.69) and the Table 3 metric comparison (TS-SS vs cosine at 0.23 MAE) are single-run results with no error bars. CARPK exemplar selection is explicitly randomized ("12 randomly selected exemplars"), adding unquantified variability. Given the small absolute differences, these comparisons are hard to interpret without at least seed-level variance.

### Trivial
None beyond parser formatting artifacts.

- Multiple seeds and confidence intervals for Table 3 and Table 5 would make the metric and verification-criteria ablations interpretable.
- Inference time comparison against TFOC and OmniCount (not only TFCAC) would more fully quantify the efficiency advantage.
- An ablation of the threshold parameter (currently fixed at 300) to demonstrate robustness or optimal selection.

## Removed Points
*These points are flagged for removal; treat them with caution.*

- **"One-shot results are uncompetitive":** The harsh reviewer frames one-shot MAE 18.57 as generous. Within the SAM-only category (Table 2), this is the best result (C-Anything: 33.53, TFOC: 22.96). Framing within the correct comparison group makes this a valid competitive result.
- **Probabilistic prompt generation novelty is overstated:** The harsh reviewer claims this is not fundamentally new. Without a specific prior art anchor (which we cannot verify), this criticism is too speculative to include.
- **Unfair framing of one-shot RMSE (135.86):** The critic says this "sits between training-based methods and other training-free methods." For SAM-only, this is the best one-shot result. Not a legitimate weakness.
- **τ = 0.066 is unexplained:** This is a legitimate minor issue but overlaps with the threshold ablation point above; merged rather than duplicated.

## Novel Insights
The most substantive observation is that SAM's decoder output tokens produce a qualitatively different feature space from image encoder embeddings — one that better separates instances even when image-level appearance is ambiguous. The PCA evidence in Figure 5 and the modest but directionally consistent Table 5 ablation suggest this is a real effect. The disconnect between this contribution and the RMSE results (essentially no improvement over vanilla SAM's tail errors) is itself informative: output token verification improves average discrimination but does not help in the dense-scene regime where SAM's segmentation itself breaks down. The community benefit lies in clearly scoping where this approach works, which the paper does not yet do rigorously.

---

## Suggestions
1. Rewrite the abstract to accurately scope results: "best among SAM-only training-free methods on FSC-147; superior to all supervised methods on CARPK" rather than a blanket superiority claim.
2. Add a density-stratified breakdown of FSC-147 results to explain the RMSE gap and characterize the method's failure regime quantitatively.
3. Extend Table 4 to include threshold sensitivity (300 ± range) alongside iteration/prompt ablation.
4. Report variance across at least 3 random seeds for the metric ablation (Table 3) and verification-criteria ablation (Table 5) given the small numeric differences that form the evidentiary base for two of the three claimed contributions.
