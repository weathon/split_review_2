## Summary
PCE (Planner-Composer-Evaluator) proposes a modular LLM pipeline for decentralized multi-agent embodied planning under partial observability. Instead of relying on frequent inter-agent communication to resolve uncertainty, it extracts implicit assumptions from LLM reasoning traces, organizes them into a decision tree (Composer), and scores each root-to-leaf path by scenario likelihood, conditional gain, and execution cost (Evaluator) — treating communication as one scoreable leaf action. Experiments on C-WAH (10 episodes) and TDW-MAT (24 episodes) across three LLM backbones, plus ablations, scaling analyses, and a user study, demonstrate consistent improvements over four communication-centric baselines.

## Strengths
- **Cross-backbone consistency (Tables 1–2, Figure 3):** PCE's performance advantage holds across GPT-4o mini, GPT-OSS:20B, and Gemma3:4B. The scaling ablation — Gemma3 4B/12B/27B and GPT-OSS:20B Low/Medium/High reasoning depth — cleanly isolates the structural benefit of the Composer–Evaluator from raw model scaling. The gap between PCE and Planner-only remains stable across all scales tested (Figure 3), making a non-trivial empirical point that explicit uncertainty structuring is orthogonal to scaling.
- **Communication as a first-class scoreable action:** The design treats communication as an evaluated leaf in the scenario tree rather than the coordination mechanism, a principled distinction from CoTS/CaPo/CoELA that is accurately characterized in Section 2. This is a concrete architectural choice with clear motivation.
- **Component ablations (Table 3):** Removing each module tells a coherent causal story. Notably, w/o Composer collapses Comm to 0.26 actions, directly confirming the Composer is responsible for generating communication branches rather than this being an emergent property.
- **User study (Section 5.3):** The three-way comparison (PCE vs. always-communicate vs. never-communicate) is the correct experimental design for the paper's thesis, and 7-point Likert responses (Appropriateness, Usefulness, Efficiency, Trust) are internally consistent with the quantitative findings.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Misleading "comparable token usage" claim in the abstract, directly contradicted by Table 2.** The abstract states PCE "consistently outperforms communication-centric baselines in success rate and task efficiency while showing **comparable** token usage." In TDW-MAT (Table 2), PCE uses 197,807 tokens vs. CoELA's 113,058 (GPT-4o mini) — 75% more; 337,225 vs. 237,499 (GPT-OSS:20B) — 42% more; 184,809 vs. 98,350 (Gemma3:4B) — 88% more. Section 5.1 argues that per-step overhead is "offset by PCE's substantial reduction in episode length," but *Table 2 already reports total episode-level token usage*, making this explanation inapplicable: the total token cost is higher regardless of per-step rates. The "comparable" claim holds in C-WAH (Table 1) but fails consistently in TDW-MAT. A reader of the abstract would conclude PCE is cheaper overall on both benchmarks; this is not what the data show. This is a misrepresentation of one of the paper's three stated advantages and requires correction before publication.

- **Very small evaluation sets with no variance reported.** C-WAH uses 10 episodes; TDW-MAT uses 24. No standard deviation, confidence interval, or statistical test appears in the main paper. The directional claims — "consistently outperforms," best performance bolded in every row — rest on sample sizes where a single episode difference could plausibly shift rankings, especially in C-WAH where margins between PCE and the second-best method (REVECA) are on the order of 4–17 steps across 10 episodes. The confident language throughout is not warranted without variance data.

### Minor

- **Circular LLM evaluation not acknowledged as a limitation in the main text.** The Evaluator uses an LLM to estimate L(S) (scenario likelihood) and G(a) (conditional gain) for scenarios generated by the LLM-based Planner. If the Planner is systematically biased toward certain room assumptions, the Evaluator may reproduce the same biases since both are reasoning over the same belief state. The paper defers reliability evidence to Appendix A.10/A.11 but includes no summary in the main body; a reader of Section 4.4 alone cannot assess whether the Evaluator scores correlate with ground truth.

- **User study order effects not addressed (Section 5.3).** Twelve participants experienced all three conditions within-subjects. The paper does not mention counterbalancing condition order or controlling for familiarity effects. In a within-subjects household task, later conditions benefit from environment familiarity, which could bias Likert ratings.

### Trivial
- **Figure 4 legend appears to list "PCE" twice** (two blue bars per category) per the figure description, suggesting a rendering or legend artifact. The paper should clarify the four-condition presentation if a fourth condition was included, or fix the legend.

## Novel Insights
The paper's most underappreciated finding is in Figure 3: explicit uncertainty structuring (PCE) and LLM scaling are **additive and non-redundant**. The Planner-only baseline improves modestly as capacity increases from 4B to 27B or as reasoning depth increases from Low to High, while PCE maintains a stable advantage above it at every scale. This cleanly isolates the "fragmented assumptions" failure mode — the tendency for large reasoning models to generate locally-grounded but globally-inconsistent beliefs in partially observable environments — as a bottleneck that neither parameter scale nor chain-of-thought depth resolves. This has implications for inference-time compute scaling research: structured external reasoning scaffolds may capture a different dimension of performance than depth-first reasoning expansion.
