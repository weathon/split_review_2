Now I have sufficient information to write the review. Let me draft it.## Summary
VisualPRM400K presents three coordinated contributions to multimodal test-time scaling: (1) VisualPRM400K, a ~400K-sample dataset generated via Monte Carlo rollout annotation using InternVL2.5 series models; (2) VisualPRM, an 8B-parameter multimodal Process Reward Model; and (3) VisualProcessBench, a 2,866-sample human-annotated benchmark for evaluating step-error detection. Under Best-of-8 evaluation, VisualPRM improves six policy models across seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks (up to +8.4 points for InternVL2.5-8B, +5.9 for InternVL2.5-78B) and also generalizes to text-only LLMs, while outperforming ORMs and self-consistency as the N scales to 128.

## Strengths

- **Broad cross-family BoN gains (Table 2).** Positive improvements across all six tested policy models—spanning MiniCPM-V2.6, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and four InternVL2.5 scales—covering independently developed model families. Cross-family generalization (MiniCPM +8.0, Qwen2.5-VL-7B +3.7) meaningfully supports the general-purpose critic claim over an in-distribution scoring artifact.

- **Informative BoN scaling curves (Figure 4).** N=1 to N=128 comparisons for SC, ORM, and PRM reveal that ORM plateaus or regresses past N=64 while PRM continues to improve, with the gap widening from 1.5 points at N=8 to 4.3 points at N=128. This is a practically significant and concretely grounded finding.

- **Text-only generalization (Table 5).** VisualPRM, trained entirely on multimodal data, lifts Qwen2.5-7B by +6.1/+5.0 on MATH-500/GPQA-Diamond and Qwen2.5-72B by +2.1/+6.6. A non-obvious finding that extends the scope beyond multimodal settings.

- **Ablation breadth (Table 4).** A single table comparing value-based vs. advantage-based PRM, three aggregation strategies (min/max/average), and early-stopping vs. full supervision—well-designed and sufficient to justify all key design choices.

- **VisualProcessBench construction quality.** 13 expert annotators with at least university-level education, 39 person-days, author review of ≥10% per split, and re-annotation of rejected splits, with macro-F1 to handle class imbalance. Credible QC process for a human-annotated benchmark.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Figure 1 is numerically inconsistent with Table 2.** Figure 1 lists InternVL2.5-78B three times with "#Pwoll" (baseline) values of 41.2, 50.7, and 51.9 respectively—yet Table 2 records a single Pass@1 baseline of 46.0 and VisualPRM BoN-8 of 51.9 for the same model. The caption reads only "Overall Best-of-8 evaluation results" with no explanation for the repeated entries or conflicting baseline numbers. The 78B model's baseline in Figure 1's first row (41.2) cannot be reconciled with Table 2 (46.0) under the caption's stated conditions. This is the paper's headline figure, and as written it is materially misleading, creating doubt about the reliability of the headline claims.

- **VisualPRM base model is never specified.** Section 3.2 gives the training objective in detail but never names the model VisualPRM is initialized from. The abstract states "8B parameters," implying InternVL2.5-8B (consistent with solutions being generated by "InternVL2.5 series models," Section 3.1), but this remains inferential. For a paper whose primary deliverable is a model artifact intended for community adoption, this is a meaningful reproducibility gap.

### Minor

- **No inter-annotator agreement statistics for VisualProcessBench.** Section 3.3 describes author review of ≥10% per split and re-annotation of rejected splits, but provides no Cohen's κ, percentage agreement, or rejection-rate count. Without this, the community cannot calibrate trust in the labels for a benchmark intended for widespread use.

- **Step-granularity heuristic not ablated.** Section 3.1 states: "we set the max number of steps to 12 and evenly merge the steps if the number of current steps exceeds the threshold." This is a genuine design choice—coarsely merged steps may confound the step-wise correctness signal—yet no ablation over this threshold is provided.

### Trivial

- Several smaller improvements (e.g., MathVision +1.3 for MiniCPM, Table 2) are cited alongside larger ones without confidence intervals; given test sets of a few hundred examples, these specific deltas may be within noise. The large overall improvements (+8.4, +5.9) are robust, but individual small per-benchmark deltas should be interpreted cautiously.

## Novel Insights

The finding that ORMs regress past N=64 while PRMs continue scaling (Figure 4) is a practically actionable and under-discussed result: it implies that ORMs suffer an exploitability ceiling absent in step-level scoring, and practitioners deploying TTS beyond moderate N should strongly prefer PRMs. The text-only generalization of a multimodal PRM (Table 5) is also non-obvious and suggests that visual reasoning supervision provides a richer or more diverse signal for reward modeling than text-only data alone—a direction worth exploring for general-purpose PRM training.
