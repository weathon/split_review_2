## Summary

This paper identifies a frequency-bias problem in sparse autoencoder (SAE) feature explanations for LLMs — linguistic patterns dominate over discourse-level semantics in raw-activation-based explanation methods (TopAct, N2G). The authors propose a fixed-vocabulary mutual-information-based explanation method that normalizes word activations across learned features to suppress frequent but uninformative words, yielding more discourse-level explanations. They further design two runtime steering strategies (Amplification and Calibration) for jailbreak defense. Experiments on Mistral-7B-Instruct show higher explanation rates than baseline methods and a reduction in attack success rate (Salad-Bench ASR: 81.6%→72.8%) when steering with the proposed explanations, while TopAct/N2G-based steering shows no improvement.

## Strengths

- **Formal theoretical derivation of the frequency bias problem (Section 3.1, Eq. 1–2):** The paper models text generation as a topic-model-based dynamic process and shows that linguistic pattern components (e_{ε_n}) outnumber discourse topic components (e_{c_0}) by a factor of N per instance. This provides a principled explanation for why raw-activation-based methods are dominated by trivial lexical patterns — a problem prior SAE explanation work identified empirically but did not formally model.

- **Mutual information-based explanation objective with fixed vocabulary (Section 3.2, Eq. 3–5):** Rather than collecting activating text spans from a corpus, the method selects words from a curated 5,000-word vocabulary using an MI objective that penalizes words with uniformly high activation across all features (line 98–99: "if a word embedding constantly leads to a significant large dot product with all features, the word will not express enough specificity"). This is a principled solution to the frequency bias, and Table 2 shows it yields higher explanation rates than both TopAct and N2G.

- **Controlled comparison showing explanation quality drives steering effectiveness (Section 4.3, Figure 3):** Applying the same Aware Security steering strategy, feature sets selected via TopAct and N2G explanations fail to reduce ASR, while the proposed method's feature set succeeds. This provides direct empirical evidence that discourse-level explanations are more useful for downstream steering — a causal claim the paper is uniquely positioned to test.

- **Honest reporting of ablation findings (Section 4.3.2):** The paper candidly reports that erasing harmful features (EH) has "no significant contribution" and all gains come from amplifying safety awareness (AS), even though this contradicts the intuitive expectation. The similarity to Self-Reminder's mechanism is acknowledged rather than obscured.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Word embeddings e_w are not specified (Section 3.2, Eq. 5):** The core explanation method computes softmax-normalized dot products ⟨e_w, W_c⟩ between word embeddings e_w and SAE feature vectors W_c. The paper never states what e_w is — whether it is the LLM's input token embedding matrix, its unembedding layer, or an external word vector. Because the geometric meaningfulness of these dot products depends on e_w and W_c residing in compatible vector spaces, and because the softmax over all vocabulary items in Eq. 5 requires these dot products to be comparable across words, this omission is a genuine reproducibility gap. The paper must clarify this to make the method reproducible.

### Minor

- **Explanation evaluation compares different output modalities without cross-validation (Section 4.2, Table 2):** TopAct and N2G produce contiguous text spans (up to 10 tokens), while the proposed method produces ranked lists of individual words from a restricted vocabulary of 5,000 common words. The GPT-4o annotator judges relevance given these different formats, introducing an uncalibrated format confound. While the annotator can assess relevance across formats (and Table 1's qualitative examples suggest the evaluation is meaningful), the paper would be strengthened by either (a) validating GPT-4o judgments against a human study on a representative subset, or (b) adapting all methods to a common output format.

- **Absolute steering performance is modest (Section 4.3, Table 3):** ASR drops from 81.6% to 72.8%, meaning the steered model still succumbs to nearly three-quarters of attacks. MT-Bench drops from 6.5 to 6.0 — a noticeable helpfulness degradation. The paper characterizes this as "strong jailbreak defense ability" (line 186), which overstates the result. The improvement is meaningful but modest.

- **No ablation of layer choice (Section 4.1):** The 8th layer (24% depth) is selected based on wanting "room to change LLM predictions," citing prior work's layer selection heuristic. No experiment tests whether different layers yield different explanation quality or steering effectiveness, leaving the sensitivity of the method to this architectural choice unexamined.

- **No SAE quality metrics reported:** The paper trains a Top-K SAE with C=2^16 features on 113M tokens but does not report reconstruction loss, fraction of variance explained, feature density, or the value of K. These are standard diagnostic metrics in the SAE literature (e.g., Gao et al., 2024; Lieberum et al., 2024) and would help assess whether the learned features are of reasonable quality.

### Trivial

- **Unsupported claim about N2G producing "fake" patterns (Section 4.2.2, line 153):** The paper states that "N2G shows a stronger preference for lexical patterns than TopAct, even if they are fake ones," but provides no evidence or citation for the "fake" characterization. This claim should be either substantiated or removed.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A cleaner ablation that fixes the SAE and the steering strategy and varies only the feature selection method (proposed MI vs. TopAct vs. N2G vs. keyword heuristic vs. random) would isolate whether the explanation method specifically drives the improvement.
- Reporting results across multiple random seeds or with confidence intervals would strengthen quantitative claims.
- A limitations/broader impact section discussing failure modes and potential for misuse would be appropriate for a paper applying its method to safety.

## Novel Insights

The reviews largely converge on the paper's own contributions. One observation worth noting: the finding that erasing harmful features (EH) has no effect while amplifying safety awareness (AS) works, combined with the paper's acknowledgment that AS is conceptually similar to Self-Reminder's approach, raises a question the paper does not fully address — whether the SAE-based steering is doing anything fundamentally different from a well-designed system prompt, or whether the advantage is simply that feature-level steering is more targeted. The paper's demonstration that TopAct/N2G explanations cannot select effective features, while the proposed method can, suggests that the explanation quality is the bottleneck, but the mechanism of the improvement (feature amplification) is not novel relative to Self-Reminder. This tension is worth exploring in future work.

## Suggestions

- **Specify word embeddings:** State explicitly what e_w is (e.g., the LLM's input token embedding matrix at the same layer, or the unembedding matrix) and justify why the dot product between e_w and W_c is geometrically meaningful.
- **Add human validation of explanation evaluation:** Conduct a small human study (e.g., 100–200 examples) to calibrate GPT-4o's relevance judgments against human raters, addressing the format-comparability concern.
- **Report SAE quality diagnostics:** Include reconstruction loss, fraction of variance explained, average number of active features per token, and the Top-K value K to enable assessment of feature quality.
- **Add layer ablation:** Test steering effectiveness at different layers (e.g., 10%, 50%, 75% depth) to verify the 24% choice is not critical.
- **Tone down steering claims:** Characterize the ASR reduction as "promising" rather than "strong," and explicitly note the 72.8% residual ASR alongside the claimed improvement.
- **Substantiate or remove the "fake patterns" claim about N2G.**
