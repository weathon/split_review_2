## Summary

This paper proposes Adversarial Preference-based Policy Optimization (APPO), an offline PbRL algorithm that replaces the intractable confidence-set constructions of prior work with a two-player game formulation between a policy and a reward/value model. The core technical contributions are (1) a reparameterization that uses the performance difference lemma to express the inner optimization purely in terms of the offline data distribution \(d^\mu\), eliminating the need for online rollouts, and (2) a trajectory-pair \(\ell_1\) loss that enables a sample complexity bound (Theorem 4.1) without explicit confidence sets. Experiments on Meta-world continuous control tasks show competitive performance against existing offline PbRL methods.

## Strengths

- **Reparameterization via induced reward models (Definition 1) is a genuine technical innovation.** The paper leverages the performance difference lemma (Eq. 6) and the Bellman equation to rewrite the inner optimization over reward models as an optimization over action-value functions \(f \in \mathcal{F}^H\), where expectations are taken under the offline data distribution \(d^\mu\) rather than requiring on-policy rollouts. This is concretely described in Section 3.3 (lines 146–174) and is what distinguishes Algorithm 2 from the rollout-based Algorithm 1.

- **Novel sub-optimality decomposition that ties adversarial training to provable conservatism.** The proof sketch (Eq. 9, lines 218–234) decomposes the sub-optimality into MLE error, optimization error, and policy update regret. The key step is showing that when \(\lambda > C_{\mathrm{TR}}\), the optimization error is bounded by the trajectory-pair \(\ell_1\) loss via the concentrability inequality — this clean argument is what allows the analysis to avoid confidence sets.

- **Sample complexity for unlabeled data does not depend on transition concentrability \(C_P\).** As noted in the discussion of Theorem 4.1 (line 214), APPO's requirement on unlabeled trajectories \(N\) avoids the \(C_P^2\) factor that appears in FREEHAND-transition and Sim-OPRL, which "may grow exponentially" (Proposition 2, Zhan et al., 2024a). This is a meaningful practical advantage backed by the theoretical comparison.

- **First sample complexity bound for a computationally tractable offline PbRL algorithm under general function approximation.** The paper provides a finite-sample bound (Theorem 4.1) while prior work (FREEHAND-transition, Sim-OPRL) relies on tri-level constrained optimization or confidence-set-width evaluation — both intractable with neural function approximation. The contrast is drawn clearly in Sections 1.1 and 3.3 (lines 176–182).

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **Significant gap between the algorithm that is theoretically analyzed (Algorithm 2) and the algorithm that is implemented (Section 5).** The theoretical analysis assumes a finite-horizon episodic MDP with a learned transition model \(\hat{P}\) used in the induced reward computation \(r_{\hat{P},f}^{\pi^t}\) (Eq. 8). The practical implementation switches to a discounted infinite-horizon setting, replaces the transition-model-based induced reward with the one-step bootstrap approximation \(P^\star(Q_\phi \circ \pi_\theta)(s_l,a_l) \approx (Q_\phi \circ \pi_\theta)(s_{l+1})\), and introduces clipped double Q-learning, target networks, a separate \(V_\psi\) function, and SAC-style entropy regularization — none of which appear in the theoretical analysis. The paper provides no argument, formal or heuristic, that the sample complexity guarantee survives these approximations. This severs the claimed connection between "provable" and "practical."

- **Theoretical requirement \(\lambda > C_{\mathrm{TR}}\) is inconsistent with the empirical choice \(\lambda = 3\times10^{-2}\).** Theorem 4.1 requires \(\lambda = \Theta(C_{\mathrm{TR}})\) with \(\lambda > C_{\mathrm{TR}}\), where \(C_{\mathrm{TR}}\) is the trajectory concentrability coefficient. The paper itself notes that concentrability coefficients "may grow exponentially" (line 214, discussing prior work's \(C_P\); a similar concern applies to \(C_{\mathrm{TR}}\)). Yet the experiments use a fixed tiny constant \(\lambda = 3\times10^{-2}\) (line 284). The paper provides no justification that \(C_{\mathrm{TR}} < 0.03\) holds in these benchmarks. Either the theoretical condition is not satisfied, or the practical algorithm operates in a regime where the guarantee does not apply. Either way, the link between theory and experiments is broken on a critical parameter.

### Minor

- **Experimental evaluation is too narrow to fully substantiate the paper's claims.** Results cover only one benchmark (Meta-world medium-replay), four tasks visible in Table 1, with baseline numbers taken from Choi et al. (2024) rather than re-run in a controlled setting. Table 1 reports only means over 5 seeds with no variance indicated. No ablation cleanly isolates the contribution of the adversarial regularizer from the reward model choice — APPO uses "the same reward model as MR" (line 284), so a direct MR vs. APPO comparison would isolate the effect of the \(\ell_1\) adversarial term, but this is not presented as a structured ablation.

- **Computational efficiency — the paper's central motivation — is asserted but not empirically demonstrated.** The paper's entire motivation is that prior offline PbRL methods are "computationally intractable" while APPO is "computationally efficient." Yet no wall-clock time, optimization iteration counts, memory usage, or comparisons against tractable approximations of prior methods are provided. The formal argument (tri-level optimization vs. standard non-convex optimization, lines 176–182) is valid at the problem-formulation level, but without empirical evidence, the reader cannot judge whether this formal distinction translates into a practical advantage.

- **Claim of "only one algorithmic hyperparameter" (line 293) is misleading.** The practical implementation inherits all hyperparameters of the deep RL framework (learning rates, network sizes, target network update rates, entropy coefficient \(\alpha\), etc.). The statement should clarify that \(\lambda\) is the *only additional* hyperparameter beyond those standard in the base architecture.

- **Equation (10) has a typo:** the left-hand side reads \(V_{1,r^\star}^{\pi^\star} - V_{1,r^\star}^{\pi^\star}\), which is identically zero and cannot equal the non-trivial right-hand side. The second term should be \(V_{1,r^\star}^{\hat{\pi}}\) (line 219).

### Trivial

- None beyond the minor issues above.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A discussion of how the one-step TD approximation used in the practical implementation could be analyzed as a form of model error, bridging the theory-practice gap.
- A sensitivity analysis on \(\lambda\) with quantitative guidance on how to set it relative to task properties.

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The adversarial game formulation for offline PbRL (borrowing ideas from offline standard RL) and the reparameterization trick are the paper's own novel insights. The reviews do not surface a genuinely novel synthesis that the paper itself missed.

## Suggestions

1. **Reduce the theory-practice gap.** Either (a) extend the theoretical analysis to cover the actual implemented algorithm (e.g., analyzing the one-step TD approximation as bounded model error), or (b) modify the practical implementation to more faithfully instantiate the theoretical algorithm (e.g., actually using the learned transition model \(\hat{P}\) in the induced reward computation).
2. **Address the \(\lambda > C_{\mathrm{TR}}\) inconsistency.** Provide at least a heuristic argument or empirical evidence that the trajectory concentrability coefficient is bounded by 0.03 in the evaluated tasks, or adjust the practical algorithm to satisfy the theoretical condition.
3. **Demonstrate computational efficiency empirically.** Provide wall-clock training time or optimization iteration counts comparing APPO against a tractable approximation of at least one confidence-set-based prior method.
4. **Expand experimental scope.** Add more tasks (e.g., D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks standard in offline RL), re-run baselines in a controlled codebase, and report variances.
5. **Fix the typo in Equation (10).** The left-hand side refers to \(V_{1,r^\star}^{\pi^\star}\) twice instead of referencing \(\hat{\pi}\).
