## Summary

This paper proposes **X** (**E**xact **U**nlearning using **P**EFT), a framework that extends the SISA exact-unlearning paradigm by (a) training LoRA layers sequentially on disjoint data slices so that deletion can be accomplished by simply deactivating the affected LoRA layer rather than retraining, and (b) pre-training multiple models on different slice permutations to survive more deletion requests before full retraining is needed. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the deletion rate (Lemma 1) and evaluates the method on vision models (86M–307M params) for unlearning and on LLMs (7B–13B) for training quality.

---

## Strengths

- **Parameter isolation via sequential LoRA deactivation is a genuinely novel mechanism for exact unlearning.** Instead of retraining or swapping checkpoints (SISA), the paper trains LoRA layers top-to-bottom on cumulative data slices (Section 3.2, lines 80–98). Deletion is achieved by deactivating the LoRA layers exposed to the deleted slice, which yields an *L*-time storage reduction over SISA (line 96). This is a clean architectural departure from prior exact-unlearning work.

- **Theoretical derivation of deletion rate with provable improvement over SISA.** Lemma 1 (line 156) defines the deletion rate and proves δ(**X**) ∼ O(*mL* log(*m* min(*B*, *L*))) versus δ(SISA) ∼ O(*mL* log *m*), formally establishing an asymptotic improvement. The additional result that *L* sequences suffice for optimal deletion rate (line 159) is a non-trivial bound that grounds the practical feasibility.

- **Empirical validation of slice-wise training quality on LLMs up to 13B parameters.** Table 1 (lines 200–210) shows that the sequential slice-wise training matches or exceeds full fine-tuning across 8 benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, PIQA, Winogrande, ARC-c/e, TruthfulQA, OBQA) for Llama2-7B, Llama2-13B, and Llama3-8B. This establishes that the training procedure does not degrade performance at practical LLM scales.

- **Concrete deletion-time gains in the tested regime.** The wrapfigure experiment (line 270) reports 2.8× reduction in total deletion time versus SISA and 25× versus full retraining after 1000 requests on CIFAR-10. This directly measures the practical benefit the framework claims.

- **Sequence selection algorithms (cyclic rotation, BMS) with empirical validation.** Section 3.3 motivates and evaluates two algorithms for selecting diverse slice permutations under a budget. Figure 10 shows these produce substantially more diverse sequences than random sampling, supporting the design.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **Missing standard-LoRA baseline for RQ1.** The paper compares its sequential slice-wise training against full fine-tuning (FT) in Table 1 and Figure 3, but never against standard (joint) LoRA training, where all LoRA layers are trained together on all data. Since LoRA is a reduced-parameter method, comparing against FT confounds two factors: (a) the expressiveness gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning, and (b) the effect of the sequential slice-wise isolation itself. RQ1 is framed as "does **X** training impact performance compared to full training?" (line 180), but the paper's own thesis concerns whether the sequential *isolation per se* causes degradation. A standard LoRA baseline would directly answer this. Without it, the reader cannot tell whether the reported "comparable to FT" result means the sequential training is lossless or whether it merely reflects LoRA's typical performance relative to FT.

- **Unlearning evaluated only on vision models (86M–307M), despite the title's emphasis on scalability and the centrality of PEFT/LoRA.** The paper evaluates LLMs (7B–13B) *only* for training quality (RQ1). The actual unlearning capability — handling deletion requests, performance under deletions, deletion rate — is tested exclusively on ViT on CIFAR-10/100. Given that the title promises "Scalable Exact Machine Unlearning Using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning" and the method's efficiency motivation (PEFT, avoiding retraining) is most relevant at LLM scale, the gap between the scale of the training demonstration and the scale of the unlearning demonstration is a significant evidential weakness. The unlearning mechanism (deactivating LoRA layers) is architecture-agnostic, but the paper provides no empirical evidence that the system works for its primary claim at LLM scale. Even a small-scale canary/golden-token test on a 7B model would substantially strengthen the scalability claim.

- **Pre-training cost not quantified in the deletion-time comparison.** **X** trains *B* models per shard (pre-computing multiple slice permutations), while SISA trains 1. The deletion-time comparison (Figure 12, line 270) reports "total deletion time" but this includes only checkpoint-swapping and retraining costs, not the upfront cost of training the *B* models. The paper acknowledges "additional offline training" (line 17), but does not report or amortize this cost. For B=4, this is 4× the pre-training compute per shard relative to SISA. While the pre-training is a one-time cost and deletion-time savings recur, the absence of this accounting means a practitioner cannot assess the total cost trade-off. This does not invalidate the headline deletion-time results, but it contextualizes them in a way the paper does not provide.

### Minor

- **No empirical verification of the exact-unlearning guarantee.** The paper argues for exact unlearning by construction (deactivating LoRA layers never updated on the deleted data), which is theoretically sound and follows the same logic as SISA. However, standard practice in the broader unlearning literature includes at minimum a membership-inference or influence test on the deleted vs. retained data to confirm that the deactivated layers actually remove detectable influence. While not a fatal gap — the theoretical argument is clear — the absence of any such test leaves an empirical loose end, especially given the subtlety that layers higher in the stack are trained on cumulative slices (e.g., Layer 2 is trained on slices 1+2, and deactivating it changes the forward-pass representations for slice 1 data passing through remaining layers).

- **Underspecified LoRA layer-to-slice mapping.** Line 91 states "we can even train multiple LoRA layers per slice" without specifying how many LoRA layers were actually trained per slice in the experiments. For a 12-layer ViT with L=4 slices, how are layers mapped to slices? This underspecification affects reproducibility.

- **Hyperparameter *t* in the non-uniform prior score function not discussed or ablated.** Line 133 introduces the score function score[𝒮, t] = Σ i·(1−Σⱼ≤ᵢ pⱼ)^t, where *t* is a hyperparameter. The paper does not describe how *t* is set, whether it is cross-validated, or how sensitive the BMS algorithm is to its value.

- **No confidence intervals or error bars for deletion experiments.** The deletion-rate comparisons (Figures 5, 6, 7) and deletion-time results (Figure 12) are reported as single curves without variance estimates. Table 1 does report standard deviations for the LLM experiments, but the unlearning experiments lack them, making it impossible to assess the significance of the reported gains (e.g., "up to 2.3× and 3× gains over SISA," line 251).

### Trivial

- **No limitations discussion.** The paper does not discuss known limitations: the coarse granularity of deletion (slice-level, not instance-level), the constraint that the topmost slice is a bottleneck (if the top slice of all *B* models is deleted, the system fails), the reliance on a pre-trained model (applicability to training from scratch is unclear), and the fact that the pre-training cost scales with B.

## Nice-to-Haves

- A standard (joint) LoRA baseline for Table 1 and Figure 3 would directly isolate the cost of sequential slice-wise isolation.
- Reporting total system cost (pre-training + amortized deletion-time cost) for a realistic deletion workload would clarify the practical trade-off.
- Adding a canary/membership-inference experiment for the CIFAR unlearning setup would close the empirical gap on the exactness claim.
