## Summary

This paper introduces GRAFT, a method to train vision-language models for satellite imagery without any textual annotations by using co-located ground-level internet images (from Flickr) as an intermediary between satellite images and CLIP's text-aligned embedding space. The authors collect two large-scale datasets (10.2M NAIP pairs, 8.7M Sentinel-2 pairs) and train both image-level and pixel-level models. Experiments across zero-shot classification, retrieval, segmentation, and VQA show that GRAFT, trained without any text annotations, outperforms existing supervised VLMs (CLIP-RSICD, RemoteCLIP) by substantial margins on several benchmarks.

## Strengths

- **Unsupervised VLM training that outperforms supervised VLMs**: The central claim is convincingly supported. GRAFT ViT-B/16 outperforms supervised models (CLIP-RSICD, RemoteCLIP) by ~10 points (20% relative) on EuroSAT classification and ~30 points on SAT-6 (lines 241–243). This directly demonstrates that the unsupervised intermediary approach is not a workaround but a superior strategy.

- **Novel ground-as-intermediary paradigm**: The paper clearly distinguishes itself from prior geo-localization work (line 94): "Different from these works, we use ground images as an intermediary to connect remote sensing images with language — a novel uncharted problem domain." This redefines the problem from "annotate satellite images with text" to "leverage existing ground-image-to-text alignment in CLIP," enabling dataset scales (10.2M/8.7M pairs) that are orders of magnitude larger than existing RS text datasets (~10k pairs).

- **Ablation that directly validates the image-intermediary design**: Section 5.4 compares using ground-image CLIP embeddings vs. alt-text embeddings. Replacing images with alt-text drops performance from 74.8% to 65.4% mAP, and even using text as auxiliary loss yields only 70% mAP. This cleanly isolates and validates the core design choice.

- **Principled contrastive loss for many-to-one satellite-to-ground mapping**: Equation 1 (line 134) defines a loss where each satellite image pulls toward *all* co-located ground images, with principled normalization. When N_i=1 it reverts to standard CLIP loss, showing thoughtful adaptation rather than naive application.

- **Large-scale dataset release**: The 10.2M NAIP and 8.7M Sentinel-2 ground-satellite pairs represent a significant community resource, dwarfing existing RS text-image datasets by orders of magnitude.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **Segmentation and VQA evaluations use limited baselines on modified benchmarks**: For segmentation (Section 5.2), only CLIPSeg is compared — a general-domain model not designed for satellite imagery. The "80% improvement" and "doubling accuracy" claims are relative to this single, weak baseline. For VQA (Section 5.3), only GLIP (via ViperGPT) is compared, and the evaluation subsamples 500 questions and replaces images with NAIP imagery — a custom protocol that departs from the standard RSVQA benchmark. While the paper discloses these choices (API restrictions for VQA, absence of categories in test set for segmentation), the most dramatic claims rest on a narrow evidential base. Adding even one or two competitive baselines per task would substantially strengthen the paper.

- **The pixel-level model's sparse supervision receives no analysis**: Section 3.1.2 computes loss only on patches containing ground image locations, with at most 25 ground images per 224×224 satellite image (~13% of patches supervised for ViT-B/16). The paper states "empirically we found that this sparse supervision suffices" (line 156) without any supporting analysis — no histogram of supervised patches per image, no ablation on the cap of 25 ground images, no correlation with downstream performance. This is a meaningful gap in understanding how the pixel-level model works and whether its performance could be improved with denser supervision.

### Minor
- **The classification/retrieval evaluation does not include zero-shot comparison with self-supervised RS foundation models**: The paper compares against supervised VLMs (CLIP-RSICD, RemoteCLIP) and provides one-shot baselines for SatMAE/SSL4EO/etc. However, an alternative zero-shot setup (e.g., using frozen features from SatMAE/SSL4EO with CLIP's text encoder as a classifier) is not explored as a baseline. The one-shot comparisons that are provided (line 249–250) are informative but are noted by the authors as noisy due to single-example evaluation.

- **No discussion of geographic/selection bias in Flickr data**: The paper shows frequency histograms (Figure 4) but does not analyze whether certain biomes, land-use types, or regions are systematically over- or under-represented in the Flickr ground images. Flickr users skew toward certain demographics and geographies, and iconic landmarks may be over-photographed. The paper's location-uniform sampling mitigates some of this, but the remaining bias is not discussed or quantified.

### Trivial
- The paper references an appendix (\cref{app:datasetstats}, \cref{app:prompting}, etc.) for details that are standard to include inline or would have been helpful to see in the main text.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Report variance (standard deviations or confidence intervals) for all results, especially one-shot evaluations.
- Evaluate on unmodified Satlas (all 11 categories, with explanation for absent ones) and standard RSVQA benchmark without image replacement to enable direct comparison with prior work.
- Analyze how pixel-level model performance correlates with supervision density (number of ground images per satellite image).
- Provide geographic/environmental diversity statistics for the collected datasets beyond frequency histograms.
- Compare against at least one RS-specific segmentation method (e.g., linear probe on SatMAE/SpectralGPT features with a segmentation decoder).

- **Speculation about segmentation category removal**: The harsh critic suggested categories "were removed because GRAFT cannot handle them." This is unsupported by the paper, which clearly states the categories do not appear in the test set. Removed as an unsubstantiated strawman.

## Novel Insights
None beyond the paper's own contributions.

## Suggestions
1. For the camera-ready version, add at least one additional baseline for segmentation (e.g., linear probe on a recent RS foundation model's features with a segmentation head) and for VQA (e.g., CLIP-based zero-shot approach or a fine-tuned RS VLM).
2. Provide quantitative analysis of the pixel-level model's sparse supervision: number of patches supervised per image, ablation on the 25-ground-image cap, and correlation with downstream segmentation accuracy.
3. Discuss and ideally quantify geographic/environmental bias in the Flickr ground image source.
4. Clarify in the abstract and claims that the 80% segmentation improvement is relative to the single CLIPSeg baseline on a modified evaluation, to avoid overclaiming.
