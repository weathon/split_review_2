## Summary

The paper proposes DetEmbedMetrics, a framework that fine-tunes a language model to generate multiple specialized embeddings (one per relationship class). These embeddings, when passed through deterministic functions (Manhattan distance for similarity, Vendi score for diversity), produce discrete probability distributions over three categories: same/related/unrelated for similarity, and no difference/diverse/totally different for diversity. The method uses a Sinkhorn distance loss that penalizes deviations from a diagonal label matrix. Experiments on synthetic LLM-generated data show the fine-tuned model outperforms zero-shot embeddings, generalizes across different deterministic functions, and can transfer across domains with modest additional data.

## Strengths

1. **Clear performance advantage over zero-shot usage of the same embedding models.** In Experiment 1 (Section 4.2, Table 1), DetEmbedMetrics substantially outperforms unmodified "all-mpnet-base-v2" and "BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5" when both use the same deterministic functions (Manhattan distance, Vendi score). This shows that the fine-tuning meaningfully reshapes the embeddings for the target classification task. The comparison is fair in that it isolates the effect of fine-tuning on the same base model.

2. **Robustness across multiple deterministic function choices.** Experiment 2 (Section 4.3, Table 2) tests Manhattan/Euclidean/Cosine similarity functions and Vendi score with Manhattan/Cosine/RBF kernels. The model maintains strong performance across variations, supporting the claim that the approach does not rely on a specific measurement technique.

3. **Cross-domain generalization demonstrated with systematic two-step design.** Experiment 3 (Section 4.4, Figure 3) trains on art-domain synthetic data and tests on sports-domain data from both LLaMA-3 8B and GPT-4o. The model achieves good performance in the new domain with only ~40–168 additional labeled samples while maintaining original-domain performance. The two-step incremental design carefully separates the learning curve from domain adaptation.

4. **Loss function and architecture choices are motivated by task structure.** The paper identifies that standard average pooling of LM outputs is inadequate for generating discrete class outputs (Section 3.2), motivating the N-embedding architecture. Similarly, the Sinkhorn distance loss is justified by the diagonal structure of the label matrix, with the paper reporting that cross-entropy empirically fails (Section 3.3).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **The evaluation does not connect to the claimed application of data valuation.** The paper is titled "Language Models for Textual Data Valuation" and repeatedly frames the method as enabling data quality assessment, data selection in marketplaces, and data-centric ML. However, every experiment only tests whether DetEmbedMetrics can classify text pairs into the three synthetic categories used during training. No experiment uses DetEmbedMetrics scores to actually select, filter, or rank training data for a downstream task, or compares the scores against any established measure of data quality from the valuation literature. The central question — "does this metric produce scores that correlate with actual data quality in ML pipelines?" — is never addressed. This gap between the paper's ambitious framing and the narrow evaluation is the most significant weakness.

2. **Comparisons are against zero-shot models only; proper baselines for isolating the contribution are missing.** Comparing DetEmbedMetrics against two embedding models used in zero-shot fashion shows only that fine-tuning on a task beats not fine-tuning. The paper does not compare against:
   - The same base model fine-tuned with standard contrastive or triplet loss (to test whether the N-embedding architecture and Sinkhorn loss provide any benefit over standard approaches)
   - A direct classification head on pooled embeddings (which would test whether the N-embedding generation is necessary)
   - Simple statistical baselines such as TF-IDF cosine similarity or n-gram overlap
   - Direct prompting of an LLM to classify the three relationship categories
   
   Without these, the specific contribution of the DetEmbedMetrics architecture (N separate embeddings per text + Sinkhorn loss + deterministic functions) over substantially simpler alternatives is not established.

3. **Evaluation is entirely on synthetic, LLM-generated data.** All data is generated by LLaMA-3 8B and GPT-4o following an artificial four-part structure (D0–D3 with predetermined relationship labels). The "cross-domain generalization" experiment moves from synthetic art to synthetic sports — both LLM-generated. No evaluation is conducted on any real, naturally occurring text dataset. The example texts shown are trivially simple ("I love dogs so much" / "I am a dog person"), and it is unclear whether the reported performance would hold on nuanced, naturally occurring text where relationship boundaries are less clear.

### Minor

4. **Interpretability claims are asserted but not demonstrated.** The paper claims that discrete probability outputs offer "clearer interpretation and decision-making" (abstract) and address "interpretability challenges" (Section 2). The only illustration is a hypothetical example with fabricated numbers (Section 2). No human evaluation, user study, or analysis is provided to substantiate that the discrete outputs are more interpretable or useful than continuous scores. While the paper hedges somewhat ("potential minor gains"), the abstract-level claim remains unsupported.

5. **No variance or uncertainty reported for main experimental results.** Experiments 1 and 2 (Tables 1 and 2) report accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 without confidence intervals, standard deviations, or run counts. Only Experiment 3 (Section 4.4) mentions averaging over 10 iterations. Without variance estimates, the reader cannot assess whether the reported improvements over baselines are statistically reliable.

6. **Application of Vendi score to pairwise comparison is underspecified.** The paper defines the Vendi score (Equation 2) for a set of items, computing diversity *within* that set. However, the task requires measuring diversity *between* two texts (D0 and D1). The paper mentions using a "Manhattan distance kernel" (Section 4.3) but never formally specifies how Vendi score is applied to a text pair — e.g., whether the kernel matrix is constructed from the concatenated embeddings of both texts, or whether some other mechanism is used. This ambiguity affects reproducibility.

7. **Failure of cross-entropy is stated but not shown.** The paper claims that cross-entropy "fails to train the model effectively" (Section 3.3) as justification for the Sinkhorn loss, but does not present the corresponding experimental results or comparison. The reader cannot assess whether cross-entropy actually fails or whether the Sinkhorn loss provides meaningful improvement.

### Trivial
- The synthetic data examples shown are very simple, making it unclear how the method generalizes to more complex text relationships.

## Nice-to-Haves
- A downstream data valuation experiment (e.g., using DetEmbedMetrics scores to select training data for a text classifier and showing improved performance over selection by random sampling, Vendi score alone, or Shapley-based methods) would directly substantiate the paper's central claims.
- An ablation study isolating the contributions of the N-embedding architecture vs. the Sinkhorn loss vs. the deterministic functions would clarify which design choices matter.
- Including at least one real-world text dataset would strengthen generalizability claims.
