## Summary
The paper proposes the Adaptive Clustering (AC) router for Sparse Mixture-of-Experts, which scales the token-expert dot-product space by per-cluster per-dimension dispersion statistics so that features where an expert clusters tightly are upweighted. The motivation is grounded in a feature-weighted clustering optimization (Section 2) whose closed-form solution (Theorem 1) yields weights inversely proportional to per-dimension cluster dispersion. The paper claims three connected benefits: faster convergence, better robustness, and improved overall performance, supporting these with theoretical lemmas and experiments on language (WikiText-103, EnWik-8, finetuning) and vision (ImageNet, adversarial/OOD) tasks.

## Strengths

- **Optimal feature-weight derivation (Theorem 1, Eqn. 6).** The paper formulates MoE routing as a feature-weighted clustering problem and derives a closed-form solution for per-cluster feature weights. The inverse-dispersion form ($w_{qk} \propto 1/s_{qk}$) has a clean operational interpretation and is the analytic optimum for the proposed KL-regularized objective. This is the paper's cleanest contribution.

- **Zero additional learnable parameters.** The AC router requires no learnable parameters beyond the existing expert embeddings; all gains come from dispersion-based feature rescaling (Definition 2). This is a genuine engineering advantage.

- **Consistent improvements claimed across two modalities and three backbones.** The paper reports improvements on language (Switch, GLaM on WikiText-103, EnWik-8, SST2/5, Banking-77) and vision (Swin on ImageNet, ImageNet-A/O/R). The 4.8% improvement on WikiText-103 PPL (GLaM-medium) and 7%/5% improvements under PGD/SPSA attacks (Swin) are described as practically meaningful margins.

- **Empirical convergence speed demonstration.** Figure 2 shows faster convergence on WikiText-103 (GLaM) and Banking-77 (Switch), providing direct empirical evidence that the method reaches lower perplexity/higher accuracy in fewer steps than baselines.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Lemma 1 is not generally true as stated.** The lemma claims $\|\mu_k - \mu_a\|^2_{M_a} \geq \|\mu_k - \mu_a\|^2$. Expanding: $\sum_q (\mu_{kq} - \mu_{aq})^2 / s_{qa} \geq \sum_q (\mu_{kq} - \mu_{aq})^2$. For this to hold without additional conditions, every $1/s_{qa} \geq 1$ (i.e., $s_{qa} \leq 1$) is required. The paper states the entries of $M_k$ are scaled to have mean 1 (line 123), but this does **not** guarantee the inequality: dimensions where $s_{qa} > 1$ will have $1/s_{qa} < 1$, potentially making the left side smaller. The inequality could hold or reverse depending on which dimensions are discriminative between clusters. No assumption connects dispersion to discriminative power. Since Proposition 1 ("The proof … is a direct result of combining Lemmas 1 and 2") rests on Lemma 1, the theoretical robustness claim is unsupported as presented.

2. **Critical implementation details are missing, preventing reproducibility.** Two decisions that dramatically affect behavior are unspecified: (a) How are the dispersion statistics $s_{qk}^\ell$ computed in practice? Definition 1 says "such as mean absolute deviation" but does not specify batch-level, EMA, or dataset-level computation — each choice produces different dynamics. (b) The regularization parameter $\lambda$ (Theorem 1) is introduced but no value or selection procedure is reported anywhere in the text. Without these, the method cannot be independently reproduced.

3. **Circular dependency acknowledged but unanalyzed.** The method requires cluster assignments to compute $M_k$, then uses $M_k$ to compute assignments. The paper's workaround — using the *previous* layer's assignment (line 123) — means $M_k$ always reflects a potentially outdated cluster structure. During training, when assignments evolve rapidly, this lag could cause instability. The paper provides no analysis (convergence, contraction, or empirical sensitivity) of this alternating optimization, yet the theoretical lemmas treat $M_k$ as if it were constructed from the *correct* assignment.

4. **Proposition 2 lacks specificity.** The claim that $\kappa(\nabla_{e_i}^2 \mathcal{L}^{\text{ACMoE}}) \leq \kappa(\nabla_{e_i}^2 \mathcal{L}^{\text{MoE}})$ "with high probability" does not specify the probability space, and no proof sketch or derivation is provided. This makes the statement unfalsifiable and the theoretical case for faster convergence incomplete.

### Minor

1. **Narrow baseline comparison.** The evaluation compares against only SMoE and XMoE. Modern routing methods such as Expert Choice routing (Zhou et al., 2022b) — cited in the paper's own introduction — DSelect-k, and Hash Layers are not included. This weakens the claim that ACMoE "outperforms baseline routing methods."

2. **No uncertainty quantification in the text.** Results are "averaged over 5 runs with different seeds" but no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or per-run ranges appear anywhere in the text. It is unclear whether reported improvements (~3%, 4.8%, 7%) are statistically significant.

3. **No ablation isolating the mechanism.** The paper does not compare against a fixed global feature standardization (e.g., dividing by the global per-dimension standard deviation rather than per-cluster dispersions). Without this, it is unclear whether the benefit comes from per-cluster adaptivity or simply from any form of feature rescaling.

### Trivial

- The claim of "negligible computational overhead" (lines 23, 166) is asserted without any runtime measurements or FLOP analysis.

## Nice-to-Haves
- The first MoE layer uses no AC transformation (no previous-layer assignment exists), effectively behaving as a standard router. The paper could discuss this initialization issue.
- An ablation on the value of $\lambda$ and its sensitivity would strengthen the paper.
- Analyzing whether the AC router could exacerbate load imbalance (a cluster that is already tight may attract even more tokens) would be useful.

## Novel Insights
