## Summary

The paper proposes Mul2vec, a framework combining Neural Tensor Factorization (NTF) with two triplet-loss objectives (ILM and DLM) to learn "semantic representations" from word co-occurrence patterns that can replace the text representations of pre-trained vision-language models under incomplete data (20% random pixel/token masking). The method is evaluated across 7 VL models (UNITER, VL-BERT, VD-BERT, ViT, Oscar, CLIP, BLIP) and shows improved I2T/T2I performance when the VL model's own text representation is swapped for Mul2vec's.

## Strengths

- **Compatibility across diverse VL architectures**: Mul2vec is evaluated with 7 different pre-trained models (Table 3), including both older (UNITER, Oscar) and more recent (CLIP, BLIP) architectures, and shows consistent improvement when its semantic representation replaces the VL model's contextual text representation under 20% random masking. This breadth provides some evidence that the approach is model-agnostic.

- **Ablation study isolates the contribution of DLM**: Tables 4 and 5 systematically decompose the framework — removing DLM degrades performance more than removing ILM, and both the TF and MLP components of NTF are necessary. The monotonic improvement with number of topics K further suggests the factorization genuinely benefits from higher-dimensional latent structure rather than overfitting. This targeted evidence supports the paper's claimed mechanism.

- **Runtime analysis shows manageable cost**: Table 6 reports the computational overhead of adding Mul2vec, and Section 4.4 argues that parameter sharing and parallelization keep costs comparable to VAE-based alternatives, partially addressing the natural concern that tensor factorization would be expensive.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **Central claim about missing modality information is not tested.** The abstract and introduction repeatedly claim the method handles "missing modality information" and "noisy data or missing modality information." However, the experimental proxy (Section 4.2) is "20% of each of these images and texts randomly masked" — i.e., random token/pixel noise, not missing modalities. A genuine missing-modality scenario (e.g., entire images or entire captions absent for some samples) is never tested. The limitations section admits "Mul2vec [is] not effective when there are many missing data" (Section 5) but neither defines a threshold nor investigates the breakdown point. The claimed scope is substantially broader than what is evidenced.

- **No comparison against any relevant baselines for representation learning or incomplete data.** The experiments compare Mul2vec-augmented VL models against the same VL models without Mul2vec, but provide no comparison against: (a) other methods designed for incomplete multi-modal data (e.g., SMIL, MMIN, AGMN, or VAE-based approaches), (b) other factorization or topic models used as text representation replacements (e.g., LDA, NMF), or (c) any other approach for learning semantic representations from word co-occurrence. The ablation study (Tables 4–5) only compares Mul2vec variants against itself. Without these baselines, it is impossible to determine whether the observed improvements are attributable to Mul2vec's specific design or to the generic benefit of using any factored word-co-occurrence representation.

- **All base VL models were retrained on Conceptual Captions (3.3M pairs) instead of their established pretraining data, and model quality is not verified.** Section 4.1 states that all seven base models' "parameters were optimized over the conceptual caption dataset ... for fair comparison." This is highly non-standard — CLIP was originally trained on 400M pairs, BLIP on COCO+CC+LAION, etc. The paper does not report whether the retrained versions achieve performance comparable to published results. Combined with the statement that "pre-training procedure was limited to about 1,000 steps" (with batch size 256, this is ~0.08 epochs of CC), the base models appear drastically undertrained. This undermines the entire experimental foundation.

- **ILM and DLM are functionally identical triplet losses.** Equations (5) and (6) are mathematically identical — the same triplet objective with the same functional form, differing only in what serves as anchor/positive/negative. The paper describes these as two distinct training objectives, but they are the same loss function applied to different pairs. The rationale for using triplet loss over contrastive loss is mentioned as a "pre-experiment" with no results reported. The claimed novelty of having two distinct objectives is substantially weaker than presented.

### Minor

- **Experimental setup replaces rather than augments VL model representations.** The paper states it "applies the semantic representation ... instead of the contextual representation" (Table 3 caption). This is a head-to-head replacement, not a demonstration of complementarity or "contribution to" pre-trained models. A genuine test of complementarity would involve using both representations together (e.g., as additional features or through joint fine-tuning). The framing overstates what is shown.

- **Contradictory training specifications raise reproducibility concerns.** Section 4.1 states both "we ran the models for 20 epochs" and "The pre-training procedure was limited to about 1,000 steps" in the same paragraph. With batch size 256 and 3.3M training samples in Conceptual Captions, 20 epochs would require ~257K steps. These cannot both describe the same training phase, but the text does not clarify which is which. This ambiguity makes the experimental setup difficult to reproduce.

- **No variance or confidence intervals reported for any result.** The only statistical claim (Table 4, p<0.01) cannot be evaluated without standard deviations. For a paper presenting comparative results across 7 models and multiple ablations, the absence of any variance information is a limitation.

- **One-sentence limitations section.** Section 5 concedes "Limitations of Mul2vec are not effective when there are many missing data" with no analysis of what "many" means, at what threshold performance degrades, or why. This directly undercuts the paper's central selling point and is given no discussion.

### Trivial

- Topic coherence evaluation (Section 5) on the Amazon dataset is tangential to the multi-modal representation learning task and does not connect back to the I2T/T2I evaluation.
- No analysis of what the learned latent topics semantically correspond to or whether they are interpretable.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Testing actual missing-modality scenarios (entire images or entire captions removed) would directly test the paper's claimed scope.
- Comparing against LDA or NMF as text representation sources would help isolate whether the tensor factorization structure is necessary.
- Jointly fine-tuning Mul2vec with the VL model (rather than freezing the VL model and swapping representations) would better demonstrate complementarity.

## Novel Insights

The two reviews, when synthesized, reveal a deeper issue than either alone captures: the paper's central claimed innovation — dual-level alignment (contextual + semantic) — is operationalized as two mathematically identical triplet losses, and the "semantic" representation is derived from a tensor factorization whose structure (document × word × entity-ID) is never theoretically justified. Meanwhile, the empirical evaluation conflates "noisy/masked inputs" with "incomplete modalities," and the absence of any non-VL baseline (e.g., LDA topics as text features) means the reader cannot distinguish whether Mul2vec's specific NTF+MLP+DLM machinery is necessary or whether any factored word-representation method would produce similar gains. The paper's strongest evidence (the ablation study) only shows that DLM matters more than ILM and that more topics help — but this is internal consistency, not external validation against alternatives. The paper thus presents a plausible architecture without demonstrating that any particular design choice beyond "use a word-co-occurrence factorization" is responsible for the improvements.

## Suggestions

1. **Experiment with actual missing modalities** (entire images or captions removed for a subset of samples), not just random masking. This directly tests the paper's stated scope.
2. **Add LDA, NMF, and word2vec/SGNS as representation baselines** — if Mul2vec outperforms these simpler factorization approaches, the neural tensor structure is justified; if not, the claimed novelty is unsupported.
3. **Clarify the training protocol**: resolve the contradiction between "20 epochs" and "1,000 steps," and report whether retrained base models match published performance.
4. **Report standard retrieval metrics** (R@1, R@5, R@10) with variance, and use actual tables rather than embedded images.
5. **Ablate the entity-ID dimension**: the paper never explains why a 3rd-order (document × word × entity-ID) tensor is necessary versus a (document × word) matrix. An ablation with and without the ID axis would clarify.
6. **Provide a more detailed limitations analysis**: at what level of missing data does the method break down, and why?
