## Summary

This paper introduces Window-based Dynamic Attention (WDA) for learned image compression, where the Swin Transformer attention matrix is sparsified by thresholding (at t=0.8) the ratio of off-diagonal to diagonal entries of the feature covariance matrix within each local window. The sparsification is applied hierarchically across multiple feature scales and extended to the entropy model via a Dynamic-Reference Entropy Model (DREM). Experiments report BD-rate savings of 13.42%, 17.74%, and 12.93% over VTM-17.0 on Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC respectively.

## Strengths

1. **Principled covariance-based sparsification criterion.** The WDA module computes the covariance matrix V of latent features within each window and creates a mask by thresholding |V(i,j)/V(i,i)| at t=0.8. The diagonal represents per-token variance (a proxy for entropy), and the ratio measures feature correlation strength — so the mask naturally suppresses connections between weakly correlated or high-entropy tokens. This provides a content-adaptive basis for sparsification that goes beyond fixed Top-K approaches (Qian et al., 2022a;b), which use a predetermined number of references regardless of content.

2. **Hierarchical multi-scale application with scale awareness.** The WDA module is applied at multiple feature scales, with the explicit reasoning that coarser-scale windows (larger receptive fields) contain more irrelevant tokens and thus warrant more aggressive sparsification (Section 3.2.2). This scale-adaptive design is a deliberate architectural choice that distinguishes the method from prior works applying a uniform attention pattern across all scales.

3. **Ablation validates progressive improvement.** The ablation study (Section 4.3) reports that replacing standard Swin-T attention with WDA improves BD-rate progressively from the no-attention baseline through plain attention to WDA with one module and finally WDA with all four modules, demonstrating that each component contributes meaningfully.

4. **Entropy compaction evidence.** Figure 5 provides visual evidence that the WDA module produces more compact latent feature distributions (smaller σ) compared to baselines without attention and with plain attention, directly supporting the claim that sparsification reduces the entropy of latent representations.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **Central overfitting claim is stated without evidence.** The paper repeatedly asserts that dense self-attention "increases the risk of overfitting" (Abstract, Introduction, Section 2.1) and frames this as the "core contradiction" driving the work, yet provides no training vs. validation curves, no generalization gap analysis, and no diagnostic that isolates overfitting as the mechanism improved by WDA. The ablation shows WDA improves RD performance, but this could be due to a beneficial inductive bias, regularization, or the covariance-based signal — not necessarily overfitting reduction. The paper would be equally strong (or stronger) by simply motivating sparsification as a way to suppress irrelevant context, without the unsupported overfitting narrative.

2. **No computational cost analysis reported.** No parameter counts, FLOPs, MACs, or encoding/decoding runtime are reported anywhere. For a compression method, efficiency is a first-class concern. The paper adds a covariance computation per window (O(N²C) where N=K²) on top of standard QKV attention but does not quantify whether the overall cost increases or decreases relative to standard Swin-T. Without this, the reader cannot assess the practical trade-off the method presents.

3. **Gradient flow through the hard mask is unaddressed.** The mask M (Eqs. 7–8) is generated by a hard threshold on |V(i,j)/V(i,i)| — a non-differentiable step function. The paper states no relaxation mechanism (Gumbel-Softmax, straight-through estimator, etc.). The mask is input-dependent (through X_i → V → M), so it changes during training as features evolve indirectly, but the masking rule itself is a fixed heuristic (threshold at 0.8), not adapted via gradient descent. The paper's language that the module "adaptively learns attention patterns from entropy information" (Contribution 2) is overstated: the features feeding into the covariance are learned, but the masking decision rule is not. This is not fatal — the method still works — but it needs clarification and the claims should be moderated.

### Minor

4. **Covariance vs. attention similarity mismatch.** The mask is computed from feature covariance (a linear measure of pixel-value correlation), but applied to prune QK^T attention (which measures pairwise query-key compatibility). These are different similarity measures. The paper acknowledges this briefly ("Although the covariance matrix only represents linear correlations, it is sufficient as a clue for attention aggregation," line 115) but does not justify why feature covariance is the right signal to prune attention, nor provide a controlled experiment validating this design choice over alternatives (e.g., pruning based on attention weights directly, or using a learned mask).

5. **No sensitivity analysis on the threshold t=0.8.** The threshold is the primary control knob controlling sparsity. No ablation over t ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} is provided to demonstrate robustness or motivate the chosen value. A method with a manually-set free parameter that is not ablated makes it difficult to assess how critical the specific value is.

6. **DREM specification is underspecified for reproducibility.** Section 3.3 does not clarify how the WDA module is adapted for cross-attention in the entropy model (queries come from the current slice yⁱ while keys/values come from previous slices ŷ<ⁱ). The checkerboard split notation contains apparent typographic issues ($y_{na}^{i}a$). The section needs significantly more detail — equations or an explicit description of how the covariance-based masking applies in the cross-attention setting — to be reproducible.

7. **No qualitative reconstruction comparisons.** The paper does not show sample reconstructed images comparing WDA-LIC against baselines, which would help demonstrate whether the dynamic attention preserves texture details and where differences are visible.

8. **No analysis of achieved sparsity patterns.** The paper does not report what fraction of attention edges are actually pruned, how this varies across layers or images, or whether the patterns are semantically meaningful, making the "dynamic" behavior difficult to interpret.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify gradient flow.** Either adopt a relaxation technique (Gumbel-Softmax, hard-concrete) for the mask, or explicitly state that the mask is a fixed heuristic function of learned features and reframe the claims from "adaptively learns" to "adaptively determines."

2. **Validate or drop the overfitting narrative.** Provide training vs. validation loss curves for WDA-LIC vs. a plain Swin-T baseline. If the generalization gap is smaller for WDA-LIC, the claim is supported. If not, replace the overfitting framing with a direct motivation (e.g., "sparsifying attention suppresses irrelevant context and improves RD performance"), which the evidence already supports.

3. **Report model size and runtime.** Provide parameter counts and encoding/decoding time (on the same hardware) against all baselines. This is standard practice for compression papers.

4. **Add threshold ablation.** At minimum, evaluate t ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95} on one dataset to demonstrate robustness and justify the chosen value.

5. **Report actual sparsity ratios.** For a typical image (e.g., Kodak 01), report the percentage of attention edges pruned per layer — this gives concrete meaning to "dynamic sparsification."

6. **Expand DREM details.** Clarify how the WDA module operates in the cross-attention setting (Q from yⁱ, K,V from ŷ<ⁱ) with explicit equations.

7. **Add qualitative reconstruction examples.** Show side-by-side comparisons (full image and zoomed crops) between WDA-LIC, a Swin-T baseline, and VTM-17.0 at similar bitrates.

## Score and Decision

The paper proposes a reasonable idea — covariance-based sparsification of window attention for learned image compression — and provides ablation evidence that it improves RD performance. The approach is novel in its application domain and grounded in a clear intuition.

However, the paper in its current form has significant gaps that prevent acceptance at a top venue: (1) the central overfitting motivation is asserted without any evidence, (2) no computational cost is reported despite being standard for compression papers, (3) the gradient flow through the hard mask is unaddressed and the claims about "learning" attention patterns are overstated, and (4) several key design choices (threshold t, DREM cross-attention adaptation) lack justification or sensitivity analysis. These issues are addressable, but the paper needs substantial revision.
