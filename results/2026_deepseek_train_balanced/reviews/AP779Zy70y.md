## Summary

This paper identifies a structural limitation of Graph Attention Networks (GATs): they are unable to switch off task-irrelevant neighborhood aggregation, even when the node's own features suffice for classification. The authors trace this to a conservation law in GAT's gradient dynamics that constrains attention parameter norms, and propose GATE—a minimal architectural modification that decouples self-attention and neighbor-attention parameters (adding $d$ parameters per layer in its weight-sharing variant). On synthetic tasks where labels depend only on a node's own features, GAT fails catastrophically (e.g., 23.6% accuracy), while GATE achieves 100%. On real-world heterophilic benchmarks and OGB datasets, GATE consistently outperforms GAT, often by large margins, and achieves 79.57% on OGB-arxiv.

## Strengths

1. **Clean, well-motivated problem and elegant fix.** The paper identifies a genuine architectural limitation of GAT—its inability to suppress aggregation—and proposes a simple, parameter-efficient modification (separate $\mathbf{a}_s$ and $\mathbf{a}_t$ vectors) that directly addresses it. The weight-sharing variant GATE$_S$ adds only $d$ parameters per layer.

2. **Striking synthetic validation that directly confirms the claimed limitation.** The self-sufficient learning task (Table "self-sufficient-learning") is a clean diagnostic: GAT's accuracy collapses with depth (e.g., 23.6% for 5-layer on ER with 8 classes), while GATE achieves 100% in every configuration. The $\alpha_{vv}$ distribution plots (Fig. "alphaDist-cora-oneHotFeats-1-and-2-layer") visually confirm that GAT cannot push self-attention coefficients toward 1, whereas GATE can.

3. **Consistent and often dramatic improvements on heterophilic benchmarks.** On five datasets following the Platonov et al. protocol (Table "real-het-results"), GATE outperforms GAT, MLP, MLP$_{+GAT}$, and FAGCN on every dataset—e.g., roman-empire: 75.55% vs. GAT's 26.10%. The extensive 14-baseline comparison (Table "real-heterophilic-baselines") further shows GATE and GATE-sep rank first or second across all datasets.

4. **Interpretable self-attention coefficients.** The paper demonstrates that GATE's learned $\alpha_{vv}$ values provide meaningful signals about whether a dataset benefits from graph structure (e.g., roman-empire with homophily 0.05 yields $\alpha_{vv}\approx 1$, minesweeper with homophily 0.68 yields lower $\alpha_{vv}$). This gives practitioners a tool for analyzing graph utility per dataset.

5. **Demonstration that the synthetic test bed serves as a useful diagnostic.** The synthetic tasks cleanly separate the ability to suppress vs. utilize aggregation. On the neighbor-dependent task, GATE still outperforms GAT (e.g., 96.4 vs. 93.6 for $k=1, L=1$), confirming the method does not simply disable aggregation but adapts it appropriately.

6. **Depth benefit is validated.** The paper shows GATE can productively use deeper networks (5–20 layers) while GAT degrades, confirming that switching off aggregation unlocks meaningful depth (e.g., OGB-mag: 35.29% with 5 layers vs. GAT's 32.61% with 2 layers).

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

None.

### Minor

1. **The claimed causal mechanism (conservation law → inability to switch off) is argued at the level of intuition, not formal proof.** Insight 1 (lines 156–166) provides a plausible qualitative story linking large attention norms to the conservation law, but the paper does not formalize this into a bound or prove that switching off *requires* large norms in the conservation-law sense. The empirical evidence stands independently, so this is not a structural flaw, but the paper's narrative arc claims the theory as the explanation. The "follows immediately" phrasing (line 198) for the GATE insight also overstates what the conservation law alone guarantees (it shows parameter budget can interchange, but does not by itself guarantee the model will *learn* to switch off).

2. **The OGB-arxiv SOTA claim (79.57%) is partially confounded with depth.** GATE uses 12 layers while the GAT baseline uses 3 layers (Table "real-ogb-results"). While the paper correctly argues that GATE's depth benefit is part of its advantage, the comparison does not fully disentangle whether the improvement comes from the architectural change or simply from additional capacity. A controlled comparison at matched depths (even if GAT degrades) would strengthen the claim. The SOTA claim is not invalidated, but it is less clean than the paper implies.

3. **Synthetic test bed evaluates only GAT-family architectures.** The neighbor-dependent task is claimed to be "of independent interest to measure progress in developing adaptive neighborhood aggregation schemes," yet only GAT variants are evaluated on it. Including non-attention baselines (e.g., GCN, GPR-GNN) would validate whether the test bed actually measures adaptive aggregation broadly, rather than a GAT-specific pathology.

4. **The parameter count and complexity claim could be clearer.** The paper states (line 120) that for GATE$_S$, "GATE adds only $d$ more parameters to GAT and thus has the same computational complexity as GAT." This is correct for GATE$_S$ vs. GAT with shared $W_s=W_t$, but full GATE (with separate $\mathbf{U}^l, \mathbf{V}^l, \mathbf{W}^l$) adds $3d^2 + 2d$ parameters vs. GAT's $d^2 + d$ (or $2d^2 + d$). The paper uses both variants in experiments and does not always cleanly separate which gains come from the architectural innovation vs. added parameters or capacity.

5. **Self-sufficient synthetic results are reported as point estimates without variance.** While the margins are so large (GAT at 23.6% vs. GATE at 100%) that variance is unlikely to change conclusions, the lack of confidence intervals on these key results is a minor methodological gap.

### Trivial

- The paper references a figure for FAGCN on synthetic tasks with an incomplete figure number ("see Fig."), likely a parsing artifact.
- The notation $\Theta(\theta) = \dotP{\theta}{\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}}$ has a garbled angle-bracket in the parser output, though the meaning is clear from context.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Directly test whether the conservation law is the causal mechanism**: artificially break the conservation law in GAT (e.g., via regularizers that decouple norm constraints) and check whether GAT can then switch off aggregation on the synthetic tasks. This would validate (or refute) the claimed explanatory narrative.
- **Include at least one non-attention baseline (e.g., GCN or GPR-GNN) on the synthetic neighbor-dependent task** to substantiate the claim that the test bed measures adaptive aggregation generally.
- **Add results on homophilic datasets** (beyond the small Cora/Citeseer table) in the main comparison to show that GATE's flexibility does not incur a penalty when aggregation is beneficial.

## Novel Insights

The reviews offer one genuinely novel observation beyond the paper's own contributions: the crucial experiment that would directly validate the causal narrative—artificially breaking the conservation law in GAT to see if suppression of aggregation becomes possible—is not performed. This gap between the claimed mechanistic explanation and the actual evidence is not a fatal flaw (the paper's empirical contributions stand independently), but it clarifies that the conservation-law argument serves as plausible motivation rather than proven causation. An astute reader should treat the theoretical framing as a compelling intuition supported by correlational evidence, not a fully verified causal chain.

## Suggestions

1. **For the OGB-arxiv SOTA claim**: either run GAT at the same depth (12 layers) to provide a controlled ablation, or contextualize the claim more carefully (e.g., "to our knowledge the best result on OGB-arxiv without pretrained embeddings"). This would avoid overclaiming while still highlighting the strong result.

2. **Add at least GCN and GPR-GNN to the synthetic neighbor-dependent test bed** to validate the claim that the test bed measures adaptive aggregation generally, not just a GAT-specific issue.

3. **Clearly separate GATE and GATE$_S$ results** when making parameter-count and complexity claims. The paper sometimes uses "GATE" loosely when the claim applies specifically to the weight-sharing variant.

4. **Report confidence intervals for the self-sufficient synthetic results.** The margins are large, so this is not urgent, but it would improve methodological rigor.

5. **Consider a head-to-head GAT vs. GATE at matched depths on OGB datasets** (even if GAT degrades severely) to make the architectural comparison cleaner.
