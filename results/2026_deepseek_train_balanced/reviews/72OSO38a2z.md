## Summary

LaGeM proposes a hierarchical (multi-scale) autoencoder for 3D geometry that extends the VecSet representation with a U-Net-style transformer architecture and a standardization-based bottleneck regularization. The paper's core contribution is demonstrating substantial training efficiency gains (0.58× memory, 0.70× time vs. VecSet) and strong reconstruction quality when trained on Objaverse-scale data, with consistent generalization across 7 diverse held-out datasets. A cascaded diffusion framework for generative modeling in the learned hierarchical latent space is also presented as a secondary contribution.

## Strengths

- **Substantial and clearly demonstrated training efficiency gains.** Table `tab:stat` shows LaGeM uses 0.58× GPU memory (31,662 MB vs 54,543 MB) and 0.70× per-iteration time (0.4853s vs 0.6902s) compared to VecSet at the large 2k-latent setting. This directly substantiates the paper's core motivation of scaling to large datasets where VecSet's quadratic self-attention becomes prohibitive.

- **Strong reconstruction quality and generalization when trained at scale.** LaGeM-Objaverse achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 2.42 (×100) on all ShapeNet categories vs VecSet's 4.68 — roughly a 48% improvement — with F-score rising from 93.25 to 98.93 (Table 1). More impressively, the model consistently outperforms VecSet across 7 diverse held-out datasets (Table 3: Thingi10k, ABO, ShapeNet-test, EGAD, GSO, pix3d, FAUST), spanning CAD models, household objects, human scans, and non-watertight meshes. This provides convincing evidence of a genuinely transferable geometry representation.

- **Novel bottleneck regularization that cleanly aligns with diffusion priors.** Replacing KL divergence with zero-mean, unit-variance standardization in the FtoL module (Section 3.2, Table `tab:ftl_ltf`) is a principled design choice that avoids a separate regularization loss term and directly matches the Gaussian prior used by diffusion models. This differs from all prior works cited.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Generative modeling claims lack any quantitative evaluation.** The cascaded diffusion model is presented as a contribution in the introduction and contribution list ("We propose a cascaded diffusion model to generate 3D geometry in the hierarchical latent space"), yet the evaluation (Section 4.2, Figures for ShapeNet and Objaverse-10k) consists entirely of visual examples with no quantitative metrics — no FID, Coverage, MMD, 1-NNA, or JSD. Models trained for 100–200 hours on 4–16 A100 GPUs merit standard evaluation. This is a critical evidential gap for any claimed contribution at a top venue. The authors should either provide standard generative metrics with baselines or explicitly re-scope the generative model as preliminary and remove it from the claimed contributions.

2. **No ablation studies isolate the sources of improvement.** Multiple design changes are introduced simultaneously: (a) hierarchical U-Net architecture, (b) standardization regularization replacing KL divergence, (c) multi-scale query function concatenating features from all levels, (d) increased total parameter count (+18%) and latent vector capacity (+31%). Without ablations, it is impossible to attribute gains to the hierarchical design rather than to simply having a larger-capacity model. At minimum, ablations of the hierarchical vs. flat structure (equal total capacity), standardization vs. KL divergence, and number of levels are needed to validate the paper's central causal claims.

3. **The primary autoencoding comparison is not capacity-controlled.** From Table `tab:stat`: LaGeM has ~18% more parameters (125.15M vs 106.13M) and 31% more latent vectors (672 vs 512) than VecSet in the small setting; similar ratios hold for the large setting. A controlled comparison — e.g., a single-level VecSet with 672 vectors vs LaGeM with 32+128+512=672 vectors — is needed to demonstrate that the hierarchical decomposition itself, not just increased capacity, drives the reported improvements.

### Minor

4. **Best reconstruction results conflate architecture and training data.** The headline 48% improvement (LaGeM-Objaverse Chamfer 2.42 vs VecSet-ShapeNet 4.68) compares models trained on different datasets. The within-ShapeNet comparison (LaGeM-ShapeNet 4.63 vs VecSet-ShapeNet 4.68) shows only a ~1% improvement — essentially tied. This pattern suggests much of the reported gain comes from training on the much larger Objaverse dataset (600k models) rather than from architectural innovations per se. The paper notes that VecSet is "difficult to scale" to Objaverse (Table 1 caption) but provides no empirical evidence of this difficulty.

5. **VecSet baseline on Objaverse is absent.** The paper does not report any attempt to train VecSet on Objaverse with comparable compute, data, and preprocessing. Without this baseline, the reader cannot assess how much of LaGeM-Objaverse's improvement is due to the architecture enabling Objaverse-scale training vs. the comparison simply being between different-scale models trained on different data.

6. **Overfitting claim about VecSet is unsubstantiated.** The introduction (Section 1) asserts that VecSet "is overfitting on smaller datasets like ShapeNet" without showing any training/validation curves or train-val gap analysis.

7. **No quantitative comparison to other recent 3D methods.** The related work table (Table `tab:geo_gen`) lists several 2024 methods (XCube, Mosaic-SDF, 3DTopia-XL, OctFusion) but provides no quantitative comparison or discussion of why direct comparison is infeasible. This weakens the paper's contextual positioning.

## Suggestions

3. **Address the generative evaluation gap.** Either add standard quantitative metrics (FID, Coverage, MMD on ShapeNet categories) with baselines, or explicitly re-scope the generative model as preliminary/exploratory and remove it from the enumerated contributions.

## Score and Decision

The paper's autoencoder contribution is real: the hierarchical design yields meaningful efficiency gains, and the generalization results across 7 datasets are genuinely impressive.
