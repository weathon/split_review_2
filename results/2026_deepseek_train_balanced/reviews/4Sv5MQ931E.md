## Summary
This paper proposes MSR-ViR, a modular VideoQA framework combining a question parser LLM (generating execution policies), a MoST-Grounding module (performing both temporal localization via UniVTG and spatial localization via YOLO-World), and a multimodal LLM answerer. The core algorithmic contribution is an alternate self-reflection training strategy where the multimodal LLM is finetuned via SFT and the question parser LLM via DPO, using the multimodal LLM's cross-entropy loss as a reward signal to distinguish good/bad policies.

## Strengths
- **Alternate Self-reflection Training with DPO is genuinely novel and ablation-supported.** Using the multimodal LLM's loss as a weak reward signal to train the question parser via DPO (Section 3.4, Eq. 6) is an interesting idea, and the ablation (Section 4.4, ~1.5 point drop on NExT-QA when removed) provides credible evidence that it contributes positively to accuracy.

- **Joint temporal + spatial grounding is a meaningful extension beyond prior work.** Most grounding-based VideoQA methods (LSTP, SeViLa) only perform temporal localization. The MoST-Grounding module adds spatial localization via YOLO-World, and the ablation shows removing spatial modules drops NExT-QA accuracy by ~1.4 points (Section 4.4), demonstrating independent contribution.

- **Strong NExT-QA and NExT-GQA results on clean experimental settings.** On NExT-QA (full dataset, no subset issues) and NExT-GQA, MSR-ViR achieves competitive results against grounding-based and modular methods. The direct baseline comparisons (MSR-ViR_Q vs Qwen-VL, same frame count 4 vs 4) provide a valid controlled comparison for one backbone.

- **Global representation as a safeguard is a practical design choice.** Supplementing grounding results with a compressed global video representation (Section 3.3) to mitigate grounding errors is sensible and ablated positively.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **The STAR-sub issue undermines the STAR comparison and the abstract's framing.** The paper creates STAR-sub by excluding 17.5% of STAR (Prediction and Feasibility question types, Section 4.1, line 138) because they "lack temporal and spatial grounding." The abstract and introduction state "Experiments on VideoQA datasets (NExT-QA and STAR)" without qualification, which is misleading — the reader naturally interprets this as the full STAR benchmark. More critically, Table 1's caption says "All models are finetuned on the corresponding training set" but does not explicitly state that all listed baselines (AIO, ATP, VGT, MIST, etc.) were re-run on STAR-sub rather than having their published full-STAR numbers shown. Given the complexity of re-running these diverse baselines, this ambiguity is significant. If the baselines' numbers come from full STAR, the comparison is invalid because baselines faced a harder problem (all four question types). If they were re-run on STAR-sub, this must be stated explicitly. Either way, the paper's STAR-related claims cannot be properly evaluated as presented.

- **Frame count imbalance confounds the MSR-ViR_L results.** The direct baseline Llava-Next uses 8 uniformly sampled frames. MSR-ViR_L uses 8 temporal-grounded frames + 8 spatial-grounded frames = 16 frames — double the visual information (Section 4.1, Implementation, line 142). The paper includes no controlled baseline where Llava-Next receives 16 uniformly sampled frames (or 16 grounded frames) to isolate whether the improvement comes from grounded selection or simply from having more frames. The MSR-ViR_Q comparison (4 vs 4 frames) is clean, but the paper's strongest results are from MSR-ViR_L, making this a real confound for the headline claims.

- **The central claim of interpretability is asserted but never evaluated.** The paper's motivation hinges on overcoming the "black-box" nature of multimodal LLMs by providing "interpretability" (abstract, contributions, Section 1). The execution policies are presented as "a clear reasoning path from the question to the answer" (Section 3.1). However, no systematic interpretability evaluation is conducted — no user study, no human evaluation of policy coherence or helpfulness, no comparison with the interpretability of competing methods. Only a single qualitative example is provided (Figure 4). A claim this central to the paper's framing should not be left purely as an architectural assertion.

### Minor
- **Self-serving characterization in Related Work without acknowledging the same limitation.** The paper criticizes modular methods like ViperGPT for relying on frame-level captions that "potentially overlook details" (Section 2), but MSR-ViR's MoST-Grounding module similarly operates on frame-level features via UniVTG and YOLO-World. This asymmetry in characterization is noted.

- **Ablation study conducted only on MSR-ViR_Q, not MSR-ViR_L.** The ablation (Section 4.4) is limited to the Qwen-VL backbone. Ablations on the stronger Llava-Next backbone would be more informative, especially given the frame-count confound for that variant.

None.
