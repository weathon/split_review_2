## Summary

This paper identifies two flaws in existing disentanglement metrics: (1) incorrect alignment (allowing the same neuron to represent multiple factors) and (2) failure to detect distributed entanglements (checking only individual neurons, missing information spread across multiple neurons). It proposes two replacement metrics—SNC (single-neuron classification accuracy) and NK (neuron knockout—accuracy drop after removing the aligned neuron)—and introduces a compositional generalization (CG) classification task as a downstream evaluation. Empirical results across six models and three datasets show that existing models perform poorly on CG and that the proposed metrics correlate most strongly with CG performance.

## Strengths

- **Formal diagnosis of the incorrect-alignment failure in existing metrics**: The paper provides both a concrete toy example (Table 1 in Section 3, lines 52–67) showing how one neuron can simultaneously encode two factors while existing metrics assign it to both, and real empirical evidence from β-TCVAE on 3dshapes (Figure 1, lines 69–74) where mutual information incorrectly aligns two factors to the same neuron. The proposed fix—enforcing injectivity via constrained optimization solvable with the Kuhn-Munkres algorithm (Eq. 80, lines 79–82)—is principled and clean.

- **CG-as-classification cleanly separates encoder quality from decoder quality**: Prior work on compositional generalization for DE relied on decoder reconstructions or pixel loss, which conflate encoder and decoder quality (lines 230–231). By treating CG as a classification task on the encoder's latent representations (lines 150–156), the paper isolates what the encoder captures—a direct methodological improvement.

- **Empirical demonstration that existing DE models largely fail at compositional generalization**: Table 2 (lines 190–220) shows that across six models and three datasets, accuracy on novel feature combinations is often at chance level after chance adjustment, even for the fully supervised MTD model. This challenges prior optimistic claims and provides a grounded benchmark for future DE research.

- **Thoughtful observation of the fault-tolerance vs. disentanglement tension**: Lines 176–177 note that strong DE (each factor in exactly one neuron) fundamentally conflicts with the fault-tolerance that originally motivated distributed representations. This is a nuanced conceptual insight that goes beyond metric design.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **NK metric's alignment dependency undermines its claim to detect distributed entanglements**. The NK metric first aligns each factor gᵢ to a neuron zᵢ via injective assignment (Eq. 80) based on pairwise mutual information I(gᵢ; zⱼ). It then knocks out zᵢ and measures the accuracy drop. However, in distributed encoding cases like XOR (g₀ = z₁ ⊕ z₂), I(g₀; z₁) = I(g₀; z₂) = 0, so the alignment step will not assign g₀ to either z₁ or z₂—it assigns g₀ to whichever neuron happens to have the highest nonzero MI (possibly an irrelevant one). Knocking out that incorrectly assigned neuron leaves z₁ and z₂ intact, so the MLP trained on z_{≠i} can still learn the XOR, Acc_{z_{≠i}} ≈ Acc_z, and NK ≈ 0—failing to detect the entanglement. This is not a hypothetical edge case: the alignment procedure uses pairwise MI, which is **exactly** the quantity that misses distributed encodings. The paper frames NK as solving the distributed-entanglement problem that existing metrics miss (lines 111–112, "Our method, however, because it uses an MLP, can detect non-linear encodings such as XOR"), but NK's own alignment step inherits the same blindness. This circular dependency is a structural limitation that must be acknowledged. A potential fix is to compute NK for every neuron (knocking each out in turn) and report the maximum drop, rather than relying on a single alignment that may be incorrect for distributed encodings.

### Minor

- **SNC metric definition is underspecified**. The formal definition (Eqs. 99–107) uses "b:X → {0, …, K-1} specify the bin index after alignment" but never explains how b(x) is computed from a single neuron's activation. Whether b uses a trained classifier, a nearest-centroid rule, simple discretization of the activation range into K equal-width bins, or some other procedure is left unspecified. The prose calls it a "single-neuron classifier" (line 16) but the equations describe a binning/discretization approach. Given that Table 1 includes results from trained classifiers (linear, MLP), SNC's procedure needs to be equally well-specified for reproducibility.

- **Correlation analysis lacks uncertainty quantification**. Table 3 (lines 242–254) reports Pearson correlations without confidence intervals or standard errors. With at most ~18 data points (6 models × 3 datasets), the uncertainty is substantial, and the advantage of SNC (0.850) over, e.g., the C component of DCI (0.819 on 3dshapes) cannot be assessed without intervals. The text mentions "p < 0." (truncated, presumably p < 0.05) but does not report the actual p-value or state the null distribution used. The "all datasets" column pools data across datasets, which could reflect dataset-level differences rather than model-level ranking.

- **Mutual information estimation details for the alignment step are unspecified**. Equation 80 uses I(g; f(g)) but the paper does not specify how mutual information is estimated for continuous latent variables with discrete factors. Common estimators (binning, KDE, MINE) give different results, and with finite data this affects the alignment outcome. This should be specified for reproducibility.

### Trivial

- The table caption for Table 1 (line 117) does not mention that SNC and NK values are averaged across all features; this is stated only in the main text (line 173).

## Nice-to-Haves

- Ablation on the MLP hidden size (fixed at 256 across all experiments) would strengthen confidence in the results.
- Computing NK for every neuron (knocking each out in turn) and reporting the maximum drop would address the alignment dependency issue and strengthen the metric's robustness.

If the encoder produces poor latents for OOD inputs despite being "perfectly disentangled," that is itself evidence that the representation is not functionally disentangled. The CG task tests exactly what it claims to test.

## Suggestions

1. **Clarify the SNC definition**: State explicitly how b(x) is computed from the single aligned neuron's activation, including any training procedure, hyperparameters, or discretization method.
2. **Address NK's alignment dependency**: Acknowledge the limitation and either (a) compute NK for every neuron and report the maximum drop, or (b) clearly characterize when NK will and will not detect distributed entanglements.
3. **Add confidence intervals or bootstrap estimates** to the correlation table (Table 3).
4. **Specify the MI estimator** used in Eq. 80 for reproducibility.
5. **Mention in the Table 1 caption** that values are averaged over features.

## Score and Decision

This paper identifies genuine flaws in existing disentanglement metrics and introduces useful ideas (injective alignment, the CG task). The empirical finding that current DE models fail at compositional generalization is valuable. However, the NK metric's alignment dependency—where the alignment step uses pairwise MI, which is exactly the quantity that misses distributed encodings—partially undermines its central claim of solving the distributed-entanglement problem. Combined with the underspecified SNC definition and the absence of uncertainty quantification for the headline correlation results, these issues make the paper fall short of the ICLR acceptance bar in its current form. With revisions addressing the NK alignment issue, improved specification of SNC, and better uncertainty quantification, the paper could be a strong contribution.
