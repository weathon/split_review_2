## Summary

The paper proposes XAIguiFormer, a transformer architecture for EEG-based brain disorder classification that uses XAI (specifically DeepLift) to refine query/key representations in self-attention, going beyond post-hoc interpretability. The model also introduces a connectome tokenizer (treating each frequency-band graph as a token via GNN readout) and a modified rotary positional encoding (dRoFE) that injects frequency-band and demographic information. Experiments on two EEG datasets (TUAB and TDBRAIN) show improved balanced accuracy over baselines, and ablations confirm that both the XAI guidance and dRoFE contribute positively to performance.

## Strengths

- **Ablation evidence isolates the contribution of XAI guidance:** Table 3 shows that removing XAI-guided self-attention (reverting to a vanilla transformer with the same backbone) reduces BAC by +1.35% on TUAB and +2.78% on TDBRAIN. This clean ablation design (rather than comparing against a completely different architecture) provides direct evidence that the XAI mechanism improves performance beyond what the base transformer can achieve on its own.

- **dRoFE ablation is compared against standard RoPE, not simply against no positional encoding:** Table 3 replaces dRoFE with standard RoPE, isolating the benefit of encoding frequency and demographic information. The improvements (+1.87% TUAB, +3.86% TDBRAIN) are attributed to domain-specific information injection rather than the mere presence of positional encoding. The temperature of 4πt/d for the short frequency sequence is a non-trivial adaptation.

- **Stronger relative gains on the smaller dataset (TDBRAIN) support the "self-refinement without external data" motivation:** The paper's premise is that XAI-based self-refinement is suited for limited data scenarios, and the experimental results bear this out—XAI guidance adds +2.78% on TDBRAIN vs. +1.35% on the larger TUAB, and the from-scratch model outperforms pretrained baselines (BIOT, Corr-DCRNN).

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The XAI-guided self-attention mechanism is critically underspecified.** The paper states that "Q_expl and K_expl are the feature importance scores generated by DeepLift" (line 151) and that "DeepLift [is used] to pinpoint which parts are most influential for the vanilla transformer." However, the following essential details are missing: (a) what exactly DeepLift explains—the target layer/output and the reference/baseline input are not specified; (b) how scalar attribution scores per token are transformed into Q/K vectors of the same dimensionality as the original Q and K—if DeepLift produces a scalar per token, this would be dimensionally mismatched with the Q/K representations; if it produces a per-element mask, this should be stated explicitly. Without these details, the core claim that XAI "refines coarse information" cannot be reproduced, evaluated, or even fully understood. This is the paper's central contribution, and the description must be precise.

- **The dRoFE formulation contains a mathematical inconsistency that undermines its claimed connection to RoPE.** Equation (12) defines R^d_{f,Demog,2t} = age·e^{i f_l θ_t} + gender. Adding a scalar (`gender`, presumably 0 or 1) to a complex exponential changes both the magnitude and the phase in a way that is no longer a unitary rotation. A core property of RoPE is that the inner product of two rotated vectors depends only on their relative position because the rotation matrix is unitary. The dRoFE matrix as defined breaks this property, and the paper provides no justification, analysis of the effect on attention geometry, or discussion of how the method relates to or departs from standard RoPE. If the method is better understood as an affine transformation rather than a rotation, this should be acknowledged.

### Minor

- **The claim that "XAIguiFormer is not very sensitive to α as long as it is larger than 0.3" (line 161) is stated without any supporting evidence.** No sensitivity analysis or ablation over α is presented. The reader cannot verify this claim, and the hyperparameter controlling the balance between vanilla and XAI-guided losses is consequential for reproducibility.

- **The claim that "the explanation algorithm can be realized with any local explanation method from XAI" (line 151) is unsupported.** Only DeepLift is tested, and the paper does not argue why the method would generalize to other explainers (e.g., LRP, SHAP, Integrated Gradients) which have different mathematical properties (e.g., different handling of reference points, saturation, or gradientshielding behavior). If the method depends on DeepLift-specific properties (e.g., its reference-based formulation), the generality claim should be scoped accordingly.

- **The warmup experiment (Figure 2) is discussed but not fully explained.** Starting XAI from epoch 0 outperforms warmup starts; the paper's explanation—"activating XAI guidance during training alters the parameter optimization space" (line 223)—is vague and does not clarify why a warmed-up vanilla transformer (which should provide "better" explanations) leads to worse final performance. This is an interesting finding that could shed light on how the XAI guidance interacts with training dynamics, but the paper does not analyze it further.

- **The dynamical system framework (Section 3) motivates refining attention inputs, but the connection to the method is indirect.** The derivation concludes that the attention value function φ refines coarse information f_v. The method then uses XAI to refine Q and K (not f_v). The gap between these two objects is not bridged mathematically, and the framework does not generate testable predictions or constrain the method's design. This reduces the claimed theoretical grounding to a loose motivation.

- Parameter counts, FLOPs, or training time comparisons would help the community assess the practical cost of the two-pass architecture.

## Removed Points

These points were flagged but are removed or downgraded from the final review:

- **Circularity concern (Harsh Critic: "self-referential loop"):** The critic speculates that the XAI explanations from the vanilla transformer cannot provide new information beyond what the vanilla already encodes. This is a conceptual question, not a demonstrated flaw. The ablation shows clear performance gains, so the concern is speculative and not supported by the paper's evidence. **Removed.**

- **No statistical significance in main results:** While reporting variance is good practice, single-run evaluation on large benchmarks is standard in this community. The warmup experiment (Figure 2) does report std dev over 5 seeds. Downgraded from weakness to nice-to-have. **Removed from weaknesses, moved to nice-to-have.**

- **Baseline fairness (pretrained vs. from-scratch):** The paper explicitly acknowledges that its model is trained from scratch while baselines use pretraining (line 198). If this asymmetry exists, it favors the baselines (which have access to additional data), not the proposed model. This strengthens rather than weakens the comparison. **Removed.**

- **Computational cost (two-pass architecture):** Criticizing the lack of FLOPs/parameter counts is a nice-to-have, not a core weakness. The paper does not claim computational efficiency as a contribution. **Removed from weaknesses, moved to nice-to-have.**

- **Strength: "Theoretical grounding from dynamical system perspective":** The strength finder overclaims this. The dynamical system framing is loosely connected to the method (it motivates refining f_v, but the method refines Q/K) and does not generate testable predictions. This is more of a motivational section than a genuine theoretical foundation. **Removed from strengths.**

## Novel Insights

None beyond the paper's own contributions. The reviews largely surface the same structural issues (underspecified XAI mechanism, dRoFE math) that the paper itself should address. The most interesting observation—that starting XAI from epoch 0 outperforms warmup starts—is raised by the harsh critic and tangentially by the paper's own Figure 2, but neither develops it into a substantive insight about training dynamics.

## Suggestions

1. **Specify the XAI mechanism in full detail.** State: (a) the target layer and output that DeepLift explains; (b) the reference/baseline input; (c) how the attribution output (scalar per token? per-element vector?) is transformed into Q_expl/K_expl of the same dimensionality as Q/K. A figure showing this flow would be invaluable.

2. **Fix or justify the dRoFE formulation.** Either modify Equation (12) so that the transformation preserves unitary/rotational properties (e.g., by encoding demographics via separate learned embeddings or through the rotation angle rather than as additive/multiplicative terms), or explicitly acknowledge that the method is not a pure rotation and analyze how the non-unitary components affect attention geometry. The term "rotation matrix" should be accurate.

3. **Provide the α sensitivity experiment** to substantiate the claim in line 161. A simple plot of BAC vs. α would suffice.

4. **Scope the claim about "any local explanation method"** or provide experiments with at least one alternative explainer (e.g., Integrated Gradients) to support it.

5. **Report variance across seeds** in the main results tables.

## Score and Decision

The paper addresses a genuine and underexplored direction—using XAI to improve transformer performance rather than merely interpreting it—and provides encouraging ablation evidence. However, the paper's central contribution (XAI-guided self-attention) is described with insufficient precision to evaluate, reproduce, or establish the novelty of the mechanism. The dRoFE formulation contains a mathematical issue that is not acknowledged. These weaknesses are structural: they affect the core claims of the paper. The paper requires major revision before it can be accepted at a top venue. The idea is promising, but the current presentation does not meet the bar for acceptance.
