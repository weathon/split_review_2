## Summary

This paper proposes "interaction asymmetry" as a formal principle for learning disentangled and composable representations: parts of the same concept interact more complexly than parts of different concepts. This is formalized via block-diagonality conditions on (n+1)-th order derivatives of the generator function. The paper proves that this condition enables both disentanglement and compositional generalization for n=0,1,2, unifies prior results by Brady et al. (2023, n=0) and Lachapelle et al. (2024, n=1) as special cases, and extends to more flexible (non-additive) generators. A practical Transformer-based VAE with an attention-weight regularizer is proposed as a proof-of-concept, showing competitive object disentanglement results on Sprites and CLEVR6.

## Strengths

- **Unified theoretical framework that subsumes two prior results as special cases.** Section 4.3 formally proves that the assumptions of Brady et al. (2023) (compositionality/irreducibility) correspond to interaction asymmetry with n=0, and those of Lachapelle et al. (2024) (additivity/sufficient nonlinearity) correspond to n=1. This provides a genuine conceptual unification where previously disconnected theoretical guarantees are recovered under a single principle.

- **Extension to n=2 enables non-additive generators.** Prior work (n=0 or n=1) restricted generators to additive forms. Equation (7) and the surrounding characterization (Eq. 232–235) show that for n=2, polynomial cross-slot terms appear, formally extending the class of generators for which disentanglement and compositional generalization can be guaranteed. This is concretely relevant for phenomena like reflections between objects (Figure 1B).

- **Novel Jacobian-based evaluation metrics (J-ARI and JIS).** These metrics measure disentanglement using slot-wise Jacobian norms of the decoder rather than attention scores, which is more principled and avoids confounding factors in attention-score interpretation. JIS directly measures whether multiple slots affect the same pixel, connecting to the paper's theoretical interaction concept.

- **Efficient attention-based regularizer and systematic ablation.** The L_interact regularizer is computationally efficient (computed directly from attention weights, not high-order derivatives). The ablation in Table 1 cleanly separates the contributions of the KL penalty (latent capacity compression) and the interaction regularizer, directly connecting to the two theoretical requirements from Theorem 1.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **No empirical validation of compositional generalization, despite prominent claims.** The paper's title, abstract, and introduction emphasize that interaction asymmetry enables *both* disentanglement *and* compositional generalization. A full theorem (Theorem 2) is dedicated to compositional generalization. Yet Section 6 explicitly states: "we focus on evaluating disentanglement, as opposed to compositional generalization." The method is never tested on compositional generalization; the paper acknowledges this is left for future work. This is an evidential gap that affects roughly half of the claimed contribution. While the theory is the main event, the headline claim about compositional generalization goes empirically unexamined.

- **The method only enforces the n=0 (no interaction) case, not the n>0 regime that constitutes the paper's claimed extension over prior work.** The attention regularizer penalizes attention weights toward having one non-zero entry per pixel, which targets *first-order* (Jacobian-level) slot independence. This corresponds to at most *zeroth*-order interaction across slots — the n=0 case already covered by Brady et al. (2023). There is no mechanism — approximate or otherwise — for distinguishing or enforcing n=1 or n=2 constraints. The paper acknowledges that exact enforcement is intractable and n is unknown (lines 298–299), but this does not change the fact that the empirical demonstration does not test a method that leverages the paper's novel theoretical capability (n>0). The method is effectively a heuristic for the already-known n=0 case.

### Minor

- **Claims of generality across concept types are untested.** The paper criticizes prior work for being "restrictive and specific to objects in simple visual scenes" (line 24) and frames interaction asymmetry as a *general* principle for concepts of any kind (objects, attributes, events, etc.). Yet the experiments only test objects in synthetic visual scenes (Sprites, CLEVR6). The paper explicitly leaves other concept types for future work (line 352), which is transparent, but the gap between the general rhetoric and the narrow evidence remains.

- **The marginal contribution of the interaction regularizer is modest, especially on CLEVR6.** On CLEVR6, the model with only the KL term (α=0, β>0) achieves J-ARI 96.6% vs. 96.5% with both regularizers. The interaction regularizer's add marginal improvement is small, and on JIS the gain is 80.3% → 83.8%. On Sprites the gains are more noticeable (J-ARI 92.6 → 93.6, JIS 92.8 → 95.0). The paper acknowledges that "training with L_KL can, in some cases, implicitly minimize L_interact" (line 401), but this raises the question of how much of the disentanglement is attributable to the novel regularizer versus the standard VAE objective.

- **Only 3 seeds are used for all experiments.** Given the variance on some conditions (e.g., unregularized Transformer on Sprites has J-ARI std 3.4 and JIS std 6.5), more seeds would strengthen confidence in the reported results.

- **The coupling between the theory (higher-order derivative constraints) and the method (attention-weight sparsity) is heuristic and unvalidated.** The paper argues that attention weights provide an approximate handle on interactions because the Jacobian of cross-attention relates to attention weights (Section 5 and Appendix). However, the theory is about (n+1)-th order partial derivatives being zero, while the regularizer targets first-order (Jacobian-level) sparsity. The paper does not establish — formally or empirically — how attention-weight sparsity relates to the higher-order derivative constraints that are the theory's central formalism. This is not a fatal flaw since the method is labeled as approximate, but it means the method is a heuristic inspired by the theory rather than a faithful instantiation, weakening the claim that the experiments validate the theory.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Test compositional generalization directly.** A simple experiment on Sprites — training on a subset of slot combinations and testing on held-out combinations — would directly validate Theorem 2 and significantly strengthen the paper.
- **Validate the connection between attention regularization and derivative constraints.** Demonstrating that the actual Jacobian-based interaction measure correlates with the attention-based regularizer during training would make the bridge from theory to method less speculative.
- **Consider datasets with n=2 interactions** where conventional methods provably fail, to demonstrate the practical value of the theoretical extension.
- **Report hyperparameter values** (α, β) and conduct sensitivity analysis.
