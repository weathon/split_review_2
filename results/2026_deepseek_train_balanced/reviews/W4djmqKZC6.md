## Summary

The paper proposes a diffusion model with two innovations: (1) a pixel-dependent forward noise schedule where each pixel's diffusion rate is proportional to its initial value (inspired by the water-pouring algorithm), and (2) a parallel reverse-time architecture where the U-Net outputs noise predictions for all T time-steps in a single forward pass rather than T sequential passes. The authors report ~4× inference speedup on CIFAR-10 (0.3s vs. 1.23s for DDPM) and CelebA (2.15s vs. 9.08s) while claiming competitive FID scores.

## Strengths

1. **Novel pixel-dependent noise schedule with closed-form analysis.** The paper is the first to make the forward diffusion rate depend on each pixel's initial value (\(\overline{\alpha}_i = e^{-\gamma i \mathbf{x}_0/T}\)). Proposition 1 analytically shows higher-valued pixels undergo faster SNR reduction, and Proposition 2 provides a conditional proof that the expected trajectory converges faster than a conventional linear DDPM schedule. The closed-form per-pixel SNR (Eqn. 11) is a clean mathematical contribution that offers more analytical traction than scalar schedules.

2. **Single-pass reverse architecture as a principled departure from sequential sampling.** The paper modifies the U-Net's last layer to output \(T\) separate noise prediction feature maps \(\mathbf{Z}^j\) (Eqn. 14) with a masking mechanism \(\mathcal{M}_j\) enforcing \(j < i\). This replaces \(T\) sequential neural network evaluations with one — a genuinely different approach from DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, and consistency models, all of which require iterative or multi-step inference. The idea is creative and structurally different from existing acceleration methods.

3. **Measured wall-clock speedup on standard benchmarks.** The paper reports concrete inference times (0.3s vs. 1.23s on CIFAR-10, 2.15s vs. 9.08s on CelebA on the same V100 GPU) against four baselines (DDPM, DDIM, SDE-based, DPM-Solver). These numbers demonstrate that the combined approach delivers a real speedup despite the increased parameter count (71.5M / 145.5M).

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Incomplete mathematical derivation for the modified forward process.** The paper defines the forward process through the reparameterized form (Eqn. 9: \(\mathbf{x}_i = \sqrt{\overline{\alpha}_i} \odot \mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \overline{\alpha}_i} \odot \tilde{\epsilon}_i\)) without specifying the one-step transition kernel or verifying that the standard reverse-posterior formulas (Eqns. 4–6) carry over. The transition kernel can be derived (element-wise: \(x_i^j = \sqrt{e^{-\gamma x_0^j/T}} x_{i-1}^j + \sqrt{1-e^{-\gamma x_0^j/T}} \epsilon_i^j\)), and the standard formulas do extend element-wise. However, the paper does not perform this derivation — it simply reuses the standard DDPM equations without comment. Given that the forward process is the paper's primary methodological contribution, this omission is a significant gap. A top-venue paper should either explicitly derive the reverse posterior for its modified process or cite a rigorous justification that the standard formulas apply.

2. **Circular dependency between the scheduling parameters and the target image at inference is not adequately resolved.** The scheduling parameters \(\overline{\alpha}_i\) depend on \(\mathbf{x}_\delta = e^{-\gamma \mathbf{x}_0}\), which is a function of the clean image — the very quantity the model seeks to generate. The paper proposes a VAE \(G_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i, i)\) to estimate \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_\delta\) from noisy images, including from \(\mathbf{x}_T\) (pure noise) as stated on line 170: "\(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\delta}\) generated by \(G_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{T},T)\)." Estimating any function of \(\mathbf{x}_0\) from pure Gaussian noise is information-theoretically impossible in the usual sense; the VAE can at most output the mean \(\mathbf{x}_\delta\) over the training distribution. The paper claims SSIM of 0.86–0.99 "across different values of \(i\)" without reporting the SSIM specifically at \(i=T\) versus earlier steps, and does not ablate the impact of the VAE estimate's quality on downstream generation. This assessment is **the central empirical claim upon which the validity of the entire inference pipeline rests**, and it is insufficiently supported. Without a clear breakdown of how the estimation degrades with noise level and how that degradation affects final FID, the reader cannot assess whether the method breaks at its first inference step.

3. **No ablation studies disentangling the two contributions.** The paper proposes two separate innovations — the pixel-dependent schedule and the parallel reverse architecture — but provides no ablation that measures their individual contributions. It is unknown whether the speedup comes primarily from the schedule (shorter forward chain), the parallel architecture (single NN pass), or their interaction. The increased parameter count (71.5M vs. typical DDPM's ~35M for CIFAR-10) is noted but never analyzed: is the parallel model's one-pass advantage simply due to having 2× more parameters, and would a standard DDPM scaled to the same parameter count match or exceed the parallel model's speed? Without ablations, the paper's claims about "why" the method works are unsupported.

### Minor

4. **Missing comparisons with modern fast sampling methods.** The paper compares against DDPM, DDIM, SDE-based models, and DPM-Solver, but does not compare to consistency models (Song et al., 2023), progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), or DPM-Solver++ at very few steps (10–20). These methods are specifically designed for fast sampling and are natural baselines. The paper only compares to DDIM with up to 1000 steps, while DDIM+ with far fewer steps (e.g., 50) can achieve strong FID in a fraction of a second on CIFAR-10. The practical significance of the claimed speedup cannot be assessed without these comparisons.

5. **No generated samples displayed anywhere in the paper.** For a generative model paper at a top venue, the absence of any generated image samples (even in the appendix) is conspicuous. Qualitative assessment is essential for generative models — FID alone can mask mode collapse, artifacts, or low visual quality.

6. **Sloppy "law of large numbers" justification for Figure 2.** The paper states (line 163) that the LLN applies because there are 16,384 pixels in a CelebA image. Pixels in a natural image are neither independent nor identically distributed — they are strongly spatially correlated. The empirical observation in Figure 2 may still be approximately correct, but the invoked justification is invalid. This is a presentation issue rather than a core flaw, but it reflects on the paper's rigor.

7. **Inconsistent speedup claims between abstract and conclusion.** The abstract claims "at least 4 times faster," while the conclusion claims "up to 10 times." These are different claims and the paper does not reconcile them.

### Trivial

- The paper says \(\gamma \gg x_0^j\) but then uses \(\gamma=20\) with normalized pixel values in (0,1] and \(\gamma=50\) similarly — the notation is imprecise.
- The limitations section acknowledges the lack of a principled \(\gamma\) selection method, but the analysis of what breaks when \(\gamma\) is too large vs. too small is useful and should be foregrounded.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An analysis of the computational cost of the larger final U-Net layer (3×T output channels vs. 3) relative to the savings from a single forward pass would substantially strengthen the "single-run" speed claim.
- The VAE's estimation quality should be reported at each noise level \(i\) separately, not as a range "across different values of \(i\)." This is critical for assessing the circular-dependency concern.
- A continuous-time SDE formulation (promised as future work) would strengthen the theoretical framework and enable direct comparison with SDE-based fast solvers.

## Removed Points

- **Harsh critic's claim that the forward process "is not a proper Markov chain (structural flaw)" and that "the entire theoretical framework... rests on the Markov property."** — Overstated. The forward process can be reinterpreted as a valid pixel-wise Markov chain conditioned on \(\mathbf{x}_0\) (since the transition coefficients are constants depending on \(\mathbf{x}_0\)). The standard reverse-posterior formulas carry over element-wise. The paper's omission of this derivation is a real weakness (kept above as Major #1), but the process is not invalid. The harsh critic's framing of this as a fatal structural flaw is incorrect; it is an incompleteness, not a contradiction.

- **Harsh critic's claim that the circular dependency makes inference "information-theoretically impossible."** — Overstated. From pure noise, a learned model can output the dataset mean \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_\delta]\), which provides a structural prior (e.g., average face structure). The paper provides some empirical evidence that this works in practice (SSIM scores, Figure 3). The criticism is valid as a concern about insufficient evidence (kept above as Major #2), but the claim of impossibility ignores that learned priors can capture dataset-level structure from pure noise inputs.

- **Strength Finder's claim #5 about "Image scale as an efficiently learnable structured prior."** — Removed because the strength conflicts with the verified weakness about the circular dependency. The "learnable structured prior" framing obscures the fact that estimation from pure noise yields the dataset mean, not a sample-specific structure. This is better described as a limitation.

- **Strength Finder's claim #4 about closed-form SNR.** — Downgraded from a core strength to a minor analytical contribution. While mathematically clean, the closed-form SNR is a direct consequence of the chosen exponential schedule and does not by itself constitute a significant result.

## Novel Insights

The reviews surface an interesting tension: the paper's most creative idea (pixel-dependent scheduling) creates the very problem it tries to solve (knowing the image to generate the image). The pixel-dependent schedule offers faster convergence precisely because it tailors the noise process to the image content, but this tailoring creates a circular dependency at inference that must be broken by external estimation of the image structure. This tension is not unique to this paper — it mirrors the challenge in conditional computation and adaptive methods where the optimal adaptation depends on the unknown target. Addressing this circularity rigorously (e.g., with a proper EM-style formulation or a provably convergent iterative estimation) would be a genuinely valuable contribution beyond what the current paper provides.

## Suggestions

1. **Derive the reverse posterior explicitly.** Show that the standard DDPM formulas (Eqns. 4–6) carry over element-wise for the proposed schedule. This is a short derivation that would eliminate the first major concern entirely.

2. **Report VAE estimation quality per noise level.** Provide SSIM of \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_\delta\) for every time-step \(i \in \{1,\dots,T\}\), not as a single range. Show how downstream FID degrades when using estimates from high-noise vs. low-noise inputs.

3. **Run ablation studies.** At minimum: (a) standard DDPM with the proposed pixel-dependent schedule but standard sequential reverse (to isolate schedule benefit), and (b) proposed parallel architecture with a standard scalar schedule (to isolate parallel architecture benefit).

4. **Include comparisons with consistency models and DPM-Solver++ at low step counts** (e.g., 10, 20, 50 steps) to contextualize the claimed speedup.

5. **Show generated samples.** A single figure with unconditional samples from CIFAR-10 and CelebA would significantly strengthen the paper.
