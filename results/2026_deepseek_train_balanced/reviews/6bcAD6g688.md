## Summary

This paper introduces *Docta*, a systematic framework that combines existing techniques (noise transition matrix estimation via k-NN label clusterability + confident learning-based label cleaning) to evaluate and improve the credibility of 11 safety alignment datasets derived from Jigsaw Civil Comments, PKU BeaverTails, PKU SafeRLHF, Anthropic Harmless, and Anthropic Red-Team. The headline finding is an average of 6.16% label errors across these datasets, and the paper shows that training on Docta-cleaned data yields consistent downstream performance improvements across BERT, GPT-2, and Llama2, with external validation via ChatGPT and human annotators on the Jigsaw Toxicity dimension.

## Strengths

- **Large-scale empirical evaluation across 11 datasets from 5 distinct safety alignment benchmarks.** The paper goes beyond typical single-dataset studies, demonstrating that data credibility problems are pervasive rather than isolated. Label error rates range from ~2% to >15%, and the findings are consolidated in Table 1.

- **External validation of label corrections via both ChatGPT and in-house human annotators.** Rather than relying solely on the algorithm's own estimates, Table VIII shows that models trained on Docta-cleaned data achieve substantially higher F1 on test sets where disputed samples were re-annotated by ChatGPT and humans (e.g., BERT: 73.26% vs. 65.69% on ChatGPT Cleaned; GPT-2: 55.16% vs. 41.27% on Human Sampled). This is the strongest evidence that the corrections are real improvements.

- **Consistent downstream performance gains across multiple model architectures (BERT, GPT-2, Llama2) and test paradigms (i.i.d. and consensus).** The gains are particularly striking on the Anthropic Harmless dataset (BERT accuracy: 49.72% → 68.81%, a 19-point absolute improvement), showing the benefit is not model-specific or test-set-dependent.

- **Extension to pairwise preference data.** Section 5.4 shows the framework generalizes beyond binary classification to preference-based ranking tasks (identifying 5.4% erroneous preference annotations in the Anthropic Harmless pairwise data and improving Llama2 accuracy by ~7%), which is directly relevant to RLHF alignment pipelines.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The central k-NN label clusterability assumption (Definition 1) is unvalidated, and the value of k is never specified.** The entire transition matrix estimation and label cleaning pipeline rests on the assumption that k nearest neighbors of any instance in embedding space share the same *true* label. Yet the paper provides no sensitivity analysis over k, no diagnostics on whether this assumption actually holds for these datasets (e.g., via embedding space structural analysis or human verification on a held-out subset), and — critically — never states what value of k was used in the experiments. Without this, the quantitative foundation of the framework is uncertain. The downstream performance improvements provide indirect evidence that the framework works, but the core assumption at the methodological heart of the paper is neither tested nor even parameterized.

### Minor

- **The headline 6.16% label error rate conflates genuine annotation errors with construction artifacts, diluting the quantitative claim.** The paper itself acknowledges in Section 5.2 that the Anthropic Harmless dataset's low initial credibility is "primarily due to the noise induced in the split of pairwise conversations" — specifically, splitting pairwise preference data into individual conversations creates "errors" that are artifacts of the binarization procedure, not original annotation errors. Since the Harmless dataset contributes 15.11% errors (the highest in the study) and Red-Team contributes 12.36%, a substantial fraction of the headline statistic may be construction artifacts. The paper should report these sources separately.

- **The "before vs. after" credibility metric comparison is partially tautological.** The estimated noise transition matrix T and credibility metric Ψ are both computed from the data using the same k-NN clusterability assumption that drives the cleaning procedure. Since cleaning removes instances where labels disagree with neighbors' labels, reporting that T is closer to identity after cleaning is partly a restatement of what the algorithm does. The paper partially addresses this through external validation (ChatGPT/human evaluation on Jigsaw Toxicity), but this validation covers only one of the 11 datasets. A synthetic-noise experiment with known ground-truth labels would strengthen the causal claim about the credibility metric.

- **No variance or confidence intervals are reported.** All numerical results in Tables III–VIII are presented as point estimates without any indication of variance across data splits, random seeds, or model initializations. This makes it impossible to assess the reliability of the reported improvements.

- **The evaluation of the credibility metric and cleaning is externally validated on only one of the 11 datasets.** ChatGPT and human annotation (Table VIII) are only applied to the Jigsaw Toxicity dimension. Expanding this to at least one conversation dataset (e.g., BeaverTails or SafeRLHF) would substantially strengthen the external validity of the claims.

### Trivial

- **The transition matrix display (Tables III–IV) shows only the diagonal entries (T₀₀ and T₁₁) as two numbers.** Showing the full 2×2 matrix would reveal whether off-diagonal noise is symmetric and provide a more complete picture of the noise structure.

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Comparison to simpler baselines.** The paper could strengthen its case by comparing Docta against simple baselines such as removing high-loss instances from a model trained on raw data or training with noise-robust loss functions (e.g., label smoothing, which the paper cites). Showing that Docta's approach is more effective than these would provide a stronger reference point.
- **Synthetic-noise experiment.** Creating a version of one dataset with known ground-truth label flips and showing that the estimated transition matrix and credibility metric recover the known noise rates would directly address the circularity concern.

## Suggestions

- Specify the value of k used for k-NN and provide sensitivity analysis over a range of k values (e.g., 3, 5, 10, 20) to demonstrate that results are not brittle to this choice.
- Report separate statistics for (a) genuine annotation errors vs. (b) construction artifacts introduced by dataset binarization/splitting procedures, particularly for the Anthropic Harmless dataset.
- Extend the ChatGPT/human external validation to at least one conversation-based dataset (e.g., BeaverTails or SafeRLHF) to broaden the evidence that the framework works beyond text toxicity classification.
- Add variance estimates (e.g., standard deviation over 3–5 random seeds) to all main experimental tables.
