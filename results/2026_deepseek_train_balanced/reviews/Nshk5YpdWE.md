## Summary
The paper introduces Lagrangian Flow Networks (LFlows), a method that models time-evolving fluid densities and velocities satisfying the continuity equation (CE) by construction. The key idea is to parameterize the Lagrangian flow map via a single time-conditioned normalizing flow Φ_t, yielding the density as a change-of-variables formula ρ̂(t,x)=ρ_base(Φ_t(x))|det JΦ_t(x)| and the velocity via the Jacobian without inverting the bijection. Experiments on synthetic fluid flow, dynamical optimal transport, and real-world bird migration data compare LFlows against PINNs, DFNNs, and SLDA.

## Strengths
- **By-construction CE satisfaction without PDE penalties or numerical solvers.** The density simplifies to the change-of-variables formula of a conditional normalizing flow (Eq. 13, lines 273‑275), so the CE is exactly satisfied analytically. This fundamentally avoids PINNs' trade-off between collocation point density and GPU memory, which the paper concretely demonstrates (PINNs' consistency sMAPE degrades severely in 3D while LFlows, SLDA, and DFNNs achieve sMAPE ≤ 1e-4).

- **Velocity computed without inverting the bijection.** The velocity expression v̂(t,x)=-(JΦ_t(x))⁻¹ ∂Φ_t/∂t(x) (Eq. 7/15, lines 287‑288) requires only the forward map Φ_t, not its inverse. This is a genuine practical contribution enabling the use of expressive bijective layers with costly or unknown inverses.

- **Principled handling of unknown total mass.** The learnable parameter c for the total mass with a mass penalty L_mass=w_c·c (lines 324‑325) addresses a practical challenge in ill-posed settings and is cleanly integrated into the NF framework.

- **Demonstrated computational advantages over competing methods.** On the bird migration task, LFlows avoid SLDA's increasing per-epoch runtime (Fig. 7e), DFNNs' high peak VRAM usage (Fig. 7f), and PINNs' need for large numbers of collocation points — showing that by-construction CE satisfaction can be had without the computational costs of prior methods.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major
- **All quantitative results are presented only in figures; no numerical tables with precise values, confidence intervals, or multi-run statistics are provided anywhere.** The synthetic experiment states that LFlows "show the highest average R² for the density in both 2D and 3D" (line 369) but gives no numerical R² values — only Figure 2. The bird migration experiment reports test MSE only through Figure 6(c,d). The OT experiment reports Wasserstein distances only through Figure 5. For a methods paper whose central claim is "higher predictive accuracy," the absence of any numerical reporting of the claimed performance metrics is a significant evidential weakness. The reader cannot independently verify the magnitude of improvements, compare effect sizes across experiments, or assess whether differences are within noise.

- **The dynamical optimal transport comparison rests on thin evidence.** Three issues compound: (i) LFlows and DFNNs are evaluated from a single training run ("for a single run," Figure 5 caption), while the discrete OT reference shows statistics over 5 runs — no error bars or variance estimates are provided for LFlows or DFNNs. (ii) The paper states it "was unable to fully reproduce results obtained by Richter et al." for DFNNs (line 425), attributing this to ODE solver instability — meaning the DFNN baseline may be underperforming relative to a properly tuned implementation. (iii) The LFlow λ hyperparameter is chosen by matching the W₂² distance between moons and swissroll with the discrete estimate (line 416) — post-hoc tuning on the evaluation metric. The qualitative conclusion ("LFlow estimates of W₂² are closest to the range of discrete estimates") is plausible but not supported by the statistical rigor expected for a new-method paper.

### Minor
- **The synthetic experiment generates data from LFlows' own model class.** The ground-truth densities and velocities are generated by "transforming a mixture of four unnormalized Gaussians" via "time-dependent bijections" (lines 354‑355) — exactly the same family of transformations that LFlows is designed to represent. The baselines (PINNs, DFNNs, SLDA) do not share this structural prior. The abstract and conclusion claim "higher predictive accuracy" without acknowledging how much of the synthetic advantage derives from this inductive-bias match. The bird migration experiment partially mitigates this, but the text does not qualify the synthetic results.

- **The training loss for setting (i) is not specified.** For the synthetic and bird migration experiments (where both density and velocity are observed), the paper says models are "optimize[d] based on the explained variance (R²) of the density on the validation set" (line 359). This describes the model selection criterion, not the actual training loss. It is unclear how velocity observations are incorporated, what loss weighting is used, or whether the loss combines both ρ and v objectives. The OT experiment (setting ii) provides its objective (Eq. 11), making this omission conspicuous.

- **The 3D synthetic experiment is effectively stacked 2D slices.** The z-velocity is 0 and xy-velocity is independent of z (line 357). What can be concluded about scaling to genuinely coupled 3D flows is limited.

- **The mass penalty weight w_c is introduced (line 325) but its values used in experiments are never reported.**
