## Summary

This paper studies attention entropy collapse in Transformers, comparing softmax-based attention (which it argues causes entropy collapse → gradient explosion → training divergence) with Lipschitz-kernel-based attention (ReLU, ELU+1, which maintains stable entropy and gradients). The paper provides a controlled experiment isolating attention-logit variance as a key factor, proposes a mechanistic link from entropy collapse to gradient explosion via the Frobenius norm of the attention probability matrix ‖P‖_F, and shows that even QK-LayerNorm fails to prevent collapse. The contribution is an empirical analysis on an in-context linear regression toy task.

## Strengths

- **Controlled experiment isolating variance sensitivity (Section 3.3, Figure 3):** Fixing the query as a unit vector and sampling keys from N(0, σ²I) with increasing σ, the paper cleanly demonstrates that softmax-based attention entropy collapses monotonically with increasing logit variance, while Lipschitz-kernel attention entropy remains stable. This directly contrasts the two function classes under identical input conditions, going beyond prior correlational observations.

- **Mechanistic pathway from entropy collapse to gradient explosion via ‖P‖_F (Section 3.5, Equation 9, Figure 5):** The paper connects entropy collapse to training instability by computing that one-hot attention probabilities yield ‖P‖_F = √N (vs. ‖P‖_F = 1 for uniform), which appears in an upper bound on the derivative of self-attention (Dasoulas et al., 2021). Figure 5 empirically validates that during training, softmax's ‖P‖_F grows to ≈√N while Lipschitz kernels maintain ‖P‖_F ≈ 1, with a clear correlation between ‖P‖_F and gradient norm.

- **Demonstration that QK-LayerNorm also fails (Section 3.2, Figure 1):** The paper shows that even after applying QK-LayerNorm — a standard mitigation that normalizes queries and keys — softmax-based attention still produces entropy collapse and gradient explosion. This indicates the issue is deeper than large Q/K magnitudes.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The central explanatory claim confounds Lipschitzness with non-exponentiation.** The paper attributes softmax's entropy collapse to its "non-Lipschitzness" and kernel attention's stability to Lipschitzness (title, abstract, Sections 3.2–3.3). However, softmax differs from ReLU/ELU+1 along multiple dimensions; the most salient is exponentiation vs. sub-linear growth. The experiments never isolate Lipschitzness as the causal factor — a more controlled comparison would involve a Lipschitz approximation to softmax (e.g., softmax with a bounded/clipped exponent) or a non-Lipschitz non-exponential function. As it stands, the paper's core claim conflates Lipschitzness with "does not exponentiate," and the result is essentially that exponential re-weighting causes entropy collapse while linear/sub-linear re-weighting does not — a less surprising finding that does not require the Lipschitzness framing.

- **No experimental hyperparameters or reproducibility information.** The paper contains zero training details: no learning rate, optimizer, batch size, number of layers, hidden dimension D, sequence length N, number of training steps, random seeds, or any measure of variance across runs. The figures show curves without indication of whether they are single runs, averages, or selected examples. For a paper whose entire contribution rests on empirical demonstrations (Figures 1–5), this omission makes it impossible to evaluate the robustness or statistical reliability of the results.

### Minor

- **Gradient explosion argument relies on an upper bound, not a demonstrated causal chain (Section 3.5):** The paper argues that because ‖P‖_F appears in an upper bound on the gradient (Eq. 9) and ‖P‖_F increases during entropy collapse, "the more extreme the distribution... the larger the gradient becomes." An upper bound can be loose — the actual gradient could be much smaller. The paper does provide empirical correlation (Figure 5 Right) partially supporting the connection, but the argument from the bound alone is logically incomplete.

- **Claims are overbroad relative to experimental scope.** The title and framing discuss "Transformer Training Instability" generally, but all experiments use a single toy task (in-context linear regression) with a stripped-down architecture (self-attention layers only, no MLPs, residual connections, or layer normalization). While proxy models are a valid methodology, the paper does not validate whether the observed dynamics manifest in more realistic settings, making it unclear whether the results are a general insight or an artifact of a highly specific fragile setup.

- **Imprecise characterization of softmax Lipschitzness (Sections 2.2, 3.2):** The paper states that softmax is "not Lipschitz continuous." Softmax is Lipschitz on any bounded domain (and during training, logits are finite at each step). The practical issue is that its local Lipschitz constant can grow very large as logit magnitudes increase. This is a technical imprecision rather than an invalidating error.
