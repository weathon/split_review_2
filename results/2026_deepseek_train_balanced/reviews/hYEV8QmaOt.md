## Summary
This paper proposes a two-stage image anti-forensics framework. The first stage, GUIDE, uses a zero-shot diffusion process that guides reconstruction using low-frequency components from authentic (untampered) image regions rather than the tampered image itself. The second stage, the Texture Refinement Module (TRM), is intended to remove residual artefacts. The method is evaluated across 6 forensic detectors and 3 datasets, with ablation studies exploring hyperparameter effects.

## Strengths

- **Novel guidance mechanism using authentic-region low-frequency content**: Section 3 (Eq. 6–9, lines 122–152) formalizes a guidance approach that extracts low-frequency components from *authentic* regions only and combines them with high-frequency components from the diffusion-refined image. This is a clear departure from Diff-cfg (Tailanián et al., 2024), which guides toward the full tampered image. The rationale is explicitly stated: "Rather than guiding the image towards the tampered image itself, which risks reverting the diffused image back into the tampered domain" (line 118). Spectrograms in Fig. 3 provide visual evidence.

- **Demonstrated complementary effect between the two stages**: Section 4.1 (line 178) reports that the GUIDE+TRM combination produces a sharp drop in IF-OSN's F1 score by over 10% compared to either module alone (with SPAN as victim detector). This is specific quantitative evidence that the modules are synergistic rather than redundant.

- **Non-trivial ablation findings**: Section 4.2 shows that the optimal denoising step count T varies across detectors (EITLNet best at T=200, IF-OSN at T=800, MMFusion-IML at T=200) and that the relationship is "not monotonous but rather dynamically changing" (line 263). This goes beyond simple hyperparameter tuning and provides genuine insight into how GUIDE interacts with different forensic trace types.

- **Broad evaluation across diverse forensic detectors**: The paper evaluates against 6 detectors (SPAN, MVSS-Net, IF-OSN, TruFor, MMFusion-IML, EITLNet) covering RGB, noise, frequency, and multi-modal traces, across 3 datasets (CASIAv2, COVERAGE, IMD2020). This is broader than typical anti-forensics evaluations.

- **Directly addresses the visual-quality gap in prior work**: Section 2.2 (line 47) explicitly identifies that prior methods "fall short of deceiving the human eye, even when they successfully evade forensic detection" and sets visual authenticity as a design goal, with Table 2 reporting image quality metrics.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

- **TRM (Texture Refinement Module) is never specified in the main paper body.** The paper repeatedly names TRM as a core component of the two-stage framework — in the abstract, introduction, Figure 2, the approach overview (line 116), and throughout results (lines 178, 192) and ablation (line 199). Yet the main paper contains zero description of what TRM is, how it works, its architecture, whether it is learned or hand-crafted, its inputs/outputs, or its hyperparameters. Section 3 describes GUIDE in full detail with equations, a low-pass filtering scheme, a resampling algorithm, and named hyperparameters. TRM receives only "TRM subsequently refines the image by addressing texture inconsistencies" (line 116) and "removing forgery artefacts via the Texture Refinement Module." The results table (Table 1) evaluates GUIDE+TRM and compares it against other methods, but since TRM is unspecified, a reader cannot determine whether the performance comes from GUIDE (which is described), from a sophisticated second module, or from a trivial post-processing step. For a paper claiming a two-stage contribution, both stages must be specified in the main text. **(If the supplementary material — typically stripped by the PDF parser — contains the full TRM specification, the main paper still lacks even a minimal description.)**

- **Circular dependency between mask source and evaluation target.** The method uses a localisation mask produced by the *victim detector* D (line 120: "a localisation mask m, generated by the victim detector D, is obtained") and later measures success against that same detector. Line 180 confirms that TruFor is the detector producing the mask: "not all detectors produce the same localisation results as TruFor, which leads to GUIDE refining only limited regions." This creates a concerning evaluation loop: the method uses TruFor's own mask to identify what to refine, and then measures success in fooling TruFor (among others). If TruFor's mask misses some artefacts or covers only a subset of the actual tampering, GUIDE refines only those regions — and TruFor's subsequent failure to detect may reflect only that the method patched what TruFor already found, not that the forgery is genuinely removed. For other detectors (SPAN, MVSS-Net, IF-OSN, etc.) that produce different masks or analyse different trace modalities, the dependency is less circular but still means the method's success against them depends on which artefacts TruFor's mask happens to cover. The paper acknowledges this partially (line 180) but treats it as a limitation rather than a confound in the evaluation.

### Minor

- **Image cropping systematically filters the evaluation set.** All images are centre-cropped to 256×256 (lines 165–166), and images whose tampered region is cropped out are "moved to the authentic test set." This means the test set is filtered to remove hard cases. For CASIAv2 and COVERAGE — where tampered regions are often not centred — this could exclude a substantial fraction of genuine manipulations, artificially making the anti-forensics task easier. The paper does not report how many images were moved per dataset, what fraction was retained, or whether the retained subset is representative.

- **Weak and incomplete baseline comparison.** The comparison set (line 163) includes Blur & Sharp and Downsize & Upsize — trivial image-level operations that any serious anti-forensics paper would outperform. These inflate the comparison count without providing meaningful competitive pressure. Additionally, StatAttack (Hou et al., 2023) and Wu et al. (2024) are discussed in Related Work (lines 43–45) as anti-forensics approaches but are not included as baselines.

- **Image quality metrics not named in the main text.** Table 2 reports "non-reference image quality results" and "reference-quality results" (lines 182–183) but the actual metric names (e.g., FID, NIQE, PIQE, BRISQUE, PSNR, SSIM) are not stated. This makes it impossible to interpret the quality-vs-anti-forensics trade-off that the paper claims to explore.

- **Ablation sample selection not documented.** The ablation study uses 108 images from IMD2020 (line 199), but how these were selected (random subset? first N? filtered to meet the 256×256 crop constraint?) is not stated. With such a small sample relative to the full dataset, selection bias is a real concern.

### Trivial

- **"Zero-shot" terminology is inflated.** The paper describes GUIDE as "zero-shot learning-based" (abstract, line 8) and "zero-shot diffusion-based refinement" (line 32). Using a pre-trained diffusion model without fine-tuning is standard practice and is more accurately described as "training-free" or "pre-trained without adaptation." Conventional "zero-shot learning" implies generalisation to unseen classes or tasks, which is not demonstrated.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Decouple the mask source from the evaluation target by evaluating the method with masks from multiple detectors (or ground-truth masks) and reporting how anti-forensics performance changes across each combination.
- Report the fraction of images discarded per dataset due to the 256×256 cropping protocol, and repeat key experiments with a resizing protocol (rather than centre-cropping) to establish that results are not artifacts of the cropping filter.
- Include StatAttack and Wu et al. (2024) as baselines, or provide a clear explanation of why they are not directly comparable.

## Suggestions
1. **Fully specify TRM in the main paper**: state its architecture (learned CNN? bilateral filter? additional diffusion pass?), its inputs/outputs, its training procedure (if any), and its hyperparameters. If TRM is a simple post-processing step, say so explicitly.
2. **Break the evaluation loop**: evaluate the method using masks from multiple detectors (including but not limited to TruFor) and report how performance changes when the mask source and evaluation target are the same vs. different.
3. **Report cropping statistics**: specify the number/fraction of images moved to the authentic test set per dataset. Run a sensitivity analysis with a resizing protocol to rule out cropping artifacts.
4. **Name the image quality metrics in the main text** (e.g., "non-reference (NIQE/PIQE/BRISQUE) and reference (PSNR/SSIM/FID) quality metrics").
5. **Document ablation subset selection criteria** for the 108-image sample.
6. **Add or justify exclusion** of StatAttack and Wu et al. (2024) as baselines.

## Score and Decision
The paper proposes a genuinely novel guidance mechanism (GUIDE) that is well-described and evaluated across a broad set of detectors, with non-trivial ablation results. However, two major issues prevent acceptance in its current form: (1) TRM, presented as half of the claimed two-stage contribution, is completely unspecified in the main paper body — making the reported results for the full framework uninterpretable; (2) the circular dependency between the mask source (TruFor) and the evaluation target creates a latent confound that undermines confidence in the results, particularly on the TruFor detector. These are not fatal flaws — GUIDE itself is a viable contribution that could stand alone — but they are serious enough that the paper cannot be accepted as written.
